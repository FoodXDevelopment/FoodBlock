## 1. The centre

### 1.1 Five questions (D2, as decided; question 5 as amended by E1)

> **1. Who said it:** which key said these bytes.
>
> **2. Whose it is:** a word acts only on what is its signer's own; a word on what is wholly hers needs no other's word.
>
> **3. How much:** a word asking for more than is left takes nothing; a word naming no lot takes from none.
>
> **4. From what a reader answers:** only from what she holds; where the record does not order two words, neither came first; never "nobody" where a signed word names someone.
>
> **5. From when it stands:** when every condition its signer stated has held; what stands stays, except where one key signed twice in one place, or where her own higher voice cuts off what a lower key of hers said after a point it names, which takes back nothing for anyone who already built on it.

In these five lines "lot" is said for a measure (Draws). Every other protocol text says "measure".

Each question's full sentence, and where it is written:

**Question 1** *(core.md, section 8, opening).* `valid` answers only: which key said these bytes. Where the block sits on its line, and whether this reader can read it, are other questions. *core*

**Question 2** *(core.md, section 10).* A record holds only words that people signed: no fact, no silence and no ruling is in it. A word acts only on what is its signer's own; whatever of another's it touches waits for that other's own word naming it. A word on what is wholly its signer's own needs no other's word: it waits only on what she herself stated before it (question 5). *core*

**Question 3** *(folds.md, What is left, opening).* An owner's words that name a measure take from it in order (question 4), along the one line each part of it is counted on, and nothing counts past what is left. A word for more than is left takes nothing and is never cut down: nobody signed the smaller figure. The amount stays its owner's on whatever line it is counted, and leaves that line only as her word says. A word that names no measure takes from none. *core*

<!-- REMOVED "Nothing orders two lines but a ref, and what cannot be ordered is not ordered." | guarded: every reader, against a guessed order | now: question 4's order sentences, which say the same and name the answer ("neither came first"). -->

**Question 4** *(core.md, section 10; folds.md, A line).* A reader answers only from words she holds, on a line read whole back to its start; otherwise she says what she holds and what she lacks, and never "nobody" where she holds a signed word that names someone. A word is after every word it names, and after the one before it on its line. Nothing else shows which word was made first: no date, no arrival, no hash, no size of past. So across two lines the record shows only after: never before, and never that nothing came. Before and nothing-between are shown only along one line read whole. Where a rule needs to know which of two words came first and the record does not show it, neither came first. *core*

**Question 5** *(blocks.md, Finality, first and last sentences).* A word stands when every condition its signer stated before it has held: its own, and those of each word it is made on. Until then it moves nothing. What stands stays: no fold moves it back. More words lower an answer only where they show that one key signed two things for one place, or where a key grant by her own voice above a key names in `state.counted_to` the last block of that key that counts: "nothing from that key after this point counts". That key's later blocks are then off her line toward every block that does not have them in its past; for anyone whose block already built on one, it takes back nothing. *core*


### 1.2 The four rules every part reads, written once

Each is stated here and in one home file. Every other text points to it and holds no copy of its own.

**Rule A. The receiver's door.** *(folds.md, Freed.)* Only the one a held thing would go to can give it up before it lands. Her own counted `declined` or `stopped` answer frees it, whether or not her yes came before. An end by its giver with her yes in its past frees nothing, except where the hold waits on payment (`terms.waits_on: price`) and no block of the giver's side accepting a payment toward the price is in the end's causal past. *core* (the exception: the meaning of the giver's own term)

**Rule B. A word given twice binds her to no more than she signed.** *(folds.md, A fork is a burden.)* Two words of one key at one place bind that key's signer's name, and never the one it speaks for past what she signed: across every line and receipt, at most her measures. A thing drawn twice lands with neither where the draws do not fit; where all fit, each takes; what no draw names stays its signer's. *core*

**Rule C. Where the record shows no order between two words, neither came first.** *(Question 4; folds.md, A line.)* No answer changes with the order a fold reads words in. A word is counted against another only where the other is in its causal past. *core*

**Rule D. A chosen rule is a value written in the word it binds.** *(core.md, section 11.)* A rule that is not derived binds a person only as a value in a word she signed, or in a block that word names. No value is read into a block from a block it does not name. Where a rule could honestly be otherwise, acting only on what is its signer's own, it is a field with stated values that every reader of the one engine reads. No value makes a thing land without its receiver's yes, cuts a draw down to what is left, or makes one amount count on two lines at once: each would act on another's thing. *core* (the rule); *her word* (each value); *convention* (each field)

### 1.3 What follows at once

Pointers, each written once in its home file.

- **The record, never the right.** *(core.md, section 10.)* A fold is arithmetic over words. Nobody signed its answer, so it acts on nothing. Who is right is a judge's word. Every answer here is the record: what signed words show.
- **A key's block is counted as hers because her list names the key.** *(identity.md.)* The record cannot tell a taken key from her hand. It never says the block is her act. What her key grant hands on of hers is her own word (People, What a key hands on).
- **An end counts from where it is shown.** *(blocks.md, The cut.)*
- **A hidden term touches nobody.** A limit, a condition or a clock the other side cannot read is no word shown to him.
- **Order is read only where two words act on one thing of one owner:** an amount, a lot, her consent. *(A note for builders; it changes no answer.)*
- **A signer may rank words by any signed field as a condition on his own thing** ("to the accept stating the earliest time", "to the highest price"). Whether a reader holds them all is the line question. *core*

### 1.4 For writers (not protocol text)

- **The sort.** For each rule, name the signed field that marks the thing a change to it would touch as someone else's. If one can be named, the rule is derived: core. If the rule only says that nothing is filled in, it is silence: core. If no field can be named and no signer's word can reach it (the shape of a sum, of a line, of the bytes), it is a convention. Otherwise it is chosen, and it is a value in her word (Rule D). Whose a thing is, for this test, is read only from signed fields, never from a fold's answer.
- **The backward check.** Before a sentence leaves the protocol, name whose own thing it guarded and what of hers guards it after. If nothing of hers does, the sentence stays. This page does it in a comment beside each sentence it removes.
- **The landing rule.** A change that alters an answer on unchanged bytes lands in the first locked set or never. *convention*

### The block and its questions

- **Header.** `{ author, seq, prev, hash }`, plus `key` when the block has `refs.key`, and `skip: true` when the block says so. Every block but a genesis is signed over its header; a genesis is signed over its own bytes and has no header. The header carries nothing else: no `draws`, no `at`, no `after`. *(privacy.md, The public header; core.md, section 2.)*
- **`refs.key`.** On every block signed by a key other than its author's genesis key: the id of the key grant that brought that key in. The header repeats it as `key`, so a header alone names its pieces. The key grant is a `grant` of kind `key` whose `refs.subject` is the block's author; its own author may be another (one of a voice of named people). The keys it brings in are each `key` voice of the list it states, the one `public_key_sign` it adds under a voice, and the key it states in `shown_key` when that key's hash is a `key_hash` voice of the list its own `refs.key` names. *(core.md, section 5.)*
- **A block's pieces.** Its author's genesis, the block its `refs.key` names, and that block's pieces. Nothing else: not `prev`, and not a grant it acts under. Whoever presents a block carries its pieces. *(core.md, section 8.)*
- **Rests on.** A block rests on its pieces, and only on them. `valid` waits for its pieces and nothing else. A line is read, never rested on.
- **Names for its meaning.** `refs.under`, `refs.answers`, `refs.updates`, `refs.for`, `refs.parent`, `refs.schema`, `state.counted_to`, a grant's `scope.on`. `protocol.json` `needs` lists exactly these.
- **Valid.** Which key said these bytes: question 1. It reads five things in order: the size, the bytes (no fraction, no whole number past 2^53 − 1), the header (`author`, `prev`, `key` one id each), the pieces, the signature (the id first, then the key over the header that names that id) (core.md, section 8). It judges no value.
- **Read.** This reader can read the block: it holds the body, every value follows its grammar and its schema, and every block it names for its meaning is held, valid and read (`names_missing`, `names_unread`). A block that fails is **not read**: kept as its header, listed with its code, counted in no fold, never refused. Every judgement of a value is `read`'s.
- **`unjudged`.** A block a node holds whose pieces are not all held, or whose suite it does not know; kept, counted in no fold, judged when it can be. Not `waiting`.
- **The chain fold.** Where a block sits on its line: gaps, pairs, blocks out of place, skips. A reading. Nothing waits on it.

### Lines

<!-- REMOVED "Line. One key's blocks, ordered by `seq` and joined by `prev`." | guarded: every reader, against a block sold again under a low `seq` | now: In its place (below): `seq` numbers the line and never orders it; a block out of its place takes nothing. -->

- **Line.** One key's blocks, joined by `prev`. A clock's receipts are a line like any other.
- **In its place.** A block is in its place on its key's line where its `prev` names a block of that key and its `seq` is above that block's, or, for a key's first block, where its `prev` names the first block that brought that key in and its `seq` is 1. A key has one line: of several blocks that bring one key in, the first is the one the others have in their causal past; two apart start none (Rule C). A block whose `prev` the reader does not hold is not shown, never out of place. A block out of its place takes nothing (`out_of_place`). A block whose `prev` names neither a block of its key nor the first block that brought its key in is on no line of that key: a draw it signs takes nothing (`not_on_line`), and it is no block of a pair. Each binds its signer as a block of a fork does. A fold reads a line in `prev` order; `seq` numbers the line and never orders it. *(folds.md, A line.)* *core*; *convention* for `seq` 1.
- **Start.** The block where the line a question reads begins. Each fold that reads a line names its start where it is defined: a draw on a measure, the block that speaks for her where she came to own her amount (an output is its inputs' owner's; her line for it starts at the first block that speaks for her and names it; a maker's block never gives the maker another's output); a block under a grant, the block the grant names in `refs.meter`; a clock's reading for a word, its receipt of the word.
- **One start.** *(folds.md, What is left.)* An amount that comes to her starts on the line of the voice her list marks `receives: true` (one voice at most; `shape` otherwise), else her first voice, at her yes signed by that voice's key (of several, the one the others have in their past; two apart start none). Another key's accept of it is her yes and starts nothing; a draw on it by that other key takes nothing (`not_on_line`). A part she passes from one of her keys' lines to another starts on the line of the key it is handed to. *her word* (the field `receives`, D7); *core* for the default, her first voice (E3).

- **Read whole.** A line is read whole for a question when each block on it, from the block back to the start, the two ends included, is held and valid, and each block that names the thing asked about is read. A block on the line that this reader holds and does not read hides each thing it could name: held as a body, each id in its `refs` but its line's own links (`author`, `prev`, `key`, `after`); held only as a header, each measure that key's line held at its place, and each word of that key it could end. A block held with its body that this reader cannot judge (`unjudged`) hides each thing its refs name. A question whose answer could turn on such a thing answers `not_shown`, the block in `lacks`. The line is what `prev` joins: a `seq` that jumps with no `skip` is no hole. A later rule counts only in a block that names it. *(folds.md, A line.)* *core*
- **`not_shown`, `lacks`, `read`.** A fold whose line is not read whole answers `{ not_shown: true, lacks }` in place of its answer: in `lacks` each block it would need (its id where a held block's `prev` names it, or where it is held only as its header or not read; past a block not held, `{ author, key, seq }` down to the next block of that key held, none under a `skip`; ids first, then the entries, each in the order of its canonical text). A fold that lists what waits lists it by block, then by `on`. Where an answer turns on a block not held, or held and not read, it is `not_shown`, never `not_owner`; a draw by one who owns none of the measure anywhere is `not_owner`, whatever her own line lacks. Every ownership answer names the blocks it read (`read`, sorted ids): the thing, the block that starts each owner's line for it, each draw listed, and each yes that landed one. A reader that stops before the end of a line (its own limit; the protocol fixes none) answers `not_shown` with `{ stopped: "depth" }` in `lacks`.
- **A line goes on.** A line is continued on another key by one block, signed by those who may speak for the line's owner there, that names in `state.counted_to` the last block of the old line that counts. A question is then read whole through that block and on back along the old line. A holder's own line under a grant does not go on: the giver signs a new grant. A clock's line goes on across its key change.
- **Pair.** Two blocks of one key that name one `prev` or state one `seq` (a block on no line of its key is none). A pair is read from its two headers alone. **Twins `for` one deal** are two blocks of a pair that name the same deal in `refs.for`, the same receiver and the same amount: a retry of one sale to one buyer; they count as one draw, each listed with its answer. Two key grants of one voice, neither in the other's causal past, are a pair of that voice.
- **A count counts ids** *(folds.md, Counts).* An id counts once, by any key that stands for it where the block stands. A count is a whole number of named ids, never `all` and never a share of a set that can move.

### Standing

- **Stands.** A word stands when every condition its signer stated before it has held: its own, and those of each word it is made on (her list for its key: its `wait`, its `reach` and her line's bounds; the grant it is under; the rows of the circle it is `as`; the deal it is `for`; each block it names for its meaning; and the ends it must pass, The cut). All must hold; no order among them is needed. *(blocks.md, Finality.)*
- **Her own word stands alone.** A block joined to no other person's block (it names none, and none names it as her yes), and made on no word but her own whose ends only her own voices sign, stands against those ends when signed. A key grant by her voice above that key whose `counted_to` is before it, or a pair at its place, puts it off her line toward every block that does not have it in its past (question 5). Nothing else does. A block another person's block is joined to is read by the rest of The cut. *(blocks.md, The cut.)* *core*
- **`wait` and `reach`.** Each voice may state `wait` and `reach` (`PT0S` states none). Nothing fills her silence: a voice that states no `wait` lets no word that lets a key or a person act for her stand by itself (a voice above says it); one that states no `reach` holds her moves for no time (end pass 2026-11-20, decided by "nothing fills silence": silence grants no one power, and asks for no delay). `reach`: a word of this key that moves, gives up or lets go anything of hers stands once her clocks have held it that long. `wait`: a word of this key that lets a key or a person act or spend for her, or more than before, stands once this key has said it again after that long on her clocks, or a voice above has said it. At `PT0S` it stands when signed. To **say it again** is to sign a later counted block of the same key that names the word in `refs.confirms` and has in its causal past the cutoffs that closed the wait. *(identity.md.)*
- **Waiting.** A word that does not yet stand. It moves nothing to anyone; a draw that is waiting still holds its amount against its own owner. A fold lists it in `waiting: [{ block, on }]` (`wait`, `reach`, `confirm`, `end`, `named`, `hands`, `condition`); a draw held because its condition has not held is listed `on: condition`.
- **Relier.** The author of a counted block that is joined to a word (answers it, accepts or names a block made under it, or acts on it), who is not the word's signer, not a tool of hers, and not acting under a grant she gave, and whose block has not seen the end.
- **Seen.** A block has seen an end where the end is in its causal past as the reader reads it (bodies only). Where the word names a clock, a clock's line counts for a block only through the receipts the relying block holds. Nothing else is seen: not when an end reached a node, not a date on it.

### Draws

- **Measure.** An amount its owner stated: a thing's or a circle's `pack`, an open offer's `terms.quantity` or that of a promise a grant names in `scope.on`, a grant's `scope.limit`, what a side of a binding deal names it to receive.
- **Draw.** A counted block that speaks for an owner, names a measure, and takes an amount of what she owns of it: a transfer `to_owner`, a let-go, a make naming it in `state.inputs`, an allocation, a payout, a block under a limited grant, a grant passed on, a grant that names an instance in `refs.what` (at its `state.amount`), a grant that names a promise in `scope.on`. A block that names an instance of its signer's and a receiver is a draw on that instance, whether it is a transfer or an offer (its giver's own first promise to a named receiver other than himself, holding its `terms.quantity` in the instance's unit, else all); a yes to an order holds nothing, and a block that names only a kind or an amount holds nothing. A transform's `inputs` are draws on the thing they name, in the pair test as everywhere. A draw signed off the measure's line takes nothing (`not_on_line`).
- **A part passed.** Another line draws on an owner's amount only from a part her word passed there. The part stays its owner's, counted on the line it left. What the new line has not drawn comes back to the line that passed it, by the holder's own word at any time, or where the passing word has ended. Coming back needs no accept.
- **Read once, along the line.** A fold reads each owner's draws on a measure once, from her start in `prev` order, against a running amount. Whether a draw takes is read from its own causal past and nothing else: what came to her counts for a later draw of hers only where the draw that brought it is in that draw's past. Of words that do not see each other, each holds against its signer; none makes a draw take on an amount another line holds. No fold orders draws by hash, by the size of a causal past, by `seq` across lines, by a date, or by arrival.
- **Hold, held.** From where it is signed on the line, a draw's amount is out of what its owner may draw on: it **holds**, and its state is `held` until it lands or is freed.
- **Lands.** The amount becomes the receiver's where the draw stands and every word it names is in the causal past: her yes and its condition. A draw that is an offer lands, or is freed, exactly where the accept that answers it binds or does not; a transfer of its giver `for` it performs it, takes its hold over, and the offer is landed where that transfer lands. A draw whose receiver's yes is already in its own causal past lands where it is signed.
- **Freed.** The amount is its owner's again, for her later draws that have the freeing in their causal past: by Rule A; by an end of its giver that she has seen before any yes, or, where the draw names a clock, once the lines that read no hold the end with no yes before it; by a condition that can no longer hold, read only from its clock's own readings (a condition that can never be shown can no longer hold from its signing). What a deal bound under a grant holds above what came comes back where the rest of that deal can no longer fall due: by the payee's signal that it is done; by her stop, where the offer said ahead that a stop ends what has not come (`terms.stoppable: true`); by a cutoff through the offer's `deliver_by` on `needed` lines of the clocks it names (`refs.subject` the offer); or by a ruling of a judge both sides named (her `observe` of kind `ruling`, `refs.subject` the offer, `state.ends_rest: true`; named by a `grant` of kind `referee` one side signs for the offer, which the other side answers `accepted`). It comes back as the quantity not come, or, in money, the total at that quantity. Her stop alone ends none of the rest, and so frees none of it (D5). *(folds.md, Freed.)*
- **What landed stays.** A payment reversed after the draw landed and stood moves nothing back. Whose the goods are, where a word was not its owner's, is a judge's word.
- **A let-go, and its first taker.** What a let-go took is no one's; the first use that takes it after is no draw. Two first takers whose uses do not see each other: neither came first, neither takes, and both are listed.
- **Reasons a draw takes nothing:** `past`, `not_on_line`, `pair`, `out_of_place`, `unit`, `no_measure`, `not_owner`. One list, in `protocol.json`. A fold lists draws as `draws: [{ block, by, to, amount, takes, why, state }]`, `state` one of `held`, `landed`, `freed`.

### Amounts

- **An amount** is `{ value, unit }` or `{ value, unit, of }`: `value` a decimal string of zero or more; `unit` the author's own text; `of` the id of the kind it is an amount of. Two amounts are of one kind when their `unit` is the same text and their `of` the same id, or neither has one. Money is an amount.
- **A figure a fold prints** is the exact value of signed numbers: the shortest decimal text, of any length, where it has one; else `n/d`, two whole numbers in lowest terms, which no block may sign. *(encoding.md, A sum.)* *convention*
- **Nothing is rounded.** Prices are tested by multiplying, never by dividing. Where a total is finer than anything payable, only the payee's word closes the gap: her `covers`, her signal, or the `step` her price names.
- **`step`.** Under a price, the least payable amount its signers take, an amount in the price's unit. The total is the least whole number of steps at or above the exact figure. With no `step`, the total is exact. *her word*
- **Paid only by the payee's word, or by the very thing named.** A block `for` a deal counts toward one half of it in one of two ways only: a draw on the instance the half names, where it lands, at what landed; or the word of the side that half goes to, in that half's `unit`. Where she said nothing in that unit, the block counts nothing; a draw on any other instance counts only by her word. `paid` is null until a block counts. No fold compares `of`. *(folds.md, The other side.)* *core*
- **Once.** The words of one side about one block count once, at the most any of them says: her own words, and each word of a tool that speaks for her. A word by a signer who does not speak for that side is that signer's reading. What came beyond is shown (`uncovered`). *core*
- **Her order.** Her order, where it is in the block's causal past and states that it is her word that the goods are in her hand (`terms.in_hand: true`), counts the block toward what moves to her, for no more than it states. An order that does not say so counts nothing toward what came. No order counts toward a price (D4). *her word* in the term.
- **Where it came** is the payee's word. One account word holds a list of her places: `destinations: [{ destination, by }]`; its head is the one no other of hers has in its causal past; two heads that do not see each other name none.
- **Added only within one measure.** No fold sums payments across deals or places.
- **`uncovered`** is `{ from, to }` at every stage: what each side handed over `for` the deal and was not paid for.

### Clocks

- **A clock.** *(blocks.md, A named clock.)* In anything a person reads, a person or service she picked to say when things happened, who signs "I held this then". In the bytes, one name in `clocks: { names, needed }` in the word whose time or order it reads. Each name is a block id and names a line, read as it goes on: a person's id, her genesis key's line; a key grant that adds one key under a voice, that key's line; any other block, its key's blocks after it. A receipt counts only where its key spoke for that clock's owner at that block. Nobody need name one, and none is ever filled in. *core* (the reading); *convention* (the field, renamed from `keepers`, D15).
- **Receipt.** A clock's counted `observe` of kind `receipt`, `refs.derives_from` the blocks it names, one or more: when it signed it, it held them. A receipt that names several blocks gives them no order. It states what its line shows for the word: that no end of the word is before it; for a meter, the running total. A receipt is its clock's word: it counts against what is the clock's owner's whenever it is held, and against another's only where his own word named that clock. A receipt names blocks of one pusher only.
- **Cutoff.** A clock's counted `observe` of kind `cutoff`: `refs.subject` the word, `state.when` the word's own text for that time: an instant, or for a span the span itself (a duration), with `refs.derives_from` the receipt or earlier cutoff it runs from; a span cutoff closes a span no longer than its own (durations compared, never added to a date). On its line it is that time's place: a receipt before it came before the time, a receipt after it came after; a receipt with no cutoff of the time before it on its line came before the time (every later cutoff is after it; stage 5 compare). A cutoff whose own `at` is before its `when` closes nothing: its own date shows it early, against its own signer; one that states no `at`, and a span's, is never early. *convention*
- **Which reading needs `needed`.** On a clock, `needed` lines must show the reading that moves a thing out of its signer's hand, or widens what a word allows (a block in time, a wait run, a reach run). The other reading takes `names` less `needed`, plus one. They never both hold, and once every line has spoken one of them holds. *core*
- **One receipt that names an end and a block.** Neither came first on that line (Rule C): the receipt shows the block neither before nor after the end. A later receipt of the block on that line is after the end (D-also). *core*
- **Read in the past of the block that relies on it.** No reading of a clock reaches outside that block's causal past, bar whether an end stands: a key grant a block names for its meaning is read, for it, within its own causal past.
- **A period** turns at the cutoffs of one line its bound names: its meter, which may be the holder's own line, or one clock of its list; a cutoff turns it where its `refs.subject` is the bound's block (the grant; for a voice, the block that brought its key in) and its `when` is a span no shorter than `every`. Only `limit` is renewed: a part is never. *her word*
- **Silence.** When a clock is silent, what waits on it waits; nothing fills its silence. A gate is never the only way: a voice above passes it.
- **A side's clock.** A clock that is a side of the word counts against that side by every receipt it signs, and against the other side only where his signed word names it (D16 of 2026-11-11).
- **A door log.** A node's line of receipts for one person's door, on a key it keeps for that door alone: what it took in for her, each receipt sealed to her and to the pusher. *(nodes.md, The arrival log.)*
- **`dropped`.** A node's counted `observe` of kind `dropped` on the same log, `refs.derives_from` the blocks (or receipts) it no longer holds: its honest drop, shown on the line that took them in.
- **`log`, `seen`.** Two fields of a pull: `log` names the log the asker reads, by the id of the block that brought its key in; `seen` names a receipt on that log the asker holds, and an answer to it is the node's word that it read its log at least that far.

### People, keys and acts

- **A voice** is a key her standing list names (a `key`, a `key_hash`, or a count of named people). It speaks as her, whole, within the bounds her list states: `wait`, `reach` and a line's bounds. Each bound is changed only by a voice above it, the first voice's by itself; only the first voice changes `clocks`, and only the first voice adds or changes a voice of other people. A **key under a voice** is a key a key grant adds below one voice, outside the list.
- **A hidden voice** `{ key_hash }` names its key by the hash of its SPKI text, with no `suite` field. A voice this reader cannot read keeps its place in the list, is compared by its bytes, and is a hole only in the lines of the keys at or below its place. Her genesis shows its own key. A genesis may state her whole list in `state.voices`; its own key is a `key` voice of it (`shape` otherwise).
- **What a key hands on.** A key grant is her word that the key's blocks are counted on her line. By itself it hands on nothing of hers that she did not sign with her own hand. A voice that states `passes: true` is her word that a thing of hers this key signs over is the receiver's, toward every block that has not seen that voice's end (D11). *her word*; *core* for the silent meaning.
- **The people of a voice reach above it** only where her list says so, by `above: true` on that voice (D8, E2): a voice of other people whose `wait` is above zero, placed by her first voice, may sign a key grant that puts a new key in a place above its own, or that leaves no voice; it stands only when `needed` of them have signed it again after their wait has run on her clocks, and no voice above them said no in that wait. An old list keeps meaning what it meant.
- **A close.** A key grant that leaves no voice. For every block that has it in its causal past, no key of hers speaks. It is an end of every standing word of hers except a transfer conditioned on her close (a will): an end by its giver of each draw of hers, read as any end of hers (Freed), so it frees a draw where its receiver has seen the close before any yes, an offer no yes answers at once, and never a draw that landed. What another accepted, before the close or unseen by it, stands as signed (stage 5 compare). A close is for good.
- **A will** is her own transfer on the condition that her list closes. While she can sign it holds nothing; an accept of it before the close is a request. There is no "everything else" rule and no successor (D9). *Lands with stage 5 (owner's ruling, 2026-11-19).*
- **A line's bounds.** Her list gives a voice, and her grant gives a tool, one line with the same bounds: `each`, `limit`, `every`, `meter`. A key grant that adds a key under a capped voice passes it a part by its own `limit` (and an `each` no higher), a draw on the voice's line. Before one who does not hold her line whole, a voice's `limit` is read only from one receipt of a clock or meter her list names that states the running total; with neither, only `each` bounds a word for him (DECIDED 11 of 2026-11-11).
- **A passkey signature** counts only where its flags state that the user was verified; a touch alone signs nothing (D10).
- **`identity_state`** is a word op (stage 7, D2): it answers which voice receives, which keys carry `passes`, and her `clocks`, which CORE-SET 3b-8, 3b-11 and 3d-3 read.
- **A tool** is an actor of its own acting under a grant she gave. No one stands over another's key. A machine or an AI speaks for a person only under her grant.
- **Type, act, move, deed.** A **type** is what a grant names (`scope.types`, the seven or `type.kind`). An **act** is the name a person gives to what she is doing; a reading, no field. A **move** is one signed step. A **deed** is something done in the world.
- **A named signer** is one some other person's word names to say a thing: a clock, a meter, a counter, a payment confirmer, a grader, a referee, a person counted in her voices.

### Grants (stage 4; text not in the one set)

- **Keeps to.** A promise C keeps to a promise A when C's `state` and `refs` are A's key for key, bar a closed list of eight: what every block has of its own (`state.draws` and `refs.as` among it: who speaks is no term of the deal, 1.9 stage 6); `state.audience`, where C is public or sealed to the grantor and the holder; C's links (`refs.for` naming A or a promise that keeps to A); a side A leaves open, which C names (C names both sides); where A names kinds, instances of them in C, each kind with one; one value inside a range or a list A states, at `terms.price`, `terms.quantity`, `terms.pay_by`, `terms.deliver_by`, or a line's `price` or `quantity` (lines matched by `id`); a price, where A states none, she pays it, and the other side signs C; C's own `expires`, and `state.occurrence` where A carries `repeat`. A term A does not carry, C does not carry. A seller never writes a date into her silence (D3). A duration is compared with a duration only, by the span each adds to one fixed instant (2000-01-01T00:00:00Z), never added to a date. *(authority.md, Keeps to.)*
- **A figure inside a range** binds the range's owner only by her own side's word. Two ranges meet in their overlap (D6). Her tool may quote any figure inside her signed range, by any formula; the buyer's yes names the figure.
- **A side admitted** (G1, stage 5 compare). A block under a grant deals only with sides the grant admits: an id `parties` lists, a side a promise of hers in `scope.on` names (her own word named him), or a side of a deal a block under the same grant made; where the grant states no `parties`, a side an `on` promise leaves open admits anyone.
- **`refs.meter`.** One id: the start of one line on which draws are counted, read by one rule. On a grant, the line every block under it is counted on; every grant that can bind (one with `scope.on` or `refs.what`) names one. On a circle's founding block or the version that stands, the line every draw that speaks for the circle is counted on (a version that names none leaves the circle with none). An open grant binds only through a meter she names, a key not the holder's.
- **`scope.deadline`.** A time in her grant, read on the line `refs.meter` names: when her tool must have chosen among offers that keep to her promise. Before and after it are places on that line: a cutoff through it (`refs.subject` the grant, `state.when` its text). With a deadline and no `scope.fallback`, her tool picks.
- **`scope.fallback`.** A closed list of one, `lowest`, stated only with `scope.deadline`, read on the line `refs.meter` names (`scope.clocks` may name more than one line). With it, nothing under the grant accepts before the deadline; after it, only her tool's accept of the lowest total at her quantity among the offers that keep to her promise, received on that line before its cutoff through the deadline and standing there, counts (D7 of 2026-11-11; T2-12).
- **`scope.ends_on`.** Her own ending term on a grant (1.9 stage 6, the owner's decision): `{ witness, said }`. The grant counts for nothing for every block that has in its causal past a counted witness (`observe` of kind `witness`) signed by that host, saying exactly `said`, `for` the grant (its `state.handle` the grant's own, where the grant names one). Read in causal order, as her own stop is. The witness binds only the host; the ending is her word. *(authority.md, A witness.)*

### Deals (stage 5; text not in the one set)

- **Request.** An answer that does not yet bind. It waits on its open points, each closed one way: *when* (an `expires`: an end, passed as the cut says; with no clock, by the offerer's own later block that has it in its past or a relier's block that names it), *which* (on an open offer: only the offerer's yes; no clock's receipt orders takers), *whose* (the word stands). An answer that is ended (her own earlier decline in its past, the offerer's decline of it on an offer with `expires`, a stop before of the offer in its past) is no request. `requests` is `[{ block, open }]`.
- **`crossed`.** Answers of one deal that bind and do not see each other. Each binds; the deal is read once under each accept (`per_accept`), and its stage is the furthest of them.
- **Bench.** The names that may lower a deal's stage: the confirmers its schema's grounds name and a referee both sides named. The bench changes what counts and what is to come, never who owns: a delivery whose accepted answers the bench all defeats counts for nothing, though it landed. With `grounds.wait` the names are a chain: a later name's turn begins where `needed` lines of the clocks the grounds name (no side's own line) each hold a cutoff, a span no shorter than the wait, running from that line's receipt of a side's dispute, with no word of an earlier name received before it; or the same on the earlier name's own line. A place, never a date (stage 5 compare).
- **`terms.waits_on`.** `goods` (the price waits on the receiver's word that the goods came) or `price` (what moves waits on the payee's signal that the price is paid). Left out, nothing is filled in. An order may say "cash" by `waits_on: price`; without it, it is a credit order and her read-back says so.
- **Due.** What a deal's terms say a side hands over is due where the block that performs it has in its causal past the deal's binding accept, standing; the deal's condition, held; and what `waits_on` names, standing. A payment is of no more than the price's total, a delivery of no more than the quantity chosen. No node adds up payments to answer it. `pay_by` and `deliver_by` are latest times, never earliest.
- **Her word about what is hers to receive.** "It came", "this much came" (`received`), a `covers` and "paid in full" (the payee's signal) are each the receiving side's word about her own. Each stands when signed, bar the conditions she stated before it, counts at the most she said (Once), and waits for nobody. **The payee's signal** is a counted `observe` of kind `paid` that speaks for the payee, `refs.subject` the deal: her word that the price is paid. A `received` by a signer her account word names (3e-8) is her word too: it counts toward her price, and a payment it answers is answered (not `unanswered`).
- **Final.** A sale that states no taking back is final: a taking back is a new promise of the one who takes.
- **Pattern.** A base schema and a worked example over the core: it adds no rule, no fold, no door op and no `protocol.json` entry. A recall, a warning and the held table are patterns.
- **What a deal names a side to receive.** Where the other side's word names an instance of hers, it is held. Where it names a kind or an amount, nothing is held for it: it is the other side's signed word, and the record shows it, what came, and when.
- **Handing on a receiving place.** The side a deal names to receive may hand that place, or a part of it, to another by a transfer that names the deal, which the other accepts. It reaches the other side only by her own word: her promise named a bearer, or she answers the hand-over `accepted`. Otherwise what she sends to the side her promise names counts, and that side is shown keeping it for the new one. **The note** is that receiving place as it passes from one holder to the next (a tab handed on); its owner is whoever holds it where a payment stands.
- **`forfeit`** stays inside one deal: the part of what the payer paid that she signed over on her own stop (D14).
- **Work done.** The job states who counts the hours. Where it names no one, the yes of the side the work goes to counts (D13).

### Circles (stage 6; text not in the one set)

- **A circle's parts.** Its founding block's `pack`, in whole parts (`unit` `part`). An amount of a measure like any other; a fraction of a part is another kind (`unit`).

- **Row.** One line of a circle's `state.rules`: `{ parts, scope }` or `{ to, needed, scope }`, every number a whole number of at least 1, `scope` a grant's scope bounding only what it states. What the owners of every part sign together is theirs to do, whatever the rows list. **One member, one vote** is a row whose `to` names a grant `as` the circle and counts its holders; there are no separate roles for it (D-also). A row that names a block signed after the founding block comes by a version.

### Rule sets

- **The test.** A rule is protocol only when, without it, two readers holding the same blocks could answer differently one of four questions about a signed block (*who signed these bytes; whom does it speak for; what of whose does it bind to whom, and on what condition; who owns what it moved*). Everything else is a **reading**, **library**, or **a node's own business**.
- **Word.** An output that answers one of the four questions. Every other output is a reading. No word reads a view, a profile, a pattern or a reader's clock. Conformance is per op.
- **Named readings.** `settled`, `paid` and a bare sensor `reading` (protocol.json `named_readings`). The core runs none: a condition that names one, with no `state.rule_set`, is not read (`condition_holds` answers null), never holds, and what it holds stays held. `ruled` is no named reading: it reads signed fields only, on every node (stage 5 compare).
- **`state.rule_set`.** A field any block may carry: its signer's word that this block is read under the rules that hash names. A reader that does not run them holds the block not read (`unknown_rules`), and the block hides what it could name (Read whole). A block that names no rule set is read by the core alone. No rule set is read into a block from another block, and nothing is routed by it (D1). *convention*
- **A rule set holds no customs.** A trade custom is a value in its sellers' words (Rule D), never a set.
- **The sound part.** `valid` and what it reads: the bytes, the id, the header, the signature; its ops' cases are in `vectors.json`. It answers who said these bytes, and gates nothing (stage 7, D1).
- **Speaks FoodBlock.** Passes the sound part and the cases of every word op (`protocol.json` `door.protocol`). Any other builder conforms for the ops it passes.
- **A reading, a renderer (as files).** A text file with a cases file beside it, named as a rule set is named: the hash of the canonical manifest over the two (readings/README.md). An engine runs one when it passes its cases. A renderer turns one reading's answer into words; it reads that answer only.
- **Read-back.** An `observe` of kind `readback`, `refs.for` the block, `refs.to` the person it is read back to: `state.readback` (the digest of its text), `reading`, `renderer`, `read` (the ids the reading read) and `digits`. Anyone holding `read` makes the same digest or names what she lacks. *convention*
- **Software release.** A person's signed word naming bytes by hash, an `observe` of kind `software_release` (patterns/releases.md); `release` stays a side letting go of her own claims. It binds only its signer; no list says which releases count.
- **A wrapped copy, a mailbox.** A copy sealed again, whole, to a key the reader gave one sender (her mailbox); a host that keeps it learns which mailbox, how big and when.
- **An old reader** answers less, never otherwise (rule 11).
- **A node's conventions.** `conventions.json`: every list code reads that is not the protocol; no word reads it.
- **A genesis.** An actor that names its own signing key (`state.public_key_sign`) and has no `refs.author` and no `state.seq`. A block that names no key and no author is no genesis and lacks the common five (`chain`) (stage 7 compare).

## 3. The cross-cutting rules

Each is one rule, stated once, in its home file. Every other file points to it.

1. **The cut** *(blocks.md, The cut).* A standing word is ended by an end: its signer's counted stop, a key grant that leaves the key out, a removal, a newer word in its place, a time it states. An end counts from where it is shown. A block made on the word counts as made on it where the end has it in its past; or where another person's block is joined to it and has not seen the end; or as Her own word stands alone says. Where the word names a clock, the join is the clocks' receipts of the block, held in the other's block's past, stating that no end is before it on their lines (`needed` of them). Any other block made on an ended word counts for nothing. Until then it is waiting.
2. **From where a stop counts** *(blocks.md).* Her word says which. If it names a clock, her stop is counted from that clock's receipt of it. If it names none, her stop is counted for each block from where that block, or the block that built on it, has it in its past. The protocol fills in neither.
3. **A named signer's word** *(promises.md).* A condition names a signature, never a fact. Her block binds her name to its truth and nothing of hers.
4. **A fork** *(folds.md, A fork is a burden).* Rule B. A pair never frees or favours its signer. A block of a fork that draws on a measure no draw of the pair names is read as any draw. A draw of the pair without its receiver's yes changes nothing on the others: where one has her yes, it is read as a draw alone. A thing drawn twice where two or more draws with her yes do not fit: neither lands, each later draw that rests on either reads `pair`, each receiver holds the word signed to her, and what each paid is `uncovered`. It ends when the receivers have given up every draw of the pair on that measure but one, by Rule A (a draw given up is freed and holds nothing against the others), or by a ruling of a judge the receivers chose. A draw of a pair that does not stand changes nothing on the other.
5. **No one is both sides of a word** *(authority.md, Who a block speaks for).* A block that gives to her speaks for no one else by a circle's row or under a grant, unless the promise the grant or row names in `scope.on` names her as that side, or a row of the circle holds for it without her hand, or every owner signs it. A part passed, and its coming back, give nothing away and are no case of this rule.
6. **What stays public** *(privacy.md).* One key writes public blocks or sealed blocks, never both. A sealed block, header and all, goes only to its audience, and by handing on to whom they choose. A node shows a block's body only to an audience it can read; a body whose audience it cannot read, it shows to no one, and of a sealed block it serves only a copy it holds for the asker (stage 7 compare; CORE-SET 3a-5). A sealed plaintext may carry `pad`, a string of spaces, which a reader ignores. A carrier sees what it is handed (privacy.md, What stays public: of a sealed block its header, the reader a push names, its size and when; of a wrapped copy only the mailbox, its size and when).
7. **No tables in the protocol** *(core.md, section 3).*
8. **One byte form** *(canonical.md).* Everything FoodBlock signs, hashes or sends as JSON is written in one form, keys in ascending order of their UTF-8 bytes.
9. **Time** *(blocks.md, Time).* The record holds one kind of time: this word has that word in its past. A block's `at` is its signer's word of what her clock showed; any block may leave it out. No rule orders two blocks by dates, adds a span to a date, or reads a date along a line. A time a word states is an end of that word: on a clock the word names, its cutoffs show it; with no clock, a block made on the word is in time only by its own `at`, before the end. A block whose `at` is at or after the end, or that states none, is not in time. A block's own date shows it late against its own signer, always.
10. **A key grant is a key grant** *(identity.md).* No kind of key grant skips the cut, the reach or the pair rule: a mail key, a suite move, a reach above and a close each go through it. A key in doubt never decides its own replacement: only a voice above a key moves it to another suite (each key's suite read from its own spelling); to a reader who believes the old suite, a suite move is an end like any other. A word at `PT0S` stands when signed: signing twice is her own choice, a `wait` above zero.
11. **Less, never otherwise** *(core.md, section 11; folds.md).* A reader that cannot read something on a line answers less about every thing that line could have touched, never something else. No answer depends on the machine that computes it. The protocol fixes no depth: a reader that stops says so (`not_shown`, with what it did not reach), never another owner; a reader that goes on answers fully.
