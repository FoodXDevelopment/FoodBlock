# Pattern: selling from lots

Not normative. A pattern adds no rule.

**Selling from lots.** A seller who would not name one buyer to the next keeps three kinds of key: one that brings keys in and signs nothing else; one that holds the stock and signs only parts passed; and one per lot that accepts its part and sells from it. A later buyer then holds the parts passed (amounts and times), the key grants (how many and when), and her own lot's chain. She is not shown who else bought. She is shown how much and when.

**What it takes**, against a plain sale: for the seller, five blocks a sale where one did (the key grant, said again, the part passed, its accept, the sale); a wait on the issuing voice for each new key, unless made ahead; for the buyer, nothing more. It suits a mill or a warehouse with many buyers who read its title. For a spot sale (goods and cash in hand) nobody reads the chain, and nothing is needed.
