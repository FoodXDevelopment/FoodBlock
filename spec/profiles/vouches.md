# Profile: vouches

Optional. "I stand behind her for up to N until D" binds only the voucher. Five base schemas in this family (`spec/schemas`): `vouch`, `payback` and `shareback` here, `loyalty` for a refrain of no side payments, and the pot they share (folds.md, A pot). Core checks the form; the fold `pot` shows what is paid and what is left.

**Adds:** the base schemas `vouch`, `payback`, `shareback`, `loyalty`.

- **A vouch** is a promise by the voucher under the schema `vouch`. `refs.from` the voucher; `refs.to` the receiver, a person or a circle (left out, it is open to any who deal with the subject); `refs.subject` the one stood behind, a person or a thing; `terms.backing` the cap N, money or a count of one named kind (an amount: "up to 2 heifers", read the same way as money; nothing is converted); `terms.until` the end date D; `terms.condition` any `signal` by the referee the voucher names about the named deal or thing. The signal may say the subject failed a deal, that another owner has a better claim to the thing (a vouch on title), or that something succeeded: *if the depot weigher confirms 2 tonnes in, £400 of mine becomes the farmer's* is a letter of credit a co-op treasurer can sign. The signal's block names the deal in `derives_from`.
- **A share of the loss, optional.** The vouch schema declares the term `fraction` (a decimal from 0 to 1): the voucher bears that share of each loss, "70%, up to N", so the receiver keeps a reason to screen. With none, she bears all of it up to the cap. It is her signed choice and never a default. The cap and a pledge's flat price may each name a thing, not only money.
- **What a vouch's date bounds.** The schema declares `until_reads`, which a vouch with an `until` states: `occurrence` (deals made before D) or `claims_made` (failures claimed before D). Silence never widens it. A stranger is covered only if her deal names the vouch in its refs: her act of naming it is her accept of the open offer. A vouch may name a rulebook of what it backs ("deals under Lake Bakers' Rulebook v3"), and may write its cap out as `all` (A cap written out, below), shown as "no limit".
- **A payment under it** is the voucher's money transfer `for` the vouch, accepted by the one it goes to, with the deal paid for in `derives_from`. The fold `pot` reads it: backing less what has been paid, `left`. The voucher is the **orderer** of her payments (folds.md, Orderer). A claim past the cap counts for nothing against it, and is the voucher's own broken word only if the receiver paid for the vouch (promises.md, Paid for, never delivered). Past D, no new claim counts: write the condition `within` the referee's signal by D on a named clock (promises.md, By a time).
- **The pay-back** is the one vouched for, as `refs.from`, promising the voucher as `refs.to`: *if you pay for me under that vouch, that amount, plus an optional fee, of mine becomes yours.* It uses the existing shapes: `terms.price` is the amount and the fee, `terms.condition` is a `deed` (or `settled`) of the voucher's payment. It is a promise like any other and binds only its signer. Several vouchers of one person may each take one; the share-back among them is the schema `shareback`, below. *Two vouchers:* Mo and Nia both vouch for Jide. Mo pays the co-op £60 when Jide fails. Nia signs a pay-back to Mo, `terms.condition` a `deed` of Mo's payment, for her half. It is a promise like any other and binds only Nia.
- **A cap written out.** `terms.cap: all`, in place of `backing`, is a cap the voucher writes and never one anybody fills in: all she owns on the day a claim is read, the referee fixing the sum. It reaches property only, never her body or labour. The fold `pot` returns `cap: "all"` and no limit, and the card prints "no limit" (folds.md, A pot).
- **A stop and its tail.** The voucher's stop is onward, like any stop (promises.md, Answers): it ends what is made under the vouch from that block on. A deal asked for after it is no deal until she allocates it, and an allocation she signs after her own stop is her own word. A deal made while the vouch stood keeps it, and its claim counts when the deal fails, whenever that is. Leaving a circle ends circle authority and never an earlier signed vouch.
- **A renewal.** A vouch survives a renewal of the deal it names, inside its own `until`: a renewal names the vouch and the deal it renews in `derives_from`, and the claim counts when the failure was received by the named clock before `until` (the payout's `within`, promises.md, By a time). A failure received after `until` gives no claim.
- **Any condition shape.** A vouch's condition may be any condition the protocol reads, such as a circle's call or a `signal` by N holders of a role. The vouch binds only its voucher.
- **Optional dials,** each written out and never filled in: `demand` (`first`: the receiver's own signal is the condition; `after_failure`: the referee's), `liability` (`joint` or `several`), `contribution` (none, or the hash of the share-back the co-vouchers signed) and `cap`. The fold reads none of them: the voucher's payout is her own word, counted against the cap.
- **Co-vouchers share back.** The schema `shareback`: each co-voucher signs to each other, under the vouch they both signed, *if you pay more than your share of a claim under our vouch, my share of the excess becomes yours.* The shares are listed by them in `terms.shares`, and nothing is equal by default. It is a promise like any other: `terms.price` is her share (a `fraction` of the claim the referee's figure fixes, promises.md, Terms: a price fixed later), `terms.condition` a `deed` of the other's payment. A co-voucher who never signed one owes nobody anything, and the claim itself is covered once, whoever pays it (promises.md, Settling).
- **A voucher's own count.** An optional `observe`: *I stand behind N in all, as of today.* It is read with its age and is her word to its truth. Never required, never a score.
- **A borrower's own signature never counts toward lending to him.** That is a term the founders of a pool may sign, never a rule of the protocol.
- **A purpose is the lender's own sentence.** *For the oven* is a line the lender writes, kept or not by a named confirmer's `observe`. There is no "productive" flag and no list of allowed purposes.
- **The operator's open bond.** The operator of an autonomous system (a delivery robot) signs a vouch on his own machine, open to any harmed stranger, up to N, with a referee named. A stranger then has a signed word to claim on. The operator answers for a crossing by his machine with or without the bond, as an owner answers for his straying cow: it is his property and his invasion (promises.md, A claim of invasion). That is liability from the act, never a default filling anyone's silence. The bond adds only a sum the stranger can see and collect without a fight.
- **A vouch on title.** A border-market broker (a dilaal) signs: *if a referee confirms another owner has a better claim to these goats, up to N of mine becomes the buyer's.* `refs.subject` is the goats; the condition is the referee's signal naming them. No new rule.
- **An introduction.** "Alice introduces Bob to Carol" is a vouch or a grant already, and needs no matcher: Alice signs a vouch for Bob with Carol as receiver ("if Bob fails a deal with you, up to N of mine becomes yours"), or a grant that lets Bob act for her toward Carol. Either binds only Alice, and only to what she names. Silence from Carol is nothing.
- **Whoever holds the lot.** `refs.to` may name a thing: whoever owns it is the receiver, read as Bearer reads it (promises.md, Bearer), so a later holder of a lot may claim under the maker's own vouch. The receiver may also be whoever owns a thing made from the lot, read along the makes (things.md, A make; a make counts only when its maker owned what it made from, where the make stands, so a stranger's "cheese" from another's lot reaches no one): a flavour house's vouch for its culture reaches the cheesemaker who used it, and the eater who bought a wedge of the cheese, as long as the payout sees her own the thing. It binds only the voucher.
- **A layer behind a pool.** A bigger party vouches to the pool's circle (`refs.to` the circle): when the vet signs that the pool is short, the layer pays into the herd's need. Its pot is its own: the pool's holder does not count it.
- **A loyalty refrain** (the schema `loyalty`) is the one acting's promise to the one acted for: *I take nothing from anyone else about what I do for you.* A refrain (promises.md, Refrain) by `refs.from`, the buyer, agent or operator, to `refs.to`, a person or circle, with `terms.until`, an optional `terms.price` (owed back if broken) and a referee named in its schema or by a `referee` grant. Rothbard (*The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 17): the payer did nothing wrong; the wrong is the agent breaking his own word to his principal. So the protocol never marks the payer, only what the agent signed. Stage and view show whether it is signed, breached or elapsed, and what was paid for it, as facts.

- **A backer behind the curtain.** A capped vouch whose receiver is the worker: *if you owe a seller for goods bought for this trip, up to 40,000 of mine becomes yours.* The sellers deal with the worker alone. What the worker buys in his own name is his until he hands it on; the backer holds his promise, not the goods. The arrangement is shown honestly: the backer's vouch is on the record for any reader.
- **A named successor.** *And if I move or die, my son*: the son signs his own vouch, conditioned on a counted close of his father (successor.md). It binds the son only because he signed, and only after a close that counts.
- **Cover that lapses when the visits stop.** The vouch is conditioned `within` a certifier's signed visit by the date, on the maker's own clock (promises.md, By a time). A visit the clock received late, or never, leaves the cover unmet for that period; each period carries its own vouch.
- **Roles inside a certifier.** A certifier is a circle. A vouch counts as the certifier's only if it is signed `as` the circle by a holder of the `standards` role (a role with `sign`), not the `sales` role (circles.md). The fold gives the sales block no side.
- **A held stake names its release.** A stake held behind a vouch or a pledge says when and how it ends. A stake that names none shows "release not named", and the reader fills nothing in.
- **A voucher's watch window.** The person vouched for signs a standing promise to send his weekly orders to his vouchers until the vouch ends. It is his own promise, with his own stake if he wishes.
- **A vouch for a new member.** A free key looks like a stranger (THREAT-MODEL.md). A known member's vouch for it, with a cap, is her word that this key is who it says: the answer to the new-key refuge.

Trust the schemas by hash (`spec/schemas/README.md`).
