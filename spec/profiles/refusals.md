# Profile: refusals

Optional. Refusing together is remedy three done together (core.md, section 10): *if a trader does not settle within a week, none of us sells to him until he does.* One base schema, `refusal`. pledges.md says "A heads count is another schema"; this is that schema. Rothbard (*The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 18): a boycott, a secondary one too, is legitimate in itself.

**Adds:** the base schema `refusal`.

- **A refusal** is a refrain (promises.md, Refrain) each signer makes to the other signers: *I do not deal with X until it ends.* `refs.from` the signer, `refs.to` the circle, `refs.subject` the one refused, `terms.until` the latest it can run. A refrain is a promise, and a promise is owed to someone: a refusal made to nobody would be owed to nobody, and a member who quietly dealt with X would break no one's word. So each signer's refusal is owed to the circle, and the circle's referee is named in its grounds. A member who deals with X before the end has broken his own signed word to the others: it is shown, and ruled on only by the judge they named. A non-signer who deals with X breaks nothing. A signer's refusal may carry a stake (the refrain's price), or a stake as a multiple of a named value (`stake`: `{ times, of }`, read by a referee, never moved by a fold).
- **The threshold counts signers, never bare keys.** Free keys are free, so the condition is a `signal` (promises.md, Conditions) by holders of a named `circle#role`, read from the circle's fold, with `signers` the number needed. It counts distinct signing keys, so a person who holds the role in several circles counts once. A key that is not a member does not count. Below the threshold the refusal binds no one; at it, the signers.
- **Its cause is a deal, and the notice came first.** When a refusal names a person, `derives_from` holds the deal it falls under and the notice the creditor sent the debtor first, with a receipt that it reached him (promises.md, A message with a receipt). Nothing fills a man's silence, so a demand that never reached him cannot be read as his default; without that receipt the refusal counts for no signer. A reader then sees a broken deal, not "he said something" (Leeson and Ellickson illustrate; the ground is the premise). A signal about anything else counts for nothing. It names only the one who signed the deal (or the circle, if the circle signed it), never his fellow members or kin.
- **A plain mutual refrain that names no one needs no cause.** Farmers jointly holding out for a price may sign it: refusing to sell is every owner's right.
- **Signers may be sealed.** Each signer may seal her refusal to the counter alone, who holds them all. The counter reads the threshold from the signals she holds; a reader who was shown none holds none, finds the threshold unmet and learns no name.
- **Optional dials,** the circle's signed choice and never filled in (the refusal schema declares them): `strikes` and `window` (how many open signals from distinct deals, in what period), `cure` (days a debtor has to settle before a signal counts; Addition 26's word), `until`, and `release` (who besides the debtor's payment may lift it; each refuser lifts only her own refusal). No fold counts signals across deals, so a signer signs "the rule is met", as for cover quotes (rulebooks.md). The period is `window`, not `within`, which already names a condition wrapper.
- **A refusal may exempt dealings with someone already refused.** The circle's grounds may say that a member's open signal toward X, while X stands refused, does not count toward the circle's refusal of that member. It never touches X's own claim: the member still owes X what he signed.
- **Its end is a fact.** It ends when the deal is `settled` (promises.md, Conditions), when the creditor takes her signal back (she `defeats` her own `observe`, a signal's way back), or when the deal's referee `defeats` her signal (Disputes). A reader checks each with the shapes that exist. No clock, unless the signers wrote one in `until`.

- **A lead may speak for refusal signals.** A member may sign a grant to a trusted lead ("your refusal signals under this rule speak for me, until D"). The threshold counts whom each signal speaks for, not the key that signed it (promises.md, Conditions: a signer is whom the block speaks for), so forty bakers who each signed one grant reach forty when their lead signs forty signals under them. A signal outside what the grant covers speaks for the lead alone.
- **A refusal for any other cause is each person's own right.** It is just not a counted threshold under this schema. FoodOS never warns on a boycott of an activity, or on a counter-boycott.
