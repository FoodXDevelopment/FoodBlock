# Profile: the short form (SMS, USSD, voice)

Optional. For a person whose link is too small for a block. It says how her yes is carried. Every step is the adopting host's own promise (nodes.md, A node's word).

**Adds:** the short code, the read-back, the short reply, the parts, the observe kind `readback`, and the label list `state.proof`.

**Three ways a yes arrives.** The carrier says which in `state.proof`, an open list.
1. **Her key signs it** (`key`). Her SIM, card or phone signs her own answer. The host carries it and signs nothing. The block is hers.
2. **Her grant backs the host's witness** (`digits`, `session`, `voice`, and any second proof). She signed one grant to this host for this handle. The host signs an accepting witness under it (authority.md, A witness), inside what her grant names. Her grant may end itself on her STOP by text, by its own term `scope.ends_on: { witness: <the host>, said: "STOP" }`: the host's witness binds only the host, and the ending is her word.
3. **She has no key.** The host signs a witness under no grant. It is the host's word and binds nothing of hers (profiles/custody.md, With no key).

**The read-back.** Before a yes counts, the host sends the exact terms of the signed block once, in her language, ending in two or three digits it drew fresh for this read-back. `readback` is the SHA-256 of that text as sent, digits included. Before it sends, the host signs an `observe` of kind `readback` carrying that digest, `for` the block. In ways 2 and 3 her reply is those digits: any other reply, the digits of another read-back, or a reply from another handle signs nothing and ends that read-back. In way 1 her key's signature is the reply. In a USSD session the session is the tie and its last screen is the read-back. A spoken read-back is the recording played, kept by its hash; a transcript is a reading (things.md, Truth).

**A read-back that can be redone.** A `readback` may also carry `state.reading` and `state.renderer`, each the name of a published file with its cases (readings/README.md); `state.read`, the sorted ids the reading read (its answer's `read`); and `state.digits`, the digits its text ends in. `refs.to` names the person it is read back to. Its text is then the renderer's output over the reading's answer for the block it is `for`, then one space, then the digits, and `readback` is the SHA-256 of that text's UTF-8 bytes. Anyone who holds the inputs runs the reading and the renderer and gets the same digest, or names the input she lacks. Words after the digits are the host's own and are not in the digest. A `readback` that carries `read` is sealed to the person and to the readers she names; a side of the deal is shown its digest only.

**The short code.** The first 5 bytes of a block's id (the digest bytes), in Crockford Base32, as `XXXX-XXXX`; typed in any case, with or without the hyphen, `O` as 0, `I` and `L` as 1. It is a lookup key, never an id or an authority. Where two blocks a receiver holds share it, the receiver asks for more characters of the id, five bits at a time. A first ask to someone off the network carries the short code of the signed ask; the receiver finds the ask among those addressed to that handle, its own or at the asker's node named in the words.

**The short reply (way 1).** Her key signs her answer as any key signs a block (core.md, section 2). Only what the receiver cannot rebuild travels: the text `FB1`, a space, and base64url with no padding of these bytes in order: the signature (64); the first 5 bytes of the id of the block answered; the answer, one byte (`01` accepted, `02` declined); `at`, 6 bytes, milliseconds since 1970, big-endian (all zero when a decline carries none); `seq`, the rest, big-endian, no leading zero. The receiver takes each held block with that code, fills the answer template with the signer's id, her head of that key it holds (or the block that brought the key in), `refs.key` the key grant that brought the key in (none for her genesis key) and these values, and checks it. The block whose signature opens is the block; if none opens, nothing was said, and the receiver asks for the whole block (Parts). The template of an accept is a `promise` with `state: { at, audience: "public", seq, status: "accepted" }` and `refs: { answers, author, derives_from, for, key, prev }`, where `for` is the block answered and `derives_from` a list of one id, a `readback` `for` that block; of a decline, an `observe` with `status: "declined"` and no `derives_from`. A key that is a tool of hers (identity.md, A voice or a tool) replies as its own actor, its block naming her grant in `refs.under`. It changes no signed byte and no id.

**Parts.** A whole public block in its compact form (encoding.md) may travel as text parts. A part is `FB0`, a space, a tag (the first four Crockford characters of the SHA-256 of the whole CBOR message), a space, `k/n`, a space, and base64url with no padding, at most 153 characters in all. A receiver joins the parts of one tag in order of `k`, refuses a set with a missing or repeated `k`, checks the tag, then decodes and checks the block as usual.

**What the short form never does.** It never makes a grant or a key grant. Her grant to a host is a block her key signed; no read-back and no reply stands in for it.
