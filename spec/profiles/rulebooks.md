# Profile: rulebooks

Optional. Trade rulebooks and a cheap judge (Bernstein's grain and cotton associations illustrate; each is a term both sides signed, which is why it binds, and never the protocol's). The cover and grader terms bind only those who signed them. Four base schemas: `rulebook`, `glossary`, `grader`, and the terms they declare.

**Adds:** the base schemas `rulebook`, `glossary`, `grader`, and the terms `cover`, `cure`, `allowance`, which only the rulebook schema declares. No fold reads them.

- **A rulebook** is a schema a circle publishes: its terms, its referee panel (`grounds.confirmers`, `needed`), its deadlines. A deal names it by hash in `refs.schema`, so both sides sign it by naming it. A new version is a new hash, and old deals stay under the old hash.
- **A glossary** is a schema of definitions: word, meaning, how it is checked, unit. A rulebook or a deal names it by hash. The protocol still keeps no list of units (encoding.md).
- **Cover price.** `cover: { quotes, within, take }`, `take` lowest or highest. Each quote is an `observe` by its giver `for` the deal. The claim is the gap between the deal price and the chosen quote, and the referee checks the arithmetic. No fold counts the quotes: the claim is signed and the referee confirms it.
- **How the judge works.** Optional grounds keys of the rulebook: `reads` (the block kinds the referee considers), `due_in`, `fee` (and who gets it back), `blind` (names hidden in the bundle, done by the autonomous system, never the protocol). They are the sides' signed terms.
- **Grader and fix window.** A grading schema: a named confirmer answers only *does the lot match the description?* in an `observe` of kind `grade`. A term `cure: { within }` is the window within which the seller may replace a lot found short. The stage already reads it so: a delivery the grader (the deal's referee) defeats counts for nothing, the deal is not done, and a replacement the buyer accepts counts. Only a refrain is ever `breached`, so nothing is broken before the window passes. If both sides signed an `allowance` (a fraction or an amount: "keep it, take 5% off"), the grader's answer may carry one, which the cover reads. With no such term, she says only whether the lot matches.
- **A unit carries its measure.** A glossary entry gives, beside its unit, its `measure` (whose scale or tin, and where: "50 kg on the warehouse weighbridge, ticket signed by both"; a bare noun is not a unit) and its `tolerance` (a signed number or "none"; silence there is where the fights are). Its `check` is a test the sides can run, or "a referee's judgment", a term both signed knowing a judge will weigh it.
- **Grade from a sealed sample.** The rulebook's term `sample: { holder, deadline, fee }` fixes who holds the sample, until when, and who pays. The grader's reading is a fact on the lot; a later reading by the `appeal` grader both sides named supersedes it.
- **A cash fallback exists only if signed.** The term `fallback: { source, date, chooser }` names the price source, the date, and who may choose cash (payer, payee or neither). With none, the debt stays in goods and failing to deliver is a breach, never a quiet conversion at one side's price. Cash counts toward a debt in goods only by the payee's own `covers` (promises.md, Settling).
- **Stock of equal value** (the soccida, the cheptel) is a later form. It needs a signed appraisal at the start and a signed way to value at the end, so it is not written yet.
- **Custom never excuses an invasion.** A rulebook or custom binds only its signers and never excuses an invasion of someone who never signed it (Rothbard, *For a New Liberty*, ch. 12; promises.md, A claim of invasion).
- **A price table binds only the deals that name it.** A table of prices inside a rulebook is the signed word of the deals that name the rulebook by hash. For any other deal it is a price list, evidence of what some people asked, and no price anyone owes. Custom never excuses an invasion (above), and a price table never fixes a price for someone who did not sign it.
- **A rule-maker never collects its own penalty.** Nobody judges their own cause (promises.md, invariant 13). A penalty term in a rulebook pays the harmed side or a third party (a burial fund, a circle's pot), never the one who writes and rules on the rulebook.
- **A new version `derives_from` the rulings it adopts.** A rulebook's next hash names, in `derives_from`, the rulings of the old one that it takes in. Old deals stay under the old hash; a reader sees which rulings the new words rest on.
- **A fault list for a sale.** The usual cow faults (lame, dry, a bad eye), each marked "send back" or "pay the difference", are a list in the rulebook or a glossary, named by hash in the deal. The seller signed the list by naming it, so the buyer's choice among the marks is a term, not a custom.
- **One word, one coin.** A glossary entry names the exact coin or banknote, so "dollar" in Zimbabwe is two entries: "US dollar, banknotes" and "Zimbabwe dollar, RTGS". A payment in the other counts only at a rate both signed (promises.md, More worked examples).
- *The weather reopener.* A buyer and a grower agree a price with a reopener on rain. A Met Office or public gauge signs nothing, so it cannot make a `reading_condition` hold (profiles/attestations.md). Write it as a `signal` by readers the sides named, who promised to sign the public reading by a deadline. An index can fire with no real loss, so the relief is in quantity (he may deliver less), paired with the seller's refrain not to sell the withheld amount elsewhere. It is never a free cancellation. If readers cannot be had, the fall-back is cover quotes up to the earnest (earnest.md).
- *The Kisumu maize deal.* A mill and a farm sign a promise naming "Lake Maize Rules v3" by hash in `refs.schema`: the circle's rulebook, with its glossary ("FAQ maize"), a panel of two graders, a cover of three quotes in two days, a cure window of three days. Both signed it by naming it. The grader says the first lot does not match; the farm replaces it; the mill accepts. Nobody has broken their word.

- **An index term names its arithmetic.** A price that follows an index (`index` in the rulebook) names the goods, the grade, the weights and the kind of average, by hash. There is no default index and no default average. A standing order may reprice each occurrence by a reading (promises.md, Terms: a price fixed later).
- **Steps in a quote.** The rulebook's term `lot: { size, up_to }` is a step in a price table: from `size` units up to `up_to` units, the line beside it carries that step's price ("12 crates, 90; 13-24, 85 each"). It is read like any term.
- **A claim period.** `claim_within` (a duration) is a signed term: a claim after it is not heard by this referee. It never says "extinguished"; the claimant may name another judge, and it never touches first use, which time does not lose.
- **A referee's pledge to a code.** A refrain by the referee, in the shape of the loyalty refrain (vouches.md): "I rule by rulebook H, on these papers, within D days; £S of mine if I rule against it, as the backup judge finds." It pairs with the judge's own cap (promises.md, Who pays a confirmer).
- **A sale rule and sealed bids.** A seller's sale rule for a lot that cannot wait is her own signed words: who wins (`highest`, `draw` or `order`), what the winner pays (`own`, `next` or `uniform`) and when it closes (a named clock). Sealed bids are commitments (profiles/bids.md); a referee recomputes the winner from the bytes. The protocol reads the commitments, not the rule.

Trust the schemas by hash (`spec/schemas/README.md`).
