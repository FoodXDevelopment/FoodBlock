# Profile: successor

Optional. A person names who acts for them after an event (death, long absence). A peer without this profile ends nothing at a close.

**Adds:** the grant kind `successor`, the observe kinds `trigger` and `close` (with `state.continues`), one right for the recovery key, and the view `successor_view`.

- **The grant.** A `grant` of kind `successor`, `refs.to` the successor. `state.witnesses`: who may record the event (a set of ids, each once, `required`). `state.needed`: how many must, a whole number from 1 to the number of witnesses (`shape` otherwise); it is a threshold over named voices (folds.md, Counts), and with none stated the trigger never fires. `state.wait`: how long the trigger waits (a duration, `required`). `state.keys`: the person's decryption keys sealed to the successor (an envelope, privacy.md; its plaintext is unchecked), never signing keys. The successor is never a witness (`shape`).
- **The trigger.** A witness records the event as an `observe` of kind `trigger` naming the successor grant in `refs.under` and the person in `refs.subject`. The witness signs as itself; the grant check reads a trigger by the listed witnesses and no one else, without asking whether the grant is `to` the witness.
- **The wait.** The trigger fires `wait` after the `needed`-th witness observe arrives; with `wait` missing or `needed` missing (Counts) it never fires. In that time any live key of the person, or the recovery key, stops it by a `defeats` of an event observe. This is a delayed power (authority.md, Ending, A delayed power).
  **The exact reading.** Events are the counted trigger observes of listed witnesses under the grant naming the person in `refs.subject`. The person's counted `defeats` of an event cancels it when it arrived on the reader's node strictly before the moment the trigger fires, when its arrival is unknown, or while nothing fires. The view is the least reading that settles: start with nothing cancelled; take each witness's earliest standing event in order of arrival (none recorded: after all that have one; ties by hash); `since` is the arrival of the `needed`-th (none if fewer, or its arrival unknown); `fires` is `since` plus `wait`; cancel every event a `defeats` cancels against that `fires`; repeat until nothing more is cancelled. A `defeats` that arrived after the trigger fired cancels nothing here, and neither does one half of a forked pair.
  The fold reads no clock: the person's counted `defeats` of a trigger voids every close resting on it, whenever it came. **The recovery key** may sign this `defeats`: an `observe` naming the key in `state.recovery_key` (it may be the recovery key's first block, `seq` 1, `prev` the genesis, identity.md), the successor grant in `refs.under` (a grant its author gave), defeating at least one block. It rests on the grant alone, not on the blocks it defeats; a block that is no trigger under the grant is cancelled by nothing. Besides key grants it is all the recovery key may sign (`recovery_scope`).
- **No power before the death.** A block under the grant, other than a witness's trigger or the successor's close, speaks for no one (authority.md, Who a block speaks for) unless a counted close of the person under that grant is in its causal past.
- **After it fires.** The successor signs as themselves, `under` the grant, and reads only what the person could read and pass on. They record the death with an `observe` of kind `close`, `refs.subject` the person. A close ends only what is listed below; the person's chain stays open.
- **When a close counts.** Read from blocks alone (no clock), a `close` of the person counts when:
  - it counts in its author's chain, and its author is not the person;
  - it is signed `under` a `successor` grant the person gave in their own name (no `refs.as`), whose `refs.to` is the close's author, that counts in the person's chain, and whose `scope.acts`, if any, covers the close;
  - that grant has not ended for the close (authority.md, Ending): no counted stop by the person, whenever it came (their stop voids every close under the grant, even one that never saw it), and no decline or leaving by the successor in the close's causal past;
  - no counted close of the successor is in the close's causal past (a dead successor closes no one);
  - the triggers of at least `needed` different listed witnesses, `under` it and about the person, are in the close's causal past (named in `refs.after`), and the person has defeated none of them at any time.
  **A voided close voids only what the successor claimed for himself.** What someone else built on stands (folds.md, Built-on stands) when the close counted in that block's own causal past: her own successor grant and the triggers of witnesses she chose were all there when the outsider relied on it. A close that never counted protects nothing. **Her later gift is her broken promise, not a cloud on his title:** a transfer she signs in her own name that has a trigger under that grant in its causal past, and crosses a transfer her successor made under it (neither sees the other), moves nothing against it (folds.md, Ownership); she owes her new receiver what she cannot give. A gift of hers that saw no trigger still crosses, and the ownership fold shows the conflict.
  A successor acts only where their own view says fired; the fold does not check it.
- **What a close ends.** A death ends authority, never a promise. A counted close ends, from that block on, every grant the person gave in their own name except the successor grant (authority.md, Ending), for blocks that have the close in their causal past, and the person's membership of every circle (circles.md, The cut). `state.continues` lists what the successor carries on: grants, a circle or a member grant. **No promise ends at a death:** what was owed is still owed, and what the person held of the other side's stays visible in the stage (folds.md, What each fold returns). The successor settles, answers or stops it as the person could; so may the other side. He settles from the dead person's property, and is never bound in his own body or property unless he signs a promise of his own (promises.md, Death).

**Heirs.** An heir holds what the dead person was owed, or her claim of harm, by the successor she named: once a close counts, the successor signs a transfer that names the deal (or the claim) in `refs.what`, `to_owner` the heir, `under` the successor grant, and the heir accepts. The grant's `scope.acts` and `scope.parties` must name the transfer and the heir, because a grant that omits them covers nothing. Before a close the same transfer speaks for no one. It moves what is owed, or standing, and never anything the dead person did not hold; the heir takes every condition of the deal (promises.md, Owed).

**The view `successor_view`** reads the arrivals of the witness observes on the reader's node and clock. It returns `events`, `cancelled`, `since`, `fires` and `fired` (true once the reader's clock reaches `fires`). It reads the events alone, never the grant: a stopped or declined grant still reads fired, and a close under it counts for nothing in the fold.
