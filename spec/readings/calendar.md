# Calendar

Not normative. A reading: which dates a calendar repeat falls on (`repeat_occurrences`, conventions.json). It changes none of the four answers (core.md, section 10): a payment names its occurrence by its start time (promises.md, Terms, `repeat`), so what was paid is a signed word; which Tuesdays fall in March is this reading. What `read` checks stays in promises.md, Terms: the RRULE grammar, the zone's shape, and that a calendar repeat carries `state.when` and `terms.zone`.

**The calendar forms.** Four everyday repeats have one spelling each, which every reader counts alike (cases: the `repeat_occurrences` cases of conventions-vectors.json, `calendar:`), and nothing else spells them: *every weekday* is `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO,TU,WE,TH,FR`; *every Friday* (any one day) is `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=FR`; *every month on day N* is `FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=N` (N from 1 to 31, or -1 for the last day, -2 the one before); *the nth weekday of the month* is `FREQ=MONTHLY;BYDAY=nXX` (n from 1 to 5, or -1 for the last, -2 the second to last; XX one of MO TU WE TH FR SA SU). They are the grammar of every other repeat (`FREQ`, `INTERVAL`, `BYDAY`, `BYMONTHDAY`, `BYSETPOS`, `COUNT`, `UNTIL`, `WKST`); "every second Friday" is `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=FR`. The rules of counting are these. (1) **The wall clock decides.** The start's time is read in `terms.zone`, and the day, the weekday and the month of every occurrence are the zone's, so Friday 08:00 in Auckland is Thursday 19:00 UTC; the wall time keeps through a change of clocks and the UTC time moves. (2) **A day that does not exist gives no occurrence that month**: day 31 skips April, day 30 and 29 skip February (29 comes in a leap year), and a fifth Friday skips the months with four. (3) **The start is counted only if the rule names it**: a monthly repeat on day 1 started on the 15th first falls on the 1st after; one on day 15 started on the 15th falls on the start itself. (4) **A week starts on Monday** unless `WKST` names another day (`MO` to `SU`); it matters only with `INTERVAL` above 1 and several days (`INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=TU,SU` is Tuesday and the Sunday that ends that week, under `WKST=SU` the Sunday that starts the next). (5) **`COUNT` counts from the start**, not from the window a reader asks about; `UNTIL` ends it, itself included. (6) A repeat the reader cannot read is refused (`repeat_occurrences`, an argument error): a number before the day in a weekly repeat, day 0, a weekday or `WKST` that is not one of the seven, a part it does not know.

**The zone.** A calendar repeat is read in `terms.zone` by the IANA time zone database, so strangers' computers count the same wall clock. Which release is the reader's own: the protocol holds no table (core.md, section 3), and a reading names none. A time the clocks skip falls as RFC 5545 puts it, by the offset before the skip; one they repeat is its first.
