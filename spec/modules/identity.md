# Identity

Keys, rotation, recovery, custody and contacts. The genesis is in core.md.

An **identity** is an `actor`; its id is its genesis hash. Its **keys** are public signing keys, and one of them signs each of its blocks.

**The recovery key** is an optional offline key that outranks the others. Only the genesis names it: `recovery_key_hash` (the hash, in the identity's suite, of its SPKI hex as UTF-8 text) and `recovery_reach` (a duration, `required` with the hash). A key grant naming either is refused (`undeclared_field`), so a thief of a device key plants none. The key is in the identity's suite unless the genesis names `recovery_suite`. It has its own chain: `seq` 1, `prev` the genesis, never a fork against another key's first block. It signs only key grants and the `defeats` a profile gives it (profiles/successor.md).

**Keys.** A key may be a passkey (encoding.md, Passkeys). Each device has its own key, so devices never fork a chain. A `grant` of kind `key` brings one in: `refs.subject` the id, `state.public_key_sign`, `state.suite` if not `std`. **Who may bring in a key:** the id itself (a live key of it, or the recovery key), or the holder of a standing operator grant naming the id in `refs.to` (Autonomous systems and machines): the key grant then names that operator grant in `refs.under`, and the grant is by the same author. That grant is one of the key grant's needs, so every node gives one verdict and no stranger plants a key. The rule is the same for every actor; `kind` is a label validity never reads. It carries `refs.subject` and `public_key_sign` or `public_key_encrypt`, and `replaces` and `counted_to` together or neither (`required`). An answer to it is exempt (core.md, section 7). It counts for the id in `refs.subject`: the id's own, or its operator's. A block with a status is never a key grant. A key ends only by rotation; a stop of its grant ends nothing. An encryption-only grant voids and freezes nothing. The identity's keys are the genesis key and every key a counting grant brings in, as a set: a key two grants bring in is one key, frozen when either grant is, void only when every one is. Rotated-out and frozen keys stay in; void keys and the recovery key are out.

- **Rotation.** A key grant naming `replaces` and `counted_to` rotates that key. **Who may rotate:** the key itself; the recovery key (`recovery_key`, its hex, says so); or a key of its operator (its key grant names the operator grant, Keys), which gives a micro that lost its counter a clean key. A new `suite` moves the key to it.
  - **A key's own rotation closes only its future.** A rotation by the key or its operator voids the blocks of the old key that have it in their causal past, whatever `counted_to` says. A seller cannot un-sign her sale.
  - **Only the recovery key reaches back** (authority.md, The cut). Its `counted_to` is the last block of the old key that counts. **The counted line** is `counted_to` and its `prev`-ancestry through blocks of the id that the old key signed. Those and the rotation count. Every other block of the old key is void, after `counted_to` or off the line at any `seq` (a sibling of a counted block, whoever signed it), and forms no pair. If `counted_to` is not a block of the old key (the genesis, or the block that brought it in), only the rotation counts. Other keys are untouched; the reach (below) protects what others built on.
  - A rotation stands even past `counted_to` unless another rotation of the same key is in its causal past: the key's speech ended at the first.
  - `counted_to` is a block the rotation rests on (core.md, check 5), so the new key sees the old chain up to it, whoever signed.
  - Recovery is never a fork: its signer differs from the key it rotates, even if that key signed blocks naming the same `prev`.
- **Void spreads.** A key brought in by a void block is void, with every key it brought in.
- **A pair.** Two counting blocks of one key naming one `prev` (nodes.md, Equivocation), or two first blocks of one key (each naming a block that brought it in), are a pair. The identity fold counts both; the other folds read folds.md, A fork is a burden: a block of a forked key binds its signer and never counts for what is owed to her. A block of a key that no longer speaks (rotated out, frozen, void) is valid, kept and counts for nothing. Validity reads only the key a block's `prev` gives (core.md, check 6). Two key grants in a pair are in Conflict whether or not they name `replaces`; so are two of the recovery key's. A pair that both rotate ends the old key's speech past every block either counts. A recovery rotation decides a pair it saw (the half on its counted line counts, the other is void); a key's own rotation decides none.
- **Conflict.** Two rotations of one key that do not see each other both stand; without a clock they cannot be told apart (either could be a thief's). Then:
  - the replaced key speaks on every block a standing rotation counts; none un-counts what another counted, and no hash picks one;
  - the keys they bring in are **frozen**: nothing they sign counts and their key grants bring in no key, except that a block of a frozen key which a block of another author holds in its causal past still speaks (folds.md, Built-on stands), while no recovery rotation has settled it;
  - a recovery rotation of the key settles it. The replaced key counts on the counted line of the latest one (it has every other in its causal past), and its own rotation counts only on that line. Of the frozen keys only those brought in by a crossing rotation in that recovery rotation's causal past speak;
  - two recovery rotations of one key that do not see each other freeze what each brings in unless another recovery rotation sees or counts to it; with no latest one the replaced key speaks on every block either counts;
  - two recovery rotations of any keys that do not see each other end the identity: they and their causal pasts stand, nothing else counts;
  - every other key keeps speaking.

  With no recovery key a crossing is never settled: the new keys stay frozen, with what no one built on. A thief of an old key can so cost the identity its new key's word, never what others built on (THREAT-MODEL.md).
- **`scope.until`** on a key grant brings its key in until then: it speaks for no block it dated at or after that time, a cut there (authority.md, Whose clock tests `until`); an undated block is cut too (silence only narrows). An operator grant with `scope.until` works the same: a block resting on it dated at or after has no operator by it.

**Recovery reach (a view).** A recovery rotation cannot void a block another identity built on (named in its causal past) that arrived before the reach began: the arrival of the key's latest recovery rotation, less `recovery_reach`. When recovery rotations cross there is no latest and no reach. A block nobody built on is void whenever it arrived, and so is one that arrived within the reach.
- The identity **fold** is pure: it voids every block off the counted line (`signed_by_author` false).
- The **view** adds the reader's arrivals: `signed_by_author` is true for the voided blocks the reach protects, and for what they bring in. `recovery_reach` on a recovery rotation returns every block whose word the arrivals restore; on any other block, none. The reader's own record decides for that reader. A block whose arrival the reader does not know is read as the reader's node chooses: `unknown_arrival` is `outside` (it arrived before the reach began, so the reach protects it when another built on it) or `inside` (the rotation voids it). The protocol picks neither: a view given arrivals requires the choice, and a call without it is an argument error. A rotation whose arrival the reader does not know has no reach start, and the fold's voiding stands.
- `ownership_view` and `promise_view` read the same way, with the same choice, so a sale the reach protects stands for that reader. `ownership` and `promise_stage` stay pure and are the word (core.md, section 11).

**Autonomous systems and machines.** An autonomous system or machine is an actor with its own genesis and keys; kind `agent` or `machine` is a label that consent reads (consent.md), never validity or the identity fold. Any actor may have an operator, who answers for it: the author of a grant of kind `operator` naming it in `refs.to`; the operator brings in its keys by key grants that name that grant in `refs.under` (Keys). **The machine consents:** the grant counts only for blocks with, in their causal past, the machine's own `grant` that `answers` it `accepted`, signed by a key that speaks for the machine (its identity fold read without its operators, so it never loops). A stranger's operator grant answers for no one and rotates no key. It stands while neither side has ended it (authority.md, Ending) and its author is not dead (a `close` of them that does not carry it on; a key grant the operator signed before then stands). **The one exception to a stop cutting where it was signed:** a key grant the operator signs under the grant counts only if every stop or decline of that grant (signed by a key that speaks for its author) has the key grant in its causal past. A stop that does not see a key grant cuts it too, so an operator cannot keep rotating a machine's keys by not citing the decline; the key grants the stop saw stand. This reading takes the operator grant and the operator's key grant only when the operator's own identity fold counts them (`signed_by_author`, without the operator's operators), reads the machine's accept as its signed word, and sets no forks aside, since judging forks needs the identity fold. That grant is the only place the operator is named. A machine acts only within the grants it holds, and each block it signs for someone names the grant in `refs.under`.

**A key per counterparty, and "one person".** She may use a different key with each buyer and prove links only to whom she chooses (`same_as`, sealed to an audience), so her whole record is not linkable by default. An issuer she freely chose may sign "this key is one person, not yet used with you"; the reader trusts it or not. There is no required registry: a registry would be a middle everyone must go through. Free keys let one person look like many (THREAT-MODEL.md); stakes and such statements are the only answers.

**Contacts.** A `contact` actor is someone with no keys, written by anyone. It may carry `state.marks` such as a phone number (profiles/addressing.md). Anyone may claim it `same_as` a genesis; each reader decides.

**A two-key link.** A `same_as` that carries weight is signed by both keys: one key writes an `observe` of kind `link` (schema `link`) with `same_as` the other key, and the other key counter-signs with its own `link` that names the first block in `confirms`. With both, a reader may treat the two keys as one hand. One signature is only its author's word, and so is any third party's `same_as`. A link may be sealed to one reader (privacy.md, Audiences).

**Held keys (custody).** A person who will not hold keys may have a custodian they chose hold them, named in `state.custodian` on the genesis or key grant.
- A held key is not lesser; a reader weighs the custodian and what it publishes about how it signs (profiles/custody.md). No fold ranks custodians.
- A custodian that promised so signs only on the person's own word (core.md, section 10). Its grounds say how it came: `state.channel` (an open label: `sms`, `ussd`, `voice`, `letter`, `meeting`) and `derives_from` the hash of the raw message. Words read by a model are a reading; the person's yes to the exact terms is what it signs on (things.md, Truth).
- The person may take their keys back any time: a key grant to a key on their own device rotates the custodian's out.
- A custodian block acting on its own judgement speaks for the custodian alone, unless a grant names it.

**Profile facts** (a name, a timezone) are the person's own `observe` of kind `profile`, not identity.

## Invariants

8. A key speaks only while the key grants that brought it in are sound and no rival rotation crosses it unsettled. It is rotated only by itself, its recovery key, or its operator. Once rotated by itself or its operator, it speaks on every block that has no rotation of it in its causal past: its own rotation closes only its future. Once rotated by the recovery key, it speaks only on that rotation's counted line (`counted_to` and its `prev`-ancestry), and the reach (a view) keeps what others built on before it. Of rotations that cross, none un-counts a block another counted, and none erases what someone else built on.
19. A key held by someone else says so, and its holder signs on the person's own word only by its own promise.
