# Identity

Voices, keys, tools and contacts, `refs.key`, a close, a will. The genesis is in core.md.

An **identity** is an `actor`; its id is its genesis hash. Who may speak as her is her own signed word, her **list of voices**, and nothing else. Nothing in this protocol names a voice, a count, a wait, a reach or a clock for her; a list with a number missing is no list.

**One signature, once.** A person binds herself only by a block a key of hers signed. Her first key is made in a thing she holds (a phone, a card, a SIM, a passkey), at her first yes that binds her, and it signs her genesis. No host, door or helper makes, sees or holds a person's key. A key someone else made or can use is that someone's key, whatever it is called, and what it signs is that someone's word. Ground: only a signed word binds, and only its signer; nobody signs as anyone else (Rothbard; Kinsella).

**Her voices.** The genesis is a list of one voice: its own key, `state.public_key_sign` (and `state.suite`). A genesis may instead state her whole list in `state.voices`, the highest voice first, as a key grant does; a hidden voice may be first. Its own `state.public_key_sign` is then the `key` voice of that list that names it, and a genesis whose key is no voice of its own list is no genesis (`shape`). A twin of a genesis is another person, so no one can twin a list stated there. A key grant (a `grant` of kind `key`, `refs.subject` her id) that states `state.voices` states her whole list again, the highest voice first, and names in `refs.updates` the list it replaces (the genesis, or a key grant that states `voices`). A voice is one of:
- `{ key, suite }`: a signing key of hers (`suite` left out is `std`).
- `{ key_hash }`: a key not yet shown, named by the hash of its SPKI text, with no `suite` field. It speaks by showing itself: a key grant it signs states the key in `state.shown_key`, and names in `refs.key` the list that holds the hash. Until shown it is only a hash. A voice this reader cannot read keeps its place in the list, is compared by its bytes, and is a hole only in the lines of the keys at or below its place. Her genesis shows its own key: a hidden voice that names it is a key of hers that speaks, listed in `identity_state.keys`. A passkey shows its holder the site that asks, never the bytes: a passkey voice's signature is its key's word, never proof she saw what it signed (encoding.md, Passkeys). A wait on that voice gives a voice above time to say no.
- `{ names, needed }`: other people, by id, counted (folds.md, Counts). It speaks when one of them signs a key grant for her list in their own name, `refs.subject` her id, and `needed` of them, that one included, have signed it or named it in `refs.confirms`.

A voice may state `wait`, a duration. A `key` voice may state `reach`, a duration; the genesis states its own voice's reach in `state.reach`, and a key grant never states one (`undeclared_field`). `PT0S` states none. `reach`: a word of this key that moves, gives up or lets go anything of hers stands once her clocks have held it that long (Reach). `wait`: a word of this key that lets a key or a person act or spend for her, or more than before, stands once this key has said it again after that long on her clocks, or a voice above has said it (When a key grant stands). Nothing fills her silence: a voice that states no `wait` lets no such word stand by itself, and only a voice above says it for that voice; a voice that states no `reach` holds her moves for no time, as `PT0S` does. A list that states a wait above zero, or any reach, states `state.clocks` (blocks.md, A named clock); one that does not is no list (`shape`). A key grant states `voices` or one `public_key_sign`, never both, and `clocks` only with `voices` (`shape`); one that states `voices` names the list it replaces (`required`). A list holds at least one voice, bar one place: `voices: []`, a key grant that leaves no voice, closes her (A close).

A voice may also state `receives: true`: an amount that comes to her starts on that voice's line (folds.md, One start); one voice at most (`shape`). A key or hidden voice may state a line's bounds (A voice's bounds).

**What a key hands on.** A key's block is counted as hers because her list names the key. The record cannot tell a taken key from her hand. It never says the block is her act. A key grant is her word that the key's blocks are counted on her line. By itself it hands on nothing of hers that she did not sign with her own hand. A voice that states `passes: true` is her word that a thing of hers this key signs over is the receiver's, toward every block that has not seen that voice's end. A block another signs with her key is still his act, and her claim is on him.

**The first voice** is a `key` or `key_hash` of hers, never other people: consent to another acting for her can always be taken back, and only title is handed on for good. It needs nothing of the clock: no voice is above it to say no, so its wait is zero whatever it states.

**A voice is her, whole.** A block signed by a key of a standing voice speaks for her. The only bounds on a voice are those her list states: its `wait`, its `reach` and its line's bounds.

**A voice changes only what is below it.** A key grant by a voice restates every voice above its signer's place as it was, and its own place's `wait` and `reach` as they were; one that does not is no key grant. It may put a new key in its own place and change, add or remove any voice below. The `wait` and `reach` of a place are changed only by a voice above it. Only the first voice changes `clocks`, and only the first voice adds or changes a voice of other people.

**The people of a voice reach above it.** Where her list says so (`above: true` on that voice, placed by her first voice), a voice of other people whose `wait` is above zero may sign a key grant that puts a new key in a place above its own (that place's wait and reach kept), or that leaves no voice at all. It is never narrowing: it stands only when `needed` of them have signed it again after their wait has run on her clocks, and where no voice above them said no in that wait. A key grant of a voice of other people is one of a pair only once `needed` of them have signed it or named it in `refs.confirms`. A list that does not say so keeps meaning what it meant.

**When a key grant stands.** A key grant that brings a key in lets a key act for her. A word of a key that lets a key or a person act or spend for her, or more than before, stands once that key has said it again after its voice's `wait` has run on her clocks, or a voice above has said it. At `PT0S` it stands when signed: signing twice is her own choice in her list, a `wait` above zero. A voice of other people speaks only when `needed` of them have signed it. To **say it again** is to sign a later counted block of the same key that names the word in `refs.confirms` and has in its causal past the cutoffs that closed the wait (blocks.md, A named clock); for a voice of named people, `needed` of them each say it again so, each having signed it or confirmed it before. A wait above zero runs only on her clock: with no clocks it never runs. A voice that states no `wait` brings in no key by itself; a voice above may.
- **Saying no is signing.** A voice says no by its own key grant that states a list and names the same list in `refs.updates`, which may restate the list unchanged; it is a no once it is counted, whether or not it stands. A key grant stands only where no voice above its signer said no first. A no narrows: on her clock it holds where `names` less `needed`, plus one, of her clock's lines hold the no's receipt before their cutoff that closes the wait (blocks.md, A named clock, Which reading needs `needed`); it holds too where the no is in a second signing's causal past.
- **Two by one voice.** Two key grants of one voice, neither in the other's causal past, are a pair of that voice: neither ever stands, and the voice brings in nothing more until a key grant of that voice has both in its causal past. No hash picks one.
- **One list at a time.** A key grant counts only for the list that stands in its own causal past; one that names in `refs.updates` any other list is no key grant. Where two key grants that replace one list both stand, the higher voice's is the list; of one place, neither is.
- **While it waits.** A key grant that is waiting speaks for no one: its key signs nothing that counts, a stop included. During a wait she has only the keys she already had.
- **Narrowing.** A key grant that adds and shows no key, and only leaves voices out (each kept one as it was, the clock unchanged), lets no one act or spend more than before: it waits on no `wait`, only on its key's reach and the cut as any grant of that key does.
- **Taking oneself out.** A key grant that adds no key and only takes its signer's own place out lets no one act: it stands when signed, whatever crosses it, and though its key has a pair: no wait, reach or cut holds it.

**A close.** A key grant that leaves no voice closes the actor. For every block that has it in its causal past, no key of hers speaks, whatever its place. A close is an end of every standing word of hers (grants, offers, requests, transfers that wait on an answer, account words, hands) except a transfer conditioned on her close. For each draw of hers it is an end by its giver, read as any end of hers (folds.md, Freed): it frees the draw, for her later draws that have it in their past, where its receiver has seen the close before any yes, and an offer no yes answers at once; it never frees a draw that landed. What another accepted, before the close or unseen by it, stands as signed. The record says closed, never why. A close is for good.

**Reach: a wait before her word stands.** A `promise`, `transfer` or `grant` (her key grants of her own list among them) signed by a key whose voice states a `reach` is waiting until her clocks have held it for the reach: `needed` of her clocks' lines each hold a receipt of it and, after it, a cutoff that runs from that receipt, its `when` a span no shorter than the reach, with no receipt before that cutoff of a key grant that leaves the key out (blocks.md, A named clock). Where `names` less `needed`, plus one, lines hold such a receipt before their cutoff, it never stands. It also stands once a voice above that key names it in `refs.confirms` of a counted block. A key grant is never its own end. Her readings (`observe`), and a key grant she signs in another's list, wait on no reach of hers. A voice that states no reach is not read on her clock: no reach delays its blocks. Whether a block of any key has passed that key's ends is the cut's question alone (blocks.md, The cut). A reach never un-counts: what stood stays.

**A voice's bounds.** A `key` voice may state `each`, `limit` and, with `limit`, `every`: one line with the same bounds as a grant's (authority.md, A limit is counted along one line). A word of that key that a reach would hold is counted on that key's line from the block that brought the key in. A word that does not fit stands only when a voice above that key has said it (blocks.md, Finality); until then it is waiting (`confirm`). A key grant that adds a key under a capped voice may pass that key a part of the voice's `limit`, by its own `state.limit`, and an `each` no higher (`state.each`); it is a draw on the voice's line at that part. The words counted are those that move an amount of hers: a transfer at its `state.amount`, and such a key grant at its part. With `every`, the period turns at a cutoff on a line of her list's clocks whose `refs.subject` is the block that brought the key in and whose `when` is a span no shorter than `every`. A word a voice above said instead holds nothing on the lower key's line. Before one who does not hold her line whole, a voice's `limit` is read only from one receipt of a clock or meter her list names that states the running total; with neither, only `each` bounds a word for him.

**Ending a key.** A standing key grant that leaves a key out is an end of that key (blocks.md, The cut); the key names a clock there exactly when its voice states a reach. A key grant by a voice above the key may name in `state.counted_to` the last block of that key that is hers. The **counted line** (that block and its `prev`-ancestry through the key's blocks) is inside the cut, and a waiting block on it stands. That key's later blocks are off her line toward every block that does not have them in its past; for anyone whose block already built on one, it takes back nothing (blocks.md, Finality, What stands stays). `counted_to` takes back no block that stood. A key's own key grant names no `counted_to`: a seller cannot un-sign her sale.

**A key under a voice.** A key grant that states one `public_key_sign` and no `voices` adds that key under its signer's voice. It is no part of the list, and no later list restates it. It speaks while that voice speaks, is bounded as that voice is (its wait, its reach and, by its own part, its line's bounds), signs no key grant, and stands as a key grant of that voice does. Its signer, or a voice above, ends it by a stop of the key grant that added it. When the key of its voice is ended it ends too, unless the block that added it is inside that cut; it is then under the key put in that place. It may be sealed (privacy.md): a key for one counterparty is one.

**A key grant is a key grant.** No kind of key grant skips the cut, the reach or the pair rule: a mail key, a suite move, a reach above and a close each go through it. A key in doubt never decides its own replacement: only a voice above a key moves it to another suite (each key's suite read from its own spelling); to a reader who believes the old suite, a suite move is an end like any other.

**Encryption keys.** A key grant that states only `public_key_encrypt` is a grant of hers: it waits on its key's reach and passes the cut as any grant of that key does, and needs no `wait`. Two of one voice, neither in the other's causal past, are a pair: neither stands. A key grant that states her list may also state `public_key_encrypt`. An end of a key ends the encryption keys it named. Which key a sealer uses is a reading: the encryption keys of her standing grants in the sealer's causal past whose key still speaks for her there, less any a later standing one of the same or a higher voice has in its past; of two that cross, the higher voice's. A sealer whose block relies on one names that grant in `refs.after`.

**A suite move** is a key grant, by a voice above a key, that puts a key of another suite in that key's place. A key's own key grant is never a suite move. To a reader who believes the old suite, a suite move is an end, read as any end is. To a reader who no longer believes it, a block of the old key counts only when it is in the move's causal past, or she held it before she stopped believing that suite. Receipts of an old-suite block count in no fold. Whether a key grant counts is read on the lists that stand in its own causal past, before any pair it would cut. A pair in a suite this reader no longer believes proves nothing. Each key's suite is read from its own spelling. A hidden voice is only a hash until shown, so it outlives a break of the suite her shown keys use; a person with no voice above a broken key has no move.

**A voice or a tool.** A **tool** is an actor of its own that she grants to (Autonomous systems and machines). It speaks for her only under her grant, bounded by amount and by terms (authority.md). There is no third kind: a grant to one of her own voices fences nothing.

**`refs.key`.** A block signed by a key other than its author's genesis key names, in `refs.key`, the key grant that brought that key in: a key grant (`refs.subject` the block's author, whoever signed it) that states it as a `key` voice of its list, adds it under a voice, or shows it. A key named by hash speaks first in the key grant that shows it, whose `refs.key` names the list that holds the hash; its later blocks name that showing key grant. One id. A block of the genesis key names none.

**Autonomous systems and machines.** An autonomous system or machine is an actor with its own genesis and voices; kind `agent` or `machine` is a label that consent reads (consent.md), never validity or the identity fold. It has its own list of voices and nothing else: nobody stands over another's keys. A machine is handed over by a key grant of its own list that puts the new holder's key first. A machine acts only within the grants it holds, and each block it signs for someone names the grant in `refs.under`. It speaks for a person only under her grant.

**A person with no key** has signed nothing. A host that hears her signs a witness in its own name (authority.md, A witness): it is the host's word. She becomes a signer by her own genesis, and from then her grant may back a host's witness. A host's block acting on its own judgement speaks for the host alone, unless a grant names it.

**A key a host made.** A key a host or door made for a person is the host's key, and every block it signed is the host's own word. The host says so by a two-key link (below) between that identity and its own; a key grant of that key taking its only voice out closes the identity. What that identity owns or has to come reaches the person only by its transfer or hand-over to her own id and her accept.

**One person, many keys.** She may seal to each counterparty with a key of its own, a key under her sealing voice brought in by a key grant sealed to that counterparty and herself, or deal under an identity of its own and prove the link only to whom she chooses (privacy.md, Keeping apart). An issuer she freely chose may sign "this key is one person, not yet used with you"; the reader trusts it or not. There is no required registry: a registry would be a middle everyone must go through. Free keys let one person look like many (THREAT-MODEL.md).

**Contacts.** A `contact` actor is someone with no keys, written by anyone. It may carry `state.marks` such as a phone number (profiles/addressing.md). Anyone may claim it `same_as` a genesis; each reader decides.

**A two-key link.** A `same_as` that carries weight is signed by both keys: one key writes an `observe` of kind `link` (schema `link`) with `same_as` the other, and the other counter-signs with its own `link` naming the first in `refs.confirms`. With both, a reader may treat the two keys as one hand. A link is read between its two ids alone: it does not chain. One signature is only its author's word, and so is any third party's `same_as`. A link may be sealed to one reader (privacy.md, Audiences).

**A will.** A will is her own transfer on `{ closed: <her id> }` (promises.md, Conditions). While she can sign it holds nothing; an accept of it before the close is a request; she may end it until the close, and her stop counts for every reader once one of her clocks received it before the cutoffs that closed the close's wait. At the close her wills take in the order of her line, after every other draw of hers, whatever her close saw; a will whose thing is gone takes nothing. The receiver signs as owner in his own name. What she willed to no one stays shown hers.

**Profile facts** (a name, a timezone) are the person's own `observe` of kind `profile`, not identity.

## Invariants

6. A key speaks for her only while it is the key of a voice of the list that stands in the block's own causal past, or a key under such a voice, and no end of it has reached the block (blocks.md, The cut). A promise, transfer or grant of a key whose voice states a reach waits until her clock has held it that long. Her list changes only by a key grant one of its voices signed, changing nothing above its signer (bar where her list lets a voice of other people reach above it), that stands (blocks.md, Finality). Nothing names a voice, a count, a wait, a reach or a clock for her.
7. Nobody signs as someone else. A key someone else made or holds is that someone's key; a block about a person's words is its signer's own, and binds her only under her own signed grant.
