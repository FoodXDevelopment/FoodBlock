# Folds and views

A **fold** reads a set of blocks into one answer. A **view** reads a fold and one more thing: the reader's clock, or when blocks arrived on the reader's node.

**A fold's promise:** two nodes that hold the same blocks compute the same answer. A fold reads no clock and no arrival, and never breaks a tie by hash, by the order it met its input, or by arrival. Where the input leaves two answers equally good, it says so (`forks`, `live`, `pair`, a null), or names one only to name it, with no authority (the lowest hash). No answer depends on the machine that computes it. The protocol fixes no depth: a fold's limits on depth and work are its reader's own. A reader that stops says so: it answers `not_shown`, with what it did not reach in `lacks` (`{ stopped: "depth" }`), and never names an owner. Its `lacks` names, on each walk it did not finish, the first block it did not read. A reader that goes on answers fully. `valid` reads only a block's bytes and its pieces, so the work of checking a push grows with what the push brings, never with the store.

**Less, never otherwise.** A reader that cannot read something on a chain answers less about every thing that chain could have touched, never something else.

**A fold reads only what is valid and read.** A block that is not valid is out: it is no word, means no one and speaks for no one. A block that is valid and not read (core.md, section 8) is only a header the reader has: it keeps its place on its chain, and no fold reads its body, so it hides what it could name (A chain, Read whole). Of a block that is not read, a reader reads its header and, to say what it cannot read, its `refs.updates`, `refs.answers` and `refs.for`; a fold that answers about a record may list in `unread` each such block that names the record there and is signed by the record's author or a side the record names (`from`, `to`). What a fold does with an `unread` block is said where the fold is defined. A sealed block a reader has by its header alone is a block not read. A fold asked about a block that is not read has no answer. A fold of a promise that is not whole or well-formed returns null.

**Facts, never verdicts.** A fold says what happened: who ended a deal, with what block and when, what moved, what was paid, when it was due. Whether someone kept their word is each reader's judgement. The spec ships no score.

**A view's promise:** it is right for the reader that computes it, and says whose clock it used. Readers may differ, and neither is wrong. A view is a reading: it binds no one, and never decides what another reader must accept. Only an owner's own blocks trigger deeds in the world.

Each fold is named in `protocol.json` (`folds`), with input and output; a reading's fold, and each view, in `conventions.json` (`folds`, `views`).

## A chain

A reader answers only from words she holds, on a chain read whole back to its start; otherwise she says what she holds and what she lacks, and never "nobody" where she holds a signed word that names someone. A word is after every word it names, and after the one before it on its chain. Nothing else shows which word was made first: no date, no arrival, no hash, no size of past. So across two chains the record shows only after: never before, and never that nothing came. Before and nothing-between are shown only along one chain read whole. Where a rule needs to know which of two words came first and the record does not show it, neither came first.

**A chain** is one key's blocks, joined by `prev`. A clock's receipts are a chain like any other.

**In its place.** A block is in its place on its key's chain where its `prev` names a block of that key and its `seq` is above that block's, or, for a key's first block, where its `prev` names the first block that brought that key in and its `seq` is 1. A key has one chain: of several blocks that bring one key in, the first is the one the others have in their causal past, and a later one starts none; where none is first (two apart), none starts a chain for that key. A block whose `prev` the reader does not hold is not shown, never out of place. A block whose `prev` names a block of its key with a `seq` not below its own, or that names the first block that brought its key in with a `seq` other than 1, is out of its place: it takes nothing (`out_of_place`). A block whose `prev` names neither a block of its key nor the first block that brought its key in is on no chain of that key: a draw it signs takes nothing (`not_on_chain`), and it is no block of a pair. Each binds its signer as a block of a fork does; `chain` lists it as `broken`. Where a draw is out of its place or on no chain, that is its answer, read before whose the measure is. A fold reads a chain in `prev` order; `seq` numbers the chain and never orders it.

**A start** is the block where the chain a question reads begins. Each fold that reads a chain names its start where it is defined: a draw on a measure, the block that speaks for her where she came to own her amount (What is left); a block under a grant, the block the grant names in `refs.meter`; a clock's reading for a word, its receipt of the word.

**Read whole.** A chain is read whole for a question when each block on it, from the block back to the start, the two ends included, is among the blocks she has, and valid, and each block that names the thing asked about is read. A block on a key's chain that this reader holds and does not read hides each thing it could name. If she has it as a body, it could name each id in its `refs` other than its chain's own links (`author`, `prev`, `key`, `after`). If she has only a header, it could name each measure that key's chain held at its place (after the block that starts an owner's chain for it, a first owner's or a receiver's a draw landed to), and each word of that key it could end. A question whose answer could turn on such a thing answers `not_shown`, the block in `lacks`, whether or not a draw this reader read reaches it. A pair is read from the two headers alone. Where either branch is not read or she has only its header, every draw of that key on a measure it could name, from the pair's place on, is `not_shown`, that branch in `lacks`. A later rule counts only in a block that names it. The chain is what `prev` joins: a `seq` that jumps with no `skip` leaves no hole in it (the chain fold shows the jump). A header a reader has beside the body of the same block is no header: the body is the block. A header message `{ header, header_signature }` a reader holds counts only as its header in every fold.

A block this reader cannot judge, signed at a place in her list, is a hole only in the chains of the keys at or below that place: a word it could reach is `not_shown` for this reader, with `lacks` naming it. A block this reader can judge that has it in its causal past, or names it in `refs.defeats`, closes the hole. A block she has with its body that this reader cannot judge (`unjudged`: she lacks some of its pieces, or a suite it does not know) is on no chain and counts in no fold; it hides each thing its refs name, as a block she has and has not read does. If she has only its header, it hides nothing more than a header does.

**A chain goes on.** A chain is continued on another key by one block, signed by those who may speak for the chain's owner there, that names in `state.counted_to` the last block of the old chain that counts (a key grant that puts a new key in an old key's place: the key grant itself, when the old key signed it). A question is then read whole through that block and on back along the old chain. A holder's own chain under a grant does not go on: the giver signs a new grant for a new hand. A clock's chain goes on across its key change. Two key grants that continue one chain are read by identity.md, Her voices (two of one voice, apart, are a pair of that voice). Two accepts of her keys are read by One start (What is left). Where a chain goes on past a key replacement, the sum of the pair's draws on a measure that did not take (those with their receiver's yes) stays out of what the new key may draw on, until the pair ends as A fork is a burden says. A block of the new key that goes on the old key's chain is read after the old key's blocks.

**`not_shown`, `lacks`, `read`.** A fold whose chain is not read whole answers `{ not_shown: true, lacks }` in place of its answer, and lists in `lacks` each block it would need: its id where a block she has names it in `prev`, or where she has it only as its header or has not read it; and past a block she lacks, `{ author, key, seq }` (`key` the id of the key grant that brought the key in, null for the genesis key) for each `seq` below the missing one down to the next block of that key the reader holds, or to the start, the walk going on from that block (none when the block that names the missing one states `skip`). `lacks` lists ids first, then the entries, each in the order of its canonical text. A fold that lists what waits lists it by block, then by `on`. A start the walk never meets, with nothing lacking on the way, is not on the block's chain. Filling a hole never changes an answer, because with the hole there was none. Where an answer turns on a block she lacks, or has and has not read, it is `not_shown`, never `not_owner`. The door op `read_whole(block, start, blocks)` gives `{ whole, lacks }`.

A fold that reads an order, a sum or what a key had seen answers only on the chain it names, read whole from the block back to the chain's start. Otherwise it answers `not_shown` with `lacks`, never from the part it holds.

Why: a reader who answers from part of a chain fills the missing blocks with her guess, and a guess binds no one; the signer's own chain is the only order she stated.

**A fold's order.** Where they reach for one thing, neither takes it; both are listed, and each binds its signer as far as its own word goes. No answer changes with the order a fold reads words in. A word is counted against another only where the other is in its causal past. Holding a block outside a word's past changes no answer about that word, except where it shows that the word's own signer signed two things for one place. A lowest hash may name one of two blocks that say the same; it never picks which one counts.

## What is left

An owner's words that name a measure take from it in order (A chain), along the one chain each part of it is counted on, and nothing counts past what is left. A word for more than is left takes nothing and is never cut down: nobody signed the smaller figure. The amount stays its owner's on whatever chain it is counted, and leaves that chain only as her word says. A word that names no measure takes from none.

A **measure** is an amount its owner stated: a thing's or a circle's `pack`, each line's quantity of an open offer or of a promise a grant names in `scope.on`, a promise's `terms.backing` with the pay-ins its holder accepted, a grant's `scope.limit`, what a side of a binding deal names it to receive. What each owner owns of a measure is drawn on along one chain (A chain). A draw holds its amount from where it is signed. It takes when it is above nothing and, with the draws before it on that chain that take and are not freed, comes to no more than what its owner owns of the measure.

**One whole.** A thing that states no `pack` is one whole: a draw on it states no amount and takes all its owner owns of it. An amount drawn on it takes nothing of it (`no_measure`) and is read as a transfer that names no instance (things.md, Transfers). Nothing is converted: an amount of another kind than the measure takes nothing (`unit`).

**Who owns it first** is said where the measure is stated: whom the thing's block speaks for (a thing made `as` a circle is the circle's; one made `as` a circle by a block for which no row holds and which every owner did not sign has no owner: circles.md, One answer for a block with no row); for an output, the owners of what the makes it names took, or of the thing a keep names (things.md, A make, Produce of a thing); the side a binding deal names to receive; whom an offer, a head or a grant speaks for. Nothing else makes an owner. A draw by one who owns none of the measure anywhere is `not_owner`, whatever her own chain lacks; where she owned some and it does not fit, `past`. A draw signed before the start of her chain for the measure is `not_owner` where she owns none of it where it stands, and `not_on_chain` where she owns some on another chain.

**Her start, and her one chain.** An owner's **start** for a measure is the block that speaks for her (signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant) where she came to own her amount: the block that states it (her thing, her make, her first use, her offer, her head), her yes on which a draw to her lands, or her accept of a part passed to that key. An output of a make with no inputs is hers from the make. An output is always the owner's of what was taken (things.md, A make); three cases say only when the record shows it:

1. **A make that speaks for her**, signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant so that it binds her (authority.md, Who a block speaks for, Blocks that bind): the outputs it names from her inputs are hers from that block, her start for each. No further yes is needed: the make is her own word.
2. **A make by someone else from her goods** (toll work: a miller, a baker, an abattoir; the produce of her hen written by a holder she dealt with): the output is hers from her `accepted` answer to it, or her tool's, inside a deal that already binds her and for no more than it names her to receive (authority.md, Blocks that bind). That answer is her start.
3. **Any other claim that a thing is hers**, by one who holds no grant of hers and has no deal with her that names the output as made from her goods: nothing is hers on the record until she herself answers `accepted`. A stranger's word never starts her chain.

Until her start, no one is shown owning the output, and a maker's own block never gives the maker the output of another's inputs. Who owns an output whose input's answer is not shown is not shown, with the input's `lacks`. Her chain for that amount is the chain of the key that signed her start. A draw on it signed by any other key, hers or anyone's, takes nothing (`not_on_chain`). Another key of hers, a till or a tool draws only on a part passed to its own chain (A part passed). Ground: "first" and "had not seen" are facts only on one chain; across keys no reader can know which came first, and a guessed order binds no one (Mises: only persons act, and the order of her acts is the order she signed them in).

**One start.** An amount that comes to her starts on the chain of the voice her list marks `receives: true`, else her first voice: at her yes signed by that voice's key (of several, the one the others have in their past; two apart start none). Another key's accept of it is her yes and starts nothing; a draw on it by that other key takes nothing (`not_on_chain`). Where another fact already fixes the chain, it wins: a part passed comes back to the chain that passed it; a part she passes from one key's chain to another (a transfer to its own giver) starts on the chain of the key it is handed to; a circle that names `refs.meter` counts on the meter's chain. An amount that comes to an id with no list she has (a circle) starts as circles.md, A circle's chain, says; until then, at its one yes, or the one the others have in their past.

**A part passed.** Another chain draws on an owner's amount only from a part her word passed there: a transfer `to_owner` its own giver, which the accept of the key it is handed to starts on that key's chain; a key grant that adds a key under a capped voice, at its own `limit` (identity.md); a grant passed on, at its own `limit`; a grant that names an instance in `refs.what`, at its `scope.limit` where that is of the instance's kind, else all she owns of it; a grant that names a promise in `scope.on`, at all of its quantity she has not drawn (authority.md, Her quantity is the measure; Toll work); or a block of a tool of hers under a grant whose `types` names `transfer` (authority.md, A pass between her own tools). The part stays its owner's, counted on the chain it left. What the new chain has not drawn comes back to the chain that passed it, by the holder's own word at any time, or where the passing word has ended. Coming back needs no accept (authority.md, Coming back).

**A draw** is a counted block that speaks for an owner, names a measure, and takes an amount of what she owns of it (all she owns of it when it states none): a transfer `to_owner`; an offer that names an instance; a let-go to an open receiver; a make naming it in `state.inputs`; an allocation; a payout; a block under a limited grant, at its `state.draws`; a word that passes a part (A part passed). A block that names an instance of its signer's and a receiver is a draw on that instance, whether it is a transfer or an offer. An offer is the draw where it is its giver's own first promise to a named receiver other than himself (no status; it answers and updates nothing); it holds the quantity of the line that names the instance where that is an amount in the instance's unit, else all he owns of it. A transfer holds the amount it states. A yes to an order holds nothing: the order is the buyer's word, and the hold starts at the seller's own word that names the instance and her (his offer, or his transfer). A block that names only a kind or an amount holds nothing. **A grant's draw never lands:** it names the grant's holder, and the part stays hers, held on her chain and drawn on the grant's chain by blocks that bind her. What a tool makes or receives for her under her grant is held on the grant's chain as hers (authority.md, Toll work). While the grant stands, her own key never draws on the tool's chain (`not_on_chain`). What a hand holds for a circle is held as the circle's (circles.md, A circle's chain). A transfer of zero is no draw. The first taker after a let-go is no draw and keeps its own rule (Ownership). Ground: title passes only by its owner's giving it up, aimed at a receiver or at whoever first takes it, and only on the conditions she wrote (Kinsella, "A Libertarian Theory of Contract", 2003, p. 28). A will is no draw while she can sign (A will).

- **Holds.** From where it is signed on the chain, a draw's amount is out of what its owner may draw on: it holds, and its state is `held` until it lands or is freed. Her own signed word counts against her at once (core.md, section 10).
- **Lands.** The amount becomes the receiver's where the draw stands (blocks.md, Finality) and every word it names is in the causal past: her yes and its condition, if it carries one. **Her yes** is read from blocks, never from a deal's stage (which reads this fold): her own counted `accepted` answer to the draw; or, in the draw's own causal past, her own counted `accepted` answer to the deal's first promise the draw is `for`, or her own first promise of that deal that the draw's signer accepted. **A draw whose receiver's yes is already in its own causal past lands where it is signed** (provisional, awaiting the owner): a word acts on what is its signer's own when signed. Any end of the draw names it, so it has her yes in its past and frees nothing (Freed). `stands` still makes such a draw wait on everything else it reads: a `wait`, a `reach`, a second signing (`confirm`), a block it names for its meaning that does not stand (`named`), the hands of a circle (`hands`), and its condition. Where her yes is not yet in its past, it waits for that yes, which, as a relier's block that answers it, also passes the cut. A draw that names no receiver lands where it stands. Her accepted answer may state `state.amount`, no more than the draw's: that much lands, and the rest is freed; one that states more, or another kind, is read as stating none. Nothing fills her silence: until her yes, no one is shown owning what is held. A draw that is an offer lands, or is freed, exactly where the accept that answers it binds, or does not. Its landing then waits on Due: a transfer of its giver `for` it, to the same receiver, on the instance, performs it. That transfer takes the offer's hold over (the hold is counted once, as the transfer's), and the offer is landed where the transfer lands. A condition is read on the words in the past of the block that relies on it, never on a reader's clock. A time in a condition is shown only by its clock's cutoffs, and against a signer by her own date. A signal counts once it is counted, whatever its `at`; a later `defeats` of it ends it only for blocks that have not used it. A draw held because its condition has not held is listed in `waiting` with `on: condition`.
- **Freed.** The amount is its owner's again, for her later draws that have the freeing in their causal past. Only the one a held thing would go to can give it up before it lands. **Freed by the one it is for.** Before a draw lands, the one it would go to frees it by her own counted `declined` or `stopped` answer to it, or to the deal it is `for`, or by her counter to the offer that is the draw, even with her yes in its past. It is freed for its giver's later draws that have her answer in their past. **Freed by its giver.** An end by its giver frees it where she has seen the end before any yes, or, where the draw names a clock, once the chains that read no hold the end with no yes before it. An end by its giver that has her yes in its past frees nothing, save where a draw's landing waits on payment (`terms.waits_on: price`): its signer's end frees it where no block of his side accepting a payment toward the price is in the end's causal past. A condition that can no longer hold, by the signed words and clock it names, frees what it held, for its giver's later draws that have that in their past. "Can no longer hold" is read only from the clock's own readings: for `within`, its no-reading; for `without`, the yes-reading of its inner condition. A condition that can never be shown (a bare `reading`, a bench of `none`) can no longer hold from its signing. What a deal bound under a grant holds above what came comes back where the rest of that deal can no longer fall due: by the payee's signal that it is done; by her stop, where the offer said ahead that a stop ends what has not come (`terms.stoppable: true`); by a cutoff through the offer's `deliver_by` on `needed` chains of the clocks it names (`refs.subject` the offer); or by a ruling of a judge both sides named (her `observe` of kind `ruling`, `refs.subject` the offer, `state.ends_rest: true`; named by a message `for` the offer stating `judge`, which the other side answers `accepted`: promises.md, A new term both sides signed). It comes back as the quantity not come, or, where the limit is of the price's kind, the total at that quantity. Her stop alone ends none of the rest, and so frees none of it.
- **What landed stays.** A payment reversed after the draw landed and stood moves nothing back: no fold takes from a later receiver what a standing draw moved. Whose the goods are, where a word was not its owner's, is a judge's word.

**Read once, along the chain.** From her start, in `prev` order along her one chain read whole, each draw of hers on the measure is read once, against a running amount; no fold compares every pair of draws. What she owns of it at a draw is what she first owned, plus the pay-ins she accepted and the landed draws to her whose start is on this chain and in the draw's causal past, less the earlier draws on her chain that take and are not freed there. The draw takes when its amount is above nothing and no more than that. One that does not fit takes nothing (`past`), is never cut down, and stays its signer's word, listed with its reason. A draw that would fit only once an end she has not seen frees an earlier draw is waiting (`on: end`). A draw that took nothing never takes later; its signer signs again. What came to her counts for a later draw of hers only where the draw that brought it is in that draw's causal past, whenever her yes was signed. Of words that do not see each other, each holds against its signer; none makes a draw take on an amount another chain holds. No fold orders draws by hash, by the size of a causal past, by `seq` across chains, by a date, or by arrival. Ground: you cannot give what you no longer have, and you did not promise more than you said; both are one subtraction (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19: title passes only by its owner's word).

A fold counts a draw only on its owner's chain read whole from the draw back to her start (A chain).

**What is taken stays in the measure or leaves it.** A draw on a thing, on a circle or on what is to come stays: the receiver owns that amount of the same measure, the same id, marks and readings. A make, a let-go, a payout, an allocation and a block under a grant leave: what a make took is ended (eaten, spoiled, made into its outputs); what a let-go took is no one's until a first use takes it (things.md, Letting go); the rest is counted on the other side.

**Each reader of a measure says only what states it, who owns it first, what draws on it, and what a draw that does not take means there.** Ownership: the receiver got nothing, and what she paid is `uncovered`. An open offer: the allocation still binds the offerer and is shown `over_allocated`. A pot: the payout is the holder's word, not the pot's. What is to come: the hand-over hands on nothing. A grant: authority.md. A circle's parts: circles.md.

**The other side.** A block `for` a deal adds to one half of it: what moves, or the price. It counts in one of two ways only. **The very thing named.** A draw on the instance a half names (the deal's `refs.what`, a line's `what`, or the `of` of its price where that block is an instance) adds to that half where it lands, at what landed. It lands as any draw does: her `accepted` answer, or her yes already in its causal past (her own first promise of the deal, which its giver accepted). Where the reader does not hold the block an `of` names, the answer is `not_shown`, lacking it. **Her word.** Anything else counts at what the side that half goes to said of it, in that half's `unit`, and at nothing else: her `accepted` answer, at the block's amount or the `state.amount` she states, no more than the block's where it states one; her `covers`; her `received`; a `received` by a signer her account word names, where it names that account word. Where she said nothing in that unit, the block counts nothing. A draw on any other instance counts only by her word, never by landing. `paid` is null until a block adds to the price. No fold compares `of`.

The words of one side about one block count once, at the most any of them says: her own words, and each word of a tool that speaks for her. A `received` that `answers` a transfer is a word about that transfer. One that answers none is a payment of its own. A word by a signer who does not speak for that side is that signer's reading. What came beyond is shown (`uncovered`). A block a word of hers counts (her accept, her `received` that answers it, her order in hand) is not `unanswered`.

**Her order**, where it is in the block's causal past and states that it is her word that the goods are in her hand, counts the block toward what moves to her, for no more than it states. An order that does not say so adds nothing to what came. No order adds to a price.

No fold sums payments across deals or places.

Each reader lists the draws it read as `draws: [{ block, by, to, amount, takes, why, state }]`, sorted by block: `by` the owner it speaks for, `to` the receiver (null for none, `open` for a let-go), `amount` what it states (null for all), `takes` true or false, `why` one of `past`, `not_on_chain`, `pair`, `out_of_place`, `unit`, `no_measure`, `not_owner` when it takes nothing, else null, `state` one of `held`, `landed`, `freed` when it takes, else null. A draw that is waiting is listed there and in `waiting: [{ block, on }]`.

## Who a block speaks for

authority.md, Who a block speaks for. Every fold asks that one question: a side of a deal, the owner of a thing, the receiver of a transfer. Where a rule says a side "signs", this is what it means.

## Counts

A **threshold** is a `needed` over a set of ids: `needed` different ids (the signers of a row's hands, named witnesses, judges, signers, clocks, the people of a voice) must each sign. A **panel** is `{ names, needed }`: `names` a list of ids, each once, `needed` how many different ones must each sign. It sits under the word that says what they sign: `clocks`, a signal's or a figure's `by`, a schema's `grounds.bench`, a voice of other people. A list that is no panel (an event repeat's `by`, an account word's `by`) lets each id it names count alone. **A count counts ids:** an id counts once, by any key that stands for it where the block stands, and only if it counts at all. The door op is `count_of`. A count states a whole number, never `all` and never a share: a set a signer can leave out of her causal past is one she can shrink. Silence counts as no: one who has not signed is not counted in.

**No count is ever filled in.** Where a set has more than one voice and the author states no `needed`, nothing holds, and the block that omits it is valid: a panel's `needed`, `per_stranger`, `order_channel`, a ballot or rekey counter. Only a `needed` that is stated and is not a whole number from 1 to the size of the set is a wrong word (`shape`). A program that drafts the block may suggest a number; the protocol never picks one. A set of one id needs no `needed`.

## Ownership

**The ownership fold reads a thing's measure** (What is left): its `pack`, or one whole. Whether a thing is an instance is its own block's word (things.md, Things): a kind has no owner and no draw on it moves anything, and no other block makes an instance a kind. **Owners change only for one of two reasons** (invariant 24): the maker's first use, or a hand-over signed for the previous owner. An attack moves nothing in the record: a draw by one who owns none of the measure takes nothing (`not_owner`), and what an attacker hands over after a ruling is a hand-over like any other (core.md, section 10). So every owner shown can name its reason from `draws`: made it, or handed by X (a landed draw); an owner with none shows "no reason shown". **Nothing reaches a person**: the owner of an actor is none (things.md). **Letting go with no receiver.** A let-go is a draw that lands where it stands; what it took is no one's until a counted `transform` with no inputs that names the thing in `refs.subject` and has the let-go in its causal past takes it, and its speaker is the first owner of what was let go. Two first takers whose uses do not see each other: neither came first, neither takes, and both are listed, unless the let-go names a clock (`state.clocks`), and then the first taker is the one that came first on that clock (blocks.md, A named clock) (things.md, Transfers). A receiver's `accepted` answer counts when it speaks for the named receiver (a circle receives by a block `as` it for which a row holds, or that every owner signs: circles.md, Rows). A transfer that carries a condition lands only where the condition has held (What is left; promises.md, Conditions).

**A will.** A will is her own transfer on `{ closed: <her id> }`. While she can sign it holds nothing; an accept of it before the close is a request; she may end it until the close, and her stop counts for every reader once one of her clocks received it before the cutoffs that closed the close's wait. At the close her wills take in the order of her chain, after every other draw of hers, whatever her close saw; a will whose thing is gone takes nothing. The receiver signs as owner in his own name. What she willed to no one stays shown hers. In life a will is no draw: it is shown with the thing, in `named_by`.

**The fold is title as the record shows it, never title itself.** Property comes from first use and from what its owner handed on, not from being first to write a block, so the fold says only who the signed record names. A block saying someone made or first took a thing another already used is disputed by that other, who answers with a `defeats`, which every reader sees beside the record. Who is right is a judge's word (promises.md, Disputes), never the fold's.

**An answer never says "nobody"** where it holds a signed word naming someone. An instance on a pair answers `pair: [{ draw, to, yes }]` and no owner; every other answer says `pair: []`. A draw that rests on a pair reads `pair`. Every ownership answer names the blocks it read (`read`, sorted ids): the thing, the block that starts each owner's chain for it, each draw listed, and each yes that landed one.

**Where a thing is now.** Its place is the `to_place` of its latest counted transfer signed by whoever owned it where that transfer stands, or by a holder such an owner named in `to_holder`, else its own `refs.place`. A transfer of an amount moves no place (things.md, Transfers). If that place is a thing, the thing is wherever that place is, found the same way (stop at a repeat). A place with no place of its own is itself the answer; a thing never moved with no `refs.place` has none, and the answer is empty. If two moves do not see each other, both places are the answer; that is not a fork. **Who holds it** is a reading, never a rule: the receiver of the latest counted `to_holder` transfer of it, signed by its owner or by a holder she named, that the receiver accepted; else its owner. A node may compute it (to reach a carrier with a recall, say); no draw, title or stage reads it.

## A fork is a burden

Two blocks of one key that name one `prev` or state one `seq`, both counting in the identity fold, are a **pair** (core.md, invariant 3); a block on no chain of its key is no block of a pair. The pair shows that one key signed two things for one place; it ends nothing the key bound. A pair needs no chain: its two headers are the whole proof. In every fold but identity, the pair, and every block of that key whose `seq` is at or above the lower `seq` of the pair or that has a block of the pair in its causal past, are blocks of a fork, until a key grant puts a new key in its place (identity.md). A block of a fork that draws on a measure no draw of the pair names is read as any draw. The identity fold counts them all. The author's other keys are untouched. A fold that meets a pair names it (`forks` in a promise, `pair` in a draw list and in ownership, `equivocations` in the chain).

A block counts only when its key speaks for its author (`signed_by_author`; identity.md, invariant 6).

**A word given twice binds her to no more than she signed.** Two words of one key at one place bind that key's signer's name, and never the one it speaks for past what she signed: across every chain and receipt, at most her measures. A pair never frees or favours its signer. A block of a fork binds its signer, and counts as the base another's acceptance rests on, and never for what is to come to its signer: its stops, declines, `confirms` and `defeats` count for nothing.

**A thing drawn twice.** The draws of a pair on a measure are read together, where the first of them is read. Where they, with the draws before them, come to no more than what was there, each takes. Twins `for` one deal count once: two blocks of a pair that name the same deal in `refs.for`, the same receiver and the same amount are one draw; each is listed with that draw's answer, and its amount is counted once. A draw of the pair that takes nothing by itself (out of its place, or past what is left alone) is read as any draw. A draw of the pair that has not its receiver's yes changes nothing on the others and takes nothing (`pair`): where one has her yes, it is read as a draw alone. Where two or more have it and they come to more, which one moved the thing is not shown: neither lands, each reads `pair`, each later draw that rests on either reads `pair`, the answer lists them in `pair`, each receiver holds the word signed to her, and what each paid is `uncovered`. No later block of that key chooses between them. A draw of the pair that takes nothing waits on nothing. It ends when the receivers have given up every draw of the pair on that measure but one, by each one's own `declined` or `stopped` answer, whether or not her yes came before; that one is read as a draw alone, and a draw given up is freed and holds nothing against the others. Or it ends by a ruling of a judge the receivers chose. A draw of a pair that does not stand changes nothing on the other. A transform's `inputs` are draws on the thing they name, in the pair test as everywhere.

A named signer's pair is an end of her word for every block that had not used it. A fork on a sealed chain is seen only by one who holds both branches. Ground: a second word on one place in her chain gives again a thing she had already given; it binds her name, and never moves the thing past what she signed (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19).

## Built-on stands

A void, stop, fork or rotation never un-counts a block that an outsider counted before seeing it. Where a cut would leave a block counting for nothing, the block stands when a counted block by another author names it or accepts it, that author is not under the same cut (for a circle: no signer of one of the move's hands, no tool of hers and no one under a grant she gave: circles.md, Hands), and the cut is not in that block's causal past. An outsider who had seen the cut is not protected, and what only the cut's own subject built on carries no authority past it. The cases are in circles.md (Hands), identity.md (crossing a rotation) and Recovery reach.

## A promise's stage

**Input.** The **deal's blocks** of a first promise P (or of an allocation) are the smallest set that holds: P; every block that names a block of the set in `for`, `answers`, `updates`, `confirms`, `defeats`, `derives_from` (receipts, signals), `subject` (releases, `not_free`, a named signer's `received`) or `what` (where it names P: a hand-over of what is to come), bar a first promise that names P in `for` (a new deal it serves); for an allocation's deal, the open offer and its deal's blocks; the consent blocks between the two named sides; the payee's account words; and the chains the stage reads: each draw's chain back to its owner's start (What is left), the offerer's chain for allocations, and a clock's chains where a term names one. A stage read from fewer of the deal's blocks is the stage of fewer blocks: behind, never wrong, bar a ruling it has not seen. A block she has and has not read counts in no fold (`read` gives its codes); a stage asked of a first promise not read has no answer. Where a chain it reads is not read whole, the answer is `{ not_shown: true, lacks }` (A chain), and a stage whose answer turns on an ownership this reader is not shown is not shown. A first promise that speaks for neither side, or whose key does not speak for its author, has no stage (null). What waits on the price is read from the payee's signal (promises.md, Terms, Due). `paid` and `uncovered` are readings over the same blocks.

**Who counts.** Only blocks that speak for a side, and that stand; one that is waiting is listed in `waiting` and counts nowhere yet. An `updates` of a promise is its author's stop (any other is refused, promises.md) and counts only when it speaks for the one the promise speaks for. A side that names a thing is the thing's owner where each block stands (promises.md, Bearer). An open side is filled by the taker whose allocation binds. A promise with no `answers` is not in the deal even if it names P in `for`: it is a new deal that P serves.

**The parts.** Each part has its own small machine, and each moves only forward.

| Part | States | Moves forward by | Moves back by |
|---|---|---|---|
| An offer | live → replaced (for the one countered) or ended (for blocks that passed its end, blocks.md, The cut) | a counter by the other side; its author's withdrawal or stop; its `expires` | never |
| An answer | request → binding | it stands and, on an open offer, the offerer's yes stands (promises.md, Who may bind) | the bench's `defeats` only |
| A settling block (a delivery, a payment, work) | handed → counted | the receiver's word (promises.md, Settling), or the bench's `confirms` | the bench's `defeats` only |
| What a payment pays for | what came → more | the payee's words about it, counted once, at the most any says (What is left, The other side) | never |
| A stop | none → stopped (by `from`, `to`, `both`) | a side's onward stop | never |
| A bench name's word on a block | none → true or false | her `confirms` or `defeats`; she changes it by a later word of her own | her own later word |

**The live offers.** P is live. A counter to a live offer (a promise that answers it, no status), between the same two sides (a counter naming another side counts for nothing, `not_live`) and signed by the side that did not sign the offer, replaces that offer, and only that one, and is live in its place. Two counters to one offer both stand; the fold lists every live offer (`live`, sorted). An open offer is live alone: a counter to it ends nothing, and the offerer's accept of it is an allocation.

**The binding answer** is the earliest answer that binds (promises.md, Who may bind) among those that see one another. Answers that do not see each other cross (`crossed`); each binds, and the deal is read under each accept (`per_accept`); the stage is the furthest of them. The order of stages, lowest first: `offered` or `asked`, `declined`, `stopped` (before), `accepted`, `underway`, `stopped` (onward), `done`, `reversed`.

**Defeated blocks.** Only the deal's bench changes what counts (promises.md, Disputes). The bench is the judges the deal's schema names in `grounds.bench`, in order, then any judge both sides named by a message `for` the first promise stating `judge` that one signed and the other accepted (promises.md, A new term both sides signed); less a name that is a side. A judge named after the deal binds counts on its bench only where each side, and every holder of what either side has to come, signed or accepted the message. A block confirms or defeats for a name only when it counts, its author holds the name where it stands, its author is no side and the block speaks for no side. A name tied to a side still counts, and the stage lists each tie in `judge_ties`. A name's word on a block is false when its `defeats` of it is in the causal past of none of its `confirms` of it, else true when it confirms, else none. The ruling is false (or true) when a threshold (Counts) of names say so, `grounds.bench.needed`; with no `needed`, one name rules alone and more than one rule on nothing until both sides sign a number. With `grounds.wait` the names are a chain, and the ruling is the word of the first name in turn that has one. The first is in turn; a later name's turn begins where `needed` chains of the clocks the grounds name (`grounds.clocks`; no side's own chain) each hold a cutoff, a span no shorter than the wait, running from that chain's receipt of a side's dispute, with no word of an earlier name received before it; or the same on the earlier name's own chain. A place, never a date. A block the ruling makes false counts for nothing (no line, payment or work; an accept binds nothing; an answer loses the `covers` it carries), though it landed; one it makes true counts whatever a side says. A ruling changes what counts toward the stage and what is to come, never who owns: it reaches a draw only where it stands in that draw's causal past (What is left, What landed stays). **A side's defeats is a dispute:** a counted `defeats` by a side of a block of the deal that speaks for the other side erases nothing; the stage lists it in `disputed` (`{ hash, of, by, at }`) and the block counts as before. A side never disputes its own block; it stops. A stranger's `defeats` counts for nothing here.

**Her plan has no stage** (promises.md, Her plan): its `stage` and `accept` are null, and nothing made `for` it is read as a stage. **For a deal, the stage is the first of these that holds, from the furthest along back to the start:**
1. **reversed:** the promise is done, and counted transfers back `for` it come to what was paid. A **transfer back** is a counted transfer `for` the promise, in the price's kind, speaking for the `from` side and going to the side that paid, counted at what that side's own word says of it (What is left, The other side).
2. **done:** the promise is not repeating (no `repeat`) and counted blocks meet the terms. For each line, counted transfers move at least its quantity chosen of its `what` to the `to` side, and what adds to the price is paid. A transfer from the `from` side adds to a line as What is left, The other side, says: the very thing named, where it lands; else by the receiver's word, or her order in hand. A line that names no `what` moves its quantity, an amount (a loan). A transfer counts only when it names the first promise in `refs.for`, and goods and payments only when they go to whoever holds the receiving place where they stand (promises.md, What a deal names a side to receive). A payment `for` several promises adds to each only by its `split` parts for it, and with no `split` toward none (things.md, Transfers). Another kind moves nothing toward the line. Let P be what counts as paid toward a price, in its unit. A flat price A is paid when P is at least A. A price `{ amount: a, per: p }` at quantity q in `per`'s unit (under `band`, what was accepted inside it) is paid when P times p's value is at least a's value times q's value. A price on `lines` is paid when P is at least the exact sum of its lines' prices. No fold rounds: each test compares products of signed numbers. A `per` of zero, a quantity not in `per`'s unit, or a figure not yet fixed has no total, and such a price is never paid: the promise is never done, and `total` is null. Where a total is finer than anything payable, only the payee's word closes the gap: her `covers`, her signal, or the `step` her price names (promises.md, Terms). A flat price is the total for its line (promises.md, Terms). A job (lines that name no `what`, its `state.what` what is done) moves no thing: it is done when the work that counted (promises.md, Settling) comes to each line's quantity chosen, and any price is paid. **A line's quantity chosen** is an amount: what the binding accept names for that line, else what the allocation names for it, else the line's own quantity. A range is what may be chosen, never a choice: work never meets a range. A repeating promise (with a `repeat`) is done occurrence by occurrence, never as a whole: it reads `underway` once anything settles toward it, until a stop ends it; an occurrence named in a `stop` is stopped. **Catch weight:** under a per-unit price with `terms.band` (promises.md, Terms), a line of a `what` with a quantity is met once counted transfers move at least the quantity chosen times one minus the band; while what moved lies within the band either way the line is priced on what moved, and `total` says so; outside it the quantity chosen is priced. A line's `what` with no quantity needs one counted transfer of it. A line with no quantity has `moved` null once a counted transfer of it exists. Work with no quantity chosen is met by one counted work block.
3. **stopped** (onward): an onward stop by either side of a deal that binds (its author's `updates`, the other side's `answers`; authority.md, Ending). A stop is a `promise`: a block of another type that says `stopped` (an answer to a delivery) stops no promise. A death ends no promise (identity.md, A will); either side may stop a repeating promise onward. `from` is the earliest onward stop in causal order (of several that do not see each other, the lowest hash names one). `by` is the side the stop speaks for, `from` or `to`; `both` when a counted block of the other side `confirms` the stop (a later stop of their own is not agreement), and `both` when the first onward stops, those that do not see each other, speak for both sides: two stops that cross read as both, whichever hash is lower.
4. **underway:** a binding answer exists (promises.md, Who may bind) and a counted block `for` it exists (promises.md, Settling).
5. **accepted:** a binding answer exists.
6. **stopped** (before): every live offer was withdrawn (or stopped, whichever word: promises.md, Answers) and no answer binds. A withdrawal stops the offer it names (its `updates`, or the other side's `answers`); an accept of it binds unless the withdrawal is in its causal past. Withdrawing an offer a counter has replaced withdraws nothing else.
7. **declined:** the other side declined every live offer. Her own binding answer (stages 4 and 5) still wins: a decline ends the word only for blocks that have it in their causal past.
8. **offered** (signed by `from`) or **asked** (signed by `to`).

**Moves, and the blocks that cause them.**

| From | To | The block | Signed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | `offered` / `asked` | a first promise | a side |
| `offered` | `offered`, new live offer | a counter | the side that did not sign the offer it answers |
| `offered` | `offered`, with a request | an answer with an open point | the other side, or a taker |
| request | `accepted` | its last open point closed: a clock's receipt by `expires`; the offerer's yes (an allocation); the answer standing (her clock's cutoff, the offerer's own later word, a relier's block) | a clock; the offerer's side; the signer, her clocks or the relier |
| `offered` | `accepted` | an answer that stands to a firm offer, or a spot exchange's transfer (things.md) | the other side; the seller |
| `offered` | `declined` | a decline of every live offer | the other side |
| `offered` | `stopped` (before) | a withdrawal of every live offer | its author |
| `accepted` | `underway` | the first settling block that counts | the giver, then the receiver's word |
| `accepted`, `underway` | `stopped` (onward) | an onward stop | either side |
| `underway`, `stopped` | `done` | the last word that meets both halves | the receiver of that half |
| `done` | `reversed` | a transfer back that comes to what was paid, and its answer | the `from` side, then the payer |
| any bound stage | a lower one | a `defeats` of an answer, a delivery, a payment or an answer to one | a bench name in turn, and no one else |

**Binding only grows.** Let S be a set of blocks closed under causal past and b a block whose past is in S. If b is not a bench name's word, the stage of the deal in S with b is not lower than in S. Every test the stage makes either reads the causal past of a block the reader already has, which a new block does not change, or asks whether a block exists, which a new block can only make true; waiting is not binding, and what stands stays (blocks.md, Finality). The sums (`paid`, `uncovered`) are facts, not stages, and move either way.

**An allocation's deal.** The request is an accept of O, or a counter in a chain on O (promises.md, A counter to an open offer); the allocation is its accepted answer. An accept of a counter keeps to the counter (promises.md, invariant 12) and binds an amount. If the allocation binds nothing (promises.md, Who may bind: a request already allocated, a withdrawn one, more than the taker asked for), it has no stage (null); one that does not take as a draw still has its stage, shown `over_allocated`. Otherwise the allocation is the head of the deal: blocks that name it in `for` are its settling, and nothing counts that names O or another allocation. The sides are O's, the open side being the taker (who made the request, or the chain's first counter); the binding accept is the allocation, `offer` is O (for a counter, the counter, with its own terms whole), the terms are O's with the quantity the allocation names (else the request's), and `takers` lists that one taker. Stages 1 to 5 read as for any deal. Delivery in an allocation's deal goes to the taker and counts on her word, never on the offerer's signature. **An open offer's own stage** is `offered` or `asked` until an allocation binds, then `accepted` (or `stopped` onward by its offerer's stop of the offer itself; a stop of one allocation ends that allocation's deal alone). Nothing settles it. Its lines' `needed` and its `total` are what its allocations bind together: an allocation stopped onward binds no more, what it drew is freed and left out of the offer's lines, and `takers` still lists it. An allocation of a counter counts its quantity like any other and its price at the counter's price: `total` is the price of what the requests bind plus each counter's total, of one kind (several kinds, or a price with no total: null).

## A pot

A **pot** is what a promise's holder put behind it and what has been paid from it: one measure (What is left), whose owner is the **holder**, whom the head (a first promise) speaks for. It takes no clock (What is left, Lands). One reading serves a vouch (the voucher's own word) and a mutual pool (members' pay-ins).
- **The measure** is `terms.backing`, the holder's own word, an amount, plus the pay-ins: transfers `for` the head `to_owner` the holder, each counted for a payout when the holder's accept of it is in the payout's causal past, in the pot's kind (The other side). The pot's kind is the backing's; with no backing, the one kind every accepted pay-in shares; with a cap of `all` and neither, the one kind the holder's own payouts share; else it has none, its sums are null, and no payout takes (`unit`). An amount kept apart for its payer is a thing handed `to_holder` (things.md, Transfers) and is no pay-in.
- **A payout** is the holder's transfer `for` the head to a named receiver: a draw on the pot. It holds from where it is signed, conditioned or not, so a payout signed ahead keeps its place and a later one that does not fit reads `past`; it lands on the receiver's yes and its condition. Two that are a pair are a word given twice (A fork is a burden). Where the head names a schema whose `grounds.bench` names judges, a transfer whose condition is not a `signal` by them is no payout of the pot (`not_confirmed`); where the head's receiver is a thing (promises.md, Bearer), a transfer to anyone but the owner, where it stands, of that thing or of a thing made from it that the transfer has in its causal past (`made_from`), is none (`not_holder`). Neither takes or holds anything of the pot: it is the holder's own word from her own goods. The protocol picks no order for a short pot; a pool's own terms may say one (`if_short`), which the holder keeps by signing in it. Ground: a cap that does not count the words it caps is no cap; the holder's signed word counts against her at once.

**A cap written out.** A head whose `terms.cap` is the word `all` (the `vouch` schema declares it) and that has no `backing` is a vouch with no limit: all the holder owns on the day a claim is read, the judge fixing the sum. The fold returns `cap: "all"` with `backing` and `left` null, and no payout is past the pot; the card prints "no limit". It reaches property only, never her body or labour (core.md, section 11). It is never a default: a vouch that says neither `backing` nor `cap` binds to nothing.

The fold returns `{ holder, kind, backing, paid_in, held, paid_out, left, cap, payouts }`: `kind` `{ unit, of }` or null; `backing`, `paid_in` (accepted pay-ins), `held` (payouts that take and have not landed), `paid_out` (payouts that landed) and `left` (backing plus `paid_in` less `held` and `paid_out`; null when `cap` is `all`) are sums (encoding.md, Shapes); `payouts` is the draw list of What is left, its `why` also `not_confirmed` or `not_holder`. A fact, never a verdict. **How much is held behind a promise** is no field: the parts are already facts (each held thing's ownership, a pot's `left`), and a reader sums them.

## Releases shown

`release_shown` of a deal's first promise or a thing returns `{ subject, released, kept_open }` (promises.md, A release). `released` is `[{ block, by, side, to }]`: the counted releases that let go of what is to come to the signer on it, `side` `from` (the price is to come to it) or `to` (what moves is to come to it), null for a thing. `kept_open` is `[{ block, by, side }]`: general releases that list the deal in `state.except`. Only a side of a deal lets go of what is to come to it. Both sorted by block.

## Views

**`promise_view`** returns the same answer, except that **offered** or **asked** becomes **expired** once the reader's clock passes `expires` and nothing binds, and a promise that is **accepted** or **underway** becomes **elapsed** once the reader's clock is past its `terms.until` and the promise moves nothing (no line of it names a `what` or a quantity: a fact of its term, not of what came). **elapsed** is a fact of its term, never that it was kept, and never **done**, which would say it was kept. An unpaid sale past its `until` is not **elapsed**. A promise a stop ended stays **stopped**. What was paid is beside it (`paid`). The view also gives `condition`, `holds`, `not` or `unread`, for a promise with `terms.condition` (promises.md, Conditions), and `condition_failed` beside it: the first promise of the deal the condition rests on that ended without it, else null. It gives `missed` too: the dates in force (the first promise's `pay_by`, then each date both sides signed, `moved`) that passed before the price was paid in full, or before the reader's clock while it is unpaid; null with no `pay_by`. Each of these is a reading, binding no one.

## The circle fold

The fold of a circle C (circles.md) reads the founding block, the version that stands for the block read (circles.md, Versions), every transfer whose `refs.what` is C, every grant `as` C that a row's `to` names, every block that answers, updates or confirms one of them, and each owner's close. Only what is in the block's causal past. No step reads arrival, a hash order or a date. A parts row reads each hand's chain read whole; else `{ not_shown: true, lacks }`.

- `row_holds(block, blocks)` reads the circle from the block's `refs.as` and returns `{ circle, holds, row, every_owner, at, hands, waiting, listed }` (circles.md, invariant 20). `holds` is whether a row holds for the block, or every owner signed it; `row` the place in `rules` of the row that holds, or null; `every_owner` whether it holds by every owner together and no row holds; `at` the block (the move or a confirm of it) where it holds, the one with the fewest blocks in its past, or null. `hands` is `[{ id, block, parts, counts, why }]` sorted by block: where it holds, each hand in the causal past of `at` (`at` itself included); else each hand the reader has (the move, and each block that names it in `refs.confirms`). `id` is whose hand it is: its signer, or the circle a confirm is signed `as`. `parts` is her parts of the circle (an amount of unit `part`) read on her chain from her hand back to her start, a draw that the move, or a move it confirms, answers added back (null with no pack). `counts` is whether it counts in the row read: the row that holds; else the first row whose scope covers the move; else the every-owner way. `why` is null where it counts, else `not_voice` (no parts or place the row counts, an end of hers seen, unissued parts, one the move gives to), `waiting` (it does not stand, or the circle it is signed `as` has no row that holds for it) or `pair`. `waiting` is `[{ block, on: hands }]` while it does not hold. `listed` is circles.md, The listing, a reading: where the move gives to an owner, a tool of hers or a circle in which her hand could count, the promises signed `as` the circle that hold, are not in the move's causal past and are not done, stopped or declined, sorted; else `[]`.
- `ways_in(circle, blocks)`, a reading, returns `{ circle, rows, every_owner }`. `rows` is `[{ row, least }]`: for a `to` row its `needed`, for a parts row the fewest owners whose parts reach it (null where none can); never above the every-owner way's. `every_owner` is `{ least }`, the number of ids that own parts; null where there is no every-owner way (no pack, or unissued parts and no meter).

## What each fold returns

Every node returns the same names (`protocol.json` `folds`, `views` give each one's input and output). Ids and hashes are hex; a list of them is sorted as text unless said otherwise. Every sum is exact decimal text (encoding.md, Shapes): fold output, not signed content. A field that does not apply is null or an empty list. Beyond that:
- `signed_by_author` is true when a key of the block's author spoke for her there (identity.md, invariant 6); a block that is waiting is true here and listed by `stands` (blocks.md, Finality).
- `by`, on a stage `stopped` onward, says which side ended it; null on every other stage.
- `disputed` lists each side's `defeats` of a block of the deal, sorted by its hash then the block it names; it changes no stage. `due` is the deal's `terms.pay_by`; `paid_at`, once the price is paid in full, is the latest date among the counted payments, each dated by the first counting answer of the side paid (or a `confirms` of a judge the grounds name), else null. A repeating promise (`repeat`) is due and paid occurrence by occurrence, so its `due` and `paid_at` are null and `occurrences` reports each instead: `{ occurrence, due, paid, paid_at, done_at }` for each occurrence a counted payment names in `state.occurrence`, the doer's counted `done` names in `state.occurrence` (promises.md, Settling), or an event started (a counted `observe` of the kind the repeat's `after` names, made `for` the promise, by a signer the repeat's `by` names), sorted. `due` is the occurrence's start time, or for an event the named clock's receipt of its observe plus the repeat's `wait` (promises.md, Terms: `repeat`), null when unknown; `paid` is what the counted payments naming it put toward the price; `paid_at`, once that comes to one occurrence's price, the latest date among those payments. `done_at` is the earliest `at` among the counted dones of the occurrence, else null. `occurrences` is empty on any other promise. `stopped` lists the occurrences a pause names, less those its own signer resumed (promises.md, Answers). `paid` and `lines` report all that moved and was paid in the deal once an accept binds, so what one side holds of the other's stays visible after a stop or a death.
- `uncovered` is `{ from, to }`, reported for each side at every stage, bound or not: what that side sent `for` the deal that counted (What is left, The other side), less what came back to her, less what the other half paid for, in its own kind, less `terms.forfeit` where it is kept (promises.md, Paid for, never delivered). `to` (the paying side) is an amount in the price's kind: what she paid, less transfers back, less the price of what moved to her (by a per-unit price, the quantity moved, the whole when it falls in a band; by a line's price, a line that moved whole; on a flat price for a whole, the price once the whole moved, else nothing; counted work at a per-unit price). `from` (the delivering side) is `[{ line, amount }]`, in each line's kind (`line` null without `lines`), one entry for each line that moved something (its amount may be 0): what moved to the other side, less what the payments paid for. Under a price `{ amount: a, per: p }`, the pieces the payments pay for are P times p over a, exact, in `per`'s unit, and they are taken only from what moved in that same unit. Under a price of zero, all that moved is paid for. On a flat price, all of it is paid for once the whole moved and the price is paid in full, else nothing: no price was struck for a part. Goods that come back are a new promise of the one who takes them back (promises.md, Final), not counted here. Never below zero; null (or `[]`) when that side sent nothing. A transfer of hers that names no instance and that no word of the other side counts is listed in `unanswered: [block]`, her own word, and never counted here. It is each side's claim shown as a fact: it changes no stage.
- `price_fixed` is null when the price is a plain number; for a price fixed later (promises.md, Terms) false until it is fixed, true once. While false `total` is null and the price is never paid. A fact: it changes no stage.
- `over_allocated`: allocations the record does not order are counted together. Where on any line they come to more than the offer's quantity, none of those not ordered before the excess takes; each is shown `over_allocated` and still binds its signer. An allocation is counted against another only where the other is in its past and not freed for it. `over_allocated` is true on an allocation's deal, and in its entry in `takers`, when the allocation does not take as a draw on the open offer's lines (What is left: `past` or `pair`); on the open offer, when any does. Two allocations of one signer that do not see each other are listed in `forks`. An allocation takes only where what it draws fits on every line: the quantity it names for a line, else the whole line (a line with no quantity is one whole). A fact: it changes no stage.
- `to_come` is null until the reader has a hand-over of what is to come (promises.md, Handing on a receiving place); then `{ to, from, draws }`: `to` and `from` each `[{ owner, amount }]`, the owners of what moves and of the price and their amounts (amount null for what moves, which is one whole), sorted by owner; `draws` the hand-overs, as draws on what is to come to each side (What is left). A part of the price handed to the side that pays it counts in `paid`, dated by the accept of the hand-over.
- `received_at` is `[{ message, side, at }]`: each counted `observe` of kind `message` a side wrote `for` the deal, with the time the named clock received it (the promise's `clocks`, and no one else's receipt), `at` null while there is no clock or no receipt. A fact: "knowing" rests on it alone (promises.md, A message with a receipt).
- `moved` is `[{ pay_by, proposal, answer }]`: each new date both sides signed, in the order the answers stand in causal order. The first promise's own `pay_by` stays in `due`; a date only one side signed is not listed (promises.md, Terms).
- `to_come_on` is `[{ from, to, amount, payment }]`: a counted payment made to an owner who had handed that part on, which the payer had not seen, so the old owner holds that amount for the new one. Sorted by payment, then `to`.
- `judge_ties` lists each bench name tied to a side, `{ judge, side, how }`, `side` `from` or `to`, `how` `member` (the name owns parts of the side's circle), sorted by judge, side, how; empty until a binding accept exists. It changes no stage.
- `accept` is the binding answer: the first allocation on an open offer (in its own deal), the earliest answer that binds on a one-to-one deal. On an open offer `takers[].who` is whom the request speaks for and `takers[].lines` what the allocation draws on each line (`{ id, quantity }`, quantity null for a line with none); each of the offer's `lines` has `needed` the sum the allocations that take bind on it, in its kind, and `moved` null; `total` is the price for them, and `paid` is null. `offer` is the offer the binding accept answers; with no accept, the live offer when there is one, else null. `live` lists every live offer, sorted. `forks` are blocks of a pair in the deal (one key, one `prev` or one `seq`), sorted. `crossed` lists answers of one deal that bind and do not see each other, sorted; `per_accept` is `[{ accept, stage, paid, uncovered, lines }]`, one per crossed answer, sorted by accept, empty when none cross. `requests` is `[{ block, open }]`: answers that do not yet bind, `open` a sorted list of `when`, `which`, `whose`; an answer that is ended (her own earlier decline in its past, the offerer's decline of it on an offer with `expires`, a stop before of the offer in its past) is no request. `waiting` is blocks.md's list, for each block of the deal that speaks for a side and does not yet stand. `bench` is `{ names, needed, wait, clocks }` read from the deal's schema's grounds and the judge grants both sides signed, or `none`; the same answer `gives` gives (promises.md). In `lines`, `needed` and `moved` are amounts in the line's kind, null where the line states no quantity: `moved` is what counted transfers moved (more than `needed` when more came; `needed` when a whole instance meets the line). A job's line (no `what`) shows `needed` its quantity chosen or null, `moved` the work that counted, in the unit of that quantity, else of the line's price per unit. `paid` is in the price's kind, else the one kind every counted payment is in (more than one: null); each payment counts what the payee's word says (What is left, The other side). Until a binding accept exists, `quantity`, `total`, `paid` and `lines` are null or empty.
- **A pure function reads only what the write check takes.** `terms_total` judges the terms it reads (`lines`, `step`) as an offer's and the lines chosen as an accept's (`{ id, quantity }`). What the check would refuse is junk and reads as absent: no total.
- `circle_state` of a circle the reader lacks, or of a block that is not an actor of kind `circle`, is null: no owners, and no block speaks `as` it. Within a block whose causal past does not hold the circle, it is null too, and a circle named in such a block's audience means no one. Else it returns `{ founder, pack, owners, unissued, rules, clocks, meter }`: `pack` the founding block's, or null (owners unsaid); `owners` `[{ owner, amount, no_person }]` sorted by owner, each id that owns parts (unissued parts left out), `no_person` true where its owners end in no person, or `{ not_said: true }` where the circle states no pack; `unissued` the parts the circle holds of itself, or null with no pack; `rules` the rows that stand (circles.md, Versions); `clocks` the clock that stands, or null; `meter` the `refs.meter` of the block that stands (the version that stands, else the founding block), or null. Where an owner's chain is not read whole, `{ not_shown: true, lacks }`.
- `circle_versions` of a circle (the founding block; null for any other block) returns `{ circle, versions, now }` (circles.md, Versions). `versions` is `[{ version, updates, author, holds, hands }]` sorted by version: each version the reader has (an `actor` of kind `circle`, `as` the circle, that `updates` the founding block or a version). `holds` is whether a row of the founding block that names `actor`, or every owner, holds for it, and what it updates is the founding block or a version that holds; `hands` the ids whose hands counted, sorted. `now` is the id of the version whose rows stand for every block the reader has, or the founding block's id where no version stands.
- `shares_on` of an instance or a place returns `{ standing, nothing }`. `standing` is `[{ share, owner }]`: the things of kind `share` that name it in `refs.subject`, made by whoever owned it where the share stands (the ownership fold of the share's causal past), each with its own owner now. They stand against every later owner of the thing; selling the thing moves none of them. `nothing` is `[{ share, reason }]` for a share that counts for nothing: `not_a_thing` (its subject is an actor, a transform or any block that is not a thing), `kind` (its subject is a kind by its own block, things.md, Things), `not_owner` (its maker did not own the thing where the share stands). A share names one thing, so a copy, an offspring or a thing made by the same method shows none. Both lists are sorted by share.
- `use_shown` of a place, with an optional named clock, returns `{ clock, users, disputes }`. A use is a counted `transform` with no `derives_from`, naming the place in `refs.subject`, with `state.use` text (things.md, Uses of a place). `users` is one entry per user (whom the use speaks for), sorted by id: `uses` (the distinct `state.use` words, sorted), `count` (uses dated), `unreceived` (uses the named clock has not received), `first` and `last` (the earliest and latest date), `seasons` (the number of different calendar years among the dates), `bounds` (the distinct polygons the user's uses carry, sorted by their coordinates as text), `resting` (the latest `state.resting` the user wrote, or null), `overlaps` (the other users whose bounds share area with hers, sorted), `layers` (the distinct `state.layer` words, sorted), `overlap_uses` (`[{ with, use }]`, one per overlap, `use` `same` when the two share a use word and `different` when they do not: shown, never decided) and `leave` (null, or `{ of, since, until }`: the place owner's live `grant` of kind `leave` to this user, things.md; a use by leave is shown "by leave of X since D" and never as a use that stands against X). `clock` is `clocks` when a clock was named, and a use is dated by the time `needed` clocks received it; `author` otherwise, and a use is dated by its author's own `at`. `disputes` is `[{ defeats, by, of, user, standing }]`, one per counted `defeats` of a use record (`of`): `standing` is `earlier_use` when its `derives_from` names an earlier use of the place signed by the disputer, `title` when it names a transfer of the place to the disputer, else `none`. No users means no use in what the reader holds. The overlap is shown to both sides and never decided.
- `not_free_shown` of a block returns `{ shown, nothing }`. `shown` is `[{ block, by, reason, threat, at }]`: the counted `observe`s of kind `not_free` that name the block in `refs.subject` and were written by the block's own author, the signer's words as written (`reason`, `threat`, `at` null when absent). `nothing` is `[{ block, why }]` for words by anyone else (`why`: `not_signer`). A stop or an `updates` of one is never read. It changes no stage and no ownership. Both lists are sorted by block.
- `invasion_shown` of a claim of invasion (an `observe` of kind `invasion`; null for any other block) returns `{ claimant, holder, evidence, judges, rulings }` (promises.md, A claim of invasion). `claimant` is whom the claim speaks for. `holder` is who holds the claim now: the one owner of the claim, where a hand-over of it (a transfer naming the claim in `refs.what`) has landed; while a hand-over is held or a pair stands, the owner it was drawn from; else the claimant. `evidence` is the claim's `derives_from`, sorted. `judges` is `[{ judge, named_by }]`: messages `for` the claim stating `judge`, by the claimant side or one the claim names in `refs.to`, naming a judge who is neither. `rulings` is `[{ block, judge, named_by, word, evidence }]`: a counted `confirms` or `defeats` of the claim by a named judge. A naming or ruling by a side, or by a judge no one named, is not listed. Nothing is decided and no stage changes.
- `where_now` is empty when no place is known, and two or more when moves cross. `ownership` of an instance returns `{ kind, stated, owners, owner, held, ended, let_go, draws, waiting, pair, named_by, read }`, or `{ not_shown: true, lacks }` when a chain it reads is not read whole, or no answer for a block that is not read: `kind` `{ unit, of }`, the unit of its `pack` and the instance's own `refs.of` (null for one whole); `stated` the `pack`, or null; `owners` `[{ owner, amount }]` sorted by owner, `amount` null where it is not said (things.md, A make), an owner whose start is her `accepted` answer listed only once it is held (What is left); `owner` the one owner shown, while nothing is held, nothing let go waits for a first use and no pair stands, else null; `held`, `ended` and `let_go` sums in the pack's unit, and null for one whole (it has no kind; its `draws` say where it is); `draws` as What is left; `pair` and `read` as Ownership, An answer never says "nobody". `named_by` is `[{ block, by, own, pair, ref }]`, sorted by block: each block the reader holds that names this thing, or a draw this answer lists, by any ref, other than the draws and yeses this answer already lists; whom it speaks for (`by`); whether it is backed by whom the named draw spoke for (`own`); whether it is one of a pair (`pair`); and the ref that names it (one entry per block: the first ref key, in byte order, that names it, never `author`, `prev`, `key` or `after`; `own` is false where the block speaks for no one; the yeses left out are each receiver's own `accepted` answer to a listed draw, and each yes that landed one, her order included). A word is listed whether or not it counts: showing it moves nothing and frees no one. A reader who holds none says nothing of whether one exists. `made_from` of an instance returns `[{ thing, from, make, took }]`: each thing written as an output of a counted make that took from it (`from` the instance it took from, `took` the amount, null for all), and so on along the makes, sorted by thing then make; a reading of what the reader holds.
- A fold that counts a block for nothing has a code (`protocol.json`, `refusals.codes.fold`). The code explains; it is not part of the answer. `promise_stage` returns the stage alone.
- `chain(blocks, brought_in)` folds the blocks of **one signing key** (the caller passes only that key's) with `brought_in`, the id of the block that brought the key in (the genesis or a key grant), which its first block names as `prev`. An identity with several keys has one chain per key. Each block takes a place: its `prev`, or for a **first block** the key's root; a first block names `brought_in`, or, at `seq` 1, a block that is none of the key's blocks given, so two first blocks of one key take one place whichever bringer they name (core.md, invariant 3). It reports `equivocations`, the pairs that prove each group of blocks taking one place or stating one `seq` (a star: each paired with the group's lowest hash, nodes.md); `head`, the hash of the block with the highest `seq` (null when two share it); `gaps`, the missing seq numbers, lowest first, at most `limits.gaps_max` (a block may say `seq` 2^53 - 1), with `ok` false whether or not all are listed (a gap before a `seq` whose blocks are all skips is explained; any other is not); and `broken`, each block whose `prev` does not name the block before it: `brought_in` at `seq` 1 (or a skip), or a block given one `seq` lower (any lower, for a skip); a block whose `prev` is neither is broken, and so is a block out of its place or on no chain (A chain, In its place). A block that is broken or follows a gap is still valid (core.md, section 8). Each is read from the block and its `prev` alone, never from the order given. `length` counts the key's blocks, leaving out the members of every pair; later blocks of the key count (A fork is a burden).

## Invariants

21. A fold reads only valid, read input, and is a function of it: the same blocks give the same answer on every node, whatever order they came in. A block `valid` refuses counts for nothing; a block not read is only a header the reader has.
22. Each owner's amount of a measure is drawn on along one chain. A draw past what is left, one of a pair, or one off the chain takes nothing and never frees its signer of what it binds.
23. No more of a measure is owned, held, ended and let go than the measure, in any fold.
24. An owner changes only for one of two reasons: the maker's first use, or a transfer signed for the previous owner. No fold, view, ruling, circle or count moves title another way.
25. An amount changes only by a block that speaks for its owner, by the answer of the receiver that block names, or by the signal of a signer that block names.
26. A block speaks for one party: its author, the circle it names in `as` where a row of the circle holds for it, or every owner signs it, or the grantor of the grant it acts under. A fold never counts it for another.
