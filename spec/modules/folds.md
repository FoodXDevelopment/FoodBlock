# Folds and views

A **fold** reads a set of blocks into one answer. A **view** reads a fold and one more thing: the reader's clock, or when blocks arrived on the reader's node.

**A fold's promise:** two nodes that hold the same blocks compute the same answer. A fold reads no clock and no arrival, and never breaks a tie by hash, by the order it met its input, or by arrival. Where the input leaves two answers equally good, it says so (`forks`, `live`, a null), or names one only to name it, with no authority (the lowest hash).

**A fold reads only what is whole and valid.** It reads the largest part of its input in which every block is valid (core.md section 8: checks 1 to 4 and 8 against that part, with its schemas as the schema store, and checks 6 and 7 on what the block rests on) and every block's needs are present (core.md, check 5; `protocol.json`, `needs`). A block that fails is out; any block that needs it is out (every later block of a chain holding an ill-formed block, too); a block whose check read a block that is out is judged again without it. A block the input leaves out, or no node would take, is no word: it means no one and speaks for no one. A sealed block held by its public header alone meets needs, and no fold reads it. A fold of a promise that is not whole or well-formed returns null.

**Facts, never verdicts.** A fold says what happened: who ended a deal, with what block and when, what moved, what was paid, when it was due. Whether someone kept their word is each reader's judgement. The spec ships no score.

**A view's promise:** it is right for the reader that computes it, and says whose clock it used. Readers may differ, and neither is wrong. A view never decides what another reader must accept. Only an orderer's own blocks trigger acts in the world.

Each fold and view is named in `protocol.json` (`folds`, `views`), with input and output.

## Orderer

A record's **orderer** is the key that signed its first block: a boss for that one record, chosen by whoever opens it (for an offer, the seller). Handing the ordering on is a grant (authority.md), never a field. Its binding blocks (an offer's allocations) must each have the one before in their causal past; two that do not are a fork (A fork is a burden), listed in `forks`. Two members who may sign for a circle and allocate from its offer offline are such a pair, and the circle owes both.

## Who a block speaks for

authority.md, Who a block speaks for. Every fold asks that one question: a side of a deal, the owner of a thing, the receiver of a transfer. Where a rule says a side "signs", this is what it means.

## Counts

A **threshold** is `{ needed, wait? }` over a set of voices: `needed` different voices (holders of a power, named witnesses, referees, signers) must each sign, and with `wait`, the count is made only after the wait has run (signed times only). A voice counts once, and only if it counts at all. The fold is `quorum`. **"All current holders"** is stated as the word `all` where the set of voices is a circle's holders of a power: `needed: { sign: "all" }` means every holder of `sign` in the fold of the block's causal past, so "all twelve of us" is 13-of-13 once someone joins, never 12-of-13. A block read in its own past keeps the count of its own day. **A share of the members** is stated `needed: { sign: { of_members: "2/3" } }`: that share of the members in the fold of the block's causal past, as whole numbers `p/q` with `0 < p <= q`, rounded up and never below one (`shape` otherwise). Silence counts as no: a member who has not signed is not counted in.

**No count is ever filled in.** Where a set has more than one voice and the author states no `needed`, nothing holds, and the block that omits it is valid: a circle's `removal.needed`, a schema's `grounds.needed`, a signal's `signers`, `per_stranger`, `order_channel`, a successor's witnesses, a ballot or rekey counter. Only a `needed` that is stated and is not a whole number from 1 to the size of the set is a wrong word (`shape`). This is the one rule; each place that reads a threshold points here. A program that drafts the block may suggest a number; the protocol never picks one. A set of one voice needs no `needed`. Each reader of a threshold says only what its voices are and what holding does.

## One whole

An unstated amount is one whole instance. A transfer with no `amount` and no `pack` moves a whole instance and covers its line. A `refs.what` with no quantity needs one counted transfer of it. A line with no quantity has `moved` null once a counted transfer of it exists. Work with no quantity chosen is covered by one accepted work block.

## Ownership

**A kind has no owner.** The owner of a thing some counted thing names in `refs.of` is none: a recipe or any kind is a description, and its instances have owners. **A thing is owned only once it shows itself as an instance**: it names its kind in `refs.of`, or it was made (it derives from a make), or it names what it is part of or where it is (`refs.subject`, `refs.place`). A bare thing has no owner, and nothing fills that in. **Produce of a thing.** The first owner of an output of a keep (a counted `transform` that names a thing in `refs.subject` and consumes nothing, one such keep among its `derives_from`) is the owner of the thing kept where the keep stands, not its writer; a share of the means does not change it. **Letting go with no receiver.** After an owner's open hand-over (no `to_owner`) the owner is none until a counted `transform` with no inputs that names the thing in `refs.subject` and has the hand-over in its causal past takes it: its speaker is the first owner, and later transfers follow from there; two that do not see each other are a conflict with no owner, unless the hand-over names a clock (`state.keepers`): then the first taker the clock received is the first owner (things.md, Transfers). **Two buyers from one seller** are compared from him: his chain orders them, the sale he signed first stands, and a second, signed when the thing was no longer his, counts for nothing. **Owners change only for one of three reasons** (invariant 32). Property comes from first use or an owner's hand-over, and the only other title shift comes from an attack: a ruling plus the attacker's own signed payment, or the victim reclaiming her own. So every change of `owner` here has a transfer signed for the previous owner, or is the maker's first use. Every owner shown can name its reason from `transfers`: made it (none), handed by X (the last one), or paid after a ruling (a transfer signed by the attacker after it); an owner with none shows "no reason shown". **Nothing reaches a person**: the owner of an actor is none (things.md). The orderer of an instance's ownership is its owner. A transfer counts only if it speaks for the owner the previous counted transfer named (the maker, for the first) and has that transfer in its causal past. The next counted transfer is the earliest the owner signed (a later one by the same owner counts for nothing); two that do not see each other are a fork. The owner is the `to_owner` of the last counted transfer, else the maker. **The maker is who the thing's block speaks for**: a thing made `as` a circle belongs to the circle, and one made `as` a circle by someone without `sign` has no maker and no owner. A receiver's `accepted` answer counts when it speaks for the named receiver (a circle may receive, by a member who may sign for it). A transfer to a named receiver moves ownership and holding when the receiver answers `accepted`; until then it stays with the giver. A transfer that carries a condition (things.md, Transfers) is a candidate only while its condition holds at the reader's clock; a reader with no clock reads only a condition that needs none. Nobody passes on more than they had: of the owner's transfers that stand, the earliest in causal order counts, so a conditioned transfer signed first comes first once it holds, and a later one by the same owner passes nothing. One its named receiver let go (her counted `stopped` or `declined`, signed before it held) is no candidate. It is read like an offer: an accept that has the giver's withdrawal in its causal past counts for nothing, and the giver's decline of the accept ends it as things.md, Transfers says (authority.md, Ending). A transfer to an open receiver (a `count`) moves on the orderer's signature.

**The fold is title as the record shows it, never title itself.** Property comes from first use and from what its owner handed on, not from being first to write a block, so the fold says only who the signed record names. A block saying someone made or first took a thing another already used is disputed by that other, who answers with a `defeats`, which every reader sees beside the record. Who is right is settled only by a referee both sides named (promises.md, Disputes), never by the fold.

**Where a thing is now.** Its place is the `to_place` of its latest counted transfer signed by whoever owned it where that transfer stands, or by a holder such an owner named in `to_holder`, else its own `refs.place`. If that place is a thing, the thing is wherever that place is, found the same way (stop at a repeat). A place with no place of its own is itself the answer; a thing never moved with no `refs.place` has none, and the answer is empty. If two moves do not see each other, both places are the answer; that is not a fork.

## A fork is a burden

Two blocks of one key that name one `prev`, or two first blocks of one key, both counting in the identity fold, are a **pair** (core.md, invariant 3). The pair proves the key lied; it ends nothing the key bound. In every fold but identity, the pair and every later block of that key (one with a block of the pair in its `prev`-ancestry through that key's blocks) are blocks of a fork, until the key is rotated (identity.md, Rotation). The identity fold counts them all, so the key can be rotated. The author's other keys are untouched. A fold that meets a pair names it (`forks` in a promise, `conflict` in ownership, `equivocations` in the chain).

A block counts only when its key speaks for its author (`signed_by_author`, pure: no arrivals). A block of a rotated, frozen or void key is no word of its author's.

**A block of a fork counts never for what is owed to its signer; it counts always as what its signer owes, or as the base another's acceptance rests on.** It counts where it binds its signer or another's word stands on it: an offer, a counter, an accept, an `accepted` answer, a transfer or work block the other side accepted or a named confirmer confirmed, a transfer to a named receiver who accepted it, a grant she gave (it keeps binding her; it is no stop), and her `accepted` answer or counter that makes the sender no stranger to her (consent.md). It counts for nothing where it would free or favour its signer on their word alone: its stops, declines, `confirms`, `defeats`, and goods moved to an open receiver on its own signature. A buyer who took the eggs and then signed two blocks naming one `prev` still owes for them.

## Built-on stands

A void, stop, fork, rotation or removal never un-counts a block that an outsider counted before seeing it. Where a cut sets a block aside, the block stands when a counted block by another author names it or accepts it, that author is not under the same cut (for a circle: no member in the circle's fold of that block's causal past), and the cut is not in that block's causal past. An outsider who had seen the cut is not protected, and what only the cut's own subject built on carries no authority past it. The cases are in circles.md (The cut), identity.md (crossing a rotation) and Recovery reach.

## A promise's stage

Input: the first promise P, and every block that names P in `for` or answers a block in the deal (and, for stage 8, the consent blocks of its sides). A first promise that speaks for neither side, or whose key does not speak for its author, has no stage (null). The input may instead be an **allocation** (promises.md, Who may bind): the offerer's `accepted` answer to a request or counter on an open offer O, read as a deal of its own (below). A promise with no `answers` is not in the deal even if it names P in `for`: it is a new deal that P serves.

**Step 0, who counts.** Only blocks that speak for a side. An `updates` of a promise is its author's stop (any other is refused, promises.md) and counts only when it speaks for the one the promise speaks for. A side that names a thing is the thing's owner where each block stands (promises.md, Bearer). An open side is filled by whoever's accept binds.

**The live offers.** P is live. A counter to a live offer (a promise that answers it, no status), between the same two sides (a counter naming another side counts for nothing, `not_live`) and signed by the side that did not sign the offer, makes that offer no longer live and is live in its place. Two counters to one offer both stand; the fold lists every live offer (`live`, sorted). An open offer is live alone: a counter to it ends nothing, and the offerer's accept of it is an allocation.

An accept binds the offer it answers when: the offer was live where the accept stands (among counters in the accept's causal past); it keeps to the offer's quantity (promises.md, Who may bind); it is not a quote request; and the offer's signer has not ended it by declining the accept (authority.md, Ending). Of all binding accepts the earliest in causal order binds. Two that do not see each other are a fork of the accepter's chain, listed in `forks`. If they consent to the same thing (one offer, one quantity or none), the deal binds, the lowest hash naming the accept only to name one. If they consent to different terms, the one the other side's act names binds (a counted block of the other side's in the deal, but a decline of it, that names it among its refs, as a delivery's `after` does). If that act names none of them or more than one, neither binds.

**Defeated blocks.** Only the deal's referee changes what counts (promises.md, Disputes). The bench is the confirmers the deal's schema names in its grounds, in order, then any referee both sides named by a grant of kind `referee` one signed and the other accepted (`refs.to` her, `refs.subject` the first promise); less a name that is a side. A block confirms or defeats for a name only when it counts, its author holds the name where it stands, its author is no side and the block speaks for no side, and its author holds the role by no grant a side signed (in its own name or `as` a side's circle) that the binding accept had not seen. A name tied to a side still counts, and the stage lists each tie in `referee_ties`. A name's word on a block is false when its `defeats` of it is in the causal past of none of its `confirms` of it, else true when it confirms, else none. The ruling is false (or true) when a threshold (Counts) of names say so, `grounds.needed`; with no `needed`, one name rules alone and more than one rule on nothing until both sides sign a number. With `grounds.wait` the names are a chain and the ruling is the word of the first name in turn that has one. The first is in turn; a later name is in turn once every name before it has no word on the block, counting only its words dated (`at`) at or after the wait has run from the earliest `at` of the signed receipt of a side's counted `defeats` of the block given by the name before it (an `observe` of kind `receipt` that `derives_from` the defeats and carries a push answer from that name's node, signed by a key that speaks for it, listing the defeats as taken). A block the ruling makes false counts for nothing (no line, payment or work; an accept binds nothing; an answer loses the `covers` it carries); one it makes true counts whatever a side says. **A side's defeats is a dispute:** a counted `defeats` by a side of a block of the deal that speaks for the other side erases nothing; the stage lists it in `disputed` (`{ hash, of, by, at }`) and the block counts as before. A side never disputes its own act; it stops. A stranger's `defeats` counts for nothing here.

**The stage is the first of these that holds, from the furthest along back to the start:**
1. **reversed:** the promise is done, and counted transfers back `for` it cover what was paid. A **transfer back** is a counted money transfer `for` the promise, speaking for the `from` side and going to the side that paid, covering what that side's own answer says (by the payee's word). Other money is neither payment nor refund.
2. **done:** the promise is not standing (no refrain, promises.md, Refrain; no `repeat`) and counted blocks cover the terms. For each line (or the whole), counted transfers move at least the quantity accepted of `what` to the `to` side, and counted money transfers that speak for the `to` side and go to the `from` side cover at least the price, each by its own money, or by what the `from` side's accepted answer says it covers (`state.covers`) where that is more, in the price's currency (promises.md, Settling: the payee may take less, or another currency, never count money that moved for less; of several answers the latest in causal order counts, and answers that do not see each other count as no `covers`). A **goods transfer** speaks for the `from` side and moves ownership (`to_owner`; a loan, `to_holder`, settles no sale); it counts toward a line only when the instance is the line's `what` or names it in `refs.of`, and its giver owned it where the transfer stands; each whole instance counts once toward a line. A transfer counts only when it names the first promise in `refs.for`, and goods and payments only when they go to the owner of what is owed to them where they stand (promises.md, Owed); money in another currency covers nothing unless the payee's answer says what it covers. A payment `for` several promises counts toward each only by its `split` parts for it, and with no `split` toward none (things.md, Transfers). A transfer moves its `amount`, else the `pack` of each thing it names, **in the line's unit**; another unit moves nothing toward the line, and with neither, One whole. A price whose total cannot be computed (money per unit with no quantity in `per`'s unit, or a `per` of zero) is never paid: the promise is never done, and `total` is null. A flat money price is the total for the whole promise or the line it sits in (promises.md, Terms). A job or time promise moves no thing: it is done when the receiving side has `answers`-ed the worker's work blocks `accepted`, they cover the quantity accepted (One whole), and any price is paid. **The quantity chosen** is an amount: the binding accept's, else the allocation's, else the terms'. A range is what may be chosen, never a choice: work never covers a range, and a promise to yourself with a range quantity has chosen none (`quantity` null, a line needs none). A standing promise with a `repeat` is done occurrence by occurrence, never as a whole: it reads `underway` once anything settles toward it, until a stop ends it; an occurrence named in a `stop` is stopped. **Catch weight:** under a per-unit price with `terms.band` (promises.md, Terms), a line of a `what` with a quantity is covered once counted transfers move at least the quantity chosen times one minus the band; while what moved lies within the band either way the line is priced on what moved, and `total` says so; outside it the quantity chosen is priced. Exact.
3. **breached:** the promise is a refrain (in the terms of the offer the binding accept answers), and either a threshold (Counts) of the deal's referees each sign a counted `defeats` of the deal's head (its first promise, or that offer) saying it happened (its `when`: an instant, or a range's `from`; else its `at`) no later than the refrain's `until`, not taken back by the same name (it defeated that `defeats`, with no later `confirms` of its own; with a `wait`, the first name in turn that has one); or the refrain's condition is a signal (promises.md, Conditions) that holds at its `until`. `from` is the hash of that `defeats` (of several, the lowest hash), null when the signal breached it. A side's `defeats` of the head is a dispute and breaks nothing. A refrain not breached, with something paid and counted transfers back covering all that was paid (price paid in full or not), is **reversed** here.
4. **stopped** (onward): a stop `stopped` with `onward`, by either side (its author's `updates`, the other side's `answers`; authority.md, Ending). A stop is a `promise`: a block of another type that says `stopped` (an answer to a delivery) stops no promise. A death ends no promise (profiles/successor.md); either side, or the successor, may stop a standing promise onward. `from` is the earliest onward stop in causal order (of several that do not see each other, the lowest hash). `by` is the side the stop speaks for, `from` or `to`; `both` when a counted block of the other side `confirms` the stop (a later stop of their own is not agreement), and `both` when the first onward stops, those that do not see each other, speak for both sides: two stops that cross read as both, whichever hash is lower.
5. **underway:** a binding accept exists (promises.md, Who may bind) and counted blocks `for` it exist (promises.md, Settling).
6. **accepted:** a binding accept exists.
7. **stopped** (before): the author withdrew every live offer (`before`) and no accept binds over the withdrawal. A withdrawal stops the offer it names (its `updates`, or the other side's `answers`); an accept of it binds unless the withdrawal is in its causal past. Withdrawing an offer a counter has replaced withdraws nothing else.
8. **declined:** the other side answered `declined`, or, on a promise with two named sides, the other side holds a live STOP naming the author of the first promise (`consent`, consent.md): a block sent despite a STOP is kept and reads as declined in advance. Her own binding accept (stages 5 and 6) still wins.
9. **offered** (signed by `from`) or **asked** (signed by `to`).

**An allocation's deal.** The request is an accept of O, or a counter in a chain on O (promises.md, A counter to an open offer); the allocation is its accepted answer. An accept of a counter keeps to the counter (invariant 15) and binds an amount. If O's stage does not list the allocation among its `takers` (it allocates a request already allocated, a withdrawn one, or more than the taker asked for), it has no stage (null). Otherwise the allocation is the head of the deal: blocks that name it in `for` are its settling, and nothing counts that names O or another allocation. The sides are O's, the open side being the taker (who made the request, or the chain's first counter); the binding accept is the allocation, `offer` is O (for a counter, the counter, with its own terms whole), the terms are O's with the quantity the allocation names (else the request's), and `takers` lists that one taker. Stages 1 to 6 read as for any deal. **An open offer's own stage** is `offered` or `asked` until an allocation binds, then `accepted` (or `stopped` onward by its offerer's stop of the offer itself; a stop of one allocation ends that allocation's deal alone). Nothing settles it. Its `quantity` and `total` are what its allocations bind together: an allocation stopped onward binds no more, its quantity is freed and left out of the offer's `quantity`, and `takers` still lists it. An allocation of a counter counts its quantity like any other and its money at the counter's price: `total` is the price of what the requests bind plus each counter's total, in one currency (several currencies, or a price with no total: null). The view reads an open refrain offer as `elapsed` once its `until` passes.

## A pot

A **pot** (a view: a payout's condition may read the reader's clock) is money put behind a promise, and what has been paid from it. One reading serves a vouch (the voucher's own word, `terms.backing`) and a mutual pool (members' pay-ins). The head is a first promise; its **holder** is whom it speaks for. The pot holds three things:
- **Backing:** `terms.backing`, the holder's own word: money, or a count of one named kind (an amount: "up to 2 heifers").
- **Pay-ins:** money transfers (or, in a count pot, transfers of an amount in its unit) `for` the head to the holder, each accepted by the holder. Whoever pays signs their own; none is forced equal.
- **Payouts:** the holder's money (or amount) transfers `for` the head to a named receiver, each accepted by that receiver. A payout that carries `state.condition` counts once the condition holds at the reader's clock, and the receiver may let it go as things.md, Transfers says. Where the head names a schema whose grounds list confirmers, a payout counts only on a `signal` by them; any other condition shows `not_confirmed`.

The holder is the **orderer** of her own payouts. They are read in causal order: a payout counts when the pot as its causal past shows it (backing, and the pay-ins and accepts it has seen), less the payouts that count there, still holds its money. Two payouts that do not see each other are also read together: if both fit alone and together pass the pot, neither counts. The protocol picks neither, and the pool's own terms may say an order (`if_short`), which the holder keeps by signing in it. A payout past the pot counts for nothing against it. It is still the holder's signed word, shown. The pot is in one measure: the backing's currency or unit, else the lowest pay-in's; nothing is converted, and money paid into a count pot is not read as the count.

**A cap written out.** A head whose `terms.cap` is the word `all` (the `vouch` schema declares it) and that has no `backing` is a vouch with no limit: all the holder owns on the day a claim is read, the referee fixing the sum. The fold returns `cap: "all"` with `backing` and `left` null, and no payout is past the pot; the card prints "no limit". It reaches property only, never her body or labour (core.md, section 11). It is never a default: a vouch that says neither `backing` nor `cap` binds to nothing.

The fold returns `{ holder, currency, unit, backing, paid_in, paid_out, left, cap, pay_in, held_back, payouts }`: `currency` for a money pot (sums as numbers), `unit` for a count pot (sums as decimal text), the other null. `left` is backing plus pay-ins (only under `kept`) less payouts that count, never below zero. **Each pay-in is kept or held.** The head's `terms.pay_in` is `kept` (a premium: leaving takes nothing back) or `held` (it comes back in full and is never paid out while held); one unit of money cannot be both. Under `kept` the payouts draw on the backing and the pay-ins; under `held` on the backing alone, and `held_back` shows the pay-ins. With neither said, `pay_in` is null, the card shows "pay-in: not said", the payouts draw on the backing alone and the pay-ins count toward no payout: nothing is spent that its payer did not call a premium. Each payout is `{ block, to, money | amount, counts, why }`, sorted by block; `why` is null, `past_the_pot`, `unseen_together`, `not_confirmed` or `not_holder`. Where the head's receiver is a thing (a vouch to whoever holds a lot, promises.md, Bearer), a payout counts as the claim of the thing's owner where the payout stands, or of the owner of a thing made from the lot that the payout sees (read along the makes, things.md, A make); a payout to anyone else is `not_holder`, shown, and takes nothing from the cap. A fact, never a verdict. **How much is held behind a promise** is no field: the parts are already facts (each held envelope's ownership, a pot's `left`), and FoodOS sums them for the reader.

## Releases and sealed bids shown

`release_shown` of a deal's first promise or a thing returns `{ subject, released, kept_open }` (promises.md, A release). `released` is `[{ block, by, side, to }]`: the counted releases that let go of what is owed to the signer on it, `side` `from` (owed the price) or `to` (owed what moves), null for a thing. `kept_open` is `[{ block, by, side }]`: general releases that list the deal in `state.except`. Only a side of a deal lets go of what is owed to it. Both sorted by block.

`bids_shown` of a deal's first promise (profiles/bids.md) returns `{ quarrel, currency, in, opened, overlap, middle }`. Each side signs one `observe` of kind `bid` with a commitment to a number; both state one `deadline`, named clock `keepers` and `currency`, else no bid counts. `in` and `opened` are the sides whose bid and opening reached the clock by the deadline. `overlap` is null until both are open, then true when the paying side's number is at least the paid side's; `middle` is their mean rounded half up, only on an overlap. With no overlap nothing more is shown. A fact: it moves nothing.

## Views

**`promise_view`** returns the same answer, except that **offered** or **asked** becomes **expired** once the reader's clock passes `expires` and nothing binds, and a refrain that is **accepted** or **underway** becomes **elapsed** once the reader's clock passes its `until`: a fact of its term, with no referee's defeat counted, never **done**, which would say it was kept. What was paid is beside it (`paid`); the condition `settled` reads a refrain elapsed with its price paid in full as settled (promises.md, Conditions). A refrain a stop ended stays **stopped**. The view also gives `condition`, `holds`, `not` or `unread`, for a promise with `terms.condition` (promises.md, The condition fact), and `condition_failed` beside it: the first promise of the deal the condition rests on that ended without it, else null. It gives `missed` too: the dates in force (the first promise's `pay_by`, then each date both sides signed, `moved`) that passed before the price was paid in full, or before the reader's clock while it is unpaid; null with no `pay_by`.

## Circle membership and roles

The fold reads the circle C (its founding block, the founder's first member grant), every member grant with `refs.subject` C, and every grant that answers or updates one of them or C, all at once by the rules in circles.md, with no step reading arrival order or hash. It also reads a member's `close` (profiles/successor.md) when it is in the input; in the fold of a block's causal past, only those in that past. A member grant counts if signed `as` C by someone holding `admit` in the fold of its causal past, and gives only roles whose powers its signer holds there; the founder is a member by the founding block. `may_act_as` reads the `confirms` that give a block signed as C the hands it needs (circles.md, Many hands). A circle whose actor lists no roles is refused (`bad_roles`).

## What each fold returns

Every node returns the same names (`protocol.json` `folds`, `views` give each one's input and output). Ids and hashes are hex; a list of them is sorted as text unless said otherwise. A counted quantity is a number in the line's unit (fold output, not signed content). A field that does not apply is null or an empty list. Beyond that:
- `signed_by_author` is true for a block that is the id's, signed by a key that speaks for it, or a key grant the recovery key signed. With no arrivals it is the pure fold: a block of a rotated key with its rotation in its causal past is false, and, for a recovery rotation, so is every block off its counted line. With arrivals it is `recovery_reach` (identity.md) applied: a block the reach protects is true as well.
- `by`, on a stage `stopped` onward, says which side ended it; null on every other stage.
- `disputed` lists each side's `defeats` of a block of the deal, sorted by its hash then the block it names; it changes no stage. `due` is the deal's `terms.pay_by`; `paid_at`, once the price is paid in full, is the latest date among the counted payments, each dated by the first counting answer of the side paid (or a named confirmer's confirms), else null. A standing promise (`repeat`) is due and paid occurrence by occurrence, so its `due` and `paid_at` are null and `occurrences` reports each instead: `{ occurrence, due, paid, paid_at }` for each occurrence a counted payment names in `state.occurrence`, or an event started (a counted `observe` of the kind the repeat's `after` names, made `for` the promise, by a side or a signer the repeat's `by` names), sorted. `due` is the occurrence's start time, or for an event its observe's `at` plus the repeat's `wait` (its `at` with none), null when unknown; `paid` is what the counted payments naming it put toward the price; `paid_at`, once that covers one occurrence's price, the latest date among those payments. `occurrences` is empty on any other promise. `paid` and `lines` report all that moved and was paid in the deal once an accept binds, so what one side holds of the other's stays visible after a stop or a death.
- `uncovered` is money, `{ minor, currency }`, or null. On a deal stopped onward, breached, or over-allocated and not done, it is what was paid and sent for the deal, less what was paid back, less the price of what moved to the payer, less `terms.forfeit` (promises.md, Paid for, never delivered); never below zero. What moved is priced by a per-unit price (the quantity moved, the whole when it falls in a band), by a line's price (a line that moved whole), or by accepted work at a per-unit price; on a flat price for a whole, by nothing until the whole moves. A refrain moves nothing. It is null when nothing was paid. It is the payer's claim shown as a fact: it changes no stage.
- `price_fixed` is null when the price is a plain number; for a price fixed later (promises.md, Terms) false until it is fixed, true once. While false `total` is null and the price is never paid. A fact: it changes no stage. A price in goods reports `total`, `paid`, `lines` money fields and `uncovered` as an amount `{ value, unit }` where the price is in goods; goods and money are never added or converted.
- `over_allocated` is true on an allocation's deal, and in its entry in `takers`, when the offerer's allocations, taken in causal order with the stopped ones left out, pass what the open offer offered (an offer with `lines` offers each line once, whole: every allocation after the first is over); on the open offer, when any is. A fact: it changes no stage.
- `owed` is null until a hand-over of what is owed counts (promises.md, Owed); then `{ to, from, forked }`: `to` the owner of what moves, `from` `[{ owner, money }]` the owners of the price and their parts (sorted by owner), `forked` the hand-overs that do not count because one key named one `prev` twice. A part of the price handed to the one who owes it counts in `paid`, dated by the accept of the hand-over.
- `received_at` is `[{ message, side, at }]`: each counted `observe` of kind `message` a side wrote `for` the deal, with the time the named clock received it (the promise's `keepers`, and no one else's receipt), `at` null while there is no clock or no receipt. A fact: "knowing" rests on it alone (promises.md, A message with a receipt).
- `moved` is `[{ pay_by, proposal, answer }]`: each new date both sides signed, in the order the answers stand in causal order. The first promise's own `pay_by` stays in `due`; a date only one side signed is not listed (promises.md, Terms).
- `owed_on` is `[{ from, to, money, payment }]`: a counted payment made to an owner who had handed that part on, which the payer had not seen, so the old owner holds that amount for the new one. Sorted by payment, then `to`.
- `referee_ties` lists each bench name tied to a side, `{ referee, side, how }`, `side` `from` or `to`, `how` one of `role` (a role in the side's circle), `member` (a member of it) or `operator` (an autonomous system the side runs: a live operator grant of the side's names it), sorted by referee, side, how; empty until a binding accept exists. It changes no stage.
- `accept` is the binding accept: the promise itself when made to oneself, the first allocation on an open offer (in its own deal), the earliest accept of a live offer on a one-to-one deal. On an open offer `takers[].who` is whom the request speaks for, `quantity` is the sum of the takers' quantities in the offer's unit (exact, no trailing zeros), `total` the price for it, and `lines`, `paid` are empty. `offer` is the offer the binding accept answers; with no accept, the live offer when there is one, else null. `live` lists every live offer, sorted. `forks` are allocations that do not see one before them, or accepts of live offers that do not see each other. In `lines`, `moved` is what counted transfers moved in the line's unit, the exact sum as a number (more than `needed` when more came); `needed` when a whole instance covers the line; null on a line with no quantity once a counted transfer of it exists (One whole). A promise that moves nothing (a job, time, money alone) has no `lines`. `paid` is in the promise's currency: its price's, else the one currency every counted payment covers (more than one: null; money in different currencies is never added); each payment covers what the payee's word says (stage 2). Until a binding accept exists, `quantity`, `total`, `paid` and `lines` are null or empty. A sum of minor units (`total`, `paid`, `terms_total`) is a number within ±(2^53 − 1), else its exact decimal text (`"18014398509480000"`).
- **A pure function reads only what the write check takes.** `terms_total` judges the terms it reads (`price`, `quantity`, `lines`) as an offer's and the quantity chosen as an accept's; `limit_within_parent` judges each fence as a grant's scope (authority.md). What the check would refuse is junk and reads as absent: no total, and not within.
- `circle_state` of a circle not held, or of a block that is not an actor of kind `circle`, is null: no members, and no block speaks `as` it. Within a block whose causal past does not hold the circle, it is null too, and a circle named in such a block's audience means no one.
- `members` maps each id to `{ roles, grant, counted_to }`: `grant` the member grant that put them in, or the circle for the founder (with several current grants, the lowest hash). `removed` maps each id that is out to null; someone who never joined (circles.md, Removed) is in neither. `counted_to` is null (a circle reads none: authority.md, The cut).
- `shares_on` of an instance or a place returns `{ standing, nothing }`. `standing` is `[{ share, owner }]`: the things of kind `share` that name it in `refs.subject`, made by whoever owned it where the share stands (the ownership fold of the share's causal past), each with its own owner now. They stand against every later owner of the thing; selling the thing moves none of them. `nothing` is `[{ share, reason }]` for a share that counts for nothing: `not_a_thing` (its subject is an actor, a transform or any block that is not a thing), `kind` (some thing names its subject in `refs.of`, so it is a kind), `not_owner` (its maker did not own the thing where the share stands). A share names one thing, so a copy, an offspring or a thing made by the same method shows none. Both lists are sorted by share.
- `use_shown` of a place, with an optional named clock, returns `{ clock, users }`. A use is a counted `transform` with no `derives_from`, naming the place in `refs.subject`, with `state.use` text (things.md, Uses of a place). `users` is one entry per user (whom the use speaks for), sorted by id: `acts` (the distinct `state.use` words, sorted), `count` (uses dated), `unreceived` (uses the named clock has not received), `first` and `last` (the earliest and latest date), `seasons` (the number of different calendar years among the dates), `bounds` (the distinct polygons the user's uses carry, sorted by their coordinates as text), `resting` (the latest `state.resting` the user wrote, or null), `overlaps` (the other users whose bounds share area with hers, sorted), `layers` (the distinct `state.layer` words, sorted), `overlap_acts` (`[{ with, act }]`, one per overlap, `act` `same` when the two share an act word and `different` when they do not: shown, never decided) and `leave` (null, or `{ of, since, until }`: the place owner's live `grant` of kind `leave` to this user, things.md; a use by leave is shown "by leave of X since D" and never as a use that stands against X). `clock` is `keepers` when a clock was named, and a use is dated by the time `needed` keepers received it; `author` otherwise, and a use is dated by its author's own `at`. `disputes` is `[{ defeats, by, of, user, standing }]`, one per counted `defeats` of a use record (`of`): `standing` is `earlier_use` when its `derives_from` names an earlier use of the place signed by the disputer, `title` when it names a transfer of the place to the disputer, else `none`. No users means no use in what the reader holds. The overlap is shown to both sides and never decided.
- `not_free_shown` of a block returns `{ shown, nothing }`. `shown` is `[{ block, by, reason, threat, at }]`: the counted `observe`s of kind `not_free` that name the block in `refs.subject` and were written by the block's own author (promises.md, The signer's word), the signer's words as written (`reason`, `threat`, `at` null when absent). `nothing` is `[{ block, why }]` for words by anyone else (`why`: `not_signer`). A stop or an `updates` of one is never read. It changes no stage and no ownership. Both lists are sorted by block.
- `invasion_shown` of a claim of invasion (an `observe` of kind `invasion`; null for any other block) returns `{ commanded, claimant, holder, standing, crossed, evidence, accused, judges, rulings, nothing }` (promises.md, A claim of invasion). `claimant` is whom the claim speaks for; `holder` is who holds the claim now (the claimant, then each hand-over in turn: a transfer naming the claim in `refs.what`, signed by the holder where it stands, accepted by the new holder; two by one holder that do not see each other are a fork and neither moves it). `standing` is `harmed` when the holder is the claimant and the claimant owns the thing crossed where the claim stands (the ownership fold of its causal past), holds a share of it (`shares_on`), or is the person crossed; `holds` when the harmed party handed the claim over; else `none`. `crossed` is `refs.subject` or null; `evidence` the claim's `derives_from`, sorted. `commanded` is `shown` when a counted block among the evidence is signed by one accused and names another accused in `refs.to` (a signed order: evidence, never a condition), else `none`. `accused` is `[{ who, named, accepted, notified }]`, one per accused sorted by id: the judges he named, the judges the claimant side named that he answered `accepted`, and `notified`, the time his own receipt reached a counted `observe` of kind `message` written `for` the claim and naming him in `refs.to` (promises.md, A message with a receipt), else null. `judges` is `[{ judge, named_by }]`: grants of kind `referee` for the claim by the claimant side or an accused, naming a judge who is neither. `rulings` is `[{ block, judge, named_by, word, up_to, evidence }]`: a counted `confirms` or `defeats` of the claim by a named judge, `up_to` only on a `confirms`. `nothing` is `[{ block, reason }]`: `side` (a judge named who is a side, or a ruling by a claimant, an accused or one who speaks for either) or `not_named`. Nothing is decided and no stage changes.
- `where_now` is empty when no place is known, and two or more when moves cross. `ownership` has `owner` null after a transfer to an open receiver; with a `conflict`, `owner` is the owner before the crossing pair. Of transfers that cross, one the person signed in her own name with a trigger under her successor grant in its causal past moves nothing against one her successor made under that grant (profiles/successor.md): her broken promise to her receiver, not a conflict.
- A fold that counts a block for nothing has a code (`protocol.json`, `refusals.codes.fold`). The code explains; it is not part of the answer. `promise_stage` returns the stage alone, `may_act_as` true or false. Two nodes agree when their answers agree.
- `chain(blocks, brought_in)` folds the blocks of **one signing key** (the caller passes only that key's) with `brought_in`, the id of the block that brought the key in (the genesis or a key grant), which its first block names as `prev`. An identity with several keys has one chain per key. Each block takes a place: its `prev`, or for a **first block** the key's root; a first block names `brought_in`, or, at `seq` 1, a block that is none of the key's blocks given, so two first blocks of one key take one place whichever bringer they name (core.md, invariant 3). It reports `equivocations`, the pairs that prove each group of blocks taking one place (a star: each paired with the group's lowest hash, nodes.md); `head`, the hash of the block with the highest `seq` (null when two share it); `gaps`, the missing seq numbers, lowest first, at most `limits.gaps_max` (a block may say `seq` 2^53 - 1), with `ok` false whether or not all are listed (a gap before a `seq` whose blocks are all skips is explained; any other is not); and `broken`, each block whose `prev` does not name the block before it: `brought_in` at `seq` 1 (or a skip), or a block given one `seq` lower (any lower, for a skip); a block whose `prev` is neither is broken. Each is read from the block and its `prev` alone, never from the order given. `length` counts the key's blocks, leaving out the members of every pair; later blocks of the key count (A fork is a burden).

## Invariants

26. A fold reads only whole, well-formed input, and is a function of it: the same blocks give the same answer on every node, whatever order they came in. A block the strict check refuses counts for nothing.
4. A record's binding blocks come from its one orderer and form one chain. Two that do not see each other are a fork, and a fork never frees its signer of what it binds.
5. An instance has at most one owner in any fold.
32. An owner changes only for one of three reasons: the maker's first use, a transfer signed for the previous owner, or the attacker's own signed payment after a ruling. No fold, view, ruling, circle or count moves title another way.
31. A block speaks for one party: its author, the circle it names in `as` if its author may sign for it, or the grantor of the grant it acts under. A fold never counts it for another.
