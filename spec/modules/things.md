# Things, transforms, transfers and truth

What the types hold, in detail. The types and verbs are in core.md.

## Things
- **Kind and instance.** A thing is a kind (eggs, a recipe) or one of a kind (this tray). An instance names its kind with `refs.of`. Offers and asks name kinds; transfers move instances. An instance carries its `lot`, `use_by` (a date) and `pack` (an amount) as data.
- **A kind has no owner.** A recipe or any other kind is a description; its instances (this page, this file) have owners, the idea never. The ownership fold names no owner for a thing that some counted thing names in `refs.of`, and a transfer whose `what` is a kind moves nothing (an idea is not property). The record does not tell a kind from an instance by looking at it, so a thing is owned only once it shows itself as an instance: it names its kind in `refs.of`, or it was made (its `derives_from` names a make), or it names what it is part of or where it is (`refs.subject`, as a share or a use does; `refs.place`). Its own record shows it, so every reader of any past of the record reads the same. A bare thing is a description until then: the fold names no owner, and a transfer of it moves nothing. No field says "kind"; the thing shows what it is by what it names.
- **Marks.** A thing carries exact marks (a lot code, a barcode, a phone number) and the words printed on it as first read (`words`). A recall matches marks exactly. On any block `state.marks` is a list of exactly `{ scheme, value }`, both text (`protocol.json`, `shapes.mark`); anything else is `shape`.
- **Containment.** A thing can be in another (`refs.place`), and what is inside moves with it until taken out. Its `refs.place` is where it was made or first seen; where it is now is a fold (folds.md).
- **Parts.** A transfer may move an amount of an instance (`state.amount`). The part keeps the instance's marks; the rest stays with the sender. A lot split into parts keeps its readings: each part names its parent lot in `derives_from`, and a signed reading on the parent is readable on each part. With no `amount` it moves the whole instance (folds.md, One whole).
- **Shares.** A share is a thing, so it can be held and sold. Whole ownership is not a thing: it is who owns after a transfer (folds.md, Ownership). A share is a `thing` of kind `share` that names the one instance or place it is a part of in `refs.subject`; what it limits is the use of that instance or place, and its own description (`state.use`, an end such as `until`) is read by its schema.
  - **A share the owner made stands against every later owner.** A transfer of the thing moves only what the giver still held (folds.md, Ownership: nobody passes on more than they had), and the owner had already given that share away. The share is its own thing with its own owner, so selling the kitchen does not move it, and the tenant may sell it on. A share counts only if its maker owned the thing where the share stands; one made by someone who never owned it, or after he sold it, counts for nothing (folds.md, `shares_on`).
  - **A share never reaches copies, offspring or methods.** It is a part of one instance or place. It never reaches copies, offspring (a replanted seed), or things made by the same method. A share that names a kind (a thing that others name in `refs.of`), a method, or a person counts for nothing. This keeps a share from becoming an idea held as property by the back door (an idea is not property). A seller may still take a signer's own promise, such as "if you replant, £X goes to me": it binds only the one who signed it.
  - *A covenant.* A "no spraying" limit on a field that an owner in its chain signed as a share stands against later owners, because it is a part the owner kept back. A limit on a kind or a method (a seed-bag licence the farmer never signed) reads as nothing. The guard bans owning an idea, not a covenant between owners.
  - *A lease.* Halima rents bench 3 in a shared kitchen every Tuesday until 30 June. The owner makes a share, `use: "bench 3, Tuesdays"`, `until` 30 June, naming the kitchen in `refs.subject`, and transfers it to Halima. In March he sells the kitchen. She keeps her Tuesdays: he could only sell the kitchen less that share (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19). Its end is in the share's own description, read by its schema; the protocol does not end it.
  - *Shares standing on a thing.* A lender holds a share "claim on this mixer until the debt is settled". A buyer reads the shares that stand on the mixer, and a second pledge is a second share, visible to both.
  - *A share cut by time.* "My cow, Monday to Wednesday": a share whose own description (`use`) says the days. The owner keeps the other days, and the share may be sold on like any other.
  - *Co-owners of a carrier owe each other no freight for using their own part.* Each owns her share of the boat or the lorry; carrying her own goods in her own part is her use, not a hire. Freight is owed only on a part she does not own, by a promise.
  - *Shares standing: one, to Femi, until paid.* The buyer of the mixer reads one live share, Femi's, whose description says "until the debt is settled"; a second pledge shows as a second share.
- **A herd is a counted lot.** A thing may carry `state.count`, a whole number (forty head). At each visit the holder or the vet signs a tally, an `observe` of kind `tally` with `count`, `refs.subject` the herd; the owner countersigns it with a `confirms` of the tally. The tallies are facts on the record. The herd is one thing, and a share or a transfer of it says how many.
- **Nothing reaches a person.** No word makes an actor property. A transfer, a share, a hold or a pawn whose `refs.what` names an actor moves no owner and counts for nothing (folds.md, Ownership). A body was never acquired, so no word hands it on.
- **A separable addition stays its adder's.** When a holder adds a part to a thing whose title later goes back to its true owner, a part that can be taken off is still the adder's, because it is its own thing. What merged, she claims from her seller (Rothbard).
- **Schemas.** A schema is a thing of kind `schema`. A law, a custom or a house rule is one too, so `under` can name it. Anyone may write one; equal schemas are linked with `same_as`. A `refs.under` naming a law counts only for readers who trust its author. A schema states:
  - `target`: the type, or `type.kind`, it is for;
  - `fields`: each field's name with a hint of its value (`text`, `integer`, `money`...); nothing checks the hint, the name declares the field;
  - `roles`: the ref keys it declares beyond the shared ones;
  - `grounds`: the real event without which a block of this kind is false (`event`), which types or roles confirm or defeat it (`confirms`, `defeats`), and who can confirm them (`confirmers`, a list of ids or `circle#role`).
  - `undo`: `never` when the act cannot be taken back, `ok` when it can (authority.md, Acts that can be undone);
  - `labels`: names for its fields and values per language, `{ lang: { name: label } }`.

  **The base schemas** are published in `spec/schemas/` (`schemas.json` is their source, `published.json` the signed things, with stable ids). They describe, as data, what the protocol reads of its own kinds and what the open types carry; the list is the schemas themselves. None is a verdict on anyone (folds.md, Facts, never verdicts). Nothing is refused for lacking one: an open type carries what it likes, and a closed type carries the fields of `protocol.json` (`fields`) and of the schema it names. The publisher is a convention for finding them, not an authority: a base schema's hash tells a reader which fields and roles those bytes declare, and who signed it adds nothing. Whether a schema counts for an act is the grantor's own word, for a base schema as for any other (authority.md, Acts that can be undone).

## Transforms and keeps
- **A make** is a `transform` that names its inputs in `refs.derives_from`, each with its share if it matters (`state.shares`: `[{ from, share }]`). Its outputs are things that name it in `derives_from`. A transform consumes its inputs; with no output, it ends them (eaten, thrown out). **A recipe is cited, never consumed.** A recipe, or any kind named in `derives_from`, needs no one's answer; only a share or promise someone signed binds anyone. The `teach` schema's learner names the recipe without the teacher's leave. An idea is not property, because it is not scarce.
- **A keep** is a `transform` that names the thing in `refs.subject` and consumes nothing. Anyone holding a thing can write a keep about it. A thing's state is the fold of what is written about it.
- **Produce of a thing.** The owner of a means (a hen, a cow, a tree, a hive) is the first owner of what it produces (eggs, calves, fruit, honey): an output of a keep belongs first to the owner of the thing kept, where the keep stands (folds.md, Ownership). A share or lease of the means does not reach its produce unless it says so (Shares). A signed term may say otherwise, and the worker's wage is a promise of the owner.
- Inputs held by others count once each holder's block `answers` the transform.
- First possession (wild food, a catch) is a transform with no inputs; its author is the first owner in the record. The record is not title (folds.md, Ownership).
- **First use of a place, or of a use over a place** (a water draw, a fishing stretch, a smell or spray over a field) is written the same way: a `transform` with no inputs, the place a thing named in `refs.subject`. The dated record is evidence a referee reads, never title (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 10; *Man, Economy, and State*, pp. 173-174: easements are homesteaded by first use). A use first made over an unowned place stands against whoever later takes the place; a thing someone made had no earlier users. See Uses of a place below.
- **Work** done is the worker's own block, signed by them (`as` the business if for one): a `transform` if a thing changed, otherwise an `observe` of kind `work`. It stands without anyone else's signature. The other side's `confirms` adds weight; its `defeats` opens a dispute.

## Uses of a place
- **A use is an act.** A use is a counted `transform` that makes nothing from anything (no `derives_from`), names the place in `refs.subject`, and says in `state.use` what was done: cleared, planted, harvested, drew water, fenced, built, kept. A block that only claims or grants is not a use, and a view shows it as none. The kinds of use are the acts a schema lists, never a list in the protocol (Rothbard, "Justice and Property Rights", p. 112).
- **A bound may be a walk of named marks.** Most smallholders have no coordinates. A place may carry `state.walk` beside or instead of `state.bound`: an ordered list of marks, each `{ kind, words, photo }` (a tree, a stream, a wall; the photo a hash). It is data for the use's own readers, like the rest of a bound.
- **Its bound is data.** `state.bound` is a polygon, GeoJSON-shaped: `{ type: "Polygon", coordinates: [ring] }`, one closed ring of at least four and at most `limits.bound_points` (256) `[lon, lat]` positions, each a decimal string of at most nine places. It is checked for shape only. Two bounds **overlap** when they share area, not only a border. The fold shows the overlap to both sides and never decides it. A bound may name more than space (hours, seasons, up and down) in the use's own description; the protocol reads only the polygon.
- **A use names its act and, if it likes, its layer.** `state.layer` (the roof deck, above the ovens, summer cropping, winter grazing) is data. Two uses over one bound that name different acts are not rival claims: a rooftop bed over a bakery, summer cropping and winter grazing. The overlap fact lists both uses and says "same act" or "different act", deciding nothing. A water draw is a rate: the schema `place_use` carries `rate` (an amount per time) beside the optional bound, and a part of a rate may be sold as a share.
- **Standing on a dispute of first use.** A counted `defeats` of a use record is shown with its `standing`: it names an earlier use of the same place by its own author (`earlier_use`), names a title it holds (a transfer of the place to its author: `title`), or `none`. A land-grabber's empty `defeats` no longer looks as loud as a neighbour's real one. The fold shows it and decides nothing.
- **Its date comes from a named clock.** With a clock (blocks.md, A named clock) a use is dated by the time `needed` keepers received it, and a use they have not received is not dated. With none, a use is dated by its author's own `at`, which only that author says. One receipt rule, written once.
- **Bounded in space by the use, never lost by time.** Non-use is not abandonment (Rothbard, ch. 10; Kinsella). A field at rest stays its user's until her own signed letting-go or hand-over. A use may carry `state.resting`, a time: "resting, until then", the user's own word, shown and never read as ending anything. **Fallow is a use.** A use that returns each season (herders who graze every dry season and leave each wet one) is a use that leaving does not end; a fence put over it is a taking.
- **Use by leave never ripens.** Property moves only by its owner's signed word, so a use over an owned thing gives no right unless the owner hands one on. A use by leave is the owner's `grant` of kind `leave` (`refs.to` the user, `refs.subject` the place, optional `scope.until`). The view shows it as "by leave of X since D" (`leave`, folds.md), never as a use that stands against X. When the owner stops the grant, the user has nothing. If a tolerated use ripened into a right, every well owner would stop lending. A first-use record therefore says whether the place was unowned or used by someone's leave. A use over an unowned place stands against whoever later takes it.
- **An easement is a share.** It begins only over a place nobody had yet used, and it moves by signed transfer like any share (Shares, with its guard).
- *A water draw since 2014.* Aisha draws water from the spring each dry season. She writes a `transform` per season, `use: "drew water"`, the spring in `refs.subject`, a `bound` round her draw. A keeper's receipt dates each. The view shows, for her: acts, first and last, three seasons, her bound. When Bela's bound overlaps hers, each is shown the other's name. A referee reads this beside any dispute; it is not title.
- Facts are only among the blocks a reader holds. "No use shown" means none in what this reader holds, never "empty land".

## Transfers
- A transfer names what changed: `refs.to_owner`, `refs.to_holder`, `refs.to_place` (any subset), and `refs.what`. Selling moves the owner; lending a specific thing moves the holder; moving stock moves the place. Lending anything interchangeable (money, flour, eggs) moves the owner, and the lender holds a promise for the same kind back. **"Same kind" is the lender's own word:** a loan moves the owner when its promise's `refs.what` is a kind (the lender asked for the same kind back), and the holder when it names this instance. The promise's own noun decides; the protocol keeps no list of interchangeable goods. Storing moves only the holder (Rothbard, *The Mystery of Banking*, p. 87).
- **Open receivers.** A transfer to someone unnamed leaves `to_owner` out and says how many received (`state.count`).
- **Letting go with no receiver.** An owner's signed hand-over to an open receiver ends her title, and her signature alone does it: the fold reads no accept for an open receiver ("free to anyone before 7, up to 20" is the same shape). Whoever next takes the thing by first use, a `transform` with no inputs that names it in `refs.subject` and has seen the let-go, is its first owner in the record, as with wild food (Rothbard; Kinsella: abandonment ends title). Two who take it without seeing each other prove a lie: the fold names no owner and lists both. A use of the thing before any let-go takes nothing. **Whoever lets go may name a clock** for those who take it: `state.keepers` (`{ names, needed }`, blocks.md, A named clock) on the hand-over, never on a transfer to a named receiver. Then the first taker that clock received is the first owner, and two it received at one moment are a conflict as above. With no clock named, or none received, the takers are read in causal order and a conflict is shown, as before: first-use evidence decides before a judge both sides chose. Example: Ada leaves a crate of windfall apples at the gate, `keepers` her neighbour Bo, `needed` 1. Cy and Di each take them and send Bo a receipt request; Bo's receipt of Cy's use is dated first, so Cy is the first owner and Di took nothing she can sell.
- **Money** moves by transfer: `refs.to_owner`, `refs.for`, `state.money`, and `state.marks` (the payment rail's reference, profiles/payment-proof.md). A refund is the same, the other way. A payment needs no `refs.what` (core.md, section 7). A payment `for` several promises says how much goes to each in `state.split`, a non-empty `[{ for, line, money }]`: each `for` one of the payment's `refs.for`, `money` money in its currency, `line` (when given) a line id, the parts together no more than the payment (`shape`); with no `split` it is refused (`required`), and a fold that meets one counts it toward none. A payment toward a standing promise names the occurrence it pays in `state.occurrence` (its start time, or the hash of the block that started it; a payment only, else `shape`), so each occurrence is seen paid or owed on its own (promises.md, Terms; folds.md, `occurrences`). The side paid says what it covers: its accepted answer may carry `state.covers` (money: the price in full though less came, or the price's currency though another came; never less than the money that moved, which counts in full whatever it says); a promise reads that word, never a rate (promises.md, Settling). A transfer naming both a thing and money is a **sale**: it moves ownership once the receiver accepts, and the money counts toward the promise it is `for`. A failed attempt (a declined card) is an `observe`, never a transfer.
- **A transfer may carry a condition.** `state.condition` is any condition promises.md reads, and the transfer counts once it holds, with no further act of the giver. The receiver's accept, if she is named, comes as for any transfer. Title moves when the condition holds: every future transfer is conditional (Kinsella; Evers). The ownership fold reads it, and a condition that reads the clock makes ownership a view (folds.md, Ownership). A later transfer by the same owner passes only what he still held, so a conditioned transfer signed first comes first once it holds, and a stranger who bought in between has what was left. A conditioned transfer of title on `settled` of the sale would wait for the sale, which waits for the title: condition it on `paid` of the price.
  - **Letting go.** The receiver of a conditioned transfer may let it go by her own counted `stopped` or `declined` answer, signed before the condition held (read on what she saw, at her own date). It binds only her. A penalty owed to her is let go by her own release, whole or in part (promises.md, A release): one form for every letting go, with no discount, waiver or forgiveness type.
  - *Title until paid.* Femi sells a café a £1,200 mixer, paid over three months. He hands over the holding now (a transfer `to_holder`). The ownership is a second transfer `to_owner` the café with `condition: { paid: <the sale> }`. Until the last payment the owner is Femi, and after it the café.
  - *The caterer's bond.* Joe puts £200 in an envelope with Aunt Gita. He signs three transfers of it: holding to Gita now; ownership to Amara `without` a signal `arrived` by Joe, by 9am; ownership back to Joe `within` the same signal, deadline and keepers (Gita). Both are read on one clock, so a late arrival cannot win by signing order. Joe cannot backdate: his `arrived` counts only if Gita received it by 9am. (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19: a penalty both sides agreed in advance, paid from property set aside.)
  - *The pawn* (Szabo). Wanjiru hands a gold bracelet to a lender. Holding moves now. Ownership moves to the lender on `{ without: { paid: <the loan> }, deadline: T, keepers }`: not repaid by T.
  - *Who bears the loss on the road* (the most repeated quarrel in the Geniza letters). Two honest forms; the sides choose. (a) Ownership moves when the goods are handed to the carrier (`to_owner` the buyer, `to_holder` the carrier): a loss on the road is the buyer's. (b) Ownership moves on arrival, a transfer conditioned on the buyer's own signal that it received them: a loss on the road is the seller's, and money paid for goods that never arrive is owed back (promises.md, Paid for, never delivered). Nothing is filled in: a deal whose transfers and terms name neither shows "risk on the road: not said". This is a reader's display; the protocol has no field for it.
- **A transfer to a named receiver is read like an offer.** The receiver owes nothing until their own accept. Until then the giver may end it (authority.md, Ending), and an accept that has the end in its causal past counts for nothing. `state.expires` is the window of the giver's decline (Ending).

## Truth

- An `observe` with no `derives_from` is direct sensing; with it, an interpretation.
- An `observe` says what is so in `state.text`, or in fields its schema declares. A reading carries its `amount`, and may add its `accuracy` and `coverage`, and names the sensor it came from, a thing, in `refs.from`. It counts as grounds (a schema's `grounds`, a condition) only when its signer speaks for the sensor (authority.md, Who a block speaks for); a reading from any other signer is not grounds, and a reader may still weigh it as evidence.
- **An assertion may name its grounding.** An `observe` that names in `derives_from` what it rests on says how she knows: experience (a photo or a reading), a calculation, or another's word (a vouch). It is a worked example, not a field: nothing is required, and the protocol reads no grounding.
- **A model's reading is not a person's observation.** An `observe` of kind `reading` is signed by the autonomous system's own key, `derives_from` the raw evidence, and states the `method`. Sharing it claims nothing. It becomes the person's claim only when she puts that fact to someone else in her own step: a block she signs, naming someone other than herself in `refs.to` or `refs.to_owner`, with the reading in `derives_from`. Her private `confirms` is not enough.
- **An import** (a record brought back from a platform) is such a reading with `method: import`, `derives_from` the export file's hash, naming its source in `state.text`. Platforms do not sign.
- A physical act carries its grounds, linked by hash (readings, a camera frame, a door sensor's observe), so a stranger can weigh a machine's claim. A sensor gives evidence; it never settles a promise alone (promises.md, Settling).
- A reading says whether it is a count or an estimate by `coverage` (how much was seen) and `accuracy`, so a stock report can say which part was counted.
- What counts as evidence for a kind of block is its schema's grounds. There is no global score.
