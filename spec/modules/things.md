# Things, transforms, transfers and truth

What the types hold, in detail. The types and verbs are in core.md.

## Things
- **Kind and instance.** A thing is a kind (eggs, a recipe) or one of a kind (this tray). An instance names its kind with `refs.of`. Offers and asks name kinds; transfers move instances. An instance carries its `use_by` (a date) and its `pack` (an amount: its measure, folds.md, What is left); a lot code is a mark.
- **A kind is a kind by its own block.** A recipe or any other kind is a description; its instances (this page, this file) have owners, the idea never. A thing is an instance when it names its kind (`refs.of`), what it is part of or where it is (`refs.subject`, `refs.place`), or a make it came from (`refs.derives_from`). Otherwise it is a kind: it has no owner, and a draw on it moves nothing (an idea is not property, because it is not scarce). No other block changes which: a stranger's thing that names an instance in `refs.of` says nothing about that instance, so no stranger unmakes Harry's sack by calling it a kind. No field says "kind"; the thing shows what it is by what it names, and every reader of any past of it reads the same.
- **Marks.** A thing carries exact marks (a lot code, a barcode, a phone number) and the words printed on it as first read (`words`). A mark is how a reader finds an instance. A recall names instances, and reaches what was made from them (folds.md, `made_from`). On any block `state.marks` is a list of exactly `{ scheme, value }`, both text (`protocol.json`, `shapes.mark`); anything else is `shape`.
- **The same thing.** Any block may say two statements are one thing (`refs.same_as`). It is its signer's word and no one else's. It moves nothing on either, merges no measure however many sign it, and says nothing of amount. No reading of one is a reading of the other.
- **Containment.** A thing can be in another (`refs.place`), and what is inside moves with it until taken out. Its `refs.place` is where it was made or first seen; where it is now is a fold (folds.md).
- **Parts.** A part of an instance is an amount of it that someone owns. It is the same instance: the same id, marks and readings. A transfer with `state.amount` hands on that amount and the rest stays where it was (folds.md, What is left); with none it hands on all its signer owns of it. A part becomes a thing of its own only by a make (a repack). **A word about an instance by one who handed on part of it is about the amount his own draws handed on, and no more:** a reader who holds 500 kg of lot L7 from Harry and 1 kg from another holds two parts of one instance, and the other's recall, warning or reading reaches the 1 kg that came through him. Only the author of the make or first use an instance came from speaks of it whole. Ground: what he may speak for follows the taking, since only what went through his hands was ever his to speak for (Rothbard: no one owns a reputation).
- **Shares.** A share is a thing, so it can be held and sold. Whole ownership is not a thing: it is who owns after a transfer (folds.md, Ownership). A share is a `thing` of kind `share` that names the one instance or place it is a part of in `refs.subject`; what it limits is the use of that instance or place, and its own description (`state.use`, an end such as `until`) is read by its schema.
  - **A share the owner made stands against every later owner.** A transfer of the thing moves only what the giver still held (folds.md, What is left: nobody hands on more than they had), and the owner had already given that share away. The share is its own thing with its own owner, so selling the kitchen does not move it, and the tenant may sell it on. A share counts only if its maker owned the thing where the share stands; one made by someone who never owned it, or after he sold it, counts for nothing (folds.md, `shares_on`).
  - **A share never reaches copies, offspring or methods.** It is a part of one instance or place. It never reaches copies, offspring (a replanted seed), or things made by the same method. A share that names a kind (a thing that is a kind by its own block), a method, or a person counts for nothing. This keeps a share from becoming an idea held as property by the back door (an idea is not property). A seller may still take a signer's own promise, such as "if you replant, £X goes to me": it binds only the one who signed it.
  - *A covenant.* A "no spraying" limit on a field that an owner in its chain signed as a share stands against later owners, because it is a part the owner kept back. A limit on a kind or a method (a seed-bag licence the farmer never signed) reads as nothing. The guard bans owning an idea, not a covenant between owners.
  - *A lease.* Halima rents bench 3 in a shared kitchen every Tuesday until 30 June. The owner makes a share, `use: "bench 3, Tuesdays"`, `until` 30 June, naming the kitchen in `refs.subject`, and transfers it to Halima. In March he sells the kitchen. She keeps her Tuesdays: he could only sell the kitchen less that share (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19). Its end is in the share's own description, read by its schema; the protocol does not end it.
  - *Shares standing on a thing.* A lender holds a share "claim on this mixer until the debt is settled". A buyer reads the shares that stand on the mixer, and a second pledge is a second share, visible to both.
  - *A share cut by time.* "My cow, Monday to Wednesday": a share whose own description (`use`) says the days. The owner keeps the other days, and the share may be sold on like any other.
  - *Co-owners of a carrier pay each other no freight for using their own part.* Each owns her share of the boat or the lorry; carrying her own goods in her own part is her use, not a hire. Freight is due only on a part she does not own, by a promise.
  - *Shares standing: one, to Femi, until paid.* The buyer of the mixer reads one live share, Femi's, whose description says "until the debt is settled"; a second pledge shows as a second share.
- **A herd is an instance measured in head** (`pack: { value: "40", unit: "head" }`); a sale of five is a draw of `{ "5", head }`. At each visit the holder or the vet signs a tally, an `observe` of kind `tally` with `count`, `refs.subject` the herd; the owner countersigns it with a `confirms` of the tally. A tally is a reading: it moves no amount, and the herd's `pack` changes only by its owner's own make (a birth written as an output, a death as a make with no output).
- **Nothing reaches a person.** No word makes an actor property. A transfer, a share, a hold or a pawn whose `refs.what` names an actor moves no owner and counts for nothing (folds.md, Ownership). A body was never acquired, so no word hands it on.
- **A separable addition stays its adder's.** When a holder adds a part to a thing whose title later goes back to its true owner, a part that can be taken off is still the adder's, because it is its own thing. What merged, she claims from her seller (Rothbard).
- **Schemas.** A schema is a thing of kind `schema`. A law, a custom or a house rule is one too, so `under` can name it. Anyone may write one; equal schemas are linked with `same_as`. A `refs.under` naming a law counts only for readers who trust its author. A schema states:
  - `target`: the type, or `type.kind`, it is for;
  - `fields`: each field's name with its shape, written as `kind_fields` writes one (`protocol.json`): `field:shape`, the shape one of the words `protocol.json` names in `field_shape` and `grammar` (`text`, `hash`, `id`, `time`, `date`, `duration`, `decimal`, `amount`, `count`) or `in:` and a list; a trailing `!` makes the field required. A block of any type that names the schema and carries such a field in another shape, or lacks one marked `!`, is not read (`shape`, `required`: `read`'s codes; core.md, section 8). Any other value in `fields` is a hint for people and checks nothing: a field with no shape is pencil, and no plain code matches on it.
  - `roles`: the ref keys it declares beyond the shared ones;
  - `grounds`: the real event without which a block of this kind is false (`event`), which types confirm or defeat it (`confirms`, `defeats`), and who can confirm them (`confirmers`, a list of ids).
  - `labels`: names for its fields and values per language, `{ lang: { name: label } }`.

  **A schema adds and never takes away.** It may declare fields, terms and roles a closed block may then carry. It cannot forbid a field or term the protocol reads, make a side be named, or set a smallest size. Its words are pencil. A shape it gives is checked by `read` and never by `valid`: a block that fails it is held, not read, never turned away. A reader that turns away a sound block because it does not match a schema's words is using its own rule of admission (consent.md), never the protocol's.

  **The base schemas** are published in `spec/schemas/` (`schemas.json` is their source, `published.json` the signed things, with stable ids). They describe, as data, what the protocol reads of its own kinds and what the open types carry; the list is the schemas themselves. None is a verdict on anyone (folds.md, Facts, never verdicts). Nothing is refused for lacking one: an open type carries what it likes, and a closed type carries the fields of `protocol.json` (`fields`) and of the schema it names. The publisher is a convention for finding them, not an authority: a base schema's hash tells a reader which fields and roles those bytes declare, and who signed it adds nothing. Whether a schema counts for a block is the grantor's own word, for a base schema as for any other.

  So a community types its own fields in its own schema, named by hash, and asks no one. Its readers name the schema's hash; a look-alike with the same kind word and another hash is another schema.

## Transforms and keeps
- **A make** is a `transform` that names its inputs in `refs.derives_from`. It speaks for one owner and draws only on what she owns: all of each input, or the amount `state.inputs` gives (`[{ from, amount }]`, `from` one of its `derives_from`, `amount` in the input's own kind). An output is a thing that names one or more makes in `derives_from`, written by their author. **The output of a make belongs to the owner of what was taken:** where the makes took from one owner, she owns it all; from several, each owns in it the amount taken from her when its `pack` is the sum of what was taken, in one kind (a mix: three farmers' wheat in one silo); otherwise each is named with no amount and hands on only all she owns of it, and one left alone owns the whole. A make with no output ends what it took: eaten, spoiled, lost, shrunk. **Only an owner ends her own stuff:** a make by anyone else takes nothing (`not_owner`), and its output has no owner; a holder's `loss` or a stranger's word is evidence. Another's goods enter a make by her transfer first, or by a make signed under her grant that names them (authority.md, Toll work); when the record shows its output as hers is her start (folds.md, What is left). Working on a thing gives no title: the worker owns his body, not a piece of the bread, and his fee is a promise (Hoppe, Kinsella: title comes from first use of the unowned or an owner's hand-over; if work made title, an attack would). **A recipe is cited, never consumed.** A recipe, or any kind named in `derives_from`, needs no one's answer; only a share or promise someone signed binds anyone. The `teach` schema's learner names the recipe without the teacher's leave. An idea is not property, because it is not scarce.
- **A keep** is a `transform` that names the thing in `refs.subject` and consumes nothing. Anyone holding a thing can write a keep about it. A thing's state is the fold of what is written about it.
- **Produce of a thing.** The owner of a means (a hen, a cow, a tree, a hive) is the first owner of what it produces (eggs, calves, fruit, honey): an output of a keep belongs first to the owner of the thing kept, where the keep stands (folds.md, Ownership); where several own the thing kept, each is named with no amount (A make). A share or lease of the means does not reach its produce unless it says so (Shares). A signed term may say otherwise, and the worker's wage is a promise of the owner.
- First possession (wild food, a catch) is a transform with no inputs, a reading (core.md, Types); its author is the first owner in the record. The record is not title (folds.md, Ownership).
- **First use of a place, or of a use over a place** (a water draw, a fishing stretch, a smell or spray over a field) is written the same way: a `transform` with no inputs, the place a thing named in `refs.subject`. The dated record is evidence a referee reads, never title (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 10; *Man, Economy, and State*, pp. 173-174: easements are homesteaded by first use). A use first made over an unowned place stands against whoever later takes the place; a thing someone made had no earlier users. See Uses of a place below.
- **Work** done is the worker's own block, signed by them: a `transform` if a thing changed, otherwise an `observe` of kind `work`. Work done for another is the worker's own word, signed in her own name, `for` the deal that pays it, and never `as` the one who pays: she is the other side. What she does for the owners she does by their row or under their grant, and what she makes from their goods is theirs (A make). The job states who counts the hours; where it names no one, the yes of the side the work goes to counts. It stands without anyone else's signature. The other side's `confirms` adds weight; its `defeats` opens a dispute.

## Uses of a place
- **A use is a deed.** A use is a counted `transform` that makes nothing from anything (no `derives_from`), names the place in `refs.subject`, and says in `state.use` what was done: cleared, planted, harvested, drew water, fenced, built, kept. A block that only claims or grants is not a use, and a view shows it as none. The kinds of use are the ones a schema lists, never a list in the protocol (Rothbard, "Justice and Property Rights", p. 112).
- **A bound may be a walk of named marks.** Most smallholders have no coordinates. A place may carry `state.walk` beside or instead of `state.bound`: an ordered list of marks, each `{ kind, words, photo }` (a tree, a stream, a wall; the photo a hash). It is data for the use's own readers, like the rest of a bound.
- **Its bound is data.** `state.bound` is a polygon, GeoJSON-shaped: `{ type: "Polygon", coordinates: [ring] }`, one closed ring of at least four and at most `limits.bound_points` (256) `[lon, lat]` positions, each a decimal string of at most nine places. It is checked for shape only. Two bounds **overlap** when they share area, not only a border. The fold shows the overlap to both sides and never decides it. A bound may name more than space (hours, seasons, up and down) in the use's own description; the protocol reads only the polygon.
- **A use names what was done and, if it likes, its layer.** `state.layer` (the roof deck, above the ovens, summer cropping, winter grazing) is data. Two uses over one bound that name different uses are not rival claims: a rooftop bed over a bakery, summer cropping and winter grazing. The overlap fact lists both uses and says "same use" or "different use", deciding nothing. A water draw is a rate: the schema `place_use` carries `rate` (an amount per time) beside the optional bound, and a part of a rate may be sold as a share.
- **Standing on a dispute of first use.** A counted `defeats` of a use record is shown with its `standing`: it names an earlier use of the same place by its own author (`earlier_use`), names a title it holds (a transfer of the place to its author: `title`), or `none`. A land-grabber's empty `defeats` no longer looks as loud as a neighbour's real one. The fold shows it and decides nothing.
- **Its date comes from a named clock.** With a clock (blocks.md, A named clock) a use is dated by the time `needed` clocks received it, and a use they have not received is not dated. With none, a use is dated by its author's own `at`, which only that author says. One receipt rule, written once.
- **Bounded in space by the use, never lost by time.** Non-use is not abandonment (Rothbard, ch. 10; Kinsella). A field at rest stays its user's until her own signed letting-go or hand-over. A use may carry `state.resting`, a time: "resting, until then", the user's own word, shown and never read as ending anything. **Fallow is a use.** A use that returns each season (herders who graze every dry season and leave each wet one) is a use that leaving does not end; a fence put over it is a taking.
- **Use by leave never ripens.** Property moves only by its owner's signed word, so a use over an owned thing gives no right unless the owner hands one on. A use by leave is the owner's `grant` of kind `leave` (`refs.to` the user, `refs.subject` the place, optional `scope.until`). The view shows it as "by leave of X since D" (`leave`, folds.md), never as a use that stands against X. When the owner stops the grant, the user has nothing. If a tolerated use ripened into a right, every well owner would stop lending. A first-use record therefore says whether the place was unowned or used by someone's leave. A use over an unowned place stands against whoever later takes it.
- **An easement is a share.** It begins only over a place nobody had yet used, and it moves by signed transfer like any share (Shares, with its guard).
- *A water draw since 2014.* Aisha draws water from the spring each dry season. She writes a `transform` per season, `use: "drew water"`, the spring in `refs.subject`, a `bound` round her draw. A clock's receipt dates each. The view shows, for her: uses, first and last, three seasons, her bound. When Bela's bound overlaps hers, each is shown the other's name. A referee reads this beside any dispute; it is not title.
- Facts are only among the blocks a reader holds. "No use shown" means none in what this reader holds, never "empty land".

## Transfers
- A transfer is a promise whose condition is already met (core.md, the one shape): its signer's property becomes the receiver's. It names what changed: `refs.to_owner`, `refs.to_holder`, `refs.to_place` (any subset), and `refs.what`. Selling moves the owner; lending a specific thing moves the holder; moving stock moves the place. Lending anything interchangeable (money, flour, eggs) moves the owner, and the lender holds a promise for the same kind back. **"Same kind" is the lender's own word:** a loan moves the owner when its promise's `refs.what` is a kind (the lender asked for the same kind back), and the holder when it names this instance. The promise's own noun decides; the protocol keeps no list of interchangeable goods. Storing moves only the holder (Rothbard, *The Mystery of Banking*, p. 87). A deposit is not a loan: stored is `to_holder`, and the goods stay the depositor's; lent is `to_owner` plus the borrower's promise of the same kind back (Rothbard, *The Mystery of Banking*, p. 87; Huerta de Soto, *Money, Bank Credit, and Economic Cycles*, ch. 1).
- **Open receivers.** A transfer to someone unnamed leaves `to_owner` out and says how many received (`state.count`).
- **Letting go with no receiver.** An owner's signed hand-over to an open receiver ends her title, and her signature alone does it: the fold reads no accept for an open receiver ("free to anyone before 7, up to 20" is the same shape). Whoever next takes the thing by first use, a `transform` with no inputs that names it in `refs.subject` and has seen the let-go, is its first owner in the record, as with wild food (Rothbard; Kinsella: abandonment ends title). Two who take it without seeing each other prove a lie: the fold names no owner and lists both. A use of the thing before any let-go takes nothing. **Whoever lets go may name a clock** for those who take it: `state.clocks` (`{ names, needed }`, blocks.md, A named clock) on the hand-over, never on a transfer to a named receiver. Then the first taker that clock received is the first owner, and two it received at one moment are a conflict as above. With no clock named, or none received, the takers are read in causal order and a conflict is shown, as before: first-use evidence decides before a judge both sides chose. Example: Ada leaves a crate of windfall apples at the gate, `clocks` her neighbour Bo, `needed` 1. Cy and Di each take them and send Bo a receipt request; Bo's receipt of Cy's use is dated first, so Cy is the first owner and Di took nothing she can sell.
- **A transfer names an instance, or names none.** One that names an instance in `refs.what` moves a thing the record holds: it is a draw on it (folds.md, What is left), and `state.amount` says how much. One that names none moves its `state.amount` of a good the record does not hold (coins, a balance on a rail, grain from an unweighed heap): it moves nothing on the record, gives no one a title the record shows, and counts toward its line only on the receiver's accept, or the signal of a signer her account word names (promises.md, Where to pay me); a receiver with nothing to answer says what came by her own `received` word (folds.md, What is left, The other side). `state.marks` may carry a rail's reference (profiles/payment-proof.md). The rule is the same for every good, money or maize; a refund is the same, the other way. A failed attempt (a declined card) is an `observe`, never a transfer.
- **Handed over or held; no third kind.** `to_owner`: it is the receiver's, and anything to come back is only her own promise. `to_holder`: it is still the giver's, and the holder's accept is her word that she holds that thing of the giver's, apart from her own. What is kept apart is a thing (a marked tin, an envelope, a lot), never an amount: an amount names no thing that could be held, so "held 50" would count the holder's promise as the giver's money (`shape`, core.md, section 7). A part is held or placed once a make has made it a thing.
- **Paying several deals at once.** A payment `for` several promises says how much goes to each in `state.split`, a non-empty `[{ for, line, amount, occurrence }]`: each `for` one of the payment's `refs.for`, `amount` of the payment's kind, `line` (when given) a line id, the parts together no more than the payment (`shape`); with no `split` it is refused (`required`), and a fold that meets one counts it toward none. A payment toward a standing promise names the occurrence it pays in `state.occurrence` (its start time, or the hash of the block that started it; a payment only, else `shape`), so each occurrence is seen paid or to come on its own (promises.md, Terms; folds.md, `occurrences`). The side paid says what the payment paid for: its accepted answer may carry `state.covers`, an amount (promises.md, Settling).
- **A transfer may carry a condition.** `state.condition` is any condition promises.md reads, and it lands where its condition has held, read in the causal past of the block that relies on it (promises.md, Conditions). The receiver's accept, if she is named, comes as for any transfer. Every future transfer is conditional (Kinsella; Evers). A conditioned draw holds its amount from where it is signed (folds.md, What is left), so a later draw of the same owner sees it held, and nobody buys what was already spoken for; what it moved, once landed and standing, stays moved. A conditioned transfer of title on `settled` of the sale would wait for the sale, which waits for the title: condition it on `paid` of the price.
  - **Letting go.** The receiver of a conditioned transfer may let it go by her own counted `stopped` answer or her decline (an `observe` that `answers` it and says `declined`; one of the transfer's own type, written before the three moods, reads the same), signed before the condition held (read on what she saw, at her own date). It binds only her. A penalty to come to her is let go by her own release, whole or in part (promises.md, A release): one form for every letting go, with no discount, waiver or forgiveness type.
  - *Title until paid.* Femi sells a café a £1,200 mixer, paid over three months. He hands over the holding now (a transfer `to_holder`). The ownership is a second transfer `to_owner` the café with `condition: { paid: <the sale> }`. Until the last payment the owner is Femi, and after it the café.
  - *The caterer's bond.* Joe puts £200 in an envelope with Aunt Gita. He signs three transfers of it: holding to Gita now; ownership to Amara `without` a signal `arrived` by Joe, by 9am; ownership back to Joe `within` the same signal, deadline and clocks (Gita). Both are read on one clock, so a late arrival cannot win by signing order. Joe cannot backdate: his `arrived` counts only if Gita received it by 9am. (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19: a penalty both sides agreed in advance, paid from property set aside.)
  - *The pawn* (Szabo). Wanjiru hands a gold bracelet to a lender. Holding moves now. Ownership moves to the lender on `{ without: { paid: <the loan> }, deadline: T, clocks }`: not repaid by T.
  - *Whose loss it is on the road* (the most repeated quarrel in the Geniza letters). Two honest forms; the sides choose. (a) Ownership moves when the goods are handed to the carrier (`to_owner` the buyer, `to_holder` the carrier): a loss on the road is the buyer's. (b) Ownership moves on arrival, a transfer conditioned on the buyer's own signal that it received them: a loss on the road is the seller's, and money paid for goods that never arrive is to come back (promises.md, Paid for, never delivered). Nothing is filled in: a deal whose transfers and terms name neither shows "risk on the road: not said". This is a reader's display; the protocol has no field for it.
- **A spot exchange.** The buyer signs first: an order naming the instance and its price. The seller's transfer `for` it, `to_owner` her, is his yes and his delivery in one block; it lands where it is signed and the deal is done there where his `covers` (`required`) states the whole price. A missing `covers` is never read as credit, and the buyer's silence never turns his receipt into anything to come. On credit it is the same two blocks with `covers` `0`, and the price is to come.
- **A transfer to a named receiver is read like an offer.** Nothing binds the receiver until their own accept. Until then the giver may end it (authority.md, Ending), and an accept that has the end in its causal past counts for nothing. `state.expires` is an end of it (blocks.md, The cut). Her yes lands it; her no, or an end she has seen, frees it (folds.md, What is left).

## Truth

- An `observe` with no `derives_from` is direct sensing; with it, an interpretation.
- An `observe` says what is so in `state.text`, or in fields its schema declares. A reading carries its `amount`, and may add its `accuracy` and `coverage`, and names the sensor it came from, a thing, in `refs.from`. It counts as grounds (a schema's `grounds`, a condition) only when its signer speaks for the sensor (authority.md, Who a block speaks for); a reading from any other signer is not grounds, and a reader may still weigh it as evidence.
- **An assertion may name its grounding.** An `observe` that names in `derives_from` what it rests on says how she knows: experience (a photo or a reading), a calculation, or another's word (a vouch). It is a worked example, not a field: nothing is required, and the protocol reads no grounding.
- **A model's reading is not a person's observation.** An `observe` of kind `reading` is signed by the autonomous system's own key, `derives_from` the raw evidence, and states the `method`. Sharing it claims nothing. It becomes the person's claim only when she puts that fact to someone else in her own step: a block she signs, naming someone other than herself in `refs.to` or `refs.to_owner`, with the reading in `derives_from`. Her private `confirms` is not enough.
- **An import** (a record brought back from a platform) is such a reading with `method: import`, `derives_from` the export file's hash, naming its source in `state.text`. Platforms do not sign.
- A block of a physical deed carries its grounds, linked by hash (readings, a camera frame, a door sensor's observe), so a stranger can weigh a machine's claim. A sensor gives evidence; it never settles a promise alone (promises.md, Settling).
- A reading says whether it is a count or an estimate by `coverage` (how much was seen) and `accuracy`, so a stock report can say which part was counted.
- What counts as evidence for a kind of block is its schema's grounds. There is no global score.

## Invariants

Holds: A model's reading is never the person's claim until she puts that fact to someone else in her own step; sharing it claims nothing.
