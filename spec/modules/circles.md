# Circles

A **circle** is an `actor` of kind `circle`, written by its founder as an ordinary block; its id is that block's hash. A business, a household, a regulator and a community are each one. The circle fold (folds.md) reads its state.

- **Roles are data.** `state.roles: [{ name, powers }]`. A `name` is any text with no `#`, unique in the circle (`bad_roles`). `powers` come from the closed list in `protocol.json`, each once (`bad_roles`): `sign` (sign blocks `as` the circle), `admit` (give member grants, change roles), `remove` (remove members). The protocol reads powers, never names. At least one role is listed (`bad_roles`).
- **The founder is an ordinary member.** The founding block is their first member grant, giving the first role; writing it accepts it. It changes, ends and is superseded like any member grant. A café is sold by adding the buyer with every power, then leaving.
- **Powers narrow as they pass.** A member grant, or a change of one, gives a role only if its signer holds every power that role carries, in the circle's fold of its causal past; otherwise it does not give that role (the rest stands). `circle#role` (`grammar.role_ref`) names a role's holders; more than one `#`, or an empty part, names no one.
- **Membership** is a `grant` of kind `member`: `refs.subject` and `refs.as` the same circle (`required`), `refs.to` the member (left out, it is open: everyone who accepts is a member by it), `state.roles` a list of non-empty names (`shape`). An open grant, or a change of one, that names a role with `sign`, `admit` or `remove` counts for nothing. A holder of `admit` signs it; it counts once the member's own `answers` accepts it. The fold reads every member grant naming the circle and every `grant` that `answers` or `updates` one or the founding block; acceptances, leavings and stops carry no `kind` or `subject`. A member's **heads** are their accepted grants that no later acceptance of theirs supersedes (two devices may accept one grant at once; both are heads). A member is in while a head has not ended (authority.md, Ending) and holds the roles of every head that has not. `scope.until` on a member grant is a cut at that time (authority.md, Whose clock tests `until`): a block dated at or after it holds no role by it, though the state still lists the member.
- **Changing roles.** A holder of `admit` `updates` the member grant, or an earlier change of it, with the new `state.roles`; changes apply in causal order. Changes that do not see each other start from the roles each saw and keep a role only where all hold it, and the member holds what every last change holds. A role added counts once the member answers; one taken away counts at once, even from an unanswered invitation. Taking away `admit` or `remove` is removal-like (the removal rule), so a change that drops it counts only from a holder of `remove`. A second `accepted` answer to a grant already held brings back no role taken away.
- **Signing for a circle.** A block names the circle in `refs.as`; its author must hold `sign` in the circle's fold at that block, else it speaks for no one (`no_role`; authority.md, Who a block speaks for).
- **Leaving.** A member leaves by an `answers` with `stopped` or `declined`, or an `updates` of their own member grant with `stopped`. It needs no power or quorum and never crosses a removal. A holder of `remove` removes by an `updates` of the grant with `stopped`, under the removal rule. A circle reads no `counted_to`: every stop cuts at itself (The cut). A stolen key is its owner's to rotate (identity.md), never a circle's to reach back for.
- **An open grant's stop** ends only future joins: after a stop by one who may remove, no one joins by it (an accept the stop has not seen joins nothing), and those who joined stay until removed by the removal rule. To remove one who joined by it, the stop names the joiner in `refs.to`, read as a stop of a named grant. A joiner's own stop ends it for them alone, in any form.
- **The cut.** A stop cuts where it was signed (authority.md, The cut). A block by a member whose grant ended, that is neither the cut nor in its causal past, carries no circle authority, seen or not: signed `as` the circle, with `admit` or `remove`, a `confirms` adding a hand, an act under a grant to a role, a signal from a holder of `circle#role`. A member's own answers to grants stay theirs, as do a `close` or trigger (profiles/successor.md). A block must be within the cut of each stop that ends a grant (a second leaving, a removal beside it, every stop of a quorum). The fold sets such blocks aside and reads again until nothing changes. A member's own leaving is never set aside. A removal by a member past their cut is set aside and counts toward no quorum, except in a crossing; a removed member's stop of any other grant (one the circle gave, a grant to a role) is set aside too. What an outsider built on stands (folds.md, Built-on stands). `may_act_as` says false for a block set aside.
- **Removed.** A member is removed when every head has ended and no newer grant brought them back; the fold lists them in `removed` with null. An acceptance of a grant that lacks the removal in its causal past counts for nothing unless the acceptance is itself in the removal's causal past: an old invitation never brings a removed member back, and rejoining needs a new grant written after it. A stop of an old grant never ends a newer one. An invitation stopped before it is answered removes no one: a stop that stands (the removal rule) and has seen none of the member's `accepted` answers comes before them, whichever a reader meets first, so they never joined.
- **Many hands.** `state.needed: { sign: N }` on the circle actor (`circle.needed`) says signing needs N holders; with none, one holder's signature speaks (a threshold, folds.md, Counts). A block signed `as` a circle whose actor states no `needed.sign` is the signer's own word, as a block with no `as`: one holder's signature speaks for the circle only where that holder holds `sign`, and no number is filled in for anyone else. A block signed `as` the circle speaks for it once N different holders of `sign` have signed it: its author, and holders who `confirms` it, each holding `sign` in the circle's fold of their own block's causal past, the confirm within the cuts of their ended grants. A confirm by one without the power, or by the author, adds no hand. Only confirms in the block's causal past count. `N` may be the word `all`: every current holder of `sign`, counted in the fold of the block's causal past (folds.md, Counts). `N` may also be a share of the members, `{ of_members: "2/3" }` (folds.md, Counts). Admitting needs one holder; removal has its own rule. `needed` and `removal` are read only on a circle actor and only as founded: an `actor` that `updates` it changes no role, `needed` or `removal`.
- **A death.** A member with a counted `close` (profiles/successor.md) is out from it unless it names the circle or that member's grant in `state.continues`; the fold lists them in `removed` with null. The close cuts like a removal (The cut).
- **The removal rule.** The founding block states `state.removal: { needed: N }` (a threshold, folds.md, Counts). With none stated, nothing holds: no one who holds `admit` or `remove` is removed, and the protocol suggests no number. Removing a member who holds `admit` or `remove` needs N different holders of `remove`, the remover among them, each stopping that same grant (stops of other grants do not add up). The member removed is never one of the N; whether they hold the power is read in the fold of the stop's causal past. Fewer than N changes nothing. Every removal-like act needs N different holders of `remove` under this rule, and none counts holders of `admit`: removing a member, taking away `admit` or `remove` (each of the N making a change that drops it, having seen a version that lists it; until then the member keeps it), voiding a grant that gives either (each stopping the grant it ended), and stopping an invitation to such a grant unanswered. A stop that does not stand changes nothing, and the member who answers is in.
  **A crossing** is a ring of removals that each would stand alone, each stopping the member grant of the next one's remover, back to the first, no stop in another's causal past (two removers removing each other; A stops B, B stops C, C stops A). Every removal in a ring stands, the founder included: each stop is kept from the cut of the one before it, so none sets another aside. No step reads `at` or a hash order.
- **The circle's key** is `state.public_key_encrypt`; changing it when members leave is profiles/rekeys.md.
- **No group may make outside dealing costly.** No circle rule binds a member's dealings with non-members except as that member's own signed promise. A fine, a boycott or an expulsion for dealing outside is a term the member signed, with a stake, or it is nothing. A circle that could tax every outside deal would be a middle everyone must go through.
- **Who may give from a thing owned together** is the signers its rule names. *A household herd:* the herd belongs to a circle whose rule says signing needs two hands (`needed.sign` is 2) and names the roles that hold `sign`. A gift of more than 3 head is signed `as` the circle by two of them, say the father and a son. A smaller gift is the father's alone, by a grant from the circle with a visible `each` of 3 head (authority.md, The limit is the principal's fence). Nobody signs for the herd by default.
- **A count by share.** "One vote per share" reads the ownership fold of the shares (the schema `share_vote`, an `observe` of kind `vote`: each owned instance is one vote), beside "one member, one vote". It is an option only; the protocol fills in no default.
- **Liability limited by what is named.** A circle signs a hire with "the pot: 120,000, nothing else of ours". It needs no charter: every promise already binds only what it names (promises.md, What a promise binds). The hirer signed the cap, so no default fills it.
- **A standing referee.** A joining term ("disputes with members go to the circle's referee") is the member's own promise to the circle. A deal naming another judge does not cancel it: it may cost him his place (the removal rule), never the deal's validity.
- **A price list for wrongs.** A circle publishes a schema of refrains with stakes (promises.md, Refrain) that each member signs on joining: *if I do this, so much of mine becomes the circle's.* A hidden wrong is priced higher than an open one, by the signers' own choice. It binds only those who signed it, and never excuses an invasion of anyone who did not (promises.md, A claim of invasion).
- **Rule changes within signed bounds.** A member may sign when she joins that her dues may change by a count of two thirds of the members, up to a figure a month (the schema `bounded_change`, profiles/pools.md). That binds her only within the bound she signed, as a visible limit does (authority.md, The limit is the principal's fence), and she may leave with what is hers before a change takes effect. "Whatever the majority decides" binds nothing past what it names. This replaces both "fixed forever" and "the majority binds the dissenter". A circle's `needed` and its removal rule may carry such a bounded change.
- **Admission by vouch.** A member signs a vouch for a newcomer (profiles/vouches.md): *if he misses his dues and our judge agrees, up to £100 of mine is the circle's.* A holder of `admit` who reads it may give the member grant. The vouch is the voucher's word and the grant is the holder's; neither binds anyone else.
- **Leaders answer by their own word.** No liability falls on a leader by custom or by the office. Each leader signs a refrain with a stake, or answers for nothing.
- **A circle holds no key to accept with.** A member grant is answered by the member's own key (Membership). A circle has no signing key, so a village circle cannot be a member of a federation. A federation is made of persons who each hold a role in it, and each village circle speaks through them.
- **Shared records.** A pot, vote or auction is a record many write to. It names its rule in `refs.under` (a schema); blocks fold in causal order under it, and the record's orderer orders what is left tied. The schema's `fields` and `grounds` say what counts; there is no rule language. A secret ballot is profiles/ballots.md.

## Invariants

27. A removed member's blocks after the cut carry no authority for the circle, whether or not they saw the stop, except what an outsider who had not seen the removal built on.
