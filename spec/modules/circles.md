# Circles

A **circle** is an `actor` of kind `circle`, written by its founder as an ordinary block. Its id is that block's hash. A business, a household, a regulator and a community are each a circle. The circle's own state is read by the circle fold (folds.md).

- **Roles are data.** The circle actor lists its roles: `state.roles: [{ name, powers }]`. A `name` is any text in any language. `powers` are chosen from a closed list of machine tokens (`protocol.json`, `powers`):
  - `sign`: may sign blocks `as` the circle;
  - `admit`: may give member grants and change roles;
  - `remove`: may remove members.

  The protocol reads powers and never reads a role's name. The founder holds the first role in the list. A circle must list at least one role (`bad_roles`); a circle with none gives its founder no power, and so no one. `circle#role` (the circle's id, `#`, the role's name) names the holders of a role.
- **Membership** is a `grant` of kind `member`: `refs.subject` the circle, `refs.to` the member (left out, for an open group anyone may join), `refs.as` the circle, `state.roles` the role names it gives (text). It must carry `refs.subject` and `refs.as` and they must be the same circle (`required`), and `state.roles`, when present, is a list of non-empty role names (`shape`). It is signed by someone holding `admit`, and counts once the member's own `answers` accepts it. The fold reads every member grant that names the circle, and every `grant` that `answers` or `updates` one of them, so an acceptance, a leaving or a stop need carry no `kind` or `subject` of its own (only `status`): what it answers or updates says which circle it is for. A member may accept more than one grant: their **heads** are the accepted grants that no later acceptance of theirs supersedes (their acceptance is seen by no other acceptance of the member's). A member is in while a head has not ended, and holds the roles of every head that has not (all of them, when two heads do not see each other).
- **Changing roles.** A holder of `admit` changes roles by an `updates` of the member grant. A role added counts once the member answers. A role taken away counts at once.
- **Signing for a circle.** A member names the circle with `refs.as`, and must hold, in the circle's fold at that block, a role with the power `sign`. Without it the block speaks for no one in any fold (`no_role`; folds.md, Who a block speaks for).
- **Leaving and removal.** A member leaves by an `answers` with `stopped`. A holder of `remove` removes a member by an `updates` of their grant with `stopped`, naming `counted_to` (identity.md), the member's last counted block; `counted_to` is a block the stop needs (core.md, check 5). A stop that names none cuts at the stop itself.
- **The cut.** A stop has a **cut**: the block it names in `counted_to`, else the stop. A block a member signed `as` the circle, or with `admit` or `remove`, that is neither the cut nor in the cut's causal past carries no authority for the circle, **whether or not it has seen the stop**: a removed member's device offline cannot go on speaking for the circle. Where more than one authorised stop ends a grant, a block must be within every cut. The fold applies this once: it reads the stops, sets those blocks aside, and reads the grants again. A removal is never set aside this way: two that cross follow the crossing rule below. `may_act_as` says false for a block set aside.
- **Removed.** A member is removed when every head has ended (their own `stopped` or `declined` answer, or a remover's `stopped` update) and no newer grant has brought them back in. The fold keeps the `counted_to` the stop named (null if it names none). An invitation stopped before it is answered removes no one, and a later answer accepts nothing: a stop that has not seen the member's answer comes before it, whichever of the two a reader meets first, so the member never joined and is not in `removed`. A stopped grant stays stopped; rejoining needs a new grant, and a stop of an old grant never ends a newer one.
- **Removing someone who holds `admit` or `remove`** follows the circle's own rule, `state.removal: { needed: N }` on the circle actor (default 1): at least N different holders of `remove`, **the remover among them**, must each stop that same grant (an `updates` of it with `stopped`; stops of other grants do not add up). With N = 1 the remover alone removes; with N = 2 the remover and one more. The member being removed is never one of the N. A holder of `admit` or `remove` removed by fewer than N stands. When two removals cross (each stops the other remover's member grant, and neither stop is in the other's causal past), the removal whose remover's own member grant is earlier wins, earlier meaning in the causal past of the other remover's member grant; the founder's removal wins over all. If neither is earlier, both removals stand. No step reads `at` or a hash order.
- **The circle's key** is an encryption key in its actor (`state.public_key_encrypt`). A circle that must change it when members leave uses the key-epoch profile (profiles/rekeys.md).
- **Shared records.** A pot, a vote or an auction is a record many write to. It names its rule in `refs.under` (a schema). Blocks fold in causal order under the rule; the record's orderer orders what the rule leaves tied. The protocol has no rule language: the schema's `fields` and `grounds` say what counts, and a reader folds them. A secret ballot is a profile (profiles/ballots.md).
