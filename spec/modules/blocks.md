# Blocks

The fields a block may carry beyond core.md, causal order, time, a named clock, the cut, finality, records and refusals.

**Fields any block may carry:**
- `refs.subject`: who or what it is about.
- `state.when`: when it happened, if different from `at`. Four forms: an instant; a range `{ from, to }` (both, `from` no later than `to`); a relative form `{ after, wait }` (both; promises.md, Relative time); or a **window** with any of `not_before`, `not_after` (times, the first no later than the second) and `takes` (a duration), at least one, which may also sit beside a range's or relative form's keys, and no other key (`protocol.json`, `shapes.when_window`; patterns/tasks.md). A window is the performer's room to move, never a promise of when. Anything else, or a value that is neither text nor an object, is `shape`.
- `state.where`: a point `{lat, lon}` (WGS84 degrees as decimal strings, `lat` -90 to 90, `lon` -180 to 180, compared exactly), or an area: a point with `radius`, an amount in metres. `refs.place`: a thing it is in or at.
- `state.kind`: a label for the sort of step or thing; open words, those the protocol reads in `protocol.json` (`kinds`). `refs.of` is different: the thing this is an instance of (things.md).
- `refs.as`: the circle the author signs for.
- `refs.under`: the grant, rule or law it acts under.
- `refs.schema`: the schema it follows.
- `state.nonce`: on a private block, so short content cannot be guessed from its hash.
- `state.skip`: `true` on a block that jumps `seq` forward (core.md, The chain).
- `state.opening`: `{ salt, value }`, the opening of a commitment (privacy.md, A commitment). A condition `opened` reads it (promises.md, Conditions).

**Causal order.** `prev` is the previous block of the same key. `refs.after` is the other heads the author had seen, at most 16. A block's **causal past** is every block reachable from it through its refs and through the block it counts to (`state.counted_to`), so a key grant sees the line it cuts. Order never reads `at`.

**Time.** The record holds one kind of time: this word has that word in its past. Everything else about time is somebody's word. A block's `at` is its signer's word of what her clock showed; any block may leave it out. No rule orders two blocks by dates, adds a span to a date, or reads a date along a line.

A time a word states is an end of that word. On a clock the word names, its clocks' cutoffs show it (The cut). With no clock, a block made on the word is in time only by its own `at`, before the end. A block whose `at` is at or after the end, or that states none, is not in time: it binds the word's signer only by her own later block, or by a relier's block that has it in its past. A block's own date shows it late against its own signer, always. A missing `at` reads the same in every part: under a grant with `until`, as under any stated end. A clock's receipt after its own cutoff dated before its time, and a signer's two dates against each other, are readings, binding no one.

**A named clock.** A **clock** is a line the word's signer named, so that her ends, the times she stated and other people's blocks sit on one line and are put in order: `clocks: { names, needed }`. In anything a person reads, it is a person or service she picked to say when things happened, who signs "I held this then". Each name is a block id and names a line, read as it goes on (folds.md, A line goes on): a person's id, her genesis key's line; the id of a key grant that adds one key under a voice, that key's line; any other block, its key's blocks after it. A receipt counts only where its key spoke for that clock's owner at that block. `needed` follows Counts (folds.md): a set of one needs none. A clock is written by whoever signs the word; whoever accepts the word or acts on it has read it. Nobody need name one, and none is ever filled in.
- A clock's reading for a word starts at her receipt of the word.
- A **receipt** is a clock's counted `observe` of kind `receipt`, `refs.derives_from` the blocks it names, one or more: when it signed it, it held them. A receipt that names several blocks gives them no order, and no rule that needs an order reads them from it. It states what its line shows for the word: that no end of the word is before it; for a meter, the running total. A receipt is its clock's word: it counts against what is the clock's owner's whenever it is held, and against another's only where his own word named that clock. A receipt names blocks of one pusher only. Where one receipt names an end and a block made on the word, neither came first on that line: the receipt shows the block neither before nor after the end. A later receipt of the block on that line is after the end.
- A **cutoff** is a clock's counted `observe` of kind `cutoff`, `refs.subject` the word, `state.when` the word's own text for that time: an instant; for a span (a wait, a reach), the span itself, a duration, with `refs.derives_from` the receipt or earlier cutoff it runs from. A span cutoff closes a span no longer than its own (durations are compared, never added to a date). On her line it is that time's place: a receipt before it came before the time, a receipt after it came after; a receipt with no cutoff of the time before it on its line came before the time. A cutoff whose own `at` is before its `when` closes nothing: its own date shows it early, against its own signer. A cutoff that states no `at`, and a span's, is never early.
- **The four readings, and no others.** *By T*: a line holds a receipt of the block before its cutoff through T. *Not by T; a wait has run*: a line holds a cutoff through T (for a wait, a span cutoff running from her receipt of its start) with no such receipt before it; until then it is pending, and no reader's clock closes it. *Which came first*: read from a line's order, never from dates. *How much, and which of many*: read along one line only; a word that needs to know either names one.
- **Which reading needs `needed`.** On a clock, `needed` lines must show the reading that moves a thing out of its signer's hand, or widens what a word allows (a block in time, a wait run, a reach run). The other reading takes `names` less `needed`, plus one. They never both hold, and once every line has spoken one of them holds.
- **Read in the past of the block that relies on it.** No reading of a clock reaches outside the causal past of the block that relies on it, bar whether an end stands. A key grant a block names for its meaning is read, for that block, within the block's own causal past: its wait and its reach run only on the receipts and cutoffs that block holds.
- **A side's clock.** A clock that is a side of the word counts against that side by every receipt it signs, and against the other side only where his signed word names it.
- **Silence.** When a named clock is silent, what waits on it waits; nothing fills its silence. A gate (a block that counts only once a clock holds it) is never the only way: a voice above passes it. A clock that cannot close leaves its question open, for the judge the sides chose.

The sides named the clock, so its word binds them, and no one else. Ground: a date binds only the one who wrote it, or the one she named to write it (Mises: only persons act; a stranger's clock is no one's word).

**The cut.** A standing word (a grant, an offer, a request, a transfer to a named receiver, a key, an account word, a claim, a named signer's word) is ended by an **end**: its signer's counted stop, a key grant that leaves the key out, a removal (circles.md), a newer word of hers in its place, a time it states (`until`, `expires`; Time), and for a named signer's word a pair on her key or her own `defeats`. An end counts from where it is shown.
- **Her own word stands alone.** A block joined to no other person's block (it names none, and none names it as her yes), and made on no word but her own whose ends only her own voices sign, stands against those ends when signed. A key grant by her voice above that key whose `counted_to` is before it, or a pair at its place, puts it off her line toward every block that does not have it in its past. Nothing else does. A block another person's block is joined to is read by the rest of The cut.
- **The join.** A block made on the word counts as made on it where the end has it in its past; or where another person's block is joined to it (names it, or is named by it as her yes) and has not seen the end. Where the word names a clock, the join is the clocks' receipts of the block, held in the other's block's past, stating that no end is before it on their lines (`needed` of them). For one who signs nothing more, an end is shown only on a clock. Any other block made on an ended word counts for nothing. Until then it is waiting.
- **Several ends.** Where several ends reach one word, a block that is inside one end's cut must still pass each other end.
- **Seen.** A block has seen an end where the end is in its causal past as the reader reads it (bodies only: a header shows nothing seen; a receipt the block's author signed of the end is this fact, because the receipt derives from the end). Where the word names a clock, a clock's line counts for a block only through the receipts the relying block holds. Nothing else is seen: not when an end reached a node, not a date on it. Whether the end stands is read on all the reader holds. A block that names the end in `refs.confirms` has not seen it: the end stands only by such a block.
- **Relier.** The author of a counted block that is joined to a word (answers it, accepts or names a block made under it, or acts on it), who is not the word's signer, not a tool of hers, and not acting under a grant she gave, and whose block has not seen the end.

**From where a stop counts.** Her word says which. If it names a clock, her stop is counted from that clock's receipt of it. If it names none, her stop is counted for each block from where that block, or the block that built on it, has it in its past. The protocol fills in neither. Ground: title moves by its owner's shown consent; toward one her end has reached, her consent is gone (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19; Kinsella).

**Finality.** A word stands when every condition its signer stated before it has held: its own, and those of each word it is made on. Until then it moves nothing. Those conditions are her list for its key (its `wait`, its `reach` and her line's bounds: identity.md); the grant it is under; the rows of the circle it is `as`; the deal it is `for`; each block it names for its meaning; and the ends it must pass (The cut). All must hold; no order among them is needed.
- **Waiting.** A word that does not yet stand is waiting. It moves nothing to anyone and binds no one else; a draw that is waiting still holds its amount against its own owner. A fold that meets it lists it in `waiting: [{ block, on }]`, one entry for each thing it waits for, in this order: `wait` (that key's wait has not run on her clock), `reach`, `confirm` (it waits for its key to say it again, or for a voice above), `end` (an end not yet passed), `named` (a block it names for its meaning that does not stand), `hands` (a block `as` a circle for which no row holds yet), `condition` (a condition that has not held; a draw held on it too). A word that can never stand waits on nothing and is listed nowhere.
- **A word that lets another act or spend.** A word of a key that lets a key or a person act or spend for her, or more than before, stands once that key has said it again after its voice's `wait` has run on her clocks, or a voice above has said it. At `PT0S` it stands when signed: signing twice is her own choice in her list, a `wait` above zero. A voice of other people speaks only when `needed` of them have signed it. Such words are a key grant that brings a key in, a grant that is not a stop (a machine lock is one) or an answer, an `account` word, her lift of a stop (`resumed`), and a hand-over (a transfer that names in `refs.what` a block that is not a thing) (identity.md, When a key grant stands, which says what saying it again is). A word that only narrows (a key grant that only takes its signer's own key out, a stop) is not under this rule.
- **What stands stays.** What stands stays: no fold moves it back. No later block changes what a standing word moved or bound, bar the new owner's own word, and, for a deal's stage only, the bench. More words lower an answer only where they show that one key signed two things for one place, or where a key grant by her own voice above a key names in `state.counted_to` the last block of that key that counts: "nothing from that key after this point counts". That key's later blocks are then off her line toward every block that does not have them in its past; for anyone whose block already built on one, it takes back nothing.

Once it stands, no later block moves back what it moved: a wrong found after that is a sum to come or a claim before a judge, never a reversal. Ground: what can still move back has not moved yet; a title given on a condition its giver stated before anyone relied is pure title transfer (Rothbard, ch. 19).

**Records.** A **record** is a block and every version after it. `updates` names the version it replaces; a record is named by its first version. (A promise is not a record: its terms are fixed, and its `updates` is its stop.)

**Shared ref keys** (`protocol.json`, `refs`): every type may use them. On a closed type, any other ref key must be declared by the block's schema.

**Micro safety.** A micro keeps its `seq` counter and last block id in storage that survives power loss, and writes them before it sends. If it loses them, it asks its edge for its head. If it cannot learn it, a lost block stops nothing: its later blocks are valid, and the chain fold shows the gap; a line read whole across the gap is not shown until the block turns up. A `skip` block jumps `seq` forward when only the counter was lost. A writer signs only what its own `read` reads. A block a node lists as unread is on her line: her next block follows it.

**Refusals.** A refusal is `{ hash, code, detail }` and is final for those bytes, bar `unmet` and `unknown_suite`, which wait (core.md, section 8). A block `read` cannot read is not refused: it is not read. `code` is from the closed list in `protocol.json` (`refusals.codes`), one per rule, the same on every node. `detail` is free text no node reads. A block that breaks several rules gets one entry per rule, in the list's order. The list also names why a fold counts a block for nothing (`fold`) and why a node refuses a message (`message`).
