# Promises

A promise moves a noun from one side to the other. Sides and what moves are in `refs`; `terms` and `what` (a description) are in `state`. The stage fold reads it (folds.md, A promise's stage).

**What a promise binds.** Every promise has one shape: *if this happens, this of mine becomes yours, and this person settles it.* The condition is `terms.condition` with the deal's dates. What becomes yours is `refs.what` and the price. A referee named in advance settles a dispute (Disputes): a side's own word that the other failed is a claim, never a verdict. A promise binds its signer's name and what they pledged, never their body: a side may always stop onward (Answers). A signed word never gives anyone a right to use force on the signer's body; only the signer's own attack, never their word, costs them that. What one can hand on is what one acquired or was handed; a body was never acquired, so no word hands it on (Kinsella, revising Rothbard). A stop owes nothing by itself; money paid for a deal that never came is still the payer's (Settling). Trade, loan, wage, deposit, warranty, stake and guarantee are all this shape. (Title-transfer theory of contract: Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19.)

**Sides.** `refs.from` gives; `refs.to` receives, each one id (a set is refused, `shape`). Either may be left out, which leaves it open: an offer open to anyone, or a want (an ask with `from` open). A promise signed by `from` is an **offer**; signed by `to`, an **ask**. What comes back flows the other way and is read from the transfers.
- **Only a side signs.** A promise is signed by a side: its author, the circle it signs `as`, or the grantor of the grant it acts under (authority.md, Who a block speaks for). Otherwise it speaks for no side and the fold counts it for nothing (`not_a_side`). The check never refuses it, because a side may name a thing whose owner the promise does not rest on (Bearer): only the fold can say (core.md, invariant 25). An answer or stop is signed by a side of its deal in the same way. What someone without a key said they would do is the receiver's own `observe` (kind `message`), never a promise.
- **Bearer.** `refs.to` may name a thing: whoever owns it is the receiving side, read where each block stands (the ownership fold of its causal past), never "now". The holder's accept binds for good, even after the note moves on. What is owed goes to whoever holds the note where the receiving answer stands: passed on before settlement, it carries what is still owed to its new holder; passed on after, nothing. A block a holder signs after the note left them speaks for no side. A thing with no owner has no one to answer.
- **Owed.** A side of a deal that binds is owed something: the `to` side what moves, the `from` side the price. The debtor's own signature settled it, so it is owed, not claimed. Its owner may hand that over by a transfer that names the deal in `refs.what`, signed by the owner, with `refs.to_owner` the new owner, who accepts it like any transfer to a named receiver (things.md). It must have the deal's binding accept in its causal past. With `state.money` it hands over that much of the price; with none, all that its signer is owed. The owner is read where each block stands, the ownership fold of its causal past, as for Bearer. A delivery or a payment counts only when it goes to the owner where it stands, and only that owner's accepted answer counts it. A payment made without the hand-over in its causal past was made to the owner it saw: it counts, and the old owner holds that amount for the new one (`owed_on`, folds.md). A payment made after seeing it, to the old owner, counts for nothing. Duties stay with whoever signed them: the buyer of a claim to goods does not owe the price, and the seller of a claim to the price still owes what moves. A part of the price handed to the one who owes it is paid. Only the signed transfer of the owner moves a claim; silence moves nothing. A hand-over by someone who is not owed counts for nothing, so does a second by one who has handed it all on, and so do two by one key that name one `prev` (a fork). A side that names a thing is Bearer's: its owner moves by the thing's own transfers, and a hand-over of that side counts for nothing. Nobody passes on more than they had: the new owner takes every condition of the deal (where, when, how it is collected). **Passing a claim on carries no promise that the debtor pays:** it is a sale of the claim. The passer may add a vouch or a recourse line, which binds only her; silence means none. (The law merchant's revived endorser liability is a custom nobody signed.) **A passed right carries its conditions.** The deliverer still owes only what he signed. Conditions on the receiver's acts (send lorries, take delivery by T) travel with what is owed and bind the new holder's reading, so no "consent to assign" default is needed. A term `personal: true` says the claim cannot be handed on; a hand-over then counts for nothing. **An heir** holds what is owed by the successor the dead person named (profiles/successor.md, Heirs). A claim of harm is handed on in the same way and moves standing, never money (A claim of invasion).
- **Who it is for.** `refs.for_whom`: a person, a circle, or open, written as a commitment (privacy.md, A commitment). It reaches the other side only when it changes their work, and only as much as they need (`terms.needs`).
- **To yourself.** A promise whose `refs.from` and `refs.to` both name its author is accepted when made. The top of a plan is one.

**What moves** is `refs.what`: a thing (a kind in an offer or ask, an instance once known), a share, a record, or a circle. Time and a job are not things: `state.what` describes them under a schema and `terms.quantity` says how much. A counter that names no `refs.what` moves what the first promise names.

**The deal** is named by its first promise, one with no `refs.answers`. Every later block in the deal names it in `refs.for`. An answer or counter whose `refs.for` is absent or names another block than the deal's first promise is refused (`deal_not_named`); that is the one answered when it has no `answers`, else the `for` of the one answered. A first promise may carry `refs.for` for what it serves (a sub-task's parent); it starts a new deal. **An allocation (Who may bind) starts a deal of its own:** it names the open offer in `refs.for`, but the blocks that settle it name the allocation, so no other taker's payment counts toward it.

**Terms:** `{ price, quantity, lines, pay_by, deliver_by, until, repeat, condition, expires, band, needs, refrain, zone, keepers, forfeit, personal, backing }`, and any term the promise's schema declares.
- **No term has a floor.** A price of one penny or a quantity of one egg is a deal; no schema or template sets a smallest size.
- `price` is money, an amount of any kind (`{ value, unit }`: "6 sacks of maize at harvest"), money per unit (`{ money, per }`, `per` an amount), or a method that fixes it later (below). Money is one good among many, the one people chose most; nothing fills the signers' silence about the unit, so the price names it. The unit carries its measure (a glossary entry, profiles/rulebooks.md): a bare noun is not a unit. A payment `for` the deal counts toward a price of its own kind; the fold never converts between kinds (Settling). Interest in kind is a plain extra quantity of the same good (5 sacks lent, 6 repaid), never a rate. A swap is one deal, its legs in sequence: only goods already held by a third party settle both at once. Under a per-unit price, `quantity` may be a range `{ min, max }` and the answer's `terms.quantity` names the quantity chosen. The total is `money.minor` times the quantity over `per`, rounded to the nearest minor unit, halves up; the quantity must be in `per`'s unit (nothing is converted); a `per` of zero has no total, and a price with no total is never paid (folds.md, stage 2). A flat price (no `per`) is the total for the whole promise, or for its line inside `lines`. An ask with no price asks for one. A price is never below zero (`shape`); paying someone to take a thing is a job the giver buys, a promise the other way.
- **A price fixed later.** `price` may name its method instead of a number: `{ figure, by, signers, deadline, keepers }`, and optionally `fraction`, `min`, `max`. The figure is an `observe` of kind `figure`, `for` the deal, with `state.money`, signed by one of the readers `by` names (ids or `circle#role`: a market's reader on delivery day, the seller, the buyer, a partner's account of profit), received by `deadline` on the named clock `keepers` (blocks.md, A named clock). It counts only inside `min` and `max` when they are named, and nothing is cut down to them. The price is fixed when `signers` different readers have each signed the same figure and no other figure reached that many; a reader who signs two different figures counts for neither. It is the figure, or `fraction` of it (exact decimal text from 0 to 1), rounded as a per-unit price is. Until then the stage reports `price_fixed` false, the total is null and the price is never paid. Nothing is filled in: with no figure by the window's end the deal shows it unfixed, and only the sides or the referee resolve it. A late figure never counts. It is on the whole promise, never on a line. A reader's figure may cite a market-price reading (the base schema `price_reading`, profiles/attestations.md) in `derives_from`.
- `lines`: `[{ id, what, quantity, price }]`, one order accepted at once. A line's quantity is an amount, never a range (`lines`). Each `id` is non-empty text, unique in the promise; blocks for one line name it (`state.line`). `refs.what` equals the set of the lines' `what`. With `lines` there is no top-level `price` or `quantity`. All prices are in one currency (`lines`).
- `until` is a return or end date; `expires` ends an unanswered offer or ask. With no `expires` it lasts until its author stops it, and an offer with none is firm.
- `pay_by` is when the price falls due. The stage reports `due` beside `paid_at`, when the price was paid in full. **A new date both sides signed.** A term binds only as signed, so changing it needs both signers, and silence moves nothing. A side writes an `observe` of kind `message` `for` the deal with a `pay_by`; the other side answers it `accepted` (an `observe` that `answers` it). The stage lists the agreed dates in order (`moved`) beside the original `due`, which stays: nothing is edited. A date only one side signed moves nothing and shows as that side's word only. The view reports which dates passed before the price was paid (`missed`). A late payment after an agreed move is a different fact from a miss with no agreement. A proposal that asks for time may cite in `derives_from` the debtor's own unpaid claim on her client (a bounced cheque); the stage names it beside the move, and the other side's yes is still what moves the date. A standing promise is due and paid by occurrence: a payment names its occurrence (`state.occurrence`: its start time, or the hash of the block that started it), and the stage reports per occurrence when it fell due, what was paid and when in full (`occurrences`, folds.md). These are facts; whether a payment was late is each reader's judgement.
- `band` is catch weight: under a per-unit price, how far what is delivered may fall from the quantity chosen, either way, as a fraction of it (decimal 0 to 1, such as `"0.05"`; `shape` otherwise). What the receiver accepted inside the band covers the line and is what is priced (folds.md, stage 2). Ignored beside any other price.
- `repeat` is an RFC 5545 RRULE value (`FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU`; markets on a 4-, 5- or 8-day week are `FREQ=DAILY;INTERVAL=4`, `INTERVAL=5`, `INTERVAL=8`): parts `KEY=VALUE`, exactly one `FREQ`; another calendar may come first by RFC 7529 `RSCALE`. It counts from the start of `state.when`, read in `terms.zone`, so a calendar repeat carries `state.when` and `terms.zone` (`shape`). An occurrence is named by its start time. A repeat may follow an event instead: `{ after, by, wait }` (`protocol.json`, `repeat`) makes an occurrence each time an `observe` of kind `after` is written `for` the promise by a signer `by` names (ids, or `circle#role`), due `wait` after the named clock's receipt of it (Relative time), named by the observe's hash. **The side that charges cannot start a charge alone.** A payer who signed "each crossing the gate sensor records" consented to that count, not to the payee's own word. Only the names in `by` start occurrences (a side counts only if it names itself). With no `by`, nothing starts one: nothing fills the sides' silence. The protocol checks the shape (`grammar.rrule_part`, `repeat.freq`); a reader works out the occurrences.
- `keepers` is the offer's named clock (blocks.md, A named clock): `{ names, needed }`, `needed` when there is more than one name (`shape` otherwise). An accept `needed` keepers received by `expires` is timely (Who may bind), and an event `when` or `repeat` runs its wait from their receipt.
- `forfeit` is money: the part of what the payer paid that stays with the payee if the deal ends before it is done (a booking fee, a stop fee). It is a term both signed, so it fills in no one's silence (Settling).
- `backing` is money, or a count of one named kind (an amount: "up to 2 heifers"): what the signer puts behind the promise, up to which the pot (folds.md, A pot) counts payouts. "I stand behind her for up to N" is a promise with `backing` N.
- `personal` is `true`: the claim to what this deal owes cannot be handed on (A claim).
- `zone` is shaped like an IANA time zone name (`grammar.zone`: the shape is checked, never a list): the zone a calendar repeat is read in. A calendar repeat with no `zone` is refused (`shape`): the protocol fills in no zone, and a block so refused is read by no fold, so it has no occurrences.
- `condition` is data under a schema: what must be true (Conditions). The schema's grounds name who confirms it and who settles a dispute.
- `needs`: what the work must meet, from a schema, never a name.
- **A schema may add terms** (its `state.terms`, `{ name: hint }`), as a closed block carries the fields its schema declares (core.md, section 7); any other term is refused (`undeclared_field`), and a checker with no schema store does not judge them. No fold reads a term it does not know: it binds no one and moves nothing.

**Refrain.** `terms.refrain: true` is the one shape with a condition of doing: *if this is done before `until`, this of mine becomes yours* (the price or stake). It binds what the signer pledged, never what they will do. It carries `terms.until`, the time it is kept (`required`). It moves nothing, so it is never **done**. It is **breached** only by a counted `defeats` of the deal's head (its first promise, or the offer the binding accept answers) by the deal's referee (Disputes), saying the thing happened no later than `until`, and not taken back (the referee's own `defeats` of that `defeats`); or by its own condition when that is a signal that holds at its `until`. It happened at the defeats' `state.when` (an instant, or the `from` of a range), else its `at`; a breach reported after the term still counts. A side's word that it was broken only disputes (Disputes). Once the reader's clock passes `until` with no breach counted, the view says **elapsed**: a fact of its term, never that it was kept. A price settles as any price does. *Example:* an employee, a buyer for a restaurant or the operator of an autonomous system signs the schema `loyalty` (profiles/vouches.md): *I take nothing from anyone else about what I do for you*, with money behind it. The payer is never marked: only what the agent signed is read.

**Conditions.** A condition holds or not on the blocks a reader holds and the reader's clock: a view (folds.md, Views). It holds, does not hold, or is **not read**. The protocol reads these shapes (`protocol.json`, `condition_shapes`; door `condition_holds`; a shape written wrong does not hold, and a wrapper written wrong is refused, `shape`). Any other shape is data for its schema's readers (null): a rain index read before the rain is known is not read, not false.
- `{ settled: <first promise> }`: that promise is done, or it is a refrain whose view is elapsed with its price, if any, paid in full.
- `{ accepted: <first promise> }`: that deal binds: its stage is accepted or further (underway, done, reversed, breached) or stopped onward, read from its promise fold, with no clock. A promise so conditioned binds only once that deal binds: an accept of it binds nothing before, and binds as soon as it does. A block the reader does not hold, or a cycle back to a deal being read, holds for nothing.
- `{ deed: <block> }`: that block is held and counts.
- `{ paid: <first promise> }`: that deal binds and its price is paid in full, read from its promise fold. It is the price side of a deal, so title may wait for the price without waiting for the goods. (A `settled` of the same deal would wait for the title that waits for it, and neither would move.)
- `{ opened: <commitment> }`: someone shows the code. It holds when a counted block carries `state.opening`, `{ salt, value }`, that opens the commitment (privacy.md, A commitment): one hash comparison, in the suite the commitment names. It needs no clock, no third party and no key. The promisor signed the term, and telling the code is his act of release. Anyone who learns the code can show it, but what it moves goes only to the receiver already named. An opening shown before the promise binds makes the condition hold, and the promise still binds only by its accept.
  *Example.* Chidi gives a rider a four-digit code at the door and signs: if anyone shows it, £5 of his becomes the rider's. He commits to the code with a salt and writes the commitment in the condition. The rider writes any block with `state.opening` `{ salt, value: "4821" }`, and the five pounds are his. Anyone who learns the code can trigger it, but it pays only the payee already named.
- A sensor's readings, under the schema `reading_condition` (profiles/tasks.md).
- **Due on her next order.** A debt that falls due when the debtor next buys from the same seller is the debtor's own promise conditioned on her own signal: `{ signal: "order", by: [her id], subject: the seller }`. Her signed order is the event, so no event clock is needed, and an order signed by anyone else starts nothing.
- **A signal:** `{ signal: <observe kind>, by: [ids or circle#role], signers, subject, op, value }`. It holds when `signers` different signers that `by` names have each signed a counted `observe` of that kind, with an `at` no later than the reader's clock, naming `subject` in `refs.subject` when given, and carrying an `amount` that keeps to `op` (`lt`, `le`, `gt`, `ge`, `eq`) and `value` when given. A holder of `circle#role` is one in the circle's fold of the observe's causal past, within the cuts of authority.md, The cut. **A signer is whom the block speaks for** (authority.md, Who a block speaks for): a signal a lead signs under a member's grant ("your refusal signals under this rule speak for me, until D") counts as that member's, once per member; a signal outside what the grant covers speaks for the lead alone. A signer takes their signal back by a counted block that `defeats` it and speaks for the same signer (a member's own block, or a lead's under the same grant), for good (a defeat of that defeat restores nothing; they sign a new signal). **Shape:** `by` is a non-empty list of ids or `circle#role`; `signers` is a whole number of at least 1 and follows Counts (folds.md); `subject`, when given, one id; `op` and `value` come together or not at all, `value` an amount (`{ value, unit }`, the value a decimal string). Any other shape does not hold.

**By a time.** Any condition the protocol reads may be wrapped: `{ within: <condition>, deadline, keepers }` holds when it held by `deadline`; `{ without: <condition>, deadline, keepers }` holds when it did not. `keepers` is the named clock (blocks.md, A named clock). All three keys are required: there is no default clock, and silence never widens. Without them the wrapper is refused (`shape`) and holds for nothing. The inner condition **held by the deadline** when it holds, read at the deadline, on the blocks that were in time. A block that decides it counts only if `needed` keepers received it by the deadline, never by its own `at`, which its signer could backdate. The deciding blocks are: for `settled`, `accepted` and `paid`, the blocks of that deal (those naming it in `for`); for `deed`, that block; for a signal or a reading, the observes of its kind; for `opened`, the blocks that carry an opening. An arrival that never reached the named clock counts as no arrival: that is a term both sides signed. `without` holds once the reader's clock is past the deadline, or `needed` keepers have signed a cutoff through it, and the inner condition did not hold by then. `within` needs no clock. From the deadline on they are exact complements, so a pair of them on one clock never both hold, whatever the order they were signed in. A wrapper reads the clock, so it is a view, as `expired` and `elapsed` are.

**The condition fact.** For a promise with `terms.condition`, the view says beside the stage whether it `holds`, does `not`, or is `unread` (a shape the protocol does not read). A promise whose condition does not hold owes nothing yet and is never breached for it. With no condition the fact is null. **Failure spreads visibly.** When the condition does not hold because the deal it names ended without it (stopped, declined, expired or breached), or because that deal's own condition failed, the view names that deal in `condition_failed`, never just "open". In a chain of three, the third shows the second, and the second shows the first.

**A soft term never stands alone.** A condition that rests on a soft word ("fresh", "ripe", "fits") names who judges satisfaction and how: a grader, a test, or a judge both sides chose. Otherwise satisfaction is the requester's free veto, and a veto breaks title. The protocol reads only the shapes above; the soft word is data for its schema, and the judge is a `signal` by the one named (`{ signal: "fit", by: [grader] }`), a reading, or a referee (Disputes).

**Relative time.** `state.when` may be `{ after, wait }`. With `after` a block id, the step is due `wait` after that block arrives on the performer's node, on the performer's own clock. This is scheduling, never authority. If the anchor never arrives, the step waits. A `repeat` counts from that start. With `after` an observe kind (any text that is not an id), the step follows an event, as an event `repeat` does: it is due `wait` after the receiving side's `observe` of that kind written `for` the promise (the holder, read as Bearer reads it). The wait runs from the named clock's receipt of that observe, never from its own `at`: the promise's `terms.keepers`, and no one else's receipt. With no `keepers` there is no clock; with no receipt the wait has not begun: the stage lists the ask in `occurrences` with `due` null. `PT0S` is on demand, `PT12H` on notice, and a date stays a date. An ask by anyone but the receiving side counts for nothing. "On demand" claims that the issuer holds what is asked. The protocol cannot see a warehouse, so that is the issuer's word, read against her own counts.

**Answers.** The other side's own block `answers` a promise. It has the type of the block it answers (an `observe` or `transfer` that says `accepted` to a promise answers nothing), names the deal in `for`, and carries `state.status`. It names no term but the quantity it chooses (`terms.quantity`); any other term on it is refused (`undeclared_field`). The type's other required fields do not apply. The statuses (`protocol.json`, `statuses`):
- `accepted`: binds.
- `declined`: ends it.
- `stopped`, with `state.stop`: `before` (a withdrawal), `onward` (from this block on: leaving a job), or `{ occurrences: [start times, or hashes of the blocks that started them] }` (named occurrences of a standing promise; nothing else is in the object). A stop names no reason; it may be said in an `observe` `for` the deal.

An answer may carry in `refs.derives_from` the hash of the exact read-back text its signer was shown, or the hashes of every rendering she met (the text, the audio played, the pictures shown), so a voice or picture deal binds to what was actually shown. Consent is to what she met. The protocol never reads the renderings: they are evidence a referee compares with the bytes, and the answer binds the same with or without them.

How a promise is ended, and what a stop or decline reaches, is one rule (authority.md, Ending). A promise has no status of its own: offered or asked is read from who signed it. Its author stops it by an `updates` with `status: stopped`; the other side by an `answers`. **That is all an `updates` of a promise is: its terms are fixed once written** (invariant 15). Any other `updates` is refused (`required`) and a fold counts it for nothing. To change terms the other side counters, or the author stops and makes a new promise. A new promise that `answers` one with no status is a **counter-offer** (the one answer without a status, core.md, section 7). It binds nothing until the other side answers it `accepted`; then the deal's terms are the counter's own, whole, and what it leaves out is not in the deal.

**Who may bind.**
- A one-to-one offer binds on the other side's accept.
- A first promise with an open side (an open offer or a want), limited in quantity or not, binds only through an **allocation**: an `accepted` answer to a request, signed by the deal's orderer (folds.md, Orderer). A request is an accept of the offer by anyone but its author, which its author answers, or a counter in a chain on it (below), which the side that did not sign it answers. How she chooses among requests is hers; she may declare a rule as a field under a schema the offer names, and the protocol reads only her allocations.
- The quantity accepted is in the answer's `terms.quantity`; on an open offer, the allocation names what binds. An accept keeps to the offer (invariant 15): where the offer's quantity is a range, the accept names a quantity in its unit from `min` to `max`; otherwise it names none, or the same. An accept that does not keep to it binds nothing. On an offer limited in quantity, an allocation (or the request it answers) names a quantity in the offer's unit, on a range no less than `min`, or it binds nothing (invariant 11). **Every allocation the offerer signed binds her:** allocations that together bind more than she offered, or that do not see each other (a fork of her chain), each bind their taker; what is past the offer is shown at once as over-allocated, a plain fact (folds.md), and is her broken word only when the taker is due and is neither served nor paid the bump she signed beforehand (Late worked examples, Overbooking honestly); a promise about a future good is kept or broken when due, not when made (A fork is a burden). **One request is allocated once:** a later allocation of a request an allocation already binds (not stopped onward in the later one's causal past) binds nothing; so does a later allocation of a counter in a chain one of whose counters an allocation already binds. **An allocation binds a taker to no more than she asked for:** where her request names a quantity, the allocation names none, or one in its unit and no larger. A taker withdraws her request as an author withdraws an offer (`updates`, `stopped` with `before`): an allocation that has the withdrawal in its causal past binds nothing. **A stopped allocation frees its quantity:** an onward stop of an allocation by either side takes its quantity off what is bound, and ends that deal alone. **An open offer with `lines`** offers each line once, whole: each allocation binds every line, and each later one is her broken promise to that taker.
- **A counter to an open offer.** Someone other than the offerer may counter an open offer: a promise that answers it with no status, keeping the offerer's side as the offer names it and naming on the open side, if anyone, the one it speaks for (the **taker**). It ends nothing: the open offer stays live. The two may counter on for any number of rounds, each counter answering the one before it and signed by the side that did not sign that one, keeping the same two sides: a counter that names other sides, or comes from anyone else, counts for nothing. An `accepted` answer to a counter of the chain, by the side that did not sign it, is an allocation (above): a deal between the offerer and the taker on that counter's own terms, whole, with its quantity counted against the open offer's. The counters below hold along the chain (sides kept, an accept in a counter's causal past, two that cross both stand, an accept binds the counter that was live where it stands). A counter no one accepts binds nothing.
- **Each allocation is a deal of its own.** Its sides are the offerer and the taker; its terms are the offer's, with the quantity the allocation names (else the request's), or the counter's own, whole. Its author stops it by an `updates`, the other side by an `answers`. The open offer is settled by no one: its stage says only that it binds, and its `quantity` and `total` are what its allocations still bind together (folds.md).
- A counter ends an offer only between those two, and is signed by the side that did not sign the offer it answers. It keeps their sides: one whose `refs.from` or `refs.to` names another side counts for nothing (`not_live`); to change sides, stop the offer and make a new one. Only the author ends an open offer. Two counters that cross both stand. A counter that has an accept of its offer in its causal past counts for nothing (`not_live`): a binding deal changes only by a stop. An accept binds the live offer it answers; of all binding accepts the earliest in causal order binds, and two that do not see each other are a fork of the accepter's chain. When the two consent to the same thing (one offer, one quantity) the deal binds; when they consent to different terms, the one the other side's act names binds: a delivery, a payment it took, any counted block of the other side's in the deal that names that accept among its refs. With none named, or several, neither binds (folds.md).
- An accept beats a withdrawal unless the withdrawal is in the accept's causal past. An accept that reaches the offerer after `expires`, on the offerer's own clock, does not bind: the offerer answers it `declined`, and the fold reads that answer and no clock. The decline reaches back only as far as the window the offerer stated (authority.md, Ending): only an offer that carried `expires` can be declined this way. It voids nothing when a counted block of the offerer's side in the deal (a delivery, an acknowledged payment) has the accept in its causal past, or when the accept has a **receipt**: an `observe` of kind `receipt` that `derives_from` the accept and carries in `state.answer` the offerer's node's signed push answer (nodes.md), signed by a key that speaks for the offer's author, listing the accept as taken, dated no later than the offer's `expires`. **An offer may name a clock instead** (`terms.keepers`, blocks.md, A named clock): the accept is timely when `needed` keepers received it by `expires`, so an offerer who stays unreachable cannot void it. A receipt by anyone else counts for nothing. With no keepers named, the rule above stands.
- **An autonomous system's ask is a quote request** unless its grant names the act. A promise with no status signed by an autonomous system under a grant that does not give it the whole authority (its `scope.acts` does not name the promise exactly, a side it deals with is outside `scope.parties`, or invariant 23 is not met: authority.md) can be answered by an offer, but no accept of it binds its principal. Any other block under such a grant speaks for the autonomous system alone. Only the principal's own `answers` (one that speaks for the principal and is itself no quote request), or a grant that names the act, makes it bind; the autonomous system's own accept of it is a quote request too. A promise signed under no grant it acts under is its author's own word and binds its author alone. A grant that is not live, or is made for someone else, is no grant it acts under, so naming one binds no principal.

**Settling.** A promise settles by folding the counted blocks made `for` it: transfers, a transform, or the worker's own work. Only the right side's word counts: a giver's transfer speaks for `from`, a payment for the side that pays (`to`), a refund for the side that refunds (`from`). A giver's transfer counts when it moves ownership (folds.md, Ownership) of an instance of what the line moves (the thing, or one whose `refs.of` names it) that the giver owned where the transfer stands, each instance once: to a named receiver, once the receiver answers it `accepted` (things.md, Transfers); to an open receiver (an allocation's deal), on the orderer's signature. On a deal with a named receiver the giver's word alone settles nothing. A transfer or work with no stated amount is one whole (folds.md, One whole). Any other counted block is one the receiver answers `accepted`, or one a confirmer the schema's grounds name confirms (an id, or `circle#role` as the circle's fold of the confirming block's causal past says, within the cuts of authority.md, The cut). **What a payment covers is the payee's word.** `covers` has one meaning: the receiving side's own signed word on how much a payment, of money or of goods, counts toward what is owed to her. A counted payment with no `split` covers what it is, and more where the accepted answer of the side it is paid to says so in `state.covers` (money, or an amount where the price is in goods; `shape` otherwise, and only on an accepted answer). A word counts only against its signer's interest: **a payment never counts for less than was sent**, and `covers` never makes another owe more. The same holds for a delivery on a flat price: the payer's own accepted answer to it may say how much of the price it covers (`covers`, money), and that lowers only what she is owed back (`uncovered`). The payee's word can raise what counts as paid, never lower what is owed back. **A payment in goods** is the paying side's transfer of goods it owned, `for` this deal alone, with an `amount`, to the side paid, who accepted it. It counts toward a price in its own unit; toward a money price, or a price in another unit, it covers nothing unless her answer says what it covers (maize accepted as covering £20). A cash payment for a debt in goods likewise covers only what she says; a cash fallback exists only if signed (profiles/rulebooks.md). One payment may pay several promises by its `split`, the payer's word (things.md, Transfers). Of several such answers by the payee, the latest in causal order counts; answers that do not see each other count as no `covers`. Money in another currency than the price's covers nothing unless her answer says what it covers; nothing is converted. A refund is read the same way, by the word of the side it goes back to. **A side's `defeats` is a claim.** It erases nothing: the stage shows the block **disputed**, by that side, on the date it gives, and counts it as before; both words stay. Only the deal's referee changes what counts (Disputes). A side never disputes its own act; each stops instead. A sensor reading is a ground a confirmer may cite; it never settles alone. A return in goods is a second promise `for` the first.

**A release.** An `observe` of kind `release` (the base schema `release`): *we are settled; nothing more is owed between us, except these listed debts.* It binds only its signer, and lets go only of her own claims: the price owed to her as the `from` side, or what moves and what is owed back as the `to` side (Rothbard: only the creditor can let a debt go). With `refs.subject` (a deal, or a thing: "I let go of any claim to parcel 7") it lets go of that; without, of her claims on every deal with `refs.to` whose first promise it has in its causal past, except the deals in `state.except`, which stay open. A mutual release is one from each side; one side's leaves the other's claims standing. It moves no title. The fold `release_shown` shows it (folds.md).

**A message with a receipt.** An ordinary signed `observe` of kind `message` `for` the deal, with a receipt (blocks.md, A named clock) from the named clock (the promise's `keepers`; with none named there is no clock, and nothing starts "knowing"), starts "knowing". The stage lists each as `received_at` beside `uncovered`: `{ message, side, at }`, `at` null while there is no clock or no receipt. Money is owed back from when the condition failed, with no intent needed (Paid for, below). "Knowingly keeping it" exists only after a notice reached the keeper. No new kind and no new word is needed: the same receipt is what a refusal needs (profiles/refusals.md), one form for both.

**Paid for, never delivered.** Money paid `for` a deal is paid on that deal's condition: the payer's signed `refs.for` says so, and nothing fills in his silence. What it bought is what moved to him. When the deal is stopped onward, breached or over-allocated, the money that is not covered by what moved is still his, owed back, unless the terms say it is kept (`forfeit`). The stage reports it as `uncovered` (folds.md), a claim of the payer's shown as a fact, never a verdict. What moved is priced only by a signed per-unit price or a line's price. On a flat price for a whole, nothing is covered until the whole moves. A promise nobody paid for that goes unkept is a broken word: shown, never a debt. **Reliance is never a ground.** What someone spends relying on an unsigned word is their own risk. The date money was held from is a fact, and a referee may rule a sum for the wait; no rate is built in. **A premium buys the promise itself.** A payment `for` a conditional promise (an option, a cover, an iddir's dues) buys the promise: when the condition never happens, the promise was kept in full, because the seller stood ready. So a lapsed option owes nothing back for that reason alone, and a premium the terms mark as kept (`forfeit`) stays with the seller. The money is owed back when the promise paid for was broken (the seller refused when called) or never signed. A payment that is part of the price, or money held, says so. **Paid with ink counts for nothing:** the price is paid by the owed thing or by money, never by a fresh promise given in its place. **A payment that names no deal** (no `refs.for`) is shown as "not said what for" and counts toward no promise, even the only open one between the two: nothing guesses the payer's meaning.

**Claims.** A **claim** is a signed assertion that something is hers, not yet settled. It binds only her name to its truth. It becomes a settled fact only by the other side's signed word or a ruling: a judge both chose for a deal, the victim's judge for an attack (Disputes). A claim of harm and a claim to be first are claims. A right to be paid is not a claim, because the debtor's own signature settled it: it is **owed** (Owed, above). An ask by the receiving side `for` a past block, when nothing was promised first, is a claim. An unanswered claim or ask binds no one and is never a debt in any fold.

**Disputes** end one of two ways: the sides counter until both accept, or the deal's **referee** rules. The referee is a confirmer the deal's schema names in its grounds; both sides signed the schema, so its word binds both. **A judge named after the quarrel:** both sides may name one for a live deal, by a `grant` of kind `referee` one side signs naming her in `refs.to` and the deal (its first promise) in `refs.subject`, which the other side answers `accepted`; she then counts as a confirmer the grounds name, after them. **A side alone never judges its own deal** (invariant 13): a name in the grounds that is a side confirms nothing; nor does any block that speaks for a side (authority.md, Who a block speaks for); nor the holder of a role a side gave, in its own name or `as` its circle, by a grant the binding accept had not seen. **A referee both sides signed for counts even when tied to a side** (a role inside a side's circle, a member of it, an autonomous system a side runs): both chose that judge; the stage shows the tie (`referee_ties`, folds.md) and each reader weighs it. **Several referees:** a deal that names more than one says how many must agree (`grounds.needed`, a whole number from 1 to how many the grounds name), by Counts (folds.md). The deal is valid without it, and has no ruling until a number is signed (in the schema, which may say 1, or by a new deal); the same where a judge named later grew the names past the grounds' own. Each name's word on a block is its own: a block it `defeats` counts for nothing unless a `confirms` of its own has every such defeats in its causal past (it changed its ruling); a block it `confirms` counts. A block as many names as are needed say counts for nothing counts for nothing; one as many say counts counts, whatever a side says of it. **A backup referee:** with `grounds.wait` (a duration), the names are a chain, each the backup of the one before. The first rules; a later one's word counts once every name before it has said nothing on the block, and only the words it dated (`at`) at or after the wait has run from the previous name's own **receipt** of a side's dispute of the block: an `observe` of kind `receipt` that `derives_from` the dispute and carries, in `state.answer`, a push answer from that name's node, signed by a key that speaks for it, listing the dispute as taken (nodes.md). Of several receipts the earliest `at` counts. The wait runs from a time the earlier referee signed, so no side can backdate it and every reader reads the same turn. With no receipt the wait has not begun and only the names before rule. The referee has the last word on any block in the deal: what settles or breaks it (a delivery, a payment, work, the `defeats` that says a refrain was broken) and what binds it. An accept the referee defeats binds nothing; nor does an allocation on an open offer, or the request it answers (the bench is the offer's schema's between its offerer and that taker, with any judge both named for the offer or for the allocation's deal); and an answer it defeats (a receiver's accept of a delivery, a payee's answer with its `covers`) counts for nothing. With no referee named, or until it rules, a dispute changes no stage: the stage lists it (`disputed`) and each reader judges it. Anyone else can only propose. A referee's fee is an ordinary offer of the referee's. **A ruling never rests on silence alone.** A side who never answers is read as nothing. The referee gives only what the papers show; a missing deposit counts against a signer only as the term he signed says, and against a non-signer never. A default ruling over the papers' amount counts only up to the papers. **A ruling has an audience.** Every block states its `audience` (core.md, section 5), so a ruling is public or sealed to the circle that would act on it, as the deal chose. A ruling shown to no one else is a fact for the sides only.

**Who pays a confirmer.** A confirmer's word binds her to its truth, and who pays her is part of what a reader weighs. Every confirmer (an attester, a referee, a grader, a checker) may state `paid_by` (an id), `funded_by` (a patron's or circle's pledge that covers the fee) and `pay_depends_on_result` (true or false). It is one optional block of terms, read the same way everywhere, and a false statement is a broken word. Nothing is filled in. FoodOS drafts a judge's fee as fixed and paid by the sides as they sign; "the loser repays the winner" is a separate transfer between the sides, never the judge's income. *A judge's own cap* is a template, not a rule: "my ruling is a fact; if it is shown wrong to a judge we both name, I return my fee and owe nothing more." The fee is a conditioned transfer back to the payers on that second judge's signal. It never shields whoever acts by force on a wrong ruling (A claim of invasion). *A stake behind a claim* is a conditioned transfer too: a claimant (A claim of invasion) or a creditor's signal (profiles/refusals.md) may carry "if the referee rules my claim false, this of mine becomes yours", and the accused may stake back. Neither is ever a default.

**A ruling and a remark.** The ruling is the referee's `confirms` or `defeats`: it moves what counts. Her remark is her own `observe` beside it. It binds nobody and moves nothing. *Example:* she defeats the delivery of a short chest and writes beside it "short by two kilos". The first changes the stage; the second is read by whoever reads it.

**No term on this.** A referee asked about a point no signed term covers rules *no term on this* and says so, in a remark, and rules nothing: the blocks count as before. Only a clause both sides signed ("gaps by the trade's reading, as the referee finds it") lets her fill the gap, and that clause is a signed delegation, never a schema default. *Example:* a buyer disputes a cake as stale; the deal says nothing of freshness. She writes "no term on this" and the delivery stands. Had both signed the trade-reading clause, she could rule by it. **A referee reads ordinary words and adds none.** She reads the sides' signed words in their ordinary sense ("10 kg apples" means apples one can eat). Filling a point nobody wrote about is the delegation that needs a signed clause. A named glossary (profiles/rulebooks.md) governs where one exists.

**More on rulings.** *A ruling about a thing compares the two who named the judge:* "Achieng has the better claim than Wanjiru, by the judge both named". It says nothing against anyone else, so it is never a registry entry. *A ruling may name the rule it applied:* it may `derives_from` the rule's text (a thing), so readers see whether two judges who disagree used the same code. *A ruling's figure is "harm found", never a price or a worth:* a price is a signed exchange that happened. *A witness owes nothing:* a third party's silence counts against no one, and a referee may ask but never require. *A ruling shows whether the accused was told:* "notified: receipt at T" or "none shown", read from the same message with a receipt (A message with a receipt); in the claim fold (`invasion_shown`) each accused carries `notified`.

**The signer's word.** The `not_free` observe: A signer may say of a block she signed, *this is not my free word*, in an `observe` of kind `not_free` that names the block in `refs.subject`, with `state.reason`: `compelled` (signed under force), `not_me` (my key was taken, I did not sign it) or `could_not_understand` (only a person who can reason consents). With `compelled`, `state.threat` says what the threat was. "Compelled" means a physical attack on a person or property, or a threat of one that is overt or spoken, "palpable, immediate and direct" (Rothbard). A fear of what might happen counts for nothing, and a boss's order alone is no threat. A hard bargain, a refusal to deal, competition, a high price and scarcity are not force. A payment made to get force used on someone else (to have a rival's stall closed) is never a compelled payment: it is the payer's own act. It says force stood behind the signing. Whether that force was just is a claim for a judge (A claim of invasion), never a word the schema decides: a creditor who holds title until he is paid compels no one. **Regret is not a reason.** Haste, anger, drink, regret and a hard bargain are choices. The reasons stay these three, and a referee may weigh drink; the protocol does not. Only the block's own signer writes one that counts as hers; anyone else's is only their own observe. The `not_free` observe is the signer's: she may seal it to any audience she picks, it has no time limit, and she cannot take it back. She states the audience; nothing fills it in, so the other side sees it only if she names them. A named complaint can be read by the official it names and by no one else. It is a claim, never a verdict. The fold shows it beside the block (`not_free_shown`, folds.md) and nothing that counts changes by itself. The deal's referee may `defeats` the block on that ground, like any dispute. Nothing is ever unsigned, and the block carries no flag: the one holding the stick would read it before she signed. (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 12: a demand backed by a threat is the attack itself, not consent.)

**A claim of invasion.** Harm with no deal behind it has no referee both sides named, and "only a referee both sides named" would give the attacker a veto. So the one harmed names her own judge. A **claim of invasion** is an `observe` of kind `invasion`: the accused in `refs.to` (one or several), what was crossed in `refs.subject`, the evidence in `refs.derives_from`, what happened in `state.text`. Only a physical crossing into a person, place or thing is an invasion. Lost sales, a lower price or a true review cross nothing, so a claim naming no crossed thing shows `crossed: null`. **A refusal is never a harm:** a refusal to sell, hire, rent or admit crosses nothing, and a claim of harm can never name one (economic power is simply the right, under freedom, to refuse an exchange; Rothbard). She names a judge by a `grant` of kind `referee` (`refs.to` the judge, `refs.subject` the claim). The judge may be a circle: a ruling signed `as` the circle by a holder of `sign` is the circle's, and a member without `sign` rules for no one. An accused may name his own the same way, and may answer hers `accepted`; his silence is never an accept, and no rule settles doubt for either side. A judge rules by a `confirms` or `defeats` of the claim. A ruling may `derives_from` the evidence it rests on, and a `confirms` may carry `up_to` (money or an amount): the harm it finds. No fold moves anything because of it, and FoodBlock carries no punishment figure. The fold (`invasion_shown`, folds.md) shows as facts: the standing, whether any evidence is cited (`evidence` empty is "evidence: none"), each accused with the judges he named or answered accepted, and each ruling beside the judge who gave it and who named her. Nothing moves and no stage changes. A side never judges its own claim: the claimant's judge is not the claimant, and a ruling by a claimant, an accused or anyone who speaks for either counts for nothing (invariant 13).
- **Standing.** Only the harmed party may bring a claim, or one who holds her claim by her signed hand-over (a transfer that names the claim in `refs.what`, accepted by the new holder: it moves the standing, never money, because the accused signed nothing). The harmed party is the owner (or a holder of a share) of the thing crossed in the ownership fold where the claim stands, or the person crossed. The fold's `standing` is `harmed`, `holds` or `none`. A circle brings no claim for members who never signed it: opting out would treat silence as consent. A claim against a business names the person who acted, and the owner only if a signed grant or order shows he ordered it.
- **The actor and whoever caused it.** A claim may name the person who acted and any person who caused the act (`refs.to` lists each). The fold shows each accused and whether a signed order is among the evidence (`commanded`: shown, or none). A signed order is evidence, never a condition. Intent is not required: a careless crossing is still a crossing, and intent is the judge's to read. The means (the sprayer, the van) show with who held them then, read from the ownership fold as of the claim, never as the accused.
- **A border a stranger could see.** A claim against someone who never signed anything with the claimant counts the crossing of a border discernible in the world: a fence, a furrow, a pipe, a visible use. It never counts a border known only from a sealed record. The place card shows the border and the worked area apart (a fenced hillside planted only in its lower half). Sealing a record changes who may read it, never whether the use was open in the world.
- **Many victims, one claim.** Twelve stalls tainted by one smokehouse each sign a hand-over of their own claim of harm to one of them (a transfer that names the claim, above), who names one judge. It moves standing, not money, and nobody is bound by anyone else's act. A stranger's claim shows no standing; each handed-over claim shows standing per stall.
- **A claim says what it asks.** It may state in its own `state` (`asks`) whether it asks "stop" or "pay N". Data only. FoodBlock enforces neither.
- **Payment for harm is not leave to repeat.** A payment settles that harm only. Leave to go on is a separate signed word ("I allow smoke 5 to 8am, Monday to Saturday, until March, for £20 a month": a lease of a use over a place).
- **Collecting past a ruling is itself claimable.** Collection by force beyond a ruling's `up_to` is a crossing the other side may claim on, to a judge of his own.
- **Separate invaders, separate claims.** The fold never adds them up.
- **Wording.** A ruling alone moves nothing and harms no one. Whoever acts by force on a wrong ruling answers for it; a judge answers only for what she herself signed. The words are claim, ruling and fact. FoodBlock enforces nothing, and the three remedies stand (core.md, section 10). (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 12-13: the aggressor does not choose.)

**Worked examples** (no new rule).
- *A knowledge sale.* Wanjiku posts a signed claim ("the river road floods in April") and a commitment (privacy.md) of the sealed content. A trader's payment is a transfer conditioned on the opening being shown (`opened`, above). Whether the content was true is the deal's referee's to rule. The price is for being first; once read, it is anyone's.
- *A paper note.* Slips each carry a commitment of a long serial. The issuer's promise is to whoever shows the serial's opening. She clears each slip once, by her settling transfer. The holder needs no key; the record is never title.
- *An honest-producer bond.* A lab's reading shows a fault in a batch (a signed `reading`). The producer signs, with that reading in `derives_from`: *if I have not signed a notice by T, £2,000 of mine is yours.* It is a transfer of an envelope of cash to a holder, ownership to the buyer conditioned `without` her own `notice` signal on a named clock (things.md, Transfers; By a time). She cannot backdate the notice: it counts only if the clock received it by T.
- *A penalty paid to someone else.* A baker signs a transfer of an envelope of £20 to a burial fund, conditioned on a referee's `signal` that the bread was late. The fund is not a side, so the cafe gains nothing from claiming a breach. A promisor may pledge his property to anyone who accepts.
- *A soft term with a judge.* "Fresh bread" is the buyer's free veto unless the deal names who decides: a grader both named, signing a `signal` "fit" on the loaf. The promise's condition is that signal, read on the named clock.
- *A cover that names a judge.* A cover network's open offer says "harm between holders goes to this panel". Each holder signs it, so the accused already chose his judge before any quarrel.

**More worked examples** (no new rule; each is built from the shapes above, and the vectors marked "batch 8" run them).
- *A slice of each payment to the seller's lender* (Raiffeisen, Rochdale). A baker sells to a café on two orders. On each she hands a slice of the price (£50 of £200) to the lender who lent her the oven money, by a hand-over of what is owed (Owed). The café pays the slice to the lender and the rest to the baker. When the loan is repaid the lender hands the slice back, and the baker is owed all of it again. A lender can also hold a share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *The tab triangle.* The customer owes the shop, the shop owes the wholesaler. The shop hands what the customer owes it to the wholesaler (Owed), the customer pays the wholesaler once, and the wholesaler, who alone can let go of what is owed to her, signs a release of the shop's debt (A release). One payment, two debts settled, each by its owner's word.
- *An early-payment discount* ("3% off if paid in 10 days"). A line has no condition, so the deal carries the dearer price and the seller signs a rebate beside it: a transfer of an envelope of cash to the buyer, conditioned `within` `{ paid: deal }` by the 10th day on a named clock (By a time). Paid late, the cash stays hers and the price stays the dearer one.
- *A credit sale paid in part, then the buyer vanishes.* Title waits for the price (things.md, Transfers). The sale is stopped onward. The paid part is returned, kept (`forfeit`), or counted as rent for the days held, as the sides signed. If they signed nothing the reader shows "not said what for": nothing is kept for either, and the money is the payer's still (Paid for, never delivered).
- *A carrier keeps the advance once a milestone passes.* The shipper signs a transfer of the advance to the carrier conditioned on a `signal` from named watchers ("once past the harbour mouth"). Carriers expect to keep it, and the owed-back rule bends only by this signed term.
- *Paying in another money.* One word may name two monies ("dollar" in Zimbabwe), so the glossary names the exact coin or banknote (profiles/rulebooks.md). A payment in another money than the price's counts at a rate both signed, reported by a named changer at a named moment: write the price in the money to be paid and fix it by the changer's figure (Terms: a price fixed later). With no signed rate it counts for nothing toward the price (Settling).
- *Recourse when selling a claim.* The seller of what is owed signs a promise of her own to the buyer, conditioned `without` `{ paid: deal }` by the date: "if he has not paid by 1 April I pay it". The deal stays the debtor's; her recourse is hers.
- *Roll the short part forward.* Half an order came, and by her own word it covers £50 (Settling). The rest is owed back. To carry it onto the next order the mill refunds it and the café pays it again for the new deal, two signed transfers a payment rail may net so that no cash moves. No carry exists as a type.
- *A normal-error band as the standard of care.* A carrier signs a per-unit price with `band` "0.05": 96 kg of 100 arriving is inside the band, so the line is covered and priced at what came, and 90 is not.
- *Interim payouts that come back if the stake is not whole.* The partner pays out each month by an ordinary transfer. The sleeping partner has signed beforehand a transfer of that cash back to him, conditioned `without` `{ paid: the stake }` by the year's end (Addition 21's conditioned transfer).
- *A gift.* A transfer with no promise back reads as a gift: the hen is the niece's and nothing is owed. The giver may sign beside it that it is a gift, so a claim cannot be invented later; a price she invents afterwards is an offer the niece never answered (Answers).
- *The estate.* A debtor dies. The claim stays against what he held and pledged, and the stage shows it unsettled; nobody signs for it unless a named heir does. An heir who signs "I take his debt" makes a promise of her own, binding her from that block, and no heir's body or other property is bound (Death). The will names a widow, children and a brother (profiles/successor.md).
- *Terms I give you today.* A supplier signs an `observe` of kind `terms`: "I sell to Achieng on 14 days, up to 40,000, today". It binds her name to its truth, never to keep giving. It is no promise, so it opens no deal. (The attestation shape does not fit: it names a register.)
- *A reading over her own figures.* A stall signs, month by month, an `observe` with `amount` of her sales. A debt "repayable when monthly sales pass 80,000" is conditioned on her own signal with `op` `ge` and `value` 80,000 (Conditions). Only her own word counts as her figure.
- *A group buy that happens only if a floor is reached.* Each member signs a pledge (profiles/pledges.md) whose threshold is the floor, and the organiser's close binds the pledges that reach it. All or nothing is the pledge's shape.
- *Goods on trust through a grant.* A wholesaler lets a stallholder sell her lot: a grant naming the lot, the sale as the act, the buyers as parties, a limit. The lot stays hers until sold. Each sale is the stallholder's act under the grant and speaks for her. The takings are hers.
- *A sole right with an overriding commission.* The owner signs a refrain, "I sell this only through you until D", with a stake, and a conditioned transfer: "if I sell it another way, 5% of the price becomes yours". It binds only the owner.
- *A loan repaid by delivering goods at a stated price, as one promise.* The lender pays £100 now; the borrower promises 10 sacks at harvest at £10 each (a per-unit price paid in advance). There is no loan type. Money paid for goods not yet come is the payer's until they do (Paid for, never delivered).
- *A co-op's flat delivery price signed by its members.* The members sign the circle's rule that sets the price (circles.md, A price list for wrongs shows the pattern). Each delivery is the circle's promise `as` the circle. No member is bound by a price he did not sign.
- *Advances before the price is known.* A trader pays a herder £1 a year on a calf whose price will be read when it is sold. The payments fold as paid with no total and nothing due. When the reader signs the figure, they stand as paid toward it.
- *Payment follows a checker's certificate.* The price is fixed by a figure the depot checker signs, on a named clock. Paid short of her figure, the deal is not done; paid her figure with the maize moved, it is.
- *Held while a signal stands.* An open-account promise may say "what I owe at the due date is paid unless a signal by the named referee stands against me at that date": the payment is conditioned `without` that signal at the due date. When the signal ends, a new date is a new term both sign (A new date both sides signed). It is a term both signed, never a default.
- *Wages held back until the season ends.* The farm hands the envelope to a third party both named (a hold) and signs a transfer to the picker conditioned on a confirmer's signal that the season is over.
- *A pitch that returns to the market's owner.* When the trader took the pitch he signed a transfer of it back to the owner, conditioned on her signal that a ruling against him went unpaid. It is one owner's term; no other stallholder is bound, so there is no joint boycott.
- *Selling a market pitch.* The pitch is a thing, so the stallholder sells it by a transfer. The market still admits the buyer as a member by its own rule (circles.md, Membership): owning the pitch makes nobody a member, and the circle's admission is the circle's signed word, never the seller's.
- *A priced duty to tell.* A member who holds an unpaid deal for N days without writing a signal owes a set sum to the circle: his own transfer of an envelope to the circle, conditioned `without` his signal by the date on a named clock. The creditor and any holder of the debtor's money each sign it.
- *A pre-signed duress cap.* A grant to the driver with a visible cap (up to 1,500 in all, to the officer named) and the boss's promise to repay him plus his lost half-day. A hand-over inside the cap speaks for the boss, one past it for the driver alone, and the driver logs the stop as compelled (The signer's word).
- *A perishable dispute.* The deal says "either side may sell the lot at once; the proceeds are held by a named holder; a sample or photo signed by the receiver". The sale is each side's own act, the proceeds a hold, the photo an `observe` for a judge to read.
- *Two named judges who disagree.* The sides name a third before the quarrel in a signed term, or after it by a `referee` grant (A judge named after the quarrel). Nobody fills the third seat.
- *A bond graded for mistake, carelessness and deliberate wrong.* Three conditioned transfers, each with its own signal from the referee and its own sum. The sides signed the grades; the protocol reads none.
- *A voucher's watch window.* The person vouched for signs a standing promise to send his weekly orders to his vouchers until the vouch ends (profiles/vouches.md).
- *A state court as a chosen confirmer.* Both sides name a clerk or firm who signs "the court ruled X", `derives_from` the judgment's hash. It is a judge both chose, the ruling is a fact, and FoodOS takes no part in enforcement.
- *A swap of records.* Two wholesalers each show the other their closed deals' outcomes only while the other does. Each signs a grant to the other (any kind the sides name; the protocol reads none for it). The second names the first in `derives_from`, because a hash cannot name a later block, and the first's `scope.condition` says "while the grantee's grant to me is live". Only the principal's own node tests that condition (authority.md, The limit is the principal's fence). No shared form was needed.
- *A referee's standing offer.* A standing open offer (Skills are standing offers, profiles/tasks.md): "Thursdays at Kisumu fish market, KES 100 a claim, paper only", with the place, the hours and the fee. A deal names her in its grounds or by a `referee` grant, and she is a judge both chose.
- *An agent's grant with a reading condition.* "Go on if the Garissa Q2 reading is at least 170 on Tuesday; otherwise wait at Dhobley." The grant's `scope.condition` names the reading; the agent's node tests it on the principal's side (authority.md). The fold reads only that a condition is there.
- *A commitment over a list of facts only the receiver knows* (privacy.md, A commitment). The sender commits to the list; the receiver shows the opening; a condition `opened` fires. Caution: a list of few likely values is a weak secret. Anyone may guess and open it, so a commitment over a short list protects nothing. Use it only where the guesses are too many.
- *A road pact between two circles.* Each signs a refrain with a stake ("my members' lorries use no other road till D"), made to the other, and both name one judge. Each binds only its signers.
- *A deal that binds only once a named witness confirms.* The promise's condition is a `signal` from the holders of `circle#witness`. Before one signs it owes nothing; after, it binds.
- *A veto window on a co-holder's act.* The treasurer's deal stands unless two members say no within five days: it is conditioned `without` a `signal` `veto` by two holders of `circle#member`, deadline the fifth day, on a named clock (By a time). Nobody's silence is read as a yes: the deal stands because the term the circle signed says so.
- *A toss by a named caster.* Two claimants of one lamb sign conditioned transfers: heads, it stays with the first; tails, it goes to the second, each on a `signal` from the caster both named. The fold never breaks a tie by hash; the toss is a signed event the sides chose.
- *An open "take up to N".* "A night's firewood from my hedge, free; for more, ask me." A free open offer (a per-unit price of zero) with a quantity range: her allocations bind her, and one past the range is her own broken word, shown. The protocol caps nothing per taker; a cap per taker is her rule, declared under her schema, and a standing gift needs nothing from the taker.
- *A vouch for a new member* answers the new-key refuge (profiles/rekeys.md): a free key looks like a stranger, and a known member's vouch for it is her signed word with a cap (profiles/vouches.md, An introduction).
- *"No claim" by those who could claim.* Each person may bind himself; his signed word estops his own later claim, binds nobody who did not sign and creates no title. A brother signs a refrain over a named plot: "if I claim any part of it before it is next sold, £500 of mine becomes the co-op's." The heirs and the brother each sign as the mother sells two acres.


*A lot of pies, vouched.* Mo vouches "up to £100" to whoever holds the lot (`refs.to` the lot). It passes maker, shop, eater. The eater, its owner where Mo's payout stands, claims; a claim by someone who never held it is `not_holder` (folds.md, A pot).

**Checks answered** (no change needed). A note for ten loaves is one instance and the ownership fold moves a whole instance, so it is not split: the issuer signs smaller notes, or a note whose terms say "redeemable in any part". A one-sided band is a quantity range under a per-unit price (80 to 100 kg). "About N" is a band. An estimate and a count differ by the reading's `accuracy` and `coverage` (things.md, Truth), so a stock report can say which part was counted. A want with no price is a valid shape: a food bank's standing want, filled by a gift. A repeat's amount per occurrence is a signed amount ("£60 after each payday"); a share of a signed figure per occurrence ("5% of each later sale until 400 is back") is a price fixed later with a `fraction`, one figure per promise, so a share of each sale is one promise per sale. A thing or a deal in a kind and a unit no schema names is valid and binds like any other: no one approves an innovation. A grant may name the owner's own rulebook by hash in `refs.schema` ("these three suppliers, never above 12% over last month"), so a helper's AI reads the owner's own words. A lapsed option, a double sale of a named lot, an overbooked offer, a lead's signals, a lease that ends with a reading, all or nothing across eight owners, and "all current holders" are run by the vectors marked "batch 9".

**Late worked examples** (no new rule; each is built from the shapes above).
- *A lease that ends with a reading.* The tenant hands £2,000 to the landlord as earnest on day one. She signs two conditioned transfers of that envelope on one named clock: ownership to the landlord `without`, and back to herself `within`, a `signal` `soil` from the lab both named with `op` `ge` and `value` 2.0%. In range at the end, the earnest comes back. Out of range, or no reading by the deadline, it stays with the landlord, because nothing fills her silence.
- *A lease that says who owns the trees she plants.* A tree she plants is hers. The lease says one of two things, both signed: she takes them out at the end, or the landlord buys them at £X each, an offer he signs now. With neither said, they are hers and nothing is filled in. A landlord's credit for measured improvement is a price per unit fixed later: "£2 a tree standing at the end, counted by an assessor both named" (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *Selling an unpaid wage claim.* The employer owes a worker £400. She hands what is owed to a buyer for £350 (Owed). The employer's debt is unchanged and the buyer is paid. She owes no recourse unless she signs a line; silence means none.
- *A recruitment-fee loan repaid on each payday.* "After each payday I write `paid`, £60 of mine becomes the recruiter's, until £990." It is a standing promise whose occurrences only the worker starts, by her own `payday` observes (Terms: `repeat`; the side that charges cannot start a charge alone). A week with no pay starts nothing, so she is never in default in it. The recruiter ends it with her own stop at £990. It binds her name and one sum, with no services and no claim on her time, her papers or her body: the shape that cannot become bondage.
- *A price made under a decree.* A deal made under a price rule names it in `refs.under` (blocks.md already carries "the grant, rule or law it acts under"). A price card shows "made under a rule: <name>" and counts such deals apart.
- *A stored-goods note.* A holder's promise to whoever owns the note, for goods held (Bearer). The note may name the lot (sacks 101-140, bin 4). Then a second transfer of that lot by the holder is not the owner's word and counts for nothing (folds.md, Ownership), so a double sale shows on its face. Unnamed means same-kind goods and the holder's own count, and the card says which. A store note names what, through whom, when and where it comes back. A mixed silo held as shares lets a miller who sells the whole bin pass only his own part (things.md, Shares).
- *Door rules accepted by a tap at entry.* "No phones in the prep room until you leave" is a refrain the visitor signs by tapping, with the end of the visit as `until` and the owner as referee. It binds only her.
- *An emergency grant woken by a reading.* "If the cold room reads above 8C for 30 minutes, Otieno may spend up to 6,000 on the repairer, until Sunday": a grant whose `scope.condition` names the reading, with a visible limit and a named party (authority.md).
- *An owner's refrain not to overrule a helper.* The owner signs, with £20 behind it, that he will not override an act the helper took inside her grant. It binds only him.
- *A helper's adoption promise.* "Any new loaf you propose that sells 30 in two weeks: 10% of its takings for three months", with an earnest held by a third party. A share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *Staged lending in three tranches.* Three promises of the lender, each conditioned on a signed reading of stock sold. None is owed before its reading.
- *Wages paid off a ruling by a three-party deed.* The debtor, the employer (for a fee) and the creditor each sign their own part: the employer pays the creditor from what he owes the debtor, and the debtor owes the employer the fee. Each signs only what is his.
- *A pre-signed late fee on an "on demand" note.* "If I can't give the plate the day you ask, I owe you a second": a conditioned transfer on the missed due date (Relative time; By a time).
- *A lender's question to the earlier owners in a chain.* "Does anything of yours still stand on this oven?" Each earlier owner answers with a signed `observe`. Silence is nothing.
- *An open vouch paying a referee's fee for members' small claims.* The fee offer names the vouch as payer, and the tie shows (`referee_ties`).
- *A baseline soil reading signed at a sale.* The seller signs the reading with the sale. A later drift claim `derives_from` it.
- *A day pass on a code with no account.* The paper-note shape at a gate: the gate's promise is to whoever shows the code's opening.
- *What I see in your kitchen I tell no one.* An auditor, a co-packer or a cook signs a refrain with a stake: "£300 of mine if I do". A secret sauce held by three is three signed refrains. A third party who hears it is bound by nothing, because an idea is not property.
- *An exclusive pouring right at a festival.* The vendor signs a refrain not to pour another brand, with a stake. If he does, he broke his own word. The second brand did nothing wrong.
- *A drought clause.* A loan where "if the named gauge reads under 300 mm, 10,000 of what she owes is let go; 2,000 more is charged for this": a conditioned let-go (A release) and a conditioned charge, each on the gauge's signal.
- *Gleaning.* A farmer lets go of a field's leftovers, Saturday 6am to Sunday 6pm: a transfer to an open receiver with an expiry (things.md, Transfers). Whoever takes them is their first owner in the record.
- *Restitution in the wrongdoer's own produce.* After a ruling the wrongdoer signs "10 litres of milk each Friday for 40 weeks": a standing promise, his own act at work (core.md, section 10).
- *Two buyers of a lorry, each for half; two guarantors each for the whole.* Each buyer's price is half and no more. Each guarantor's vouch is for the whole, and one payment of the debt clears it: the second voucher's claim then shows no debt left ("covered once").
- *A permit note.* "A loaf any day this month while I have bread." It is honest while she never refuses a holder while she holds goods. The referee's question is "did she refuse while she held it?"
- *A refrain with a priced exit.* "No other cold truck on this road until 2029", with the operator's signed right to end it for 30,000. A reader sees "can be ended by: paying X".
- *Overbooking honestly.* An offer says "up to 3 taken for 2 baked; a bumped taker gets £2 and the next day's box", and offers 3. The fold shows `over_allocated` as a plain fact at once for the fourth taker; no stage reads "breached" (a promise is kept or broken when due, not when made: Hoppe, fraud only at redemption). The bump is a pre-signed conditioned transfer to each taker, `without` a deed that a box reached her by the named clock. Served, the bump stays the baker's. Unserved, it is the taker's. Taking a fourth against a printed 3 is the false word.
- *An option.* A premium paid `for` an option buys the promise (Paid for, never delivered). Lapsed with no kept term, nothing is owed back; with `forfeit` it stays with the seller; refused when called, it is owed back.
- *All or nothing across many owners.* Each of eight owners signs "if all eight of us have signed, my strip becomes the co-op's", a transfer conditioned on a `signal` `in` with `signers` 8 from the eight named owners. Nobody is bound until all are. That is the premise's answer to the hold-out, with no taking. The deals do not wait on each other, so there is no cycle.
- *Pools priced by class.* See profiles/pools.md.
- *A revolving line.* A lender signs a standing offer to lend up to N (`terms.backing`, the schema `loan`); each draw is her transfer `for` it, accepted by the borrower, and interest is owed only on what is drawn. A draw past the limit counts for nothing against it (folds.md, A pot). The lender may stop new draws any day, and what is drawn stays drawn and owed.
- *A watched loan.* The call-in is a step: the repayment falls due a wait after a named watcher's `observe`, as an event repeat whose `by` names the watcher (Terms: `repeat`), on the promise's named clock. *Not used for the oven*, or *the voucher no longer enough: replace in 28 days or the loan falls due*, starts the step; it never moves a signed `pay_by`.
- *A loan the lender re-signs each quarter.* A chain of short offers, each with its own `expires`. A lapse is silence: the view says expired and nothing more, and the lender owes no next quarter.
- *Past due is not breached.* After `pay_by` with nothing paid the stage still reads underway with its `due` date. Breached comes only from a refrain's signed terms or a referee's ruling. Late is the normal case.
- *A top-up when what is pledged falls.* The duty is the borrower's own promise conditioned on a `signal` by the assessor both named, with `op` `lt` and the figure both signed (*sacks 10% below the loan: add four, or the rest falls due*). The reading condition holds only on that reader's word.
- *A sea loan.* One contract, one named clock, both ways: the master's conditioned transfer of a forfeit to the lender `without` the captain's signal *loaded* by T, and the repayment conditioned `within` the signal *arrived* by T2. A ship that does not arrive leaves the repayment unmet, and a pledge written with the same condition fails with it.
- *A sale's finality is a signed term.* A sale that says nothing about taking back is final. *Redeemable at P until T* is the seller's separate promise to buy back, conditioned `within` the buyer's signal by T. Nothing is filled in.
- *A crop pledged twice.* The earlier conditioned transfer wins, sealed or not: nobody passes on more than they had. *Nothing else is pledged on this* is the borrower's signed word, and a false one is his lie, shown.
- *A "shall" binds nobody.* A duty in a group's rules binds only if someone signs it with a stake: *if I sign a loan with no inspection in 90 days, £X of mine is the bank's*, a conditioned transfer `without` the inspector's signal by the date on a named clock.
- *Paying a lender through a third party.* A payment by the debtor's granted agent speaks for the debtor (authority.md), and the payee's accepted answer covers the old deal.
- *No credit-limit field and no group field.* The limit is whatever she signs next, and nothing in a block reads a person by a group she belongs to: a one-off case has no class.
- *A bag is a bag she defines.* In Malawi the bag has eleven sizes, so the offer defines it: *bag = 50 kg on the buyer's scale* (profiles/rulebooks.md, A unit carries its measure).
- *A rebate by purchases.* The circle's promise to each buyer under the schema `rebate`: her settled purchases times a figure the bookkeeper signs, `derives_from` naming the transfers. With no figure the stage shows "figure not signed" and nothing is owed. It is a promise of its own, never a term of each sale; the order of payment (interest first) is as signed. Dues paid from the rebate are a conditioned transfer she signs.
- *An officer's bond.* The officer's security is his own shares, with interest, and a pledge he still owns.
- *A finder's reward* is the owner's open offer: finding never moves title. *A standing reward* where each of k signers pays x/k and non-signers pay nothing is k promises.
- *The accused speaks first.* The accused signs an `observe` (*my goats ate 40 kg of Mo's maize*) and an offered transfer; the claim is then his own word.
- *A perishable-evidence panel with a two-day window.* The panel and its window are in the deal's schema; a reading signed inside the window is evidence for a judge.
- *A lease's last round.* The referee for the end is named in the lease, the deposit sits with a holder, and goods left after the ruling go to the holder to store. Her body is never touched.
- *Notice then proceed on a shared oven.* The co-owner's notice is a message with a receipt (A message with a receipt); the wait he promises before he proceeds is his own refrain with a stake.
- *"Paid" sent to the same audience as "owed".* The creditor's optional signed promise to say so, to the audience she told of the debt.
- *A remedy chosen at signing.* *If I break it, tell our circle* is a term the signer writes: telling the circle is the remedy, and the circle is the audience she named.
- *A pitch or route share with entry and exit terms.* A share of the pitch (things.md, Shares) with a transfer back conditioned on the exit term both signed.
- *A grader picking one of the offer's lines.* The grader's `grade` observe names a line in `state.line`; the offer's `lines` carry each line's price.
- *A rent-to-own barrow.* Title waits for the price (things.md, Transfers, Title until paid), with the holding moved now.
- *A livestock lease with half the calves.* Calves are produce, and the owner of the means is their first owner (things.md, Produce of a thing); the lease's signed term hands half to the keeper.
- *The village cow.* *Milk and calves are hers from day one* is a signed term, because produce is the owner's first unless a signed term says otherwise (things.md, Produce of a thing).
- *A pool pays the lender if the borrower dies.* The pool's payout to the lender is conditioned on its confirmer's signal that the borrower died (profiles/pools.md); it binds the pool only.
- *Wages owed only if the quarter shows a surplus.* The wage is conditioned on a `signal` by the bookkeeper with `op` `ge` and the surplus figure both signed.

**Many parties.** A promise is between two sides. A group buy is the circle's promise to the seller, plus each member's promise to the circle.

**Two people on one side.** A signed word binds only its signer, so nobody owes for another's word. A side is one id. Two who share a side each sign their own promise saying their part, "the whole" or a share, with no default; or they act as a circle under its rule (circles.md). *Two buyers of one lorry:* each signs a promise to the seller for half. *Two guarantors:* each signs a vouch for the whole; one payment clears all, and the fold shows it covered once (profiles/vouches.md).

**No partnership type.** A signature can do only three things with property: hand it over (a stake), let another act for you (a grant), or put it behind another's debt (a vouch). Every partnership is a choice of those, plus the split. *A baker's pot:* two stakes (a transfer each into the pot), mutual grants with a cap (each may act for the other up to N), and, if they choose, mutual vouches. A profit split is a share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later). Who bears a loss follows who owns the stock (things.md, Ownership), unless a signed term moves it. "Partnership", "commenda" or "loan" is never a field or a fold result: the kind is a reading of the signed terms.

**Taking an act as yours afterwards.** An act outside a grant speaks only for the agent, and for a promise it is a quote request until the principal's own `answers` makes it bind (An autonomous system's ask). That is the rule for taking an act: a person's later consent is her consent, it binds from when she gives it and never backwards, and nobody's word removes another's. Her answer is her own block, so the earlier blocks stay as they were. The agent stays bound by what he signed unless the one he dealt with lets him go (A release: only the one owed can). A block speaks for one party (folds.md, invariant 31), so "both bound" is two blocks, his and hers, never one.

**Death.** Only a signed word binds, and only its signer. A dead person signs nothing more, and nothing fills the silence. A promise's right to be paid stays against what the promisor pledged and held, and the fold shows it unsettled (an estate: "owed by one who can no longer sign"). What passes on death passes only by the dead person's own conditioned transfers signed while alive (things.md, Transfers; the will in profiles/successor.md, naming a widow, children and a brother). No heir's body or own property is bound. An heir who signs "I take this debt" makes a new promise of their own, binding them from that block. Which comes first, a creditor's right or an heir's conditioned gift, the protocol never decides: the deal's referee reads the signed words. Grants end at death (Ending). A will fires only on a true condition: a condition that reads a `close` reads whether that close counts, by the successor profile's own test (profiles/successor.md, When a close counts).

## Invariants

11. What is bound from an offer is counted against what is offered; what its offerer's own allocations bind past it is shown at once as over-allocated and is her broken word only when the taker is due and neither served nor paid the bump she signed beforehand, shown, never erased. A taker who paid is owed back what he paid for what was never allocated (Settling); a taker who paid nothing sees the broken word and no debt.
12. A block counts in a deal only if it speaks for a side (folds.md, invariant 31).
13. A promise is settled only by blocks the receiving side accepts or the grounds confirm, and that the deal's referee has not defeated: only a referee named in advance, or named by both sides after, changes the stage, and as many of its names as are needed. A side's defeats is a dispute, shown, never a verdict, and a side never rules, by any name or hand.
14. An unanswered ask or claim binds no one.
15. `answers` is the only consent to a deal, and it consents to the terms of the block it names: a promise's terms are fixed once it is written.
22. A background ask never binds before the person's yes: an autonomous system's ask is a quote request unless its grant names the act.
