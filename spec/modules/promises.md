# Promises

A promise moves a noun from one side to the other. Sides and what moves are in `refs`; `terms` and `what` (a description) are in `state`. The stage fold reads it (folds.md, A promise's stage).

**What a promise is.** A promise is a transfer of title on a condition: *if this happens, this of mine becomes yours, and this person settles it.* Three signed words make title wait on a condition: a **promise** (a kind, a quantity, a price and terms; where it names an instance of its signer's, it holds it, folds.md, What is left); a **transfer with `state.condition`** (one thing or one amount, moved when the condition has held, with no further word of the giver); a **transfer to a named receiver** (its one condition is her yes). Every other block (an answer, a receipt, a signal, a stop, a ruling) says whether a condition was met; none states one.

**When title passes.** A thing or an amount passes where its owner's draw lands (folds.md, Lands): it stands, the receiver's yes is in its causal past or answers it, and its condition has held there. Not at the accept: an accept fixes the terms by which later blocks `for` the deal are read, and, where the offer is the draw, it lands or is freed exactly where that accept binds or does not.

**What binding does, when no body is bound.** A deal binds when an answer to a standing word stands (Who may bind). It gives no one a right to an act of another. It does three things, all about title: it fixes the terms by which the blocks made `for` it count; it is the condition on which anything handed over `for` it is held, so what was paid for a deal that never came is still the payer's (`uncovered`); and it makes true the conditions others hang on it. A promise binds its signer's name and what she pledged, never her body: a side may always stop onward (Answers). A signed word never gives anyone a right to use force on the signer's body; only her own attack, never her word, ends that. Trade, loan, wage, deposit, warranty, stake and guarantee are all this shape.

**A ruling, and finality.** A bench name's word changes which blocks of the deal count toward its stage and what is to come; it reaches a draw only where it stands in that draw's causal past, so a thing that landed and stood stays where it is. **A sale that states no taking back is final:** a taking back is a new promise of the one who takes.

**Final.** A sale that states no taking back is final: a taking back is a new promise of the one who takes.

Ground: title moves only by its owner's word, on the condition she stated before anyone relied on it; a body was never acquired, so no word hands it on (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19; Kinsella, revising Rothbard).

**A promise that names a thing.** Where a promise or a transfer names an instance of its signer's and a receiver, it holds that instance from its signer's word, and lands or is freed as folds.md, What is left, says: by the same rule that says whether her yes binds. A yes to an order holds nothing.

**Sides.** `refs.from` gives; `refs.to` receives, each one id (a set is refused, `shape`). Either may be left out, which leaves it open: an offer open to anyone, or a want (an ask with `from` open). A promise signed by `from` is an **offer**; signed by `to`, an **ask**. What comes back flows the other way and is read from the transfers.
- **Only a side signs.** A promise is signed by a side: its author, the circle it signs `as`, or the grantor of the grant it acts under (authority.md, Who a block speaks for). Otherwise it speaks for no side and the fold counts it for nothing (`not_a_side`). The check never refuses it, because a side may name a thing whose owner the promise does not rest on (Bearer): only the fold can say (core.md, invariant 4). An answer or stop is signed by a side of its deal in the same way. What someone without a key said they would do is the receiver's own `observe` (kind `message`), never a promise.
- **Bearer.** `refs.to` may name a thing: whoever owns it is the receiving side, read where each block stands (the ownership fold of its causal past), never "now". The holder's accept binds for good, even after the note moves on. A thing with no owner has no one to answer.
- **What a deal names a side to receive.** Where the other side's word names an instance of hers, it is held. Where it names a kind or an amount, nothing is held for it: it is the other side's signed word, and the record shows it, what came, and when.
- **Handing on a receiving place.** The side a deal names to receive may hand that place, or a part of it, to another by a transfer that names the deal, which the other accepts. It is a draw on his own place, counted along his line, so one place goes once; it holds nothing against the other side. It reaches the other side only by her own word: her promise named a bearer, or she answers the hand-over `accepted`. Otherwise what she sends to the side her promise names counts, and that side is shown keeping it for the new one.
- **The note.** The note is that receiving place as it passes from one holder to the next (a tab handed on). A payment counts toward whoever owned the note where the payment stands. That owner's answer counts it wherever the answer stands. Where the note moved after the payment, its old owner is shown keeping that amount for the new one. The stage shows these in `to_come` and `to_come_on` (folds.md, A promise's stage).
- **Where to pay me.** A person's own signed word of where what is paid to her goes is an `observe` of kind `account` (the base schema `account`). An account word states `destinations: [{ destination, by }]`: each `destination` a place in the payee's own words (an account number, a wallet, an address), and `by` the signers whose word that a payment came there counts. It names places for its signer alone, and a block signed `under` a grant, or by anyone else, names none. An account word signed `as` a circle counts for the circle where a row whose `scope.types` names `observe.account` holds for it, or by every owner together (circles.md, A circle's account word). No one is paid by another's word, and a place in a message, a deal or an invoice is not this word. Of one signer's account words the head is the one no other of hers has in its causal past. A new word replaces the old, and two heads that do not see each other name none (nothing fills the choice in); a payment made to an older place stays what it was. A head that lists no place ends the account. A word that a payment came may name, in `refs.derives_from`, the account word of the one it speaks for, and state in `state.destination` one place that word lists. It then says the payment reached that place. A word that names none says only that it came. A pointer to a word that is not the payee's, or is not her head where it is signed, or a place that word does not list, names no place. A `received` by a signer her account word names in `by` counts toward her price only where it names that account word and the place. The door's `account_of(who, blocks)` reads it: `{ who, destinations, hash, at }`, or null.
- **Who it is for.** `refs.for_whom`: a person, a circle, or open, written as a commitment (privacy.md, A commitment). It reaches the other side only when it changes their work, and only as much as they need (`terms.needs`).
- **To yourself.** A promise whose `refs.from` and `refs.to` both name its author is accepted when made. The top of a plan is one.

**What moves** is `refs.what`: a thing (a kind in an offer or ask, an instance once known), a share, a record, or a circle. Time and a job are not things: `state.what` describes them under a schema and `terms.quantity` says how much. A counter that names no `refs.what` moves what the first promise names.

**The deal** is named by its first promise, one with no `refs.answers`. Every later block in the deal names it in `refs.for`. An answer or counter whose `refs.for` is absent or names another block than the deal's first promise is refused (`deal_not_named`); that is the one answered when it has no `answers`, else the `for` of the one answered. A first promise may carry `refs.for` for what it serves (a sub-task's parent); it starts a new deal. **An allocation (Who may bind) starts a deal of its own:** it names the open offer in `refs.for`, but the blocks that settle it name the allocation, so no other taker's payment counts toward it. A buyer's order that names an instance is a first promise like any other, and the seller's transfer `for` it is his yes (things.md, A spot exchange).

**Terms:** `{ price, quantity, lines, pay_by, deliver_by, until, repeat, condition, expires, band, needs, zone, clocks, forfeit, backing, conciliation, fee_back, waits_on, in_hand, step, stoppable }`, and any term the promise's schema declares.
- **No term has a floor.** A price of one penny or a quantity of one egg is a deal; no schema sets a smallest size.
- `price` is an amount ("6 sacks" with `of` the maize kind), an amount per unit (`{ amount, per }`, `per` an amount), or a method that fixes it later (below). The unit carries its measure (a glossary entry, profiles/rulebooks.md): a bare noun is not a unit. A block `for` the deal counts toward the price only as Settling says; nothing is converted between kinds. Interest in kind is a plain extra quantity of the same good (5 sacks lent, 6 repaid), never a rate. A swap is one deal, its legs in sequence: only goods already held by a third party settle both at once. Under a per-unit price, `quantity` may be a range `{ min, max }` and the answer's `terms.quantity` names the quantity chosen; the quantity must be in `per`'s unit (nothing is converted). A flat price (no `per`) is the total for the whole promise, or for its line inside `lines`. An ask with no price asks for one. A price is never below zero (`shape`); paying someone to take a thing is a job the giver buys, a promise the other way.
- **Multiply, never divide.** Let P be what counts as paid toward a price, in its unit. A flat price A is paid when P is at least A. A price `{ amount: a, per: p }` at quantity q in `per`'s unit (under `band`, what was accepted inside it) is paid when P times p's value is at least a's value times q's value. A price on `lines` is paid when P is at least the exact sum of its lines' prices. No fold rounds, and no test divides. A `per` of zero, a quantity not in `per`'s unit, or a figure not yet fixed has no total, and such a price is never paid. Where a total is finer than anything payable, only the payee's word closes the gap: her `covers`, her signal, or the `step` her price names.
- `step`: under a price, the least payable amount its signers take, an amount in the price's unit. The total is the least whole number of steps at or above the exact figure. With no `step`, the total is exact.
- **A price fixed later.** `price` may name its method instead of a number: `{ figure, by, signers, deadline, clocks }`, and optionally `fraction`, `min`, `max`. The figure is an `observe` of kind `figure`, `for` the deal, with `state.amount`, signed by one of the readers `by` names (ids: a market's reader on delivery day, the seller, the buyer, a partner's account of profit), received by `deadline` on the named clock `clocks` (blocks.md, A named clock). It counts only inside `min` and `max` when they are named, and nothing is cut down to them. The price is fixed when `signers` different readers have each signed the same figure and no other figure reached that many; a reader who signs two different figures counts for neither. It is the figure, or `fraction` of it (exact decimal text from 0 to 1), exact. Until then the stage reports `price_fixed` false, the total is null and the price is never paid. Nothing is filled in: with no figure by the window's end the deal shows it unfixed, and only the sides or the bench resolve it. A late figure never counts. It is on the whole promise, never on a line. A reader's figure may cite a market-price reading (the base schema `price_reading`, patterns/attestations.md) in `derives_from`.
- `lines`: `[{ id, what, quantity, price }]`, one order accepted at once. A line's quantity is an amount, never a range (`lines`). Each `id` is non-empty text, unique in the promise; blocks for one line name it (`state.line`). `refs.what` equals the set of the lines' `what`. With `lines` there is no top-level `price` or `quantity`. Every line's price is of one kind (`lines`).
- `deliver_by` is the time by which the goods are to arrive. The three dates `deliver_by`, `until` and `expires` are plain signed times, read as written: a value that is not a valid time is no date, and nothing is read into it.
- `until` is a return or end date; `expires` ends an unanswered offer or ask. With no `expires` it lasts until its author stops it.
- `pay_by` is when the price falls due. The stage reports `due` beside `paid_at`, when the price was paid in full. **A new date both sides signed.** A term binds only as signed, so changing it needs both signers, and silence moves nothing. A side writes an `observe` of kind `message` `for` the deal with a `pay_by`; the other side answers it `accepted` (an `observe` that `answers` it). The stage lists the agreed dates in order (`moved`) beside the original `due`, which stays: nothing is edited. A date only one side signed moves nothing and shows as that side's word only. The view reports which dates passed before the price was paid (`missed`). A late payment after an agreed move is a different fact from a miss with no agreement. A proposal that asks for time may cite in `derives_from` the debtor's own unpaid claim on her client (a bounced cheque); the stage names it beside the move, and the other side's yes is still what moves the date. A standing promise is due and paid by occurrence: a payment names its occurrence (`state.occurrence`: its start time, or the hash of the block that started it), and the stage reports per occurrence when it fell due, what was paid and when in full (`occurrences`, folds.md). These are facts; whether a payment was late is each reader's judgement.
- `waits_on`: `goods` (the price is due only once the receiver's `accepted` answer to the delivery stands) or `price` (what moves is due only once the payee's signal stands). Left out, nothing is filled in. An order may say "cash" by `waits_on: price`; without it, it is a credit order and her read-back says so.
- **The payee's signal** is a counted `observe` of kind `paid` that speaks for the payee, `refs.subject` the deal (`state.line` or `state.occurrence` where it is for one): her word that the price is paid.
- **Due.** What a deal's terms say a side hands over is due where the block that performs it has in its causal past the binding accept, standing; the deal's condition, held; and what `waits_on` names, standing. A payment is of no more than the price's total, a delivery of no more than the quantity chosen. No node adds up payments to answer it. `pay_by` and `deliver_by` are latest times, never earliest. `missed` stays as the view's name.
- `band` is catch weight: under a per-unit price, how far what is delivered may fall from the quantity chosen, either way, as a fraction of it (decimal 0 to 1, such as `"0.05"`; `shape` otherwise). What the receiver accepted inside the band fills the line and is what is priced (folds.md, A promise's stage). Ignored beside any other price.
- `repeat` is an RFC 5545 RRULE value (`FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU`; markets on a 4-, 5- or 8-day week are `FREQ=DAILY;INTERVAL=4`, `INTERVAL=5`, `INTERVAL=8`): parts `KEY=VALUE`, exactly one `FREQ`; another calendar may come first by RFC 7529 `RSCALE`. It counts from the start of `state.when`, read in `terms.zone`, so a calendar repeat carries `state.when` and `terms.zone` (`shape`). An occurrence is named by its start time. A repeat may follow an event instead: `{ after, by, wait }` (`protocol.json`, `repeat`) makes an occurrence each time an `observe` of kind `after` is written `for` the promise by a signer `by` names (ids), due `wait` after the named clock's receipt of it (Relative time), named by the observe's hash. **The side that charges cannot start a charge alone.** A payer who signed "each crossing the gate sensor records" consented to that count, not to the payee's own word. Only the names in `by` start occurrences (a side counts only if it names itself). With no `by`, nothing starts one: nothing fills the sides' silence. The protocol checks the shape (`grammar.rrule_part`, `repeat.freq`); a reader works out the occurrences.
- **The calendar forms.** Four everyday repeats have one spelling each, which every core and the oracle count alike (the vectors, `calendar:`), and nothing else spells them: *every weekday* is `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO,TU,WE,TH,FR`; *every Friday* (any one day) is `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=FR`; *every month on day N* is `FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=N` (N from 1 to 31, or -1 for the last day, -2 the one before); *the nth weekday of the month* is `FREQ=MONTHLY;BYDAY=nXX` (n from 1 to 5, or -1 for the last, -2 the second to last; XX one of MO TU WE TH FR SA SU). They are the same grammar as every other repeat (`FREQ`, `INTERVAL`, `BYDAY`, `BYMONTHDAY`, `BYSETPOS`, `COUNT`, `UNTIL`, `WKST`), so "every second Friday" is `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=FR`. The rules of counting are these. (1) **The wall clock decides.** The start's time is read in `terms.zone`, and the day, the weekday and the month of every occurrence are the zone's, so Friday 08:00 in Auckland is Thursday 19:00 UTC; the wall time keeps through a change of clocks and the UTC time moves. (2) **A day that does not exist gives no occurrence that month**: day 31 skips April, day 30 and 29 skip February (29 comes in a leap year), and a fifth Friday skips the months with four. Nothing falls on the nearest day. (3) **The start is counted only if the rule names it**: a monthly repeat on day 1 started on the 15th first falls on the 1st after; one on day 15 started on the 15th falls on the start itself. (4) **A week starts on Monday** unless `WKST` names another day (`MO` to `SU`); it matters only with `INTERVAL` above 1 and several days (`INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=TU,SU` is Tuesday and the Sunday that ends that week, under `WKST=SU` the Sunday that starts the next). (5) **`COUNT` counts from the start**, not from the window a reader asks about; `UNTIL` ends it, itself included. (6) A repeat the reader cannot read is refused (`repeat_occurrences`, an argument error): a number before the day in a weekly repeat, day 0, a weekday or `WKST` that is not one of the seven, a part it does not know. It never guesses.
- `clocks` is the offer's named clock (blocks.md, A named clock): `{ names, needed }`, `needed` when there is more than one name (`shape` otherwise). An accept is in time on it as Who may bind says (*when*), and an event `when` or `repeat` runs its wait from their receipt.
- `forfeit` is an amount of the price's kind: the part of what the payer paid that she signed over on her own stop. It stays with the payee where an onward stop that speaks for the paying side is among the first onward stops of the deal and none that speaks for the payee is. In every other ending it is the payer's still. A payer who never comes signs nothing, so what is put up against a no-show is a bond (things.md, The caterer's bond).
- `backing` is an amount ("£800", "up to 2 heifers"): the most her word under this promise comes to. It holds nothing; a thing put behind the promise is a thing handed to a holder (things.md, Transfers), up to which the pot (folds.md, A pot) counts payouts. "I stand behind her for up to N" is a promise with `backing` N.
- `conciliation` is `true`, and `fee_back` a decimal from 0 to 1 (`shape` otherwise; a `fee_back` with no `conciliation` is `shape` too): the terms of a judge's offer (Rulings). `conciliation` is her word that she tries to settle the sides before she rules; `fee_back` is the part of her fee she gives back if they settle. Neither is read on any other promise.
- `in_hand: true` on an order is her word that the goods are in her hand: the order, where it is in a block's causal past, counts that block toward what moves to her, for no more than it states. An order that does not say so counts nothing toward what came. No order counts toward a price.
- `stoppable: true` on an offer says ahead that her stop ends what of the deal has not come (The rest of a deal; authority.md, The stop of a grant). Left out, her stop ends none of it.
- `zone` is shaped like an IANA time zone name (`grammar.zone`: the shape is checked, never a list): the zone a calendar repeat is read in. It is read by the IANA time zone database, release 2026e, so that strangers' computers count the same wall clock (a time the clocks skip falls as RFC 5545 puts it, by the offset before the skip; one they repeat is its first). A calendar repeat with no `zone` is refused (`shape`): the protocol fills in no zone, and a block so refused is read by no fold, so it has no occurrences.
- `condition` is data under a schema: what must be true (Conditions). The schema's grounds name who confirms it and who settles a dispute.
- `needs`: what the work must meet, from a schema, never a name.
- **A schema may add terms** (its `state.terms`, `{ name: hint }`), as a closed block carries the fields its schema declares (core.md, section 7); any other term is refused (`undeclared_field`), and a checker with no schema store does not judge them. No fold reads a term it does not know: it binds no one and moves nothing.

**A promise not to do.** A promise not to do names no `refs.what`; `state.what` says what its signer will not do and `terms.until` until when. It binds its signer's name. What stands behind it is her transfer `for` it, conditioned `{ ruled: <the promise>, by: <a judge both named>, awards: <the other side> }`. That transfer lands where the judge rules for the other side, whatever stop came before. It moves nothing itself, so it is never **done**; once the reader's clock is past its `until`, the view says **elapsed** (folds.md, Views), a fact of its term, never that it was kept. *Example:* Ben promises Leila's café no work for a rival café until 30 June; Leila accepts. Ben transfers an envelope of £500 `for` that promise, conditioned on `ruled` by Jo, awarding Leila; Leila accepts. Ben stops onward on day 2. On 10 July Jo rules and awards the envelope to Leila: it lands with her. If Jo never rules, the envelope stays held, and Leila's own decline of it frees it (folds.md, Freed).

**Conditions.** A condition holds or not on the blocks in the causal past of the block it is read for, and a time in it only on the clock its signers named (blocks.md, Time). It holds, does not hold, or is **not read**. The shapes are these (`protocol.json`, `condition_shapes`; door `condition_holds`; a shape written wrong does not hold, and a wrapper written wrong is refused, `shape`). Any other shape is data for its schema's readers (null): a rain index read before the rain is known is not read, not false.
- `{ settled: <first promise> }`: that promise is done.
- `{ accepted: <first promise> }`: that deal binds: its stage is accepted or further (underway, done, reversed) or stopped onward, read from its promise fold. A promise so conditioned binds on its own accept, and what it names is due only once that deal binds (Due). A block the reader does not hold, or a cycle back to a deal being read, holds for nothing.
- `{ deed: <block> }`: that block is held and counts.
- `{ paid: <first promise> }`: that deal binds and its price is paid in full, read from its promise fold. It is the price side of a deal, so title may wait for the price without waiting for the goods. (A `settled` of the same deal would wait for the title that waits for it, and neither would move.)
- `{ stopped: <first promise>, side: "from" | "to" }`: an onward stop that speaks for that side is among the deal's first onward stops, and none that speaks for the other side is. Read from its promise fold. (A stake behind a worker's word to stay.)
- `{ closed: <id> }`: holds for a block where a key grant that leaves no voice of that id's list stands in its causal past. A transfer on this condition by that id is a will (identity.md, A will): while she can sign it holds nothing; an accept of it before the close is a request; she may end it until the close. At the close her wills take in the order of her line, after every other draw of hers, whatever her close saw, and a will whose thing is gone takes nothing. There is no "everything else".
- `{ ruled: <deal>, by: <judge>, awards: <person> }`: holds for a block where the judge's head ruling on the deal, in that block's causal past, awards something to that person. Of her rulings on one deal the head is the one no other of hers in that past has in its causal past. A later ruling of hers that has seen it is a newer word in its place: an end of the earlier only for blocks that had not used it (blocks.md, The cut). The ruling counts only if its key speaks for her. With no time stated it has no other end; wrapped `within` a deadline on a clock, it ends there, and what it held is freed (folds.md, Freed).
- `{ opened: <commitment> }`: someone shows the code. It holds when a counted block carries `state.opening`, `{ salt, value }`, that opens the commitment (privacy.md, A commitment): one hash comparison, in the suite the commitment names. It needs no clock, no third party and no key. The promisor signed the term, and telling the code is his release. Anyone who learns the code can show it, but what it moves goes only to the receiver already named. An opening shown before the promise binds makes the condition hold, and the promise still binds only by its accept.
  *Example.* Chidi gives a rider a four-digit code at the door and signs: if anyone shows it, £5 of his becomes the rider's. He commits to the code with a salt and writes the commitment in the condition. The rider writes any block with `state.opening` `{ salt, value: "4821" }`, and the five pounds are his. Anyone who learns the code can trigger it, but it pays only the payee already named.
- A sensor's readings, under the schema `reading_condition` (patterns/tasks.md).
- **Due on her next order.** A debt that falls due when the debtor next buys from the same seller is the debtor's own promise conditioned on her own signal: `{ signal: "order", by: [her id], subject: the seller }`. Her signed order is the event, so no event clock is needed, and an order signed by anyone else starts nothing.
- **A signal:** `{ signal: <observe kind>, by: [ids], signers, subject, op, value }`. It holds when `signers` different signers that `by` names have each signed a counted `observe` of that kind, naming `subject` in `refs.subject` when given, and carrying an `amount` that keeps to `op` (`lt`, `le`, `gt`, `ge`, `eq`) and `value` when given. A signal counts once it is counted, whatever its `at`. **A signer is whom the block speaks for** (authority.md, Who a block speaks for): a signal a lead signs under a member's grant ("your refusal signals under this rule speak for me, until D") counts as that member's, once per member; a signal the grant does not let speak for the member speaks for the lead alone. A signer takes their signal back by a counted block that `defeats` it and speaks for the same signer (a member's own block, or a lead's under the same grant); it ends the signal only for blocks that have not used it, and for good (a defeat of that defeat restores nothing; they sign a new signal). **Shape:** `by` is a non-empty list of ids; `signers` is a whole number of at least 1 and follows Counts (folds.md); `subject`, when given, one id; `op` and `value` come together or not at all, `value` an amount (`{ value, unit }`, the value a decimal string). Any other shape does not hold.

**By a time.** Any condition the protocol reads may be wrapped: `{ within: <condition>, deadline, clocks }` holds when it held by `deadline`; `{ without: <condition>, deadline, clocks }` holds when it did not. `clocks` is the named clock (blocks.md, A named clock). All three keys are required: there is no default clock, and silence never widens. Without them the wrapper is refused (`shape`) and holds for nothing. The inner condition **held by the deadline** when it holds on the blocks that were in time. A block that decides it counts only if `needed` clocks received it before their cutoff through the deadline (a receipt with no such cutoff before it on its line came before the time), never by its own `at`, which its signer could backdate. The deciding blocks are: for `settled`, `accepted` and `paid`, the blocks of that deal (those naming it in `for`); for `deed`, that block; for a signal or a reading, the observes of its kind; for `opened`, the blocks that carry an opening. An arrival that never reached the named clock counts as no arrival: that is a term both sides signed. `without` holds where `needed` lines hold a cutoff through the deadline with no deciding block received before it. `within` needs no cutoff. A pair of them on one clock never both hold, whatever the order they were signed in.

**The condition fact.** For a promise with `terms.condition`, the view says beside the stage whether it `holds`, does `not`, or is `unread` (a shape the protocol does not read). A promise whose condition does not hold has nothing due yet. With no condition the fact is null. **Failure spreads visibly.** When the condition does not hold because the deal it names ended without it (stopped, declined or expired), or because that deal's own condition failed, the view names that deal in `condition_failed`, never just "open". In a chain of three, the third shows the second, and the second shows the first.

**Named readings.** `settled`, `paid` and `reading` are named readings. A block whose condition names one states, in `state.rule_set`, the rules that hold it; a reader that does not run them holds the block not read (`unknown_rules`). The core runs none: with no `state.rule_set` such a condition is not read (`condition_holds` answers null), never holds, and what it holds stays held. `ruled` reads signed fields only and every node reads it. The payee's signal, written `{ signal: "paid", by: [payee], subject: deal }`, needs no reading and is read on every node.

**A sum is a signal.** No fold adds across deals. Where a promise turns on a sum over many deals or many signers (pledges reached, quotes taken, strikes, a pot short), its condition is a `signal` by the counter its signer names, with `op` and `value`. The counter's `observe` carries the sum in `amount` and names what it counted in `derives_from`, so anyone redoes the sum. A sum that must *not* be reached is written `without` the `ge` signal by a deadline on a named clock, never a bare `lt`: a running count is below every threshold on its first block. A later sum by the same counter is a newer word of hers (blocks.md, The cut): it ends the earlier only for blocks that had not used it.

**What a condition moved stays moved.** A condition is read where the next taking stands: it has held for a block when the blocks that decide it (By a time) are in that block's causal past and stand there, and no end of them has been seen by it (blocks.md, The cut). A draw that carries a condition lands where it has held (folds.md, Lands); once landed and standing, it stays: a signal taken back, a payment reversed or a ruling changed after that is a new fact, for a judge both named, never a reversal. An end that comes before any block relied on the deciding blocks stops the condition: nothing was built on it. What a named signer's word moves, it moves by the word of the one who named her (A named signer's word). Ground: title moves by its owner's word when the condition she stated came true, and never moves back by a stranger's.

**A named signer's word.** A clock, a meter, a counter, a payment confirmer, a grader, a referee and a person counted in her voices are each named by another's word to say a thing. A condition names a signature, never a fact. Her block binds her name to its truth and nothing of hers. Her own promise, with `terms.backing`, is the only thing that adds to that. **Who pays her.** Any named signer may state `paid_by` (an id), `funded_by` (a pledge that pays her fee) and `pay_depends_on_result` (true or false), one optional block of terms, read the same way everywhere. Nothing is filled in. *A judge's own cap* is her promise with her fee as backing: "if a judge we both name finds my ruling wrong, I return my fee and nothing more."

**Relative time.** `state.when` may be `{ after, wait }`. With `after` a block id, the step is due `wait` after that block arrives on the performer's node, on the performer's own clock. This is scheduling, never authority. If the anchor never arrives, the step waits. A `repeat` counts from that start. With `after` an observe kind (any text that is not an id), the step follows an event, as an event `repeat` does: it is due `wait` after the receiving side's `observe` of that kind written `for` the promise (the holder, read as Bearer reads it). The wait runs from the named clock's receipt of that observe, never from its own `at`: the promise's `terms.clocks`, and no one else's receipt. With no `clocks` there is no clock; with no receipt the wait has not begun: the stage lists the request in `occurrences` with `due` null. `PT0S` is on demand, `PT12H` twelve hours after it is asked for, and a date stays a date. A request by anyone but the receiving side counts for nothing. "On demand" claims that the issuer holds what is asked for. The protocol cannot see a warehouse, so that is the issuer's word, read against her own counts.

**A name is pencil.** What a deal is, is what its blocks say: its sides, what moves, its typed terms, and the blocks made `for` it. A name for it (selling spare food, khata, a lease, a loan, a partnership) is a reading of those blocks, and its reader's own. Any reader may give one; two readers may give two, and neither is wrong: the two sides of one deal are often doing different things, and each names her own. No field of a block states a name, and no fold answers one. A person may sign her own name for a deal, an `observe` whose `refs.subject` is the deal: it binds her name to it and no one else's, changes no stage of the deal, and nothing writes one unasked. Sides who want words they share name one schema in `refs.schema` (things.md, Schemas). **An open offer is its own template:** it carries every term the deal will have, and a taker answers it, choosing only what the offer leaves open. Ground: only a signed word binds, and only as written; what a person is doing, and for what end, is hers to say (Mises: only persons act).

**What becomes whose.** The door's `gives(block, person, blocks)` is the one reading of a block for a person: what of hers becomes whose if the block stands, and whether it can come back. It reads typed terms and refs and never a word of a schema or text. It answers `{ gives: [line], bench }`, and `unplaced: [thing]` when the block names things the record does not hold (no line, since only what the record shows is hers, but never "nothing": a reader that lowers its bar for a block that gives nothing must not lower it for one that has an `unplaced`); `bench` is the bench of the deal the block belongs to, as the stage gives it, or `none`. A line is `{ what | amount, to, back, held }`: `what` a thing with `amount` when the terms say how much, or `amount` alone (a price, or an amount moved with no instance); `back` set when it comes back; `held` true where the block is a draw that holds that instance from its signer's word (an offer to a named receiver other than its signer, or a transfer, naming an instance). `to` is who it becomes the property or use of (null while the side is open). `back` is the time it comes back, the deal's `terms.until`; with none, it is null and cannot be counted on to. An amount is never read from words: a price is read by `terms_total`, in any kind it is stated. For a promise, its `from` side gives each thing it names (`refs.what`, with the quantity of `terms.lines` or `terms.quantity`; one its held record shows another owns is not hers, and is left out; so is one the record does not hold at all, but that one is also named in `unplaced`), or, naming no thing, the amount its quantity states; its `to` side gives the price; a thing named in a promise with `terms.until` is a `use`, since it comes back at that time, and any other is `goods`. A promise of a kind or of an amount gives goods, never a use: the same kind comes back by the other half of the deal. An answer `accepted` reads the promise it answers at the quantity the answer chooses; any other status gives nothing, since a stop moves nothing by itself. A transfer, for the one whose word it is (the circle it is signed `as`; for a block under a grant, the signer of the top grant of its chain; else its author; whether each grant is live is `backed`'s question), gives its `state.amount` to `to_owner`, and each `refs.what`: to `to_owner` as goods (one the record shows another owns is left out), else to `to_holder` as a use that comes back at the `until` of a deal it is `for`. A line to the person herself is left out. Work and time are not things (What moves): a promise of work only gives what it prices, since a signed word of work binds the signer's name and her stake, never her body. **Whether a draft binds anyone.** The door's `binds(draft, blocks)` answers true where the draft is an accept, or where `gives` of the one whose word it is (the circle it is signed `as`, else its author) is not empty; else false. Ground: only what one acquired or was handed can be handed on, so what of hers becomes another's is exactly her property and its use, read from what she signed, never from what anyone says about it. **What is to come back.** The door's `to_come_back(person, blocks)` reads the same lines for the person's own remedy: every `transfer` among `blocks` whose amount is hers by `gives` (signed by her, as her circle, or under a chain of her grants) and that `backed` (authority.md) says she does not back, `{ to_come: [{ block, amount, to, why }] }`. What moved in her name on a signature or a grant that does not stand behind it is to come back, exactly that amount; what her own word or a live chain of her grants backs is hers to have moved, and is not to come back. A transfer no node would hold (a forged signature, a chain not held) is not backed either. Who returns it is whoever a signed promise names; the protocol writes no such promise.

**Answers.** The other side's own block `answers` a promise. It has the type of the block it answers (an `observe` or `transfer` that says `accepted` to a promise answers nothing); the exceptions are a decline, which is an `observe`, names the deal in `for`, and carries `state.status`, and an accept under her standing rule (below). It names no term but the quantity it chooses (`terms.quantity`); any other term on it is refused (`undeclared_field`). The type's other required fields do not apply. The statuses (`protocol.json`, `statuses`):
- `accepted`: binds. An accepting answer is a promise, signed by the one who accepts. **Accepted under her standing rule.** Her AI may write a reading, "I accepted this under rule R": an `observe` that `answers` the offer, says `accepted`, names the deal in `for` and R in `refs.under`, R being a grant of hers that she signed. It folds as her accept, not the machine's: what binds her is her own grant (authority.md, Who a block speaks for), as a signed word binds only its signer. It counts as hers only where it binds her under that grant (authority.md, Blocks that bind): the offer it accepts keeps to a promise the grant names, its draw fits on the grant's line, and it stands. It carries no `terms` (`undeclared_field`). A reading under no grant, or one that does not bind her, accepts nothing: a machine's bare word binds no one, and nothing fills in her silence. The stage then reads it as an accept in every place it reads one (stage accepted, `accept` its hash); the quantity of a range offer is chosen only by a promise accept.
- `declined`: ends it. It binds nothing of its signer's: it is a reading of her refusal, an `observe` that `answers` the block it declines (and names the deal in `for`). A decline written before the three moods, with the type of the block it answers, is read the same, and stays readable forever. An observe that says any other status answers nothing. **A decline ends a standing word for its signer:** no later yes of hers to it counts. It reaches nothing that already binds; a late answer is read by the cut, never by a later decline.
- `stopped`, with `state.stop`: `before` (a withdrawal), `onward` (from this block on: leaving a job), or `{ occurrences: [start times, or hashes of the blocks that started them] }` (named occurrences of a standing promise; nothing else is in the object). A stop names no reason; it may be said in an `observe` `for` the deal. **The rest of a deal.** What a deal names a side to receive and has not come can no longer fall due where one of these stands: the payee's signal that the price is paid; her stop, where the offer said ahead that a stop ends what has not come (`terms.stoppable: true`); the end of the offer's `deliver_by` on a clock the deal names; or a ruling of a judge both sides named that says so (`state.ends_rest: true`). Her stop alone ends none of the rest. Where the rest can no longer fall due, what her grant's line holds for it comes back (authority.md, A limit is counted along one line).
- `resumed`, on the author's own `updates` of a pause (a stop with `occurrences`), never an answer: the pause ends early. The occurrences it named that start at or after the resume's `at` stand again; earlier ones stay skipped, and one named by a hash (an event's) stays stopped. Only the pause's own signer's word counts (the `updates` rule, below: it speaks for the one the pause speaks for): a pause is ended by whoever signed it, alone. The other side, who wants it ended early, asks for it in an `observe` `for` the deal, and the signer's resume is her yes; nothing fills her silence. A resume of any other stop (`before`, `onward`) ends nothing: those are never taken back by a word.

An answer may carry in `refs.derives_from` the hash of the exact read-back text its signer was shown, or the hashes of every rendering she met (the text, the audio played, the pictures shown), so a voice or picture deal binds to what was actually shown. Consent is to what she met. The protocol never reads the renderings: they are evidence a judge compares with the bytes, and the answer binds the same with or without them.

How a promise is ended, and what a stop or decline reaches, is one rule (authority.md, Ending). A promise has no status of its own: offered or asked is read from who signed it. Its author stops it by an `updates` with `status: stopped`; the other side by an `answers`. **That is all an `updates` of a promise is (bar the author ending a pause of their own, `resumed`): its terms are fixed once written** (invariant 12). Any other `updates` is refused (`required`) and a fold counts it for nothing. To change terms the other side counters, or the author stops and makes a new promise. A new promise that `answers` one with no status is a **counter-offer** (core.md, section 7). A counter replaces only the offer it answers; every other live offer stays live. It binds nothing until the other side answers it `accepted`; then the deal's terms are the counter's own, whole, and what it leaves out is not in the deal. A stop of an offer that no accept binds is a withdrawal, whichever word it carries.

**Who may bind.** An offer, a request (an accept or a counter by a taker, or any answer that waits on the other side's yes) and a transfer to a named receiver are **standing words**: each says *if you answer, I am in*, and each has ends (blocks.md, The cut: its signer's stop or withdrawal, a newer word of hers, its `expires`). An answer **binds** the live offer it answers when it **stands** (blocks.md, Finality) and, on an offer that leaves a side open, when the offerer's yes to it stands. Until then it is a **request**: it binds neither side, and its signer withdraws it as an author withdraws an offer. Three points may be open, and each closes one way (the stage lists each request in `requests`, `[{ block, open }]`):
- **When**, if the offer carries `expires`: with a clock (`terms.clocks`), when `needed` lines received the accept before their cutoff through `expires` (a receipt with no such cutoff before it on its line came before the time). With none, an accept is in time only by its own `at`, before `expires`; one whose `at` is at or after it, or that states none, is not in time, and binds only by the offerer's own later block that has it in its past, or a relier's block that names it (blocks.md, Time). An accept the offerer declined, on an offer with `expires`, that binds by none of these is ended: it is no request. A receipt or a date of the offerer's side is his word, read against him alone.
- **Which**, if the offer leaves a side open: only by the offerer's yes, the **allocation** (an `accepted` answer of her side to the request; her tool's under her grant, authority.md, Blocks that bind). No clock's receipt orders takers: a side's clock counts only against that side, so every receipt her own till signs binds her and orders no one.
- **Whose**, if the answer or the offer is waiting (a key with a `reach` or a `wait` not yet run, a grant whose end has not been reached, a circle's move whose hands are not yet gathered): when it stands, by her clock, by her own later word, or by a relier's counted block that uses it unseen (blocks.md, Finality, The cut). A circle's move is signed by all its hands together, so no one hand's later word makes it stand (circles.md, Rows).

**The offerer's yes** is a counted block of the offerer's side that has the request in its causal past and answers it `accepted`, or is her own settling block made `for` the deal, or her `accepted` answer to one. By the cut, her yes gets past every end of her own offer (its signer named it in a later block of her own); it gets past the request's ends only as the cut says (blocks.md, From where a stop counts), and one who wants an end writes `expires` on her accept.

- **An accept keeps to the offer** (invariant 12): where the offer's quantity is a range, the accept names a quantity in its unit from `min` to `max`; else none, or the same. One that does not keep to it binds nothing.
- **An allocation is a draw on the offer's quantity** (folds.md, What is left). It binds a taker to no more than she asked for. Every allocation the offerer signed binds her; one that does not take is shown `over_allocated` at once. **One request is allocated once:** a yes to a request that has an earlier yes to it in its causal past binds nothing; two that do not see each other are a pair (folds.md, A fork is a burden): each binds her, listed in `forks`. An onward stop of an allocation by either side frees its draw and ends that deal alone. An open offer with `lines` offers each line once, whole.
- **A counter to an open offer** is signed by a taker, keeps the offerer's side as named and names the taker on the open side. The two may counter on, each answering the one before it and signed by the side that did not sign that one; a counter that names other sides, or comes from anyone else, counts for nothing (`not_live`). The offerer's yes to a counter is an allocation on the counter's own terms, whole, its quantity drawn on the open offer's. Each allocation is a deal of its own: its sides are the offerer and the taker. The open offer is settled by no one.
- **A counter ends nothing that binds:** one that has an accept of its offer in its causal past counts for nothing. Only the author ends an open offer.
- **Accepts that cross.** Of binding accepts in one deal that see one another, the earliest binds. Two that do not see each other **cross**, and are listed in `crossed`. Each binds: both sides signed each set of terms. The deal is read once under each accept (`per_accept`); its stage is the furthest of them, and what was paid is reported per accept. A side ends the one it does not want by an onward stop that answers that accept. Two of one key are a pair as well (`forks`); a pair never frees its signer.
- **Binding only grows.** No block of a side, and no stranger's, makes an answer that stands bind nothing or a counted block count for nothing. Only the bench does (Disputes).
- **An offer that states no end has none.** One with no `expires` and no clock cannot be withdrawn from one who accepts it unseen; who wants an end writes one. (No protocol without a shared clock can order a withdrawal against an unseen accept; the read-back says so.)
- **A tool's own promise.** A promise a tool signs under a grant that keeps to no promise the grant names speaks for its signer alone. An offer may answer it and binds its offerer; an accept of the tool's promise binds no one (`quote_only`). Her own accept of that offer binds her.

Ground: each side's word binds only as she wrote it; a time she wrote binds her and no one else; the one who chooses among takers is the one who offered, by her own signed yes (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19; Mises: only persons act).

**Settling.** A promise settles by folding the counted blocks made `for` it: transfers, a transform, or the worker's own work. Only the right side's word counts: a giver's transfer speaks for `from`, a payment for the side that pays (`to`), a refund for the side that refunds (`from`). **Paid only by the payee's word, or by the very thing named.** A block `for` a deal counts toward one half of it in one of two ways only: a draw on the instance the half names, where it lands, at what landed; or the word of the side that half goes to, in that half's `unit`. Where she said nothing in that unit, the block counts nothing. No fold compares `of` (folds.md, The other side). Her word is her `accepted` answer, her tool's inside a deal her grant names (authority.md, Blocks that bind), or her order of that instance (things.md, A spot exchange); in an allocation's deal the receiver is the taker. Any other counted block counts only where the bench confirms it (Disputes: as many names as `needed`). The giver's word alone settles nothing. A transfer or work with no stated amount is one whole (folds.md, What is left). **What a payment pays for is the payee's word.** `covers` has one meaning: the receiving side's own signed word on how much a payment, of any kind, counts toward what is to come to her. A counted payment with no `split` counts for what it is, and more where the accepted answer of the side it is paid to says so in `state.covers` (an amount; only on an accepted answer). A word counts only against its signer's interest: **a payment never counts for less than was sent**, and `covers` never raises what another is to hand over. The same holds for a delivery on a flat price: the payer's own accepted answer to it may say how much of the price it pays for (`covers`, an amount), and that lowers only what is to come back to her (`uncovered`). The payee's word can raise what counts as paid, never lower what is to come back. **One payment counted once.** The words of one side about one block count once, at the most any of them says: her own words, and each word of a tool that speaks for her. A `received` that `answers` a transfer is a word about that transfer; one that answers none is a payment of its own. A word by a signer who does not speak for that side is that signer's reading. What came beyond is shown (`uncovered`). How each half is counted is folds.md, What is left, The other side. One payment may pay several promises by its `split`, the payer's word (things.md, Transfers). A refund is read the same way, by the word of the side it goes back to. **A side's `defeats` is a claim.** It erases nothing: the stage shows the block **disputed**, by that side, on the date it gives, and counts it as before; both words stay. Only the bench changes what counts (Disputes). A side never disputes its own block; each stops instead. A sensor reading is a ground a confirmer may cite; it never settles alone. A return in goods is a second promise `for` the first.

**Work, counted by whom the job names.** A `work` or `done` for a deal is its doer's word, in her own name. It counts toward what moves where the job's terms name whose word counts it (`terms.condition`, a `signal` of that kind `by` those named); where the job names no one, where the side the work goes to answers it `accepted`. On a deal priced per unit of work, a payment `for` it counts the work its doer stated in the payment's causal past, oldest first, at the price per unit. A job (no `refs.what`, no `lines`, its `state.what` what is done) shows one line, id null: `needed` the quantity chosen or null, `moved` the work that counted, in the unit of that quantity, else of the price per unit.

**An occurrence kept with no price.** A standing promise with no price (*walk the dog every day*) is one promise; nothing is signed again each day. An occurrence is kept by the doer's own `observe` of kind `done`, `for` the promise, with `state.occurrence`: the occurrence's start time, or the hash of the block that started it, as a payment names one. It speaks for the `from` side (the side that gives), and it counts alone: the other side's word is not asked, and its `defeats` of it is a dispute, which only the bench settles (Disputes). The view's `occurrences` lists it with `done_at`, the earliest `at` of its counted dones; an occurrence with no done, no payment and no pause is simply not kept, a fact each reader weighs. A stop of a named occurrence (a pause) and a done of it are separate words; the view shows both.

**Finding: an index's answer.** Public blocks are public, so anyone may keep a list of what is on offer and wanted, and sell it or give it away; no list is the list. A **lookup** is an `observe` of kind `lookup` (the base schema `lookup`): `state.side`, `offer` or `want`; `refs.what`, the things or kinds it looks for (left out, any); `state.terms`, `{ term: value }`, each term an item must carry equal to the value; `refs.to`, optionally the index it asks. A **listing** is an index's `observe` of kind `listing` that `answers` the lookup and names what it found in `refs.derives_from` (none named: it found none). Its items are blocks, never claims: the door's `listing_check(lookup, listing, blocks)` answers `{ ok, why, bad, left_out }`, and a listing is `ok` when every item is held and is a real signed offer (or want): a first promise (it answers and updates nothing) whose key speaks for its author, backed (authority.md) by the side that makes it (`from` for an offer, `to` for a want) while the other side is left open, still open (its stage is `offered`, or `asked`), naming at least one of the lookup's `refs.what` when it names any, and carrying every term of the lookup's `state.terms` equal. `bad` lists each item that is not, with a sentence. `left_out` lists the held blocks that fit the lookup and the listing does not name: an index can omit but never fake, and a reader who asks several indexes, or holds blocks of her own, sees what one left out. A listing says nothing of an offer's expiry; the reader's clock does (Terms: `expires`). Ground: only a signed word binds, so a list of anyone's words binds no one and is true only as far as the blocks it names are there; an index is an entrepreneur who answers for its own signature and nothing else.

**A signed check of someone's record.** "I read her record: 200 deliveries, all done." It is an `observe` of kind `check` (the base schema `check`), `refs.subject` the one whose record it read, `refs.derives_from` the blocks it read, and optionally the typed claims `count` (how many it read), `of` (the type of each) and `stage` (the stage each promise it read is at); `text` is pencil. The door's `check_reads(check, blocks)` answers `{ ok, why, read: [{ hash, held, concerns, stage }] }`: every named block is held and concerns the subject (they signed it, or are a side of it, or it is signed as the circle they name), and every typed claim holds. Whether the signer read well, or whether "all on time" is a fair summary, is the signer's own word and the signer answers for it; the protocol only lets a reader check the blocks against the claim. Nobody must be checked and nobody must check: a reader weighs whose check she chooses to read.

**Clearing: a circle of IOUs.** Where A is to pay B, B is to pay C and C is to pay A, the same amount may be cancelled all round. A **clearing** is an `observe` of kind `clearing` (the base schema `clearing`), signed by anyone, whose `state.lines` are `{ deal, reduce, applies?, rate? }`: a first promise and the amount it is reduced by. It binds only when everyone in it has signed: each person who is to pay a price in it (who answers for the `to` side) and each to whom one is to come (the holder of the receiving place, The note above, else the `from` side) has signed the clearing, or a block that `confirms` it, backed by them (authority.md); the proposer's own signature counts only for the proposer. The door's `clearing_applied(clearing, blocks)` answers `{ binds, why, who, missing, lines }`; a line is `{ deal, ok, why, to_come, reduce, left }`, `left` what is still to come after the reduction, and the clearing binds when every line is `ok` and `missing` is empty. A line is `ok` when the deal binds (it has been accepted), states a price, and `reduce` (an amount above nothing) is in the price's own kind and no more than is still to come (the price less what was paid). A clearing only clears like kinds: a line in another kind than the deal's price (pounds against vouchers) is `ok` only when it names a `rate`, an `observe` of kind `rate` (`give`, `get`: this much of one kind is that much of another) that both sides of that deal signed (or a block that `confirms` it, backed by them), and `applies`, the amount in the deal's kind, is exactly the rate's worth: `reduce` times `get` equals `applies` times `give`. Nothing is converted unless a rate says so. A binding clearing moves no title; it is every person's own word that this much of what is to come to them, and of what they are to pay, is let go together, so each is as well off as before in the kind cleared and none is a middle. Ground: only the one a thing is to come to can let it go (A release), and a word binds only its signer; the circle is nothing but each one's own yes.

**Rulings: judges and what they release.** A judge is a signer with a published offer: a promise under the base schema `judging`, `refs.from` the judge, `refs.to` left open, `terms.price` her fee, `state.what` what she will judge, `terms.rules_within` how fast. Whether her rulings are public is the term `terms.rulings`: `"public"` or left out, and **left out means private**, sealed to the two sides alone. The door's `ruling_audience(ruling, blocks, now)` reads it, and nothing reads her prose. It makes her no one's judge: both sides' own signed choice does, by naming her in a deal's referee grounds (Disputes) or in a condition `ruled`. A **ruling** is her signed fact, an `observe` of kind `ruling` (the base schema `ruling`) with `refs.subject` the deal she rules on, `state.text` what she found happened, and `state.awards`, `[{ to, amount | what }]`: what of whose becomes whose under that deal. It transfers nothing; it is her word, and she answers for it (A ruling and a remark). A deposit, a guarantee or an escrow is a promise (or a transfer) whose condition names a ruling by a named judge, `{ ruled: <deal>, by: <judge>, awards: <person> }` (Conditions); a stake given by a transfer rests on every deal it is `for` and on the deal its condition `ruled` names, when that is a first promise: it releases only once each of them binds; a ruling about a claim or a thing rests on no deal. The door's `ruling_releases(stake, blocks)` answers `{ releases, to, amount, ruling, why }`: it releases when that condition holds, read in the past of the block that relies on the stake, and `amount` is what the award states in the stake's own kind, capped at the stake (the whole stake when the award states none; an award in another kind releases nothing, and nothing is converted). Both sides signed the stake, so both chose the judge: an autonomous system both chose is a judge. A ruling's audience is the author's own (core.md, section 5) and she keeps the term she signed. Two things are public whatever the offer says (`ruling_audience` says so for the first: an award line with a `due` time at or before the reader's clock and no counted transfers `for` the ruling to the one it awards that add up to the award's sum in its own kind (anyone may pay it: the sum matters, not the payer; an award with no sum, goods or a deed, is honoured only by a release), and no release (A release) with `refs.subject` the ruling signed by her, the awardee, or by one who speaks for her under her grant: her own word that it is settled, in cash or goods off the rail; no one else's counts), because they are facts about her and not about the sides: that a ruling went unhonoured (the award shown unpaid at its due date, a fact and never a verdict), and her track record, how many rulings she gave and how many were honoured, which anyone reads as a list. An appeal is a new stake or ruling under a judge both choose. **A fee back when the sides settle.** A judge may offer to try to settle the sides first, and to give back part of her fee if they do: her offer carries `terms.conciliation: true` and `terms.fee_back`, the part of the fee (a decimal from 0 to 1). It is her signed promise and binds her alone; nothing makes a side use it. The sides **settle** through her when each of the deal's two sides has signed a release of it (A release: an `observe` of kind `release` with `refs.subject` the deal) and neither release has a ruling of hers on the deal in its causal past; once she has ruled, no release is a settling through her. The fee a side paid her is what it signed over to her: counted `transfer`s from that side to her, `for` her offer, with `refs.subject` the deal. The door's `fee_back(offer, deal, blocks)` answers `{ settled, releases, to_come, why }`: `to_come` is, per payer and kind, that payer's fee times `fee_back`, exact, then `returned` (what she has since sent that payer the same way, from her `for` the offer with `refs.subject` the deal) and `left` (the rest, never below zero). It is empty unless the offer states a conciliation and a `fee_back`, the offer is hers and backed by her, and the sides settled. The fold counts what is to come back to each payer; it moves nothing, and her paying it is an ordinary transfer she signs. Rothbard: the fee is title she was handed on a condition she herself named, so when the condition comes true the part is the payer's again by her own word; nothing comes on silence. A ruling about an attack with no deal behind it is the victim's own judge's (A claim of invasion).

**An IOU or a voucher is issued and handed on.** Anyone may issue her own currency: it is her promise, worth what her word is worth, and the reader sees who stands behind it (its `from` or `to` side, and any vouch behind it, profiles/vouches.md). An IOU is an ask by the issuer (`refs.to` the issuer, `refs.from` the first holder, `terms.price` the sum) that the holder accepts; the holder hands on her receiving place (The note), and the new holder accepts it. A voucher for goods ("five coffees") is a bearer note: a thing of its own that the issuer promises to honour for whoever owns it (Bearer, `refs.to` the thing), handed on by the thing's ordinary transfers. A hand-over of a receiving place moves what is to come, so a loaf to come from Anna is handed on like £5 to come from her; a voucher that goes from hand to hand without a named holder is still a bearer note.

**An answer to a question.** An `observe` of kind `answer` (the base schema `answer`) `answers` the block it replies to (`refs.answers`; the question is any block, a word, a deal, an `observe` of kind `question`) and says what it is in two typed fields: `state.field`, the name of what is answered, and `state.value`, text, a number or true or false; `state.text` may add the words. A reader checks an answer by its field and value, never by reading its words; a grant's `scope.types` may name `observe.answer` (authority.md), and the fields and values it allows are the principal's own fence, read on the principal's node. The protocol reads no question and no field name: whose word the answer is, is who the block speaks for.

**A release.** An `observe` of kind `release` (the base schema `release`): *we are settled; nothing more is to come between us, except these listed debts.* It binds only its signer, and lets go only of what is to come to her: the price as the `from` side, or what moves and what is to come back as the `to` side (Rothbard: only the creditor can let a debt go). With `refs.subject` (a deal, or a thing: "I let go of any claim to parcel 7") it lets go of that; without, of her claims on every deal with `refs.to` whose first promise it has in its causal past, except the deals in `state.except`, which stay open. A mutual release is one from each side; one side's leaves the other's claims standing. It moves no title. The fold `release_shown` shows it (folds.md).

**A message with a receipt.** An ordinary signed `observe` of kind `message` `for` the deal, with a receipt (blocks.md, A named clock) from the named clock (the promise's `clocks`; with none named there is no clock, and nothing starts "knowing"), starts "knowing". The stage lists each as `received_at` beside `uncovered`: `{ message, side, at }`, `at` null while there is no clock or no receipt. What was paid is to come back from when the condition failed, with no intent needed (Paid for, never delivered). "Knowingly keeping it" exists only after a message reached the one keeping it. No new kind and no new word is needed: the same receipt is what a refusal needs (profiles/refusals.md), one form for both.

**Paid for, never delivered.** What is paid `for` a deal is paid on that deal's condition: the payer's signed `refs.for` says so, and nothing fills in his silence. What it bought is what moved to him. What was paid that what moved does not pay for is still the payer's, at every stage, bound or not, unless the terms say it is kept (`forfeit`). The stage shows it as `uncovered` on each side: arithmetic over what each side sent `for` the deal. It says nothing of whose anything is; the ownership fold answers that. What moved is priced only by a signed per-unit price or a line's price. On a flat price for a whole, nothing moved counts until the whole moves. **A premium buys the promise itself.** A payment `for` a conditional promise (an option, insurance, an iddir's dues) buys the promise; a premium the terms mark as kept (`forfeit`) stays with the seller, and whether the promise was kept is a judge's word. A payment that is part of the price, or held, says so. **A payment that names no deal** (no `refs.for`) is shown as "not said what for" and counts toward no promise, even the only open one between the two: nothing guesses the payer's meaning.

**Claims.** A **claim** is its signer's word that something is hers, or that she or what is hers was crossed: an `observe` naming in `refs.subject` what it is about, in `refs.to` whom she says did it or holds it, in `refs.derives_from` what she shows. No fold reads its truth. A hand-over of a claim is a transfer that names in `refs.what` any block that is not a `thing`; it moves something of hers, so it waits on its key's `reach`. A claim of harm and a claim to be first are claims. An ask by the receiving side `for` a past block, when nothing was promised first, is a claim. An unanswered claim or ask binds no one and moves nothing in any fold.

**Disputes** end one of two ways: the sides counter until both accept, or the deal's **bench** rules. The bench is the names that may lower a deal's stage: the confirmers the deal's schema names in its grounds, and a judge both sides named. **A judge named after the quarrel:** both sides may name one for a live deal, by a `grant` of kind `referee` one side signs naming her in `refs.to` and the deal (its first promise) in `refs.subject`, which the other side answers `accepted`; she then sits on the bench after the names the grounds give. **A judge named late** counts on the bench only where each side, and every holder of what either side has to come, named her or answered her `accepted`. **A side alone never judges its own deal** (invariant 10): a name in the grounds that is a side confirms nothing; nor does any block that speaks for a side (authority.md, Who a block speaks for); nor one a side appointed, in its own name or `as` its circle, by a grant the binding accept had not seen. **Who says a thing happened never rules.** A signer a condition names in `by`, or an account word names in `by`, makes that condition hold, and nothing else: her `confirms` or `defeats` of a block of the deal is only her own reading. Only a name on the bench changes what counts. One who is to do both is named in both places (a grader who rules on a lot sits on the bench; one who only signs that it arrived is named in `by`). **A judge both sides signed for counts even when tied to a side** (an owner of parts of a side's circle): both chose that judge; the stage shows the tie (`referee_ties`, folds.md) and each reader weighs it. **Several names:** a deal that names more than one says how many must agree (`grounds.needed`, a whole number from 1 to how many the grounds name), by Counts (folds.md). The deal is valid without it, and has no ruling until a number is signed (in the schema, which may say 1, or by a new deal); the same where a judge named later grew the names past the grounds' own. Each name's word on a block is its own: a block it `defeats` counts for nothing unless a `confirms` of its own has every such defeats in its causal past (it changed its ruling); a block it `confirms` counts. A block as many names as are needed say counts for nothing counts for nothing; one as many say counts counts, whatever a side says of it. **A backup judge:** with `grounds.wait` (a duration), the names are a chain, each the backup of the one before. A later name's turn begins where `needed` lines of the clocks the grounds name (`grounds.clocks`; no side's own line) each hold a cutoff, a span no shorter than the wait, running from that line's receipt of a side's dispute, with no word of an earlier name received before it; or the same on the earlier name's own line. A place, never a date. The later name's word counts when it has that turn's start in its causal past and no word of an earlier name on the block. With neither, the wait has not begun: nothing fills the earlier name's silence. The bench has the last word on any block in the deal: what settles it (a delivery, a payment, work) and what binds it. An accept the bench defeats binds nothing; nor does an allocation on an open offer, or the request it answers (the bench is the offer's schema's between its offerer and that taker, with any judge both named for the offer or for the allocation's deal); and an answer it defeats (a receiver's accept of a delivery, a payee's answer with its `covers`) counts for nothing; a delivery whose accepted answers the bench all defeats counts for nothing, though it landed. With no bench, or until it rules, a dispute changes no stage: the stage lists it (`disputed`) and each reader judges it. Anyone else can only propose. A judge's fee is an ordinary offer of the judge's. **A ruling has an audience.** Every block states its `audience` (core.md, section 5), so a ruling is public or sealed to the circle that would act on it, as the deal chose. A ruling shown to no one else is a fact for the sides only.

**The law is not in the protocol.** No fold reads how a judge should rule, and the spec states none. A judge's offer may name a rulebook by hash; the sides who name her have named it. The spec names no rulebook. **Two doors, one record.** A dispute over a deal is settled between the sides only by a judge both named; an attack goes to the victim's judge. The record cannot tell them apart: a claim is her word, her judge's word is read by those who named that judge, and the judge answers for it if wrong; whoever acts on it answers for the act.

**A ruling and a remark.** The ruling is the bench name's `confirms` or `defeats`: it moves what counts. Her remark is her own `observe` beside it. It binds nobody and moves nothing. *Example:* she defeats the delivery of a short chest and writes beside it "short by two kilos". The first changes the stage; the second is read by whoever reads it. *A ruling may name the rule it applied:* it may `derives_from` the rule's text (a thing), so readers see whether two judges who disagree used the same code. *A ruling shows whether the accused was told:* "notified: receipt at T" or "none shown", read from the same message with a receipt (A message with a receipt).

**The signer's word.** The `not_free` observe: a signer may say of a block she signed, *this is not my free word*, in an `observe` of kind `not_free` that names the block in `refs.subject`, with `state.reason`: `compelled` (signed under force), `not_me` (my key was taken, I did not sign it) or `could_not_understand` (only a person who can reason consents). With `compelled`, `state.threat` says what the threat was. The reasons are data: what counts as force is the judge's to read. Only the block's own signer writes one that counts as hers; anyone else's is only their own observe. The `not_free` observe is the signer's: she may seal it to any audience she picks, it has no time limit, and she cannot take it back. She states the audience; nothing fills it in, so the other side sees it only if she names them. A named complaint can be read by the official it names and by no one else. It is a claim, never a verdict. The fold shows it beside the block (`not_free_shown`, folds.md) and nothing that counts changes by itself. The bench may `defeats` the block on that ground, like any dispute. Nothing is ever unsigned, and the block carries no flag: the one holding the stick would read it before she signed. (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 12: a demand backed by a threat is the attack itself, not consent.)

**A claim of invasion.** Harm with no deal behind it has no judge both sides named, so the one harmed names her own (Two doors, one record). Its claim is an `observe` of kind `invasion` (Claims): the accused in `refs.to` (one or several), what was crossed in `refs.subject`, the evidence in `refs.derives_from`, what happened in `state.text`. She names a judge by a `grant` of kind `referee` (`refs.to` the judge, `refs.subject` the claim); so may whoever holds her claim by her hand-over. The judge may be a circle: a ruling signed `as` the circle speaks for its owners only where a row holds for it or every owner signs it; any other rules for no one. An accused may name his own the same way, and may answer hers `accepted`. A judge rules by a `confirms` or `defeats` of the claim, and a ruling may `derives_from` the evidence it rests on. No fold moves anything because of it. The fold (`invasion_shown`, folds.md) shows as facts: the claimant, who holds the claim, the evidence it cites (`evidence` empty is "evidence: none"), each judge with who named her, and each ruling beside the judge who gave it and who named her. Nothing moves and no stage changes. A side never judges its own claim: the claimant's judge is not the claimant, and a ruling by a claimant, an accused or anyone who speaks for either counts for nothing (invariant 10).
- **Many victims, one claim.** Twelve stalls tainted by one smokehouse each sign a hand-over of their own claim to one of them (Claims), who names one judge. Nobody is bound by anyone else's word.
- **A claim says what it asks.** It may state in its own `state` (`asks`) whether it asks "stop" or "pay N". Data only. FoodBlock enforces neither.
- FoodBlock enforces nothing, and the three remedies stand (core.md, section 10).

**Worked examples** (no new rule).
- *A knowledge sale.* Wanjiku posts a signed claim ("the river road floods in April") and a commitment (privacy.md) of the sealed content. A trader's payment is a transfer conditioned on the opening being shown (`opened`, above). Whether the content was true is for a judge both named. The price is for being first; once read, it is anyone's.
- *A paper note.* Slips each carry a commitment of a long serial. The issuer's promise is to whoever shows the serial's opening. She clears each slip once, by her settling transfer. The holder needs no key; the record is never title.
- *An honest-producer bond.* A lab's reading shows a fault in a batch (a signed `reading`). The producer signs, with that reading in `derives_from`: *if I have not signed a warning by T, £2,000 of mine is yours.* It is a transfer of an envelope of cash to a holder, ownership to the buyer conditioned `without` her own `warning` signal on a named clock (things.md, Transfers; By a time). She cannot backdate the warning: it counts only if the clock received it by T.
- *A penalty paid to someone else.* A baker signs a transfer of an envelope of £20 to a burial fund, conditioned on a judge's `signal` that the bread was late. The fund is not a side, so the cafe takes nothing by claiming the bread was late. A promisor may pledge his property to anyone who accepts.
- *Insurance that names a judge.* An insurance network's open offer says "harm between holders goes to this panel". Each holder signs it, so the accused already chose his judge before any quarrel.

**More worked examples** (no new rule; each is built from the shapes above, and the vectors marked "batch 8" run them).
- *A slice of each payment to the seller's lender* (Raiffeisen, Rochdale). A baker sells to a café on two orders. On each she hands a slice of her receiving place (£50 of £200) to the lender who lent her the price of the oven (The note). The café pays the slice to the lender and the rest to the baker. When the loan is repaid the lender hands the slice back, and all of it is to come to the baker again. A lender can also hold a share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *The tab triangle.* The customer is to pay the shop, the shop is to pay the wholesaler. The shop hands its receiving place on the customer's deal to the wholesaler (The note), the customer pays the wholesaler once, and the wholesaler, who alone can let go of what is to come to her, signs a release of the shop's debt (A release). One payment, two debts settled, each by its owner's word.
- *An early-payment discount* ("3% off if paid in 10 days"). A line has no condition, so the deal carries the dearer price and the seller signs a rebate beside it: a transfer of an envelope of cash to the buyer, conditioned `within` `{ paid: deal }` by the 10th day on a named clock (By a time). Paid late, the cash stays hers and the price stays the dearer one.
- *A credit sale paid in part, then the buyer vanishes.* Title waits for the price (things.md, Transfers). The sale is stopped onward. The paid part is returned, kept (`forfeit`), or counted as rent for the days held, as the sides signed. If they signed nothing, the paid part less what moved is `uncovered`, the payer's still (Paid for, never delivered).
- *A carrier keeps the advance once a milestone passes.* The shipper signs a transfer of the advance to the carrier conditioned on a `signal` from named watchers ("once past the harbour mouth"). Carriers expect to keep it, and the to-come-back rule bends only by this signed term.
- *Paying in another money.* One word may name two monies ("dollar" in Zimbabwe), so the glossary names the exact coin or banknote (profiles/rulebooks.md). A payment in another kind than the price's counts at a rate both signed, reported by a named changer at a named moment: write the price in the kind to be paid and fix it by the changer's figure (Terms: a price fixed later). With no signed rate it counts only by the payee's own word in the price's unit (Settling).
- *Recourse when selling a claim.* The seller of a receiving place signs a promise of her own to the buyer, conditioned `without` `{ paid: deal }` by the date: "if he has not paid by 1 April I pay it". The deal stays the debtor's; her recourse is hers.
- *Roll the short part forward.* Half an order came, and by her own word it pays for £50 (Settling). The rest is to come back. To carry it onto the next order the mill refunds it and the café pays it again for the new deal, two signed transfers a payment rail may net so that no cash moves. No carry exists as a type.
- *A normal-error band as the standard of care.* A carrier signs a per-unit price with `band` "0.05": 96 kg of 100 arriving is inside the band, so the line is filled and priced at what came, and 90 is not.
- *Interim payouts that come back if the stake is not whole.* The partner pays out each month by an ordinary transfer. The sleeping partner has signed beforehand a transfer of that cash back to him, conditioned `without` `{ paid: the stake }` by the year's end.
- *A gift.* A transfer with no promise back reads as a gift: the hen is the niece's and nothing is to come. The giver may sign beside it that it is a gift, so a claim cannot be invented later; a price she invents afterwards is an offer the niece never answered (Answers).
- *A reading over her own figures.* A stall signs, month by month, an `observe` with `amount` of her sales. A debt "repayable when monthly sales pass 80,000" is conditioned on her own signal with `op` `ge` and `value` 80,000 (Conditions). Only her own word counts as her figure.
- *Goods on trust through a grant.* A wholesaler lets a stallholder sell her lot: a grant naming the lot in `refs.what` and her open offer for it in `on`, `transfer` in `types`, a `limit`. The lot stays hers until sold. Each sale is the stallholder's block under the grant and speaks for her. The takings are hers.
- *A sole right with an overriding commission.* The owner signs a promise not to sell this other than through you until D, with a stake on `ruled` (A promise not to do), and a conditioned transfer: "if I sell it another way, 5% of the price becomes yours". It binds only the owner.
- *A loan repaid by delivering goods at a stated price, as one promise.* The lender pays £100 now; the borrower promises 10 sacks at harvest at £10 each (a per-unit price paid in advance). There is no loan type. What was paid for goods not yet come is the payer's until they do (Paid for, never delivered).
- *A co-op's flat delivery price signed by its members.* The members sign the circle's rule that sets the price (circles.md, Rows). Each delivery is the circle's promise `as` the circle. No member is bound by a price he did not sign.
- *Advances before the price is known.* A trader pays a herder £1 a year on a calf whose price will be read when it is sold. The payments fold as paid with no total and nothing due. When the reader signs the figure, they stand as paid toward it.
- *Payment follows a checker's certificate.* The price is fixed by a figure the depot checker signs, on a named clock. Paid short of her figure, the deal is not done; paid her figure with the maize moved, it is.
- *Kept while a signal stands.* An open-account promise may say "what is due from me at the due date is paid unless a signal by the named judge stands against me at that date": the payment is conditioned `without` that signal at the due date. When the signal ends, a new date is a new term both sign (A new date both sides signed). It is a term both signed, never a default.
- *Wages kept back until the season ends.* The farm hands the envelope to a third party both named (a holder) and signs a transfer to the picker conditioned on a confirmer's signal that the season is over.
- *A pitch that returns to the market's owner.* When the trader took the pitch he signed a transfer of it back to the owner, conditioned on her signal that a ruling against him went unpaid. It is one owner's term; no other stallholder is bound, so there is no joint boycott.
- *Selling a market pitch.* The pitch is a thing, so the stallholder sells it by a transfer. The market still seats the buyer only by a grant `as` its circle: owning the pitch seats nobody, and that grant binds the market's owners only where a row holds for it (circles.md, Rows), never by the seller's word.
- *A priced duty to tell.* A member who holds an unpaid deal for N days without writing a signal hands a set sum to the circle: his own transfer of an envelope to the circle, conditioned `without` his signal by the date on a named clock. The creditor and any holder of the debtor's money each sign it.
- *A pre-signed duress cap.* The boss's promise to pay the officer named up to 1,500, named in a grant to the driver with `limit` 1,500, and the boss's promise to repay him plus his lost half-day. A hand-over inside the limit speaks for the boss, one past it for the driver alone, and the driver logs the stop as compelled (The signer's word).
- *A perishable dispute.* The deal says "either side may sell the lot at once; the proceeds are kept by a named holder; a sample or photo signed by the receiver". The sale is each side's own move, the proceeds a holding, the photo an `observe` for a judge to read.
- *Two named judges who disagree.* The sides name a third before the quarrel in a signed term, or after it by a `referee` grant (A judge named after the quarrel). Nobody fills the third seat.
- *A bond graded for mistake, carelessness and deliberate wrong.* Three conditioned transfers, each with its own signal from the judge and its own sum. The sides signed the grades; the protocol reads none.
- *A state court as a chosen confirmer.* Both sides name a clerk or firm who signs "the court ruled X", `derives_from` the judgment's hash. It is a judge both chose, the ruling is a fact, and no one else takes part in enforcement.
- *A swap of records.* Two wholesalers each show the other their closed deals' outcomes only while the other does. Each signs a grant to the other (any kind the sides name; the protocol reads none for it). The second names the first in `derives_from`, because a hash cannot name a later block, and each stops her grant when the other's ends; "while the other's grant is live" is a line her own node keeps in her product's field, which no fold reads (authority.md). No shared form was needed.
- *A judge's standing offer.* A standing open offer (Skills are standing offers, patterns/tasks.md): "Thursdays at Kisumu fish market, KES 100 a claim, paper only", with the place, the hours and the fee. A deal names her in its grounds or by a `referee` grant, and she is a judge both chose.
- *An agent's grant with a reading condition.* "Go on if the Garissa Q2 reading is at least 170 on Tuesday; otherwise wait at Dhobley." Her promise in `on` carries `terms.condition`: a `signal` by the one she names to read Garissa Q2, that it is at least 170 (Conditions; a condition names a signature, never a fact: A named signer's word), so a deal her agent makes under it binds only once that signal is held. A fence only her own node checks is her product's field, never the grant's.
- *A commitment over a list of facts only the receiver knows* (privacy.md, A commitment). The sender commits to the list; the receiver shows the opening; a condition `opened` fires. Caution: a list of few likely values is a weak secret. Anyone may guess and open it, so a commitment over a short list protects nothing. Use it only where the guesses are too many.
- *A road pact between two circles.* Each signs a promise not to do ("my members' lorries use no other road till D"), made to the other, with a stake on `ruled` by one judge both named (A promise not to do). Each binds only its signers.
- *A deal that binds only once a named witness confirms.* The promise's condition is a `signal` from the witnesses it names in `by`. Before one signs nothing of it is due; after, it is.
- *A veto window on a co-holder's move.* The treasurer's deal stands unless two members say no within five days: it is conditioned `without` a `signal` `veto` by two of the members it names in `by`, deadline the fifth day, on a named clock (By a time). Nobody's silence is read as a yes: the deal stands because the term signed `as` the circle says so.
- *A toss by a named caster.* Two claimants of one lamb sign conditioned transfers: heads, it stays with the first; tails, it goes to the second, each on a `signal` from the caster both named. The fold never breaks a tie by hash; the toss is a signed event the sides chose.
- *An open "take up to N".* "A night's firewood from my hedge, free; for more, ask me." A free open offer (a per-unit price of zero) with a quantity range: her allocations bind her, and one past the range is shown `over_allocated`. The protocol caps nothing per taker; a cap per taker is her rule, declared under her schema, and a standing gift needs nothing from the taker.
- *"No claim" by those who could claim.* Each person may bind himself; his signed word estops his own later claim, binds nobody who did not sign and creates no title. A brother signs a promise not to claim any part of a named plot before it is next sold, with a stake on `ruled`: "£500 of mine becomes the co-op's" (A promise not to do). The heirs and the brother each sign as the mother sells two acres.

**Checks answered** (no change needed). A note for ten loaves is an instance with `pack` ten loaves; it is handed on whole or in parts like any thing (things.md, Parts). A one-sided band is a quantity range under a per-unit price (80 to 100 kg). "About N" is a band. An estimate and a count differ by the reading's `accuracy` and `coverage` (things.md, Truth), so a stock report can say which part was counted. A want with no price is a valid shape: a food bank's standing want, filled by a gift. A repeat's amount per occurrence is a signed amount ("£60 after each payday"); a share of a signed figure per occurrence ("5% of each later sale until 400 is back") is a price fixed later with a `fraction`, one figure per promise, so a share of each sale is one promise per sale. A thing or a deal in a kind and a unit no schema names is valid and binds like any other: no one approves an innovation. A grant may name the owner's own rulebook by hash in `refs.schema` ("these three suppliers, never above 12% over last month"), so a helper's AI reads the owner's own words. A lapsed option, a double sale of a named lot, an overbooked offer, a lead's signals, a lease that ends with a reading, all or nothing across eight owners, and "all current holders" are run by the vectors marked "batch 9".

**Late worked examples** (no new rule; each is built from the shapes above).
- *A lease that ends with a reading.* The tenant hands £2,000 `to_holder` the landlord, an envelope, on day one. She signs two conditioned transfers of that envelope on one named clock: ownership to the landlord `without`, and back to herself `within`, a `signal` `soil` from the lab both named with `op` `ge` and `value` 2.0%. In range at the end, the envelope comes back. Out of range, or no reading by the deadline, it stays with the landlord, because nothing fills her silence.
- *A lease that says who owns the trees she plants.* A tree she plants is hers. The lease says one of two things, both signed: she takes them out at the end, or the landlord buys them at £X each, an offer he signs now. With neither said, they are hers and nothing is filled in. A landlord's credit for measured improvement is a price per unit fixed later: "£2 a tree standing at the end, counted by an assessor both named" (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *Selling an unpaid wage claim.* The worker has £400 to come from her employer. She hands that receiving place to a buyer for £350 (The note). The employer's debt is unchanged and the buyer is paid. She gives no recourse unless she signs a line; silence means none.
- *A recruitment-fee loan repaid on each payday.* "After each payday I write `paid`, £60 of mine becomes the recruiter's, until £990." It is a standing promise whose occurrences only the worker starts, by her own `payday` observes (Terms: `repeat`; the side that charges cannot start a charge alone). A week with no pay starts nothing, so she is never in default in it. The recruiter ends it with her own stop at £990. It binds her name and one sum, with no services and no claim on her time, her papers or her body: the shape that cannot become bondage.
- *A price made under a decree.* A deal made under a price rule names the rule in `refs.derives_from`. A price card shows "made under a rule: <name>" and counts such deals apart.
- *A stored-goods note.* A holder's promise to whoever owns the note, for goods held (Bearer). The note may name the lot (sacks 101-140, bin 4). Then a second transfer of that lot by the holder is not the owner's word and counts for nothing (folds.md, Ownership), so a double sale shows on its face. Unnamed means same-kind goods and the holder's own count, and the card says which. A store note names what, through whom, when and where it comes back. A mixed silo held as shares lets a miller who sells the whole bin hand on only his own part (things.md, Shares).
- *Door rules accepted by a tap at entry.* "No phones in the prep room until you leave" is a promise not to do that the visitor signs by tapping, with the end of the visit as `until`, and a stake on `ruled` by a judge both named if the owner asks for one (A promise not to do). It binds only her. Past `until` the view says `elapsed`.
- *An emergency grant woken by a reading.* "If the cold room reads above 8C for 30 minutes, Otieno may spend up to 6,000 on the repairer, until Sunday": her promise to the repairer, conditioned `within` the cold-room signal by Sunday on a named clock, named in a grant's `on` with a limit of 6,000 (authority.md, Blocks that bind).
- *An owner's promise not to overrule a helper.* The owner signs, with £20 behind it on `ruled` (A promise not to do), that he will not override a move the helper made inside her grant. It binds only him.
- *A helper's adoption promise.* "Any new loaf you propose that sells 30 in two weeks: 10% of its takings for three months", with an earnest kept by a third party. A share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later).
- *Staged lending in three tranches.* Three promises of the lender, each conditioned on a signed reading of stock sold. None comes before its reading.
- *Wages paid off a ruling by a three-party deed.* The debtor, the employer (for a fee) and the creditor each sign their own part: the employer pays the creditor from the wage he is to pay the debtor, and the debtor pays the employer the fee. Each signs only what is his.
- *A pre-signed late fee on an "on demand" note.* "If I can't give the plate the day you ask, a second is yours": a conditioned transfer on the missed due date (Relative time; By a time).
- *A lender's question to the earlier owners in a chain.* "Does anything of yours still stand on this oven?" Each earlier owner answers with a signed `observe`. Silence is nothing.
- *An open vouch paying a judge's fee for members' small claims.* The fee offer names the vouch as payer (`paid_by`), and the tie shows (`referee_ties`).
- *A baseline soil reading signed at a sale.* The seller signs the reading with the sale. A later drift claim `derives_from` it.
- *A day pass on a code with no account.* The paper-note shape at a gate: the gate's promise is to whoever shows the code's opening.
- *What I see in your kitchen I tell no one.* An auditor, a co-packer or a cook signs a promise not to tell, with a stake on `ruled`: "£300 of mine if I do" (A promise not to do). A secret sauce held by three is three signed promises. A third party who hears it is bound by nothing, because an idea is not property.
- *An exclusive pouring right at a festival.* The vendor signs a promise not to pour another brand, with a stake on `ruled`. If he does, he broke his own word. The second brand did nothing wrong.
- *A drought clause.* A loan where "if the named gauge reads under 300 mm, 10,000 of what is to come to me is let go; 2,000 more is charged for this": the lender's hand-over of that part of her receiving place to the borrower (The note), and a charge, each conditioned on the gauge's signal. An `observe` carries no condition, so a release cannot be conditioned.
- *Gleaning.* A farmer lets go of a field's leftovers, Saturday 6am to Sunday 6pm: a transfer to an open receiver with an expiry (things.md, Transfers). Whoever takes them is their first owner in the record.
- *Restitution in the wrongdoer's own produce.* After a ruling the wrongdoer signs "10 litres of milk each Friday for 40 weeks": a standing promise, his own deed at work (core.md, section 10).
- *A permit note.* "A loaf any day this month while I have bread." It is honest while she never refuses a holder while she holds goods. The judge's question is "did she refuse while she held it?"
- *A promise not to do with a priced exit.* "No other cold truck on this road until 2029", with a stake on `ruled` and the signer's own right to end it for 30,000. A reader sees "can be ended by: paying X".
- *Overbooking honestly.* An offer says "up to 3 taken for 2 baked; a bumped taker gets £2 and the next day's box", and offers 3. The fold shows `over_allocated` as a plain fact at once for the fourth taker. The bump is a pre-signed conditioned transfer to each taker, `without` a deed that a box reached her by the named clock. Served, the bump stays the baker's. Unserved, it is the taker's. Taking a fourth against a printed 3 is the false word.
- *All or nothing across many owners.* Each of eight owners signs "if all eight of us have signed, my strip becomes the co-op's", a transfer conditioned on a `signal` `in` with `signers` 8 from the eight named owners. Nobody is bound until all are. That is the premise's answer to the hold-out, with no taking. The deals do not wait on each other, so there is no cycle.
- *A revolving line.* A lender signs a standing offer to lend up to N (`terms.backing`, the schema `loan`); each draw is her transfer `for` it, accepted by the borrower, and interest comes only on what is drawn. A draw past the limit counts for nothing against it (folds.md, A pot). The lender may stop new draws any day, and what is drawn stays drawn and to come back.
- *A watched loan.* The call-in is a step: the repayment falls due a wait after a named watcher's `observe`, as an event repeat whose `by` names the watcher (Terms: `repeat`), on the promise's named clock. *Not used for the oven*, or *the voucher no longer enough: replace in 28 days or the loan falls due*, starts the step; it never moves a signed `pay_by`.
- *A loan the lender re-signs each quarter.* A chain of short offers, each with its own `expires`. A lapse is silence: the view says expired and nothing more, and no next quarter is to come from the lender.
- *Past due.* After `pay_by` with nothing paid the stage still reads underway with its `due` date.
- *A top-up when what is pledged falls.* The duty is the borrower's own promise conditioned on a `signal` by the assessor both named, with `op` `lt` and the figure both signed (*sacks 10% below the loan: add four, or the rest falls due*). The reading condition holds only on that reader's word.
- *A sea loan.* One contract, one named clock, both ways: the master's conditioned transfer of a forfeit to the lender `without` the captain's signal *loaded* by T, and the repayment conditioned `within` the signal *arrived* by T2. A ship that does not arrive leaves the repayment unmet, and a pledge written with the same condition fails with it.
- *A sale's finality is a signed term.* *Redeemable at P until T* is the seller's separate promise to buy back, conditioned `within` the buyer's signal by T. Nothing is filled in.
- *A crop pledged twice.* The earlier conditioned transfer holds its amount from where it is signed (folds.md, What is left): a later draw on the same line takes nothing (`past`), and one from another key of hers takes nothing either (`not_on_line`); a key writes sealed blocks or public ones, never both, so sealing changes neither. *Nothing else is pledged on this* is the borrower's signed word, and a false one is his false word, shown.
- *A "shall" binds nobody.* A duty in a group's rules binds only if someone signs it with a stake: *if I sign a loan with no inspection in 90 days, £X of mine is the bank's*, a conditioned transfer `without` the inspector's signal by the date on a named clock.
- *Paying a lender through a third party.* A payment by the debtor's agent under her grant binds the debtor where her grant names `transfer` and the deal, and it is due (authority.md, Blocks that bind), and the payee's accepted answer counts it toward the old deal.
- *No credit-limit field and no group field.* The limit is whatever she signs next, and nothing in a block reads a person by a group she belongs to: a one-off case has no class.
- *A rebate by purchases.* The circle's promise to each buyer under the schema `rebate`: her settled purchases times a figure the bookkeeper signs, `derives_from` naming the transfers. With no figure the stage shows "figure not signed" and nothing is to come. It is a promise of its own, never a term of each sale; the order of payment (interest first) is as signed. Dues paid from the rebate are a conditioned transfer she signs.
- *An officer's bond.* The officer's security is his own shares, with interest, and a pledge he still owns.
- *A finder's reward* is the owner's open offer: finding never moves title. *A standing reward* where each of k signers pays x/k and non-signers pay nothing is k promises.
- *The accused speaks first.* The accused signs an `observe` (*my goats ate 40 kg of Mo's maize*) and an offered transfer; the claim is then his own word.
- *A perishable-evidence panel with a two-day window.* The panel and its window are in the deal's schema; a reading signed inside the window is evidence for a judge.
- *A lease's last round.* The judge for the end is named in the lease, the deposit sits with a holder, and goods left after the ruling go to the holder to store. Her body is never touched.
- *A word before he proceeds on a shared oven.* The co-owner's word is a message with a receipt (A message with a receipt); the wait he promises before he proceeds is his own promise not to proceed sooner, with a stake on `ruled` (A promise not to do).
- *"Paid" sent to the same audience as "to come".* The creditor's optional signed promise to say so, to the audience she told of the debt.
- *A remedy chosen at signing.* *If I break it, tell our circle* is a term the signer writes: telling the circle is the remedy, and the circle is the audience she named.
- *A pitch or route share with entry and exit terms.* A share of the pitch (things.md, Shares) with a transfer back conditioned on the exit term both signed.
- *A grader picking one of the offer's lines.* The grader's `grade` observe names a line in `state.line`; the offer's `lines` carry each line's price.
- *A rent-to-own barrow.* Title waits for the price (things.md, Transfers, Title until paid), with the holding moved now.
- *A livestock lease with half the calves.* Calves are produce, and the owner of the means is their first owner (things.md, Produce of a thing); the lease's signed term hands half to the herdsman.
- *The village cow.* *Milk and calves are hers from day one* is a signed term, because produce is the owner's first unless a signed term says otherwise (things.md, Produce of a thing).
- *Wages due only if the quarter shows a surplus.* The wage is conditioned on a `signal` by the bookkeeper with `op` `ge` and the surplus figure both signed.

**Many parties.** A promise is between two sides. A group buy is the circle's promise to the seller, plus each member's promise to the circle.

**Two people on one side.** A signed word binds only its signer, so nobody is bound by another's word. A side is one id. Two who share a side each sign their own promise saying their part, "the whole" or a share, with no default; or they act as a circle under its rule (circles.md). *Two buyers of one lorry:* each signs a promise to the seller for half. *Two guarantors:* each signs a vouch for the whole; one payment clears all, and the fold shows it paid once (profiles/vouches.md).

**No partnership type.** A signature can do only three things with property: hand it over (a stake), let another act for you (a grant), or put it behind another's debt (a vouch). Every partnership is a choice of those, plus the split. *A baker's pot:* two stakes (a transfer each into the pot), mutual grants with a limit (each may act for the other up to N), and, if they choose, mutual vouches. A profit split is a share of a signed figure (Terms: a price fixed later). A loss falls on whoever owns the stock (folds.md, Ownership), unless a signed term moves it. "Partnership", "commenda" or "loan" is a name (A name is pencil).

**Taking a block as yours afterwards.** A block outside a grant speaks only for the agent, until the principal's own `answers` makes it bind (A tool's own promise). That is the rule for taking a block: a person's later consent is her consent, it binds from when she gives it and never backwards, and nobody's word removes another's. Her answer is her own block, so the earlier blocks stay as they were. The agent stays bound by what he signed unless the one he dealt with lets him go (A release: only the one it is to come to can). A block speaks for one party (folds.md, invariant 26), so "both bound" is two blocks, his and hers, never one.

**Death.** Only a signed word binds, and only its signer. A dead person signs nothing more, and nothing fills the silence. What she signed stands as signed, and the fold shows what is to come. What moves on death moves only by her own wills: transfers on `{ closed: <her id> }` signed while alive (Conditions; identity.md, A will). At the close her wills take in the order of her line, after every other draw of hers; a will whose thing is gone takes nothing. There is no "everything else" and no one who takes her place. No heir's body or own property is bound. An heir who signs "I take what is to come from her" makes a new promise of his own, binding him from that block. Grants end at her close (authority.md, Ending).

## Invariants

8. An allocation is a draw on its offer's quantity: one that does not take still binds the offerer and is shown `over_allocated` at once.
9. A block counts in a deal only if it speaks for a side (folds.md, invariant 26).
10. A promise is settled only by blocks the receiving side accepts or the grounds confirm, and that the bench has not defeated: only the bench changes the stage, and it moves no thing; as many of its names as are needed. A side's defeats is a dispute, shown, never a verdict, and a side never rules, by any name or hand.
11. An unanswered ask or claim binds no one.
12. `answers` is the only consent to a deal, and it consents to the terms of the block it names: a promise's terms are fixed once it is written.
13. A background ask never binds before the person's yes: a tool's own promise speaks for the tool alone.
14. What a block gives is read from its typed terms and refs alone, never from a word of a schema or a text.
Holds: A list an index gives is only blocks others signed: it can leave an offer out and cannot make one up, and a reader proves it from the blocks themselves.
Holds: A check binds only its signer, to the truth of what it says it read; the blocks it names are checked against its typed claims by the reader, never taken on its word.
Holds: A clearing binds only when every person in it has signed, and it clears only like kinds unless a rate both sides signed is named; it moves no title.
Holds: A ruling is a signed fact and moves nothing; a stake is released only by the ruling its condition names, in its own kind, capped at the stake.
