# Consent

Not normative. A reading: what a person's word says about who may reach her. It changes none of the four answers (core.md, section 10): a STOP refuses no block and binds no one; her own `accepted` binds her. Who may write to her first is her node's own rule (nodes.md, Admission); a grant that ends on a witnessed STOP is her own ending term (authority.md, A witness, `scope.ends_on`).

A **stranger** to a sender is someone who has dealt with none of the blocks of the sender, or of those it is named by (below), and has no live YES of their own for the sender (a recorder's `yes` lifts only a stop that recorder recorded; it is never her consent). **Only her accept, her counter-offer or a deal she opened makes him no stranger:** a block of hers that binds her (a fork's included, folds.md) and answers one of his `accepted`, or answers his promise with a counter (a promise with no status), or a promise of hers that names him as a side. Her `declined` or `stopped` answer keeps him a stranger, and so does any other reply: saying no to someone, or asking who they are, is not dealing with them. A block of hers that is half of a fork counts for nothing (core.md, invariant 3). The door's `is_stranger` reads this; given the sender's block, it names whom that block speaks for too (below).

## Consent is a block

A person's answer to contact is an `observe` of kind `consent`. It carries `state.word`, `yes` or `stop`:
- `yes`: you may deal with me about this.
- `stop`: do not contact me.

A word outside these two counts for nothing, and so does a `state.to` that is no id. `refs.subject` names who it is about: the sender, or a principal (a person or a circle whose autonomous systems it reaches). A `yes` counts only where it names one, because it widens what reaches her, and silence never widens; a word left without a subject may be only `stop`, which only narrows, and is about every sender with no live YES. `refs.for` may name an ask or deal. The person signs it, or a host signs a witness of her word in its own name under her grant naming that handle (authority.md, A witness), with the raw message's hash in `derives_from`. `refs.from` names whose word it is when someone else records it; left out, the author.

**Consent follows the principal.** A sender is named by itself and by whom its block speaks for (authority.md, Who a block speaks for: the one `speaks_for` rule, grant scope included; the circle if signed `as` one). The `consent` fold is given the sender's block for this; without it, the sender alone. So a stranger's `yes` to a person reaches her autonomous systems acting under her live grant, and her `stop` binds them.

**Recording a STOP.** A person with no keys (a contact, identity.md) cannot sign. Whoever received her STOP (gateway, host, channel provider, the asker's own app) records it: the recorder writes a `stop` consent `from` her, with the raw message in `derives_from`. **Anyone may record a STOP, and it binds only the recorder's own side**: the recorder, and every sender whose block speaks for the recorder (a tool under the recorder's grant).

**Recognising an opt-out is the recorder's reading, not a word list.** The protocol holds no list of stop words. The recorder puts the right question to a model, or takes the carrier's opt-out signal, and records the block above.

**Her own word is a block backed by her** (authority.md, Backed by a person): signed by her, or by a tool of hers under a grant of hers where that block speaks for her (authority.md, A tool's word). Her own word counts for every word and binds every sender. A witness is the witness's own word and binds only its signer (A witness): a host's witness of her STOP by text counts for the host's own side. Her STOP by text reaches further only through her own word: a grant she gave may carry its own ending term (`scope.ends_on`, authority.md, A witness).

**Only the person lifts a STOP.** A `yes` lifts a `stop` only when it is the person's own block (backed by her) and the stop is in its causal past, so she said it after she knew of the stop. A model's reading of a reply never lifts it. **What a recorder records counts for the recorder's own side alone, both ways:** its recorded `stop` binds its own side, and its recorded `yes` counts only as the lift of a stop that same recorder recorded, in its causal past. Any other consent block by someone who is not the person counts for nothing.

**The live word.** The `consent` fold takes a person, a sender (or none), optionally the sender's block, and the blocks the caller holds. It reads the latest word that names the sender (or one it is named by), else the latest that names no one, in causal order. A recorder's blocks are ordered by the recorder's own chain, so the fold works when the person has no keys. A `stop` wins any tie. A tie between other words leaves no live word. A tie of the same word stands, and the fold names the block with the lowest hash (only to name one; no authority).

**There is no public STOP list** (a hashed phone number can be reversed): a STOP lives with its recorder and the person's chosen host, and a sender checks with them privately first.

**A STOP refuses nothing.** A block sent despite a live STOP is **valid and kept**: it is judged on the block alone and what it rests on (core.md, section 8), so the sender's chain goes on. The reading shows it as **stopped**, and a node delivers it to no one; it may decline to relay its body to the stopped party. An ask nobody answered binds no one anyway (promises.md, invariant 11). A node that holds no STOP cannot know, and delivers as usual.

**A first ask off the network.** A first ask to someone off the network travels in the asker's own name from the asker's own channel and carries the code of the signed ask; the reply binds only when she sends YES with that code to a host she chose, which signs a witness of it in its own name (authority.md, A witness). A host's block outside that conversation speaks for no one she chose; a host that promised to speak only for its own client, in her name, to people already in that conversation, never first to a stranger nobody chose and never after STOP, keeps its own word, not a duty of the protocol (core.md, section 10).
