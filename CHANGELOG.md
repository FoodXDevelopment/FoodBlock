# Changelog

What changed in each version. The spec says what each version means; this page says what moved.

## Text pass, 2026-10-29 (no rule changed)

The normative text (core, protocol, transport, modules) went from about 41,500 to about 30,200 words and the profiles from about 9,800 to about 7,500. Each rule is now stated once, under the name of its primitive, and the rest point to it: who a block speaks for, ending, the cut, a delayed power (authority.md); built-on stands, a fork is a burden, counts, one whole (folds.md); a node's promise (nodes.md); a commitment (privacy.md). Decision tags were removed from the spec text; this page keeps them.

## The named clock and conditions on time, 2026-10-30 (adds to 1.7)

- **A named clock** (blocks.md): `keepers: { names, needed }`. A keeper's `receipt` of a block (an `observe`, `derives_from` the block, `state.at`) dates it; a block's own `at` dates nothing for anyone else. A keeper may sign an `observe` of kind `cutoff` ("closed at T"). One primitive, read four ways: a condition `within` or `without`, an offer's `terms.keepers`, an event `when`, and an event `repeat`. New term `keepers`.
- **Conditions** (promises.md): `{ without: <condition>, deadline, keepers }` ("did not hold by T") and its twin `within`; `{ paid: <deal> }` (the price side of a deal); `{ opened: <commitment> }` (someone shows the code, `state.opening`). All three keys of a wrapper are required (`shape`): no default clock. The view gives `condition`: `holds`, `not` or `unread`.
- **A transfer may carry a condition** (`state.condition`, things.md): it counts once the condition holds, and the receiver may let it go by her own `stopped` or `declined`. The ownership fold reads it; `ownership` and `ownership_view` take the reader's clock. A conditioned goods transfer counts toward a deal's delivery only once its condition holds.
- **Breaking:** an event `repeat` is now due `wait` after the clock's receipt of the event (the promise's `keepers`, else its issuer's), not after the event's own `at`; with no receipt its `due` is null. A `when` may follow an event the same way (`{ after: <observe kind>, wait }`: on demand, on notice). An offer that names `terms.keepers` is timely if the keepers received the accept by `expires`.
- New fields: `state.condition` (transfer), `state.opening` (any block).

## Leases as shares and first use of places, 2026-10-30 (adds to 1.7)

- **A lease is a share** (things.md, Shares): a `thing` of kind `share` naming the one instance or place it is a part of in `refs.subject` stands against every later owner of that thing; the share is its own thing with its own owner. Fold `shares_on` (folds.md): `{ standing, nothing }`. A share counts for nothing when its subject is no thing (a person), is a kind (some thing names it in `refs.of`), or was not its maker's where it stands. A share never reaches copies, offspring or methods.
- **Uses of a place** (things.md): a use is a counted `transform` with no inputs naming the place in `refs.subject`, `state.use` an act. Fold `use_shown` (folds.md): per user, acts, count, first, last, seasons, bounds, resting, and the users whose bounds overlap hers; dated by a named clock when one is given.
- New fields: `state.bound` (a polygon, GeoJSON-shaped, nine places at most; shape `bound`), `state.resting` (a time). New door ops: `shares_on`, `use_shown`.
- The will (profiles/successor.md): a worked example of conditioned transfers, no new rule. Title kept until paid was done with the conditioned transfer (the named clock section above).

## Paid for, never delivered, and selling what you are owed, 2026-10-30 (adds to 1.7)

- **Paid for, never delivered** (promises.md, Settling): money paid `for` a deal and not covered by what moved is still the payer's unless the terms say it is kept. The stage of a deal stopped onward, breached or over-allocated reports `uncovered` (a claim, shown as a fact). What moved is priced only by a per-unit price or a line's price; on a flat price for a whole nothing is covered until the whole moves. New term `forfeit` (money kept). A promise nobody paid for that goes unkept is a broken word, shown, never a debt. Invariant 11 and the line on stopping (promises.md) say so. New stage field `over_allocated` (also in `takers`).
- **A claim can be sold** (promises.md, A claim): the side owed what moves, or the price, hands it over by a signed transfer that names the deal in `what`, `to_owner` the buyer; with `state.money` it hands over part of the price. Deliveries and payments count only to the owner where they stand (their causal past); a payment the payer made without seeing the hand-over counts and the old owner holds it for the new one (`owed_on`). Duties stay with whoever signed them. A claim handed to the one who owes it is paid. New term `personal: true` (cannot be passed on). New stage fields `claims`, `owed_on`. The ownership fold is not changed: Bearer still reads a side that names a thing.
- Wording: lending anything interchangeable (money, flour, eggs) moves the owner, and the lender holds a promise for the same kind back; storing moves only the holder (things.md). The deposit profile says a deposit paid for admission, which was delivered when the ask was read.

## The signer's word, claims of invasion and rulings, 2026-10-30 (adds to 1.7)

- **The signer's word** (promises.md): an `observe` of kind `not_free` naming a block she signed in `refs.subject`, `state.reason` `compelled`, `not_me` or `could_not_understand`, `state.threat` for the first. Shown beside the block by the fold `not_free_shown`; it changes nothing that counts, cannot be taken back and may be sealed. Only the deal's referee rules, by a `defeats`. THREAT-MODEL.md: a stolen key's past blocks are shown as owed by your key, with your note beside them.
- **A claim of invasion** (promises.md): an `observe` of kind `invasion` (accused in `refs.to`, crossed in `refs.subject`, evidence in `derives_from`), a judge named by a `grant` of kind `referee` for the claim. Fold `invasion_shown`: standing, the judges and who named them, the rulings, evidence none. Nothing moves; the accused's silence is never an accept. A claim can be handed on (standing moves, never money).
- **A ruling's `up_to`** (money or an amount): the harm a `confirms` finds. A fact only. New field shape `up_to`.
- Wording: a ruling and a remark; no term on this; a circle's standing referee (circles.md); an answer may carry the hash of the read-back text in `derives_from` (the protocol never reads it); the refrain is the one shape (*if this is done before `until`, this of mine becomes yours*); the owner in core.md is the owner as the record shows it; the three remedies (core.md, section 10).
- New base schemas: `not_free`, `invasion`, `ruling`. New door ops: `not_free_shown`, `invasion_shown`.

## Vouches, pools, refusing together, rulebooks and the two-key link, 2026-10-30 (adds to 1.7)

- **A pot** (folds.md): a view, `pot`, for money put behind a promise and what has been paid from it: `{ holder, currency, backing, paid_in, paid_out, left, payouts }`. The holder is the orderer of her payouts, read in causal order; a payout past the pot counts for nothing against it, and two that do not see each other and together pass it both count for nothing. New term `backing` (money). New door op `pot`.
- **The vouch and the pay-back** (profiles/vouches.md): base schemas `vouch` (a promise by the voucher, `terms.backing` the cap, a referee's signal the condition) and `payback`. Worked examples: the operator's open bond, a vouch on title, a vouch to whoever holds the lot, a layer behind a pool. **The loyalty refrain** (base schema `loyalty`): "I take nothing from anyone else about what I do for you".
- **Mutual pools** (profiles/pools.md): base schema `pool`; payouts are the holder's pre-signed transfers conditioned on the named confirmers' signal; terms `ceiling`, `above`, `if_short` declared by the schema only.
- **Refusing together** (profiles/refusals.md): base schema `refusal`, a refrain each signer makes, bound by a signal counted by distinct signers holding a named `circle#role`. Guards: the cause is a deal in `derives_from` with the creditor's notice, only the debtor is named, a plain mutual refrain needs no cause. It ends on `settled`, the creditor's withdrawal, or the referee's `defeats`.
- **The two-key link** (identity.md): base schema `link`; one key's `same_as` plus the other key's countersign (a `link` naming the first in `confirms`). **Trade rulebooks** (profiles/rulebooks.md): base schemas `rulebook`, `glossary`, `grader`; terms `cover`, `cure`, `allowance` declared by the rulebook schema only. Worked examples: the Kisumu maize deal, the weather reopener, a place certificate, a bookkeeper's figure, a knowledge sale, a paper note, an honest-producer bond, a price list for wrongs, a cover that names a judge.
- **Never their body** (core.md, promises.md): the cornerstone says "never their body", not "never their will". A body was never acquired, so no word hands it on (Kinsella, revising Rothbard).
- **Breaking:** the profile `deposits` and the admission field `deposit` are now `earnest`; the door ops `deposit_shape_ok` and `deposit_paid` are `earnest_shape_ok` and `earnest_paid`; the Cargo feature `deposits` is `earnest`. "Deposit" keeps one meaning: custody.
- New base schemas: `loyalty`, `vouch`, `payback`, `refusal`, `pool`, `link`, `rulebook`, `glossary`, `grader`.

## 1.7.0 (2026-10-27, in this repository, not yet published to package managers)

1.7 adds to 1.6, except a few deletions (`state.confidence`, `state.orderer`, the recovery pair on a key grant: a 1.6 block that carries one is refused); where a reading changed, the entries below say so. It came from testing the protocol against how people really organise food work and life (a survey of 50 parts of the food economy, and attempts to break the idea that everything above a single act is a promise or a permission with terms). Each rule has vectors the Rust core and the oracle both pass (`spec/vectors.json`, made by `sdk/javascript/test/make-vectors-1-7.cjs`).

1. *(Superseded below by M2 and P3: the spec gives facts, never kept or broken.)* **Kept and broken is fair.** It is read for each side. Broken is only a one-off you stopped alone before doing it, or a promise not to do something that you broke. A stop by the other side, one both agreed, a death, a refund, and ending a repeating promise are "released", never broken. A promise to yourself is never counted. A buyer who never pays shows as open on their side. `promise_stage` says who ended a stopped promise (`by`).
2. **A promise not to do something.** `terms.refrain: true` with an `until` (no pesticide, sell only to me, never meat). It is broken only when the side it was made to, or a named judge, shows it was (`breached`), within its term and before they let it go, and done once its `until` passes with nothing broken. After a counter-offer, the accepted terms decide.
3. *(Superseded below by P7: a death ends authority and no promise.)* **A death ends standing arrangements.** A successor's `close` ends the person's repeating promises, refrains, grants and memberships, except what it lists in `state.continues`. One-off debts stay owed. A close counts only under a successor grant still live, with the needed witnesses' triggers in its past and none denied by the person, and it ends grants only for blocks that see it.
4. **Conditions the protocol can check.** "After that is settled", "after this deed", a sensor's readings, and a signal: a signed reading from people the condition names (a moon sighted, a price that crossed a line, an award). Door `condition_holds`.
5. **Many hands.** A circle can say signing for it needs N holders: `state.needed: { sign: 2 }`. It counts once N holders have signed or confirmed it. A removed member's confirm, act under a role grant, or signal does not count from outside the cut.
6. **A grant to a role.** `state.to_role` with `refs.to` the circle: whoever is treasurer acts under it, and only while they are.
7. **Limits in any unit, and per act.** A limit can be kilos, covers or hours as well as money, and `scope.each` caps any single act. Both can be sealed.
8. **Repeats in real calendars.** `terms.zone` (an IANA time zone), other calendars by RFC 7529 `RSCALE`, and repeats on an event (`{ after, by, wait }`: each batch, after a sighting).
9. *(Superseded below by P6: `state.reason` is deleted.)* **Endings say why.** A stop may carry `state.reason`: done, let go, replaced, ran out, paused or lost. No fold reads it.

A review by twenty readers (2026-10-27) then closed what it found before release. These ride with 1.7:
- A grant whose `refs.to` is not one id names no one (a set had been read as open).
- Limits and caps compare amounts exactly at any size, and an issuer's currency by its canonical value; `within_limit` takes what was spent exactly.
- A stop of a circle's grant counts only from someone who may sign for the circle.
- Conditions of a malformed shape never hold, in either engine, and a signer can withdraw a signal by defeating it.
- `circle#role` has exactly one `#`. Stop occurrences are start times or hashes and nothing else. A repeat's signers are ids or `circle#role`. A grant to a role is not passed on.
- The founder holds the first role beside any grant they accept, and their powers are read from it like anyone's. A role taken off an invitation counts whichever a reader meets first.
- The base schema `kept_promises` keeps its id; the 1.7 reading is the new `kept_and_broken`.

A second review (seven readers and a randomized run of 17,000 stories through both engines) closed the rest:
- **A refrain's breach is dated by when it happened** (the defeats' own `when`, else its `at`), so a breach found after the term still counts. **Only the side it was made to releases a refrain**, alone or by agreeing: the promiser leaving it alone before `until` is broken, and a breach after that still counts.
- **A clock that is not a time is refused** at the door (an argument error), in every op that takes one.
- Decimals are exact at any size, in both engines, and a string outside the grammar (`01`) is not a decimal. A link is never an empty set.
- A forked stop, decline or grant binds no one in grant liveness, as everywhere else.
- **The successor view is the least reading that settles**: a defeats that arrives after the trigger fired cancels nothing; one with no recorded arrival cancels; a forked one cancels nothing. A successor grant's `needed` is 1 to the number of witnesses.
- **A death ends the person's operator grants** too, for blocks that see the close; a key the operator rotated before stands. The identity fold reads its operators from signatures alone and no longer loops.
- Circles: a change of a change is a change of the same grant; a second accept brings back no role taken away; a decline after an accept is leaving; the founder's accepted grants can be stopped or left, but the founder is never removed; an admin's removal threshold is read at the stop; `removed` keeps the last stop's `counted_to`; a member invited back keeps the cut of the grant that ended; only a removal is exempt from the cut.
- An autonomous system's promise under no grant is a quote request. A child grant keeps each of its parent's conditions exactly. A number from 1e21 on is a float. Refusals come in `protocol.json` order in both engines.
- `condition_holds` has a wrapper in every SDK, and Swift and Kotlin take `spent` as a decimal.

A third round (fuzzing and fresh readers, 2026-10-28):
- **A member's own leaving cuts at the stop**, whatever `counted_to` it names: what they signed and whom they admitted before it stand. An open grant's joiner who stops it leaves alone, in any form.
- **A key is rotated by itself, its recovery key or its operator**: `valid` takes an operator's rotation and refuses a stranger's (`not_own_rotation`) in both engines.
- **The recovery key defeats only triggers** under a successor grant its author gave, named in `refs.under` (`recovery_scope` otherwise).
- Goods settle a promise only when they move to the `to` side (the oracle had counted any). A stop or an answer restating a scope or a parent is read by no grant rule.
- `state.marks` is exact `{ scheme, value }` on every type. An audience commitment with an unknown hash name is `unknown_suite`. A whole number past 64 bits written in digits is `integer_range`, and `-0` reaches the core from JavaScript. A line's id is non-empty. The year 0000 is a leap year in the oracle. Only a thing of kind schema declares fields.
- New door op `terms_total`: what a promise's terms come to. The MCP door holds an agent's limit against it, plus what it already bound in the period.

Patrick's approved round-1 decisions, and the places the market-design study found the two engines disagreeing (2026-10-28):
- **An open member grant carries no power**: one naming a role with `sign`, `admit` or `remove` (or a change of it that does) counts for nothing.
- **Only an explicit `confirms` makes a stop `both` sides'**; a later stop by the other side is not agreement. **An `updates` stop counts only from the author**; the other side stops by `answers`.
- **A close ends a refrain's term**: its `at`, when earlier than `until`, is the `until` a breach is dated against.
- **A stop ends a grant only for the blocks that see it**, as a death does: what was done under it before stands, and a close that had not seen a stop of its successor grant counts *(superseded below by security D: the person's stop of the successor grant voids every close under it, even one that never saw it)*. **A dead successor closes no one**: a close with the successor's own counted close in its past counts for nothing.
- **A child's `scope.per` is no shorter than its parent's**. New door op `limit_within_parent`: the principal's node checks a child's sealed limit against its parent's.
- A schema's `fields`, `grounds` and `roles`, and the items of `shares`, are data, not places (`shapes.places.data`). Legacy zone names (`GMT`, `CET`, `EST5EDT`, `Zulu`) are shaped like zone names. The signal's `needed` is now `signers`.
- **Two allocations that do not see each other are a fork**: neither binds, in any order (the oracle had kept one, by arrival). An open offer with `lines` binds its first allocation only. A payment for several promises carries a `split` (`required`), in its own currency and adding up to no more than it (`shape`); a fold counts one with no split toward none, and every split part for a promise toward it.

Repair after the code simplification (2026-10-28), where the two engines still read the same blocks differently:
- **A promise names its act only under the grant it acts under**: live, and made for its signer. An autonomous system's offer under a stopped grant, or one made for someone else, is a quote request and binds no one (the core had let it bind); a person's offer naming such a grant speaks for the person (the oracle had made it a quote request).
- **The quantity chosen is an amount, never a range.** A job accepted at 7 g of a 2 to 10 g offer is done when accepted work covers 7 g (the core had read it done with no work); a promise to yourself with a range has chosen none.
- **One receiver rule** for `under_ok`, the speaker and `consent`: a `refs.to` that is not one id, or a `to_role` that is not a role's name, names no one, in every build.
- The oracle reads the door's `role` (`may_act_as`), `within` (`circle_state`) and `key_timeline`'s `by` and `signer`, and no longer throws on an allocation from an offer that speaks for no side.

Patrick's decisions F1 to F7 (2026-10-28): groups, refrains, crossing stops and the small fixes. Vectors in `make-vectors-1-7.cjs` section 16; each vector an approved rule flipped is retired and has a twin:
- **Every removal-like act needs N people** (`removal.needed`): taking `admit` or `remove` away by a change (N different holders of `admit` each dropping the role), and a stop that never saw the member's answer to a grant that gives `admit` or `remove` (taking back such an invitation, or stopping it unseen). A stop short of N changes nothing.
- **A circle reads no `counted_to`:** every stop cuts at itself; `removed` maps each id to null. A stolen key is handled by rotating it.
- **What someone outside the circle built on before the stop stands**, whatever the cut: a deal a removed treasurer signed offline that the mill accepted before seeing her removal binds the circle.
- **An answer after a removal counts only to an invitation written after it.** **A crossing stop is kept only from the cut of the stop it crosses**: a remover a third person removed removes no one past that cut. **A ring of removals** (A stops B, B stops C, C stops A) is read like a crossing pair: the earliest member wins, otherwise all stand.
- **A stop or a close counts as after a refrain's `until`** only if its own `at` is later and the other side confirms it, or something someone else signed later is in its past: the view no longer reads a refrain kept on its promiser's own date.
- **Two crossing onward stops, one by each side, read `both`**: released, whatever their hashes.
- Role names with `#` and repeated powers are `bad_roles`. A total past 2^53 - 1 is exact decimal text (`total`, `paid`, `terms_total`; the MCP door adds spent amounts exactly). A child of a parent with no receiver is `not_receiver` in the core too. `circle_state` within a block whose past does not hold the circle is null (and `members_then` there means no one). `key_timeline` has no `void` field (a void key is never listed).

Patrick's decision F8 (2026-10-28): folds read only blocks that pass the write check. Vectors in `make-vectors-1-7.cjs` section 17; each vector the rule flipped is retired and has a twin:
- **An ill-formed block counts for nothing.** A fold judges every block of its input by the strict check (core.md section 8, checks 1 to 4 and 8) against that input, with the input's schemas as its schema store, as a node judges a block against its store. A block that fails is not read, and neither is any block that needs it: so every later block of an author's chain after a refused block is out too. A sealed block held by its public header alone has no body to check and still meets needs. On a node nothing changes (a node never holds such a block); a fold given one now reads what a node would.
- **Real types.** Because every block a fold reads has passed the check, the core reads each field as the shape the check proved: an id or a name as text, a whole number as a number, a decimal exactly. JavaScript's readings (`truthy`, `text`, `strict_eq`, `number_of`) are gone from the protocol's code; only the one-time converter keeps them, for old data written by JavaScript. Two readings that differed between the engines on odd values now agree: a `kind` that is not text names no act (`observe`, not `observe.5`), and a schema's roles are text.
- What became dead was deleted: the junk cases of the receiver rule, the fold's handling of an `updates` of a promise that is not a stop (the check refuses it), and the spec's "a fold that meets one anyway" clauses. A circle with no encryption key lists none in `circle_key`. A pull's `heads` is read only when it is `true`.
- `make-vectors-1-7.cjs` writes the same file on every run (a sealed case's nonce and salts are fixed).

Patrick's market-design decisions M1 to M10 (2026-10-28). Vectors in `make-vectors-1-7.cjs` section 18; each vector a rule flipped is retired and has a twin:
- **M1, the cornerstone repairs.** An allocation binds a taker to no more than her request named, and never below a range's `min`. A taker may withdraw her request, as an offerer withdraws an offer. A stopped allocation frees its quantity for the allocations that see the stop, and stops that allocation's deal alone, not the offer (the offer used to read stopped). A transfer to a named receiver is read like an offer: the giver may withdraw it before it is accepted, and a transfer may carry `expires` (on it, the giver's `declined` voids a late accept), so an unanswered transfer is no free option. An answer that carries a status names no term but `quantity` (`undeclared_field`). Then **the compatibility law** (core.md section 11): a signed block means tomorrow what it meant when it was signed; it binds from the lock, once two nodes and one implementation that FoodX did not write pass the vectors.
- **M2, facts in the spec, verdicts in readers.** folds.md, Kept and broken, now lists the facts each promise gives (who ended it, with what, against `deliver_by`, whether its condition held, what moved and was paid); "kept" and "broken" are each reader's judgement. The base schemas `kept_promises` and `kept_and_broken` keep their ids, as one reader's shape.
- **M3, a referee.** A confirmer the deal's schema names in its grounds has the last word on any block in the deal: what it defeats counts for nothing unless its own later confirm saw that defeat, and what it confirms stands whatever a side says. It overrules a refrain's breach by defeating the defeats that claims it. With none named, the side decides, as before.
- **M4, firm offers.** The offerer's `declined` answer voids an accept only on an offer that carried `expires`; with no `expires` an offer is firm.
- **M5, forks.** A fork voids what the forker claims, never what they owe: a forked key's offers, accepts, allocations and acknowledgements of what reached them still bind it in a deal, and its transfers count by the other side's word; its stops, declines, confirms and defeats count for nothing. Two accepts that do not see each other and consent to the same thing (one offer, one quantity) bind; with different terms, neither does.
- **M7, catch weight.** `terms.band` (a decimal from 0 to 1): under a per-unit price, a delivery inside the band around the quantity chosen covers the line and is priced on what was accepted.
- **M9.** The protocol already lets a seller declare how she chooses among requests (a field under a schema the offer names); promises.md says so. Nothing else changed.
- **M6, M8 and M10** are not protocol: a circle's threshold founder key and migration pattern (M6) and FoodOS's default of sealing sales (M8) belong to FoodOS; FoodX's signed public promise (M10) is drafted for Patrick, outside this repository.

Patrick's security decisions A to E (P1, 2026-10-28; the five root causes that let a party with no key, or a stale key, forge a binding, void another's word or take an identity). Vectors in `make-vectors-1-7.cjs` section 19, each with a negative case built from the repro that broke it; each vector a rule flipped is retired and has a twin:
- **A, a machine consents to its operator.** An `operator` grant counts only for blocks that have, in their causal past, the machine's own `grant` that `answers` it with `status: accepted`. A stranger who names someone else's machine answers for no one: their rotation of its keys is `not_own_rotation`, and their recorded STOP binds it in nothing (consent follows operators the machine accepted). The MCP door's assistant accepts the operator grant the person signs.
- **B, a rotated key cannot fork the past.** A rotation's **counted line** is `counted_to` and its `prev`-ancestry in the old key's chain; only those blocks (and the rotation itself) still count. A sibling off the line, at any `seq`, is void, and a void block forms no pair, so a stale key can no longer erase a sale, plant a device key or poison the new key's chain. Rotations that cross count only on the part of their lines both share and freeze their keys until the recovery key settles them; the latest recovery rotation's line decides. A later rotation by a key that saw one of its own is a block of the old key like any other. Forks are marked by author and key: a fork of one key leaves the author's other keys alone. Every fold now counts a block only when its key speaks for its author (the core read rotated-out blocks the oracle did not). A node lists no pair with a block the round voids, and the recovery reach protects only what another identity built on that arrived before the reach (the core had also protected blocks nobody used).
- **C, the recovery key is named in the genesis or never.** `recovery_key_hash`, `recovery_reach` and `recovery_suite` are genesis fields (`protocol.json` `genesis.recovery`); a key grant that names them is `undeclared_field`, and a key grant names `public_key_sign` or `public_key_encrypt`. The `recovery` option of `key_grant` is gone from every SDK. The migration escape (one later add signed by two different live keys) was approved only if real people already hold identities without one; none do in 1.7 (nothing of 1.7 is published, and the converter writes fresh geneses), so it was not added.
- **D, a successor grant has no power until the death.** A block under a successor grant, other than a witness's trigger or the successor's close, acts under no grant unless a counted close of the person under that grant is in its causal past. The successor is never a witness (`shape`). The person's counted stop of the successor grant voids every close under it, whenever it came.
- **E, the suite comes from the signing key.** A message is `{ version, block, signature }` and a CBOR message `[t, block, signature]`: nothing names a suite. A block's id is the hash of its bytes in the suite of the key that signed it; read from the block alone, a genesis names its own and a later block hashes as its `prev` does, and a node computes the id with the author's keys before it judges (copies of one block are taken once). A message that names a suite is refused (`bad_hash`), as is a CBOR message with a fourth entry, and an id in any other hash is `bad_hash`. Door `unwrap` and `cbor_decode` take `held`; new door `id_of`; `seal_copy` signs its header in the signing key's suite.
- **The two engines read rotations alike** (a fuzzer aimed at A to E found where they still split; both now agree on every world it makes). ### 1.6 revisions after the audit (2026-10-19)

A read-only audit compared the Rust core with the oracle and tried the spec against strangers. These nine rules close what it found. 1.6 is still year zero and still unpublished to any registry, so they are revisions of 1.6, not a new version: nobody holds a 1.6 block or package that they could break. Each rule has vectors that the core and the oracle both pass (`spec/vectors.json`, made by `sdk/javascript/test/make-vectors-refine.cjs`); the audit worlds are permanent tests in `test/audit/`.

1. A block speaks for one party: a block signed `as` a circle counts for the circle only if its author holds `sign` there (`may_act_as`); otherwise for no one, in every deal and ownership fold.
2. The maker of a thing made `as` a circle is the circle, so shares and stock made for a business are the business's.
3. A bearer promise's side is the named thing's current owner, so the holder's accept binds; `valid` leaves that judgement to the fold.
4. An allocation starts a deal of its own, so several takers on one open offer no longer share a stage or count each other's payments.
5. A promise's terms are fixed once written: an `updates` of a promise is its author's stop and nothing else (refused as `required`, counted for nothing by a fold).
6. A transfer or work block that a side or a named confirmer `defeats` stops counting toward the stage.
7. Reversibility is closed by default: an act that moves things or binds someone is irreversible for an agent unless a trusted schema (the grantor's, or one the grantor confirmed), named in `refs.schema`, says `undo: ok`; a stranger's schema can neither block nor unblock another person's act.
8. A reading counts as grounds only if the sensor itself or an operator of the sensor (an `operator` grant naming it in `refs.subject`) signed it.
9. Member acceptances and stops are derived from the member grant they answer or update; no hidden `kind` or `subject` is needed.

Also: the core and the oracle now give identical answers for every fold over every audited world and every vector story (`node test/audit/run.cjs`), which settled seven small differences the spec text decides (what a stage reports before a binding accept, confirmers named as `circle#role`, the chain's head, the earliest owner transfer, and others). Door ops `ownership`, `where_now` and `under_ok` are named in `protocol.json`.

### Phase 4 of the 1.6 refinement (2026-10-19): a sealed limit, the sensor's operator, the signed chunk count

Still year zero and unpublished. Patrick approved each decision.

- **The limit stays secret and still counts.** A grant's `scope.limit_commitment` is a salted commitment (privacy.md) to `{ limit, per }`. Every reader can see that a limit exists and was fixed at signing (a grant never changes; a new amount is a new grant that `updates` the old one). The amount, the period and the salt sit in the `observe` of kind `limit` sealed to the receiver; `limit_opens` checks that the observe opens the grant. Rule 7 (reversibility) now asks for "a commitment to a limit, or a visible one". A visible `scope.limit` stays allowed; a grant carries one or the other. A child of a sealed grant must carry a limit; whether its amount is no larger is checked only on the principal's node. Door ops `limit_commitment` and `limit_opens`; SDK wrappers in all five languages.
- **The sensor's operator is the existing `operator` grant.** The sensor's owner signs it, `refs.subject` the sensor, `refs.to` the one who may sign its readings. No new field. An operator grant that names a `subject` makes its author no one's operator (`operators_of`, the identity fold and the oracle skip it), so an owner cannot rotate the keys of the device she appointed.
- **The Merkle chunk count is signed with the root.** `state.chunks` sits beside the `{ scheme: "merkle", value }` mark; `merkle_verify(block, chunk, index, proof)` reads both from the block. The count is no longer an argument. Wrappers `merkle.root`, `proof`, `verify` in all five SDKs.
- **Base schemas are trusted by hash, never by publisher** (`spec/schemas/README.md`, a pointer in `core.md` and `things.md`).
- **Wording.** In prose, "agent" is now "autonomous system" (spec, README, CLAUDE, doc comments). The actor kind value `agent`, field and function names are unchanged, and the spec says so. `TODOS.md`: decide before the freeze whether to rename the value.
- **Decisions recorded** in `spec/EXPERIMENTS.md`: `confirms` and `defeats` stay two verbs; `under` and `schema` stay two keys.

## Batch 7: the evening's decisions, the ten sharpenings and the late protocol follow-ups (adds to 1.7)

## Batch 12: Patrick's decisions A to F on the final checks (2026-10-31) (adds to 1.7)
- **A. A pay-in that says neither `kept` nor `held` is never spent (Rust core and oracle together).** Claims draw only on the backing others signed; her pay-in counts toward no payout. Only `kept` is spent. The nine pool vectors of batches 5 and 8 keep their answers: their heads now say `kept`, and the schema they name declares `pay_in`.
- **B. No `keepers` named means no receipt clock (core and oracle).** The fallback that made the issuer's, or the other side's, own receipt the clock is deleted: no `due` on an event wait, no `received_at`, so no "knowing". A claim of invasion still shows `notified` from the accused's own signed receipt, because a claim has no promise to name a clock (an open question in the batch 12 report).
- **C. A repeat with no `by` starts no occurrence (core and oracle).** Only the names in `by` start one. An event `when` (not a repeat) is unchanged: the receiving side's ask.
- **D. A thing is owned only once its own record shows it is an instance (core and oracle).** It names its kind (`refs.of`), or it was made (`refs.derives_from`), or it names what it is part of or where it is (`refs.subject`, `refs.place`). A bare recipe or kind has no owner and a transfer of it moves nothing. No new field. 243 older door cases and 2 fold cases gave their instances a kind, so their answers stand.
- **E. Whoever lets a thing go may name a clock for its takers (core and oracle).** `state.keepers` on an open hand-over (a new field of a transfer; never on a transfer to a named receiver). The first taker the clock received is the first owner; two received at one moment are a conflict. With no clock named, or none received, the takers are read in causal order, as before.
- **F. The schemas' words match the spec text.** "note" stays for the bearer slip only (refusal dials say "signal"; the keeping schema says `loss` by name), and "keeper" is only the clock (the sample term's `keeper` is `holder`, the keeping holder is the "holder"). Every schema block has a new hash, so every vector that holds or names one was re-hashed and re-signed (`sdk/javascript/test/rewrite-batch12.cjs`; 349 door cases were re-hashed in all, for the schema words, the kinds and the pool terms).
- **G. `limits.bound_points` = 256 stays.** The several-referees rules and the backup chain stay in the core for 1.7.
- **Audit and necessity.** The audit worlds' instances name their kind. `tools/necessity.cjs` aborted at once because its scratch copy lacked `sdk/rust/src`, which the audit's FoodX check reads; the copy now includes it.
- **Vectors:** 25 new door cases, "batch 12"; 8 flipped in place (named in the batch 12 report); 10 re-sorted (same answer, new hash order).
