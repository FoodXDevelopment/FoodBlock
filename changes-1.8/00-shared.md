# FoodBlock 1.8: the shared ground

Every stage of 1.8 stands on this page. A stage writer uses these words with these meanings, puts these sentences where this page says, and defines none of them again. Where a stage needs a word this page lacks, it adds it here first. Where this page and a stage's text disagree, this page wins until the owner says otherwise. Nothing is locked until every stage is in (decision 3).

Sources: `CHANGES-1.8.md`, "Decided 2026-11-11" (the six sentences, the seven decisions, the settled points), and the review in `~/foodx/research/2026-11-11-foodblock-1.7-review/`. Report numbers below are batch 3 (`b3/NN`) or batch 2 (`b2/NN`).

## 1. The six sentences, made exact

**1. `valid` means only: who said these bytes.** *(core.md, section 8, replacing its opening and checks 5 to 7.)*

> `valid(block, held)` answers one question: who said these bytes. It is true when the block follows sections 1 to 4 and canonical.md, its pieces are held (A block's pieces, below; `unmet` naming each one missing), and the key its pieces name, in a suite this reader knows (`unknown_suite`), opens its signature over its header (`bad_signature`, `bad_hash`). It reads no `prev`, no other link, no schema and no clock. A refusal is final for those bytes. `unmet` and `unknown_suite` are no refusal: this reader cannot judge the block yet, so it is kept, counted in no fold, and judged when the piece is held or the suite is known. Where the block sits on its key's line is the line's question (folds.md, A line). Whether this reader can read it is `read`'s. A block that is valid and not read is kept as its header and counts in no fold; it is never refused for being unreadable.

**2. What is left.** *(folds.md, a new section "What is left", replacing Orderer, One whole, and the order sentences of Ownership and A pot.)*

> A **measure** is an amount its owner stated: a thing's or a circle's `pack`, an open offer's `terms.quantity` or that of a promise a grant names in `scope.on`, a promise's `terms.backing` with the pay-ins its holder accepted, a grant's `scope.limit`, what a side of a binding deal is owed. What each owner owns of a measure is drawn on along one line (A line). A draw holds its amount from where it is signed. It takes when it is above nothing and, with the draws before it on that line that take and are not freed, comes to no more than what its owner owns of the measure. A draw that does not fit takes nothing and is never cut down to what is left. A payment toward a price is not a draw: it is a plain sum of what the payee accepted, counted toward the line or price it is `for`.

**3. A date binds only the one who wrote it, or the one she named to write it.** *(blocks.md, Time, written by stage 1.)*

> A block's `at` is read only against its signer. A time is read against anyone else only from a clock named, in the word the time is about, by the one it is read against (A named clock). A block's date on a line is the latest `at` from it back to the start of the line the question reads, read whole; on a line not read whole it has no date for that question. A block with no `at` is past every end.

**4. Under a grant, a block binds the giver only if it matches a promise she signed.** *(authority.md, Who a block speaks for, replacing the grant bullet's test.)*

> A block under a grant binds the grant's giver only where it keeps to a promise she signed that the grant names in `scope.on` (Keeps to), its draw fits on the grant's line (A limit is counted along one line), and it stands (blocks.md, Finality). Any other block speaks for its signer alone.

**5. Answer only from a line read whole.** *(folds.md, A line, last paragraph.)*

> A fold that reads an order, a sum or what a key had seen answers only on the line it names, read whole from the block back to the line's start. Otherwise its answer is `not_shown` with `lacks`, the blocks it would need. It never answers from the part it holds.

**6. What can still move back has not moved yet.** *(blocks.md, Finality, first and last sentences.)*

> A word stands when nothing its signer stated before it can still set it aside. Until then it is waiting: it moves nothing to anyone and binds no one. Once it stands, no later block moves back what it moved: a wrong found after that is a sum owed or a claim before a judge, never a reversal.

## 2. One vocabulary

Each word has one meaning. A stage that needs another meaning picks another word.

### The block and its three questions

- **Header.** `{ author, seq, prev, hash }`, plus `key` when the block has `refs.key`, and `skip: true` when the block says so (stage 7 deletes `withheld`, which stage 2 built here: row 110). Every block but a genesis is signed over its header; a genesis is signed over its own bytes and has no header. The header carries nothing else: no `draws`, no `at`, no `after`. *(privacy.md, The public header; core.md, section 2.)*
- **`refs.key`.** On every block signed by a key other than its author's genesis key: the id of the key grant that brought that key in. The header repeats it as `key`, so a header alone names its pieces. The key grant is a `grant` of kind `key` whose `refs.subject` is the block's author; its own author may be another (one of a voice of named people, an operator). The keys it brings in are each `key` voice of the list it states, the one `public_key_sign` it adds under a voice, and the key it states in `shown_key` when that key's hash is a `key_hash` voice of the list its own `refs.key` names. A hash opens no signature, so a key named by hash speaks first in the key grant that shows it, and its later blocks name that key grant. *(core.md, section 5.)*
- **A block's pieces.** Its author's genesis, the block its `refs.key` names, and that block's pieces. Nothing else: not `prev`, and not a grant it acts under, an operator grant included. Who said a machine's bytes is its own key, for a machine is an actor with its own genesis; whether its block binds its operator is whom it speaks for, which reads the operator grant as a block named for its meaning. They are grounded in the genesis by construction, so a ring of forged key grants has no pieces. A key grant that is valid brings its key in for `valid` whether or not this reader can read it; one that is not read gives its key no voice in any fold.
- **Rests on.** A block **rests on** its pieces, and only on them. `valid` waits for its pieces and nothing else. A line is read, never rested on.
- **Names for its meaning.** The blocks a block names that its meaning reads: `refs.under`, `refs.answers`, `refs.updates`, `refs.for` (on every type), `refs.parent`, `refs.schema`, `state.counted_to`, a grant's `scope.on`. `protocol.json`, `needs`, keeps its name and lists exactly these; `refs.author` and `refs.prev` leave it.
- **Valid** (in plain words, *sound*). Who said these bytes: sentence 1. The op is `valid`.
- **Read.** This reader can read the block: it holds the body, the body passes check 7 against the schemas it holds, and every block it names for its meaning is held, valid and read (`names_missing`, listing the id of each one not held, whose cure is to fetch it; `names_unread`, listing the id of each one held and not valid or not read, which fetching does not cure: `read`'s own codes, never `unmet`). A block that fails is **not read**: kept as its header, listed with its code, counted in no fold, never refused. The op is `read(block, held)` (check 7); it gives every code it finds. A named schema not held leaves the fields it might declare unjudged.
- **`unjudged`.** A block a node holds whose pieces are not all held, or whose suite it does not know; it is kept, counted in no fold, and judged when it can be. It is not `waiting`, which is said only of a word that does not yet stand.
- **The chain fold.** Where a block sits on its key's line: gaps, pairs, skips. It is a reading. Nothing waits on it and nothing is refused by it.

### Lines

- **Line.** One key's blocks, ordered by `seq` and joined by `prev`. A keeper's receipts are a line like any other; that is all a clock's order is. Nothing orders two blocks of different lines but a ref: a block is after every block it names.
- **Start.** The block where the line a question reads begins. Each fold that reads a line names its start where it is defined:
  - a draw on a measure: the block that speaks for her (signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant), where its owner came to own her amount (her thing, her make, her first use, her offer, her head, her yes on which a draw to her lands, or her accept of a part passed to that key). A part passes to another key's line only as One start and rule 5 say. The output of a make is always the owner's of what was taken; three cases say only when the record shows it, and so where her line for it starts:
    1. **A make that speaks for her**, signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant so that it binds her (sentence 4; authority.md, Blocks that bind): the outputs it names from her inputs are hers from that block, and it is her start for each. No further yes is needed: the make is her own word.
    2. **A make by someone else from her goods** (toll work: a miller, a baker, an abattoir working on what she owns): the output is hers from her `accepted` answer to it, or her tool's under rule 10, inside a deal that already binds her and for no more than it owes her. That answer is her start.
    3. **Any other claim that a thing is hers** (a block by one who holds no grant of hers and has no deal with her that names an output as made from her goods): nothing is hers on the record until she herself answers `accepted`. A stranger's word never starts her line.

    In none of them does a maker's own block give the maker the output of another's inputs;
  - a block under a grant: the block the grant names in `refs.meter`, which every grant that can bind names; only for a grant that binds nothing and names none, the block that brought the acting key in;
  - a keeper's reading for a word: her receipt of the word;
  - a date: the start of the line the question reads.
- **One start.** An accept that brings an amount onto a line (her accept of a draw to her, a key's accept of a part passed to it, a hand's accept of a hand-over, a take-up) is that line's start for it, and an amount has one start. Where two such accepts of one draw, or two take-ups of one freed amount, by two keys that may each give it, do not see each other (neither in the other's causal past), neither starts a line: the yes is given, so the amount is its receiver's (hers; the circle's for a hand-over or take-up), but on no line, and every draw on it by either key takes nothing (`not_on_line`); each receiver of such a draw holds its signer's word as in a fork (rule 4). A later block of a key that may give that accept, whose `after` names both, is the one start. This is two keys acting apart, perhaps two people: it proves no one lied, lists no `pair` and taints no later block (two blocks of one key on one `prev` or one `seq` stay a pair: Pair). With no clock, the two are seen only by a reader who holds both; a reader holding one reads it as the start. Who wants a start final names a clock whose one line orders the accepts (the first it received is the start, and a draw on the other takes nothing, `not_on_line`), or keeps one key for accepting. Where another fact already fixes the line, it wins and no second start can arise: a grant's end frees to the line of the key that signed the grant; a circle that names `refs.meter` counts on the meter's line; a part a voice passes lands only on the key its key grant names. *(folds.md, What is left.)*
- **Read whole.** A reader holds a line **read whole** from a block back to a start when it holds every block reached from the first by `prev` until the start, the two ends included, and each is valid and read. The line is what `prev` joins: a `seq` that jumps with no `skip` is no hole. A block held only as its header leaves the line not whole, whatever the header says: a header shows no `after`, so it cannot show what its key had seen.
- **`not_shown`, `lacks`.** A fold whose line is not read whole answers `not_shown` (a new reason word: `unmet` means a missing piece and `not read` means one block; this means an answer) and lists in `lacks` each block it would need: its id where a held block's `prev` names it, or where it is held only as its header or not read; past a block not held, `{ author, key, seq }` for each `seq` down to the next block of that key held, or to the start (none under a `skip`). Its answer is then `{ not_shown: true, lacks }` and nothing else, in every fold. Filling a hole never changes an answer, because with the hole there was none. An end this reader holds and cannot read (a stop that is not read) is no end and no proof of none: a block whose answer turns on it answers `not_shown`, the end in `lacks`. *(authority.md, When an end reaches a block under a grant.)*
- **A line goes on past its key's replacement only for what its person owns.** A key grant that puts a new key in an old key's place continues the old key's line from the old key's last block that counts (the key grant itself, when the old key signed it; else the block it names in `state.counted_to`). A grant's line never follows: the giver signs a new grant for a new hand. Two key grants that continue one line are read as Pair says; two accepts or take-ups apart, by One start (no pair). A **take-up** continues a hand's line for the circle's things, never for her own (Circles, What a hand holds for the circle): from the block of her line it continues from, the amount is drawn along the line of the key that signed the take-up, and that line is read whole from a later draw back through the take-up and on back along her line to its start. Two take-ups by one key are on one line: its `seq` orders them, or they are a pair of that key. A take-up is a start: two by different keys apart, or a take-up and her own later block that still counts for the circle past the block it continues from, are read by One start.
- **Pair.** Two blocks of one key that name one `prev` or state one `seq`: a pair of that key, which proves the key lied (Cross-cutting rules, A fork). Two key grants of one voice, neither in the other's causal past, are a pair of that voice: neither stands until one block of that voice has both in its causal past. *(identity.md, Her voices.)*

### Standing

- **Stands.** A word **stands** when every block it names for its meaning stands; it has passed every end that could reach it (The cut); if its key's voice states a `reach`, her clock is closed through her keepers' receipt of it plus the reach with no end of the key among the receipts the closing names, or a voice above that key has confirmed it; if its key's voice states a cap, it fits the cap, or a voice above that key has confirmed it; for a move `as` a circle, a row holds for it, counted at one block, with hands that each stand and have passed every end of their hand (Circles); and its own condition has held where it stands (read in the causal past of the block it is read for: promises.md, What a condition moved stays moved). *(blocks.md, Finality.)* A `reach` reaches only her words that move or bind: her promises, transfers and grants, her key grants of her own list among them; a key grant is never its own end. Her readings (`observe`), and a key grant she signs in someone else's list, wait on nothing of hers. *(identity.md, Reach.)*
- **Signed again.** A later counted block of the same key that names the word in `refs.confirms` and has in its causal past the cutoffs that closed that key's wait; for a voice of named people, `needed` of them each so, each having signed it or confirmed it before. *(identity.md, When a key grant stands.)*
- **That key's wait.** The `wait` of the voice the key signs as (for a key under a voice, its voice's), in the list of the one the word speaks for, run from her keepers' receipt of the first signing (A named clock). A wait of zero has run at once and asks nothing of the clock; a wait above zero runs only on her clock. The first voice's wait is always zero, and so is an operator's. A voice that states no `wait` brings in nothing by itself: a voice above must sign.
- **Waiting.** A word that does not yet stand is **waiting**. It moves nothing to anyone and binds no one else; a draw that is waiting still holds its amount against its own owner. A fold that meets it lists it in `waiting: [{ block, on }]`, one entry for each thing it waits for (`wait`, `reach`, `confirm`, `end`, `named`, `hands`, `condition`; `hands`: a move `as` a circle until the stated number of its hands that still stand are gathered, Circles); a word that can never stand is listed nowhere. **What stands stays:** no later block changes what a standing word moved or bound, bar the new owner's own word, and, for a deal's stage only, the bench.
- **Relier.** One who used a word without having seen its end: the author of a counted block that answers the word, accepts or names a block made under it, or acts on it, who is not the word's signer, not an actor she operates, and not acting under a grant she gave or one passed on from it, and whose block has not seen the end. A move `as` a circle has as its signer all its hands together (Circles, Hand). *(blocks.md, The cut, written by stage 1.)*
- **Seen.** A block has seen an end, for a reader, by exactly two facts: the end is in the block's causal past as that reader reads it (bodies only: a header shows nothing seen; a receipt the block's author signed of the end is this fact, because the receipt derives from the end); or the word names a clock and one keeper's line holds her receipt of the end before her receipt of the block. Nothing else is seen. When an end reached a node, and a date on the end, show nothing. Whether the end stands is read on all the reader holds; a block that names the end in `refs.confirms` has not seen it. *(blocks.md, The cut, written by stage 1.)*

### Draws

- **Draw.** A counted block that speaks for an owner, names a measure, and takes an amount of what she owns of it (all she owns of it when it states none): a transfer `to_owner`, a let-go to an open receiver, a make naming it in `state.inputs`, an allocation, a payout, a block under a limited grant (at its `state.draws`), a grant passed on (at its own `limit`), a grant that names an instance in `refs.what` (at its `scope.limit`: the part it passes to its holder), a grant that names a promise in `scope.on` (at all of its quantity she has not drawn, passed to the grant's line; so a second grant naming it while the first holds it takes nothing, `past`). A draw signed off the measure's line takes nothing (`not_on_line`).
- **A grant's draw never lands.** It names the grant's holder, and the part stays hers: held on her line and drawn on the grant's line by blocks that bind her, until it is freed (Freed: the holder's own decline or stop, or an end of the grant he has seen), and then only what the grant's line has not drawn comes back. *(folds.md, What is left; authority.md, Her quantity is the measure, Toll work.)*
- **What a tool holds for her.** What a tool makes or receives for her under her grant is held on the grant's line as hers: it is part of what that grant holds, exactly as a part passed to the grant is. **A hand-back:** a tool may always hand back to its principal what it holds for her, by a draw `to_owner` her of an amount on the grant's line. It needs no promise in `scope.on`, no type in `scope.types` beyond the grant's own existence, and no room under the limit, because it gives nothing away and only returns her own. It lands on her own key's accept, which starts that key's line for the part (Start, One start). It needs no meter's receipt, even where `refs.meter` names her node: a meter counts only what is spent. It binds her to nothing, so `under_ok` (which answers for binding only) is false for it, and the draws fold counts it. It is not a case of No one is both sides (rule 6): no one else is a side. **At the grant's end** (an end that stands: her stop, its `until`, the removal of the tool's key), what is still held on its line and is in no draw that has landed or still holds for another is freed to her, on the line of the key that signed the grant, its one start (One start): a later draw by that key that has the end in its causal past may draw on it, and any other key's takes nothing (`not_on_line`); until the end is seen as the cut requires (rule 1), such a draw is waiting. While the grant stands, her own key never draws on the tool's line (`not_on_line`): one measure, one line. She gets it by the tool's hand-back or by ending the grant. What a hand holds for a circle is this same rule (Circles, What a hand holds for the circle). *(folds.md, What is left; authority.md, Blocks that bind, A hand-back.)*
- **Read once, along the line.** A fold reads each owner's draws on a measure once, from her start in `seq` order, against a running amount. No fold compares every pair of draws, or orders draws by hash, by the size of a causal past, or by arrival. *(folds.md, What is left.)*
- **A first taker is no draw.** What a let-go took is no one's; the first use that takes it after is not a draw and keeps its own rule. *(folds.md, Ownership.)*
- **Holds.** From where it is signed on the line, a draw's amount is out of what its owner may draw on. Her own signed word counts against her at once.
- **Lands.** The amount becomes the receiver's where the draw stands and every word it names is in the causal past: her yes (her own counted `accepted` answer to the draw; or, in the draw's own past, her own counted accept of the deal's first promise it is `for`, or her own first promise of that deal that the draw's signer accepted: read from blocks, never from the deal's stage) and its condition, if it carries one. A draw whose receiver's yes is already in its own causal past lands where it is signed (provisional, awaiting the owner: row 121). A draw that names no receiver lands where it stands.
- **Freed.** The amount is its owner's again, for her later draws that have the freeing in their causal past, by the receiver's own counted `declined` or `stopped` answer, or by an end of the draw that the receiver has seen before any yes. An end she has not seen frees nothing yet: a later draw counting on it is waiting; once she answers or sees it, that draw reads `past` unless the freeing is in its own past (row 123). A no or an end that has her yes in its past frees nothing.
- **Reasons a draw takes nothing:** `past` (more than is left), `not_on_line`, `pair` (one of a pair, and each later draw of that key on the same measure: A fork), `unit` (another unit or kind), `no_measure` (the thing states no `pack`, or her amount of it is not said), `not_owner`. One list, in `protocol.json`; no other names. `pair` is the one fold code for a block of a fork, a draw or not (row 41). A fold's answer lists them in `draws: [{ block, by, to, amount, takes, why, state }]`, `state` one of `held`, `landed`, `freed`.

### Amounts

- **An amount** is `{ value, unit }` or `{ value, unit, of }`: `value` a decimal string of zero or more where it is promised, moved or paid; `unit` the author's own text (`kg`, `sack`, `GBP`, `KES`); `of`, when given, the id of the kind it is an amount of. Two amounts are of one kind when their `unit` is the same text and their `of` the same id, or neither has one. **Money is an amount.** The money shape (`minor`, `currency`, `currency_unit`, `decimals`) leaves. *(encoding.md, Shapes.)*
- **A sum** a fold gives is printed in one form, so two builds agree: the shortest decimal text, with no exponent, no leading zero but a single `0` before the point, no trailing zero after the point, no point when there is no fraction, `0` for nothing, and never `-0`. *(encoding.md, Shapes.)*
- **Nothing is rounded.** Where a weighed price does not come to a payable sum, the payee's own `covers` closes the gap. *(promises.md, Terms.)*
- **`covers`: the most.** Of several answers of the payee to one payment, the one whose `covers` says most counts: each is her word against herself, and binding only grows. *(promises.md, Settling; folds.md, A promise's stage.)*
- **Who holds a thing** is a reading, never a rule: the receiver of the latest counted `to_holder` transfer of it, by its owner or a holder she named, that the receiver accepted; else its owner. A node may compute it; no draw, title or stage reads it. *(folds.md, Where a thing is now, stage 3.)*
- **A pot's kind** is its backing's kind; with no backing, the one kind all its pay-ins share; else it has no kind and pays nothing out. *(folds.md, A pot.)*
- **`uncovered`** is `{ from, to }` at every stage: `to`, the paying side, one amount in the price's kind; `from`, the delivering side, one amount per line (`[{ line, amount }]`). *(folds.md, What each fold returns.)*
- **One set of field names**, everywhere:

| What | Field |
|---|---|
| The stated measure of a thing or a circle | `state.pack` (a circle's parts: `{ value: "100", unit: "part" }`) |
| What a transfer or a payment moves | `state.amount` (replaces `state.money` and 12's `state.count`) |
| What a make takes | `state.inputs: [{ from, amount }]` (not `takes`) |
| What a block under a limited grant draws | `state.draws: { grant, amount }`, in the body only |
| A price, a limit, a cap on each block, a backing, a forfeit, a covered sum | `terms.price` (per unit: `{ amount, per }`), `scope.limit`, `scope.each`, `terms.backing`, `terms.forfeit`, `state.covers`: each an amount |
| A payment split | `state.split: [{ for, line, amount, occurrence }]` |

- **Added only within one measure.** An amount is added only to amounts of the same measure (the block its transfer names in `refs.what`), or, with no measure named, toward the one line or price its transfer is `for`. No fold adds across measures, so "what Harry is owed, £80" never adds to £80.
- **A transfer names an instance, or names none.** One that names none (cash, an unweighed pile) moves nothing on the record: it counts toward its line only on the receiver's accept, or a signer her account word names (`state.by`). A claim to money is a deal, never an amount. What is kept apart is a thing (a marked tin), never an amount handed `to_holder`.

### Clocks

- **A clock** (a named clock) is `keepers: { names, needed }` in the word whose time or order it reads. Each name is a block id and names a **line**: a person's id, her genesis key's line; the id of a key grant that adds one key under a voice, that key's line; any other block, its key's blocks after it. `circle#role` leaves `keepers`. A clock is written by whoever signs the word; whoever accepts the word or acts on it has read it. Nobody need name one, and none is ever filled in.
- **A keeper** is whoever keeps a line a clock names: a person at a phone shop, a node, a till. Her reading for a word starts at her receipt of the word.
- **Receipt.** A keeper's counted `observe` of kind `receipt`, `refs.derives_from` the blocks it names, one or more: when she signed it she held them. A receipt that names several blocks gives them no order, and no rule that needs an order reads them from it.
- **Cutoff.** A keeper's counted `observe` of kind `cutoff`: `state.when` an instant T, `refs.subject` the word. It closes over its own line: every receipt of hers before it on her line, dated at or before T, of a block the word's rule reads, was received by T, named or not. `refs.derives_from` lists them for a reader who does not hold the line. A receipt of hers after the cutoff on her line, dated at or before T, of a block the word's rule reads, was left out: it shows the cutoff false, and the cutoff closes nothing.
- **The four readings, and no others.** *By T*: `needed` lines each hold a receipt of the block dated no later than T. *Not by T; a wait has run*: `needed` lines each hold a cutoff through T (for a wait: through the keeper's receipt of its start plus the wait) that leaves no such receipt before it; until then it is pending, and no reader's clock closes it. *Which came first*: read from a line's order, never from dates. *How much, and which of many*: read along one line only; a word that asks either names one.
- **Two powers, kept apart.** A clock **dates** when it only says when. It **gates** when the word says a block made on it counts only once the clock holds a receipt of it. A gate is never the only way: a gated block also counts once the word's signer names it in a later counted block of her own.
- **One to narrow, the stated number to widen.** That an end, a stop or a "no" came first: one keeper's line is enough. That a block came in time, that a wait has run, that a key grant has closed: `needed` lines.
- **A side's clock counts only against that side.** A keeper who is a side of the word, or whom a side operates, binds that side by every receipt another holds in his block's causal past, and by nothing of hers does another lose what his block already holds. Her order is never read against the other side (an offerer's own till does not choose between takers: each receipt binds the offerer).
- **What a keeper signed and another built on stands against those who named her.** A clock is read in the causal past of the block that relies on it. One who relies on a clock he did not name is safe when his block has the keeper's block in its past, and bound by nothing of hers that it has not.
- **Silence.** When a named clock is silent, what waits on it waits; nothing fills its silence. A clock that cannot close leaves its question open, for the judge the sides chose.
- **Changing a clock.** A clock is changed by those who named it: a counted block by each, `refs.subject` the word, stating new `keepers` and, for each old line, the last receipt that counts. What was read on the old clock and built on stands. A keeper's own key replacement continues her line (A line goes on).
- **A door log.** A node's line of receipts for one person's door, on a key it keeps for that door alone: what it took in for her, each receipt sealed to her and to the pusher. *(nodes.md, The arrival log; stage 2.)*
- **`dropped`.** A node's counted `observe` of kind `dropped` on the same log, `refs.derives_from` the blocks (or receipts) it no longer holds: its honest drop, shown on the line that took them in.
- **`log`, `seen`.** Two fields of a pull: `log` names the log the asker reads, by the id of the block that brought its key in; `seen` names a receipt on that log the asker holds, and an answer to it is the node's word that it read its log at least that far.

### People, keys and acts

- **A voice** is a key her standing list names (a `key`, a `key_hash`, or a count of named people). It speaks as her, whole. The only bounds it may carry are a `wait` (on the words that let another act), a `reach` (a wait before its word stands) and, on a `key` voice, a **cap** (below). Each is changed only by a voice above it, the first voice's by itself, and only the first voice changes `keepers` (row 11). An operator is a voice above a machine's whole list. `voices` and `keepers` are typed in `protocol.json` (`shapes.voice`, `shapes.keepers`), so check 2 reads them. Any key may be a voice, a synced passkey too (row 70). A **key under a voice** is a key a key grant adds below one voice, outside the list: it speaks while that voice does, signs no key grant, and is bounded as its voice is (a key for one counterparty is one).
- **A voice's cap.** A `key` voice may state `each` (an amount: the most one word of that key may move or bind), `limit` (an amount: the most its words may move or bind together) and, only with `limit` and with the list's `keepers`, `every` (a duration: the limit renews each period, read on her clock). A word of that key that a reach would hold is counted as authority.md, A draw, counts a block under a grant (its deal's total at the quantity it binds, with the backing and forfeit that bind her; a transfer at its `amount`; a payment or delivery `for` a deal a word of that key bound, of no more than it owes, at nothing more; a grant at its `worst_case` `at_most`), and a `limit` is drawn on along that key's own line from the block that brought it in (What is left). A word with no amount of the cap's kind to read is above any cap. A word that fits stands as any word of that voice does; one above the cap stands only when a voice above that key has signed it (rule 13), and until then is waiting (`on: confirm`). A word that only narrows is never above it. **A key under a capped voice draws only on a part passed to it**, as two devices sharing one stock each hold a stated share (rule 5): the key grant that adds it may state a `limit`, a part of the voice's, and an `each`, no more than the voice's; that key grant is a draw on the voice's line at its `limit`, never coming back; the key counts its words along its own line against that part; so the voice and its keys together never pass the voice's `limit`. With no part passed, a key under a voice that states a `limit` has nothing to draw on: every word of it above zero is above the cap. A thief who adds a key gets no room that was not first the voice's. The cap is in her standing list, so any stranger reads it (rule 9); it bounds her voice, and only a grant bounds a tool. *(identity.md, A voice's cap: stage 1's file, built in stage 4.)*
- **A tool** is an actor of its own that she operates. It speaks for her only under her grant, bounded by amount (the grant's line) and by terms (`scope.on`). There is no third kind: a grant to one of her own voices fences nothing.
- **Type, act, move, deed** (decision 5). A **type** is what a grant covers (`scope.types`, the seven or `type.kind`). An **act** is the name a person gives to what she is doing; it is a reading and no field. A **move** is one signed step; a circle row's field `move` names which kind of step `as` the circle the row governs (Circles). A **deed** is something done in the world.
- **A named signer** is one some other person's word names to say a thing: a keeper, a meter, a counter, a payment confirmer, a grader, a referee, a person counted in her voices.

### Grants

- **Keeps to.** A promise C keeps to a promise A when C's `state` and `refs`, in canonical form, are A's key for key, bar a closed list of eight: what every block has of its own (`author`, `prev`, `after`, `key`, `seq`, `at`, `nonce`, `skip`); `state.audience`, where C is public or sealed to the grantor and the holder; C's links (`answers` and `for` naming A or what keeps to A, `under` naming the grant); a side A leaves open; instances of a kind A names; a quantity inside A's range; a price, where A states none, she pays it, and the other side signs C; C's own `expires`. Nothing else may differ, so no block that binds her under a grant carries the other side's free words: a counter that adds a note keeps to nothing. She may always accept anything herself. An open side in her promise (item 4) is what lets her tool answer a stranger. *(authority.md, Blocks that bind.)*
- **`refs.meter`.** One id: the start of one line on which draws are counted, read by one rule (authority.md, A limit is counted along one line, A meter she names). It is named in two places: on a grant, the line every block under it is counted on, and every grant that can bind (one with `scope.on` or `refs.what`) names one; on a circle's founding block, the line every draw that speaks for the circle is counted on (circles.md, A circle's line), and with none each thing the circle owns is drawn on along the line of the key that signed its start, and past a hand-over or a hand's seat, along the line that took it up (Lines, A line goes on; Circles, What a hand holds for the circle). A grant to a role, or an open grant, has no holder's line: it binds only through a meter she names, a key not the holder's.
- **`state.drawn`.** On a receipt of a meter she names: `[{ of, amount, total }]`, one item per measure the block draws on (`of` the grant for its `limit`, or the promise in `on` or the instance in `refs.what` for its part), `amount` the block's draw, `total` the running total on the meter's line with it. The receipt is the meter's word and binds the giver who named her (rule 3).
- **`terms.deadline`.** A time in her promise, read on the clock its `terms.keepers` names: when her tool must have chosen among offers that keep to it. The same word as a condition's `deadline`: a time on a named clock. Read only for a block under a grant; her own accept is hers at any time.
- **`terms.fallback`.** A closed list of one, `lowest`, stated only with `terms.deadline` and a clock that names one line. After the deadline only her tool's accept of the lowest offer counts: among the offers that keep to her promise which the keeper's line received before her cutoff through the deadline, and still stood there, the lowest price (of two equal, the first received); the accept has that cutoff in its causal past. Nothing binds until her tool signs it. Any other accept after the deadline is `after_deadline`. *(authority.md, Blocks that bind, Her deadline; decision 7.)*
- **A stop of one thing.** Her own counted `observe` with `state.status: stopped`, `state.stop: onward`, `refs.what` one instance, `state.amount` optionally, and no `refs.updates` or `refs.answers`. It means: no block under any grant of hers, or passed on from one, draws on or moves that thing (with `amount`, none that would leave its grant's line holding less than that much of it undrawn) until her later `updates` of it with `status: resumed` lifts it. It is an end, under the cut, for each such block, read on that grant's own clock (`scope.keepers`). It only narrows: it is not signed twice, no reach holds it, and one keeper's line is enough to show it came first. Her own key is not stopped by it, and a hand-back never is: it returns the thing to her. The lift gives power back, so it is signed twice (rule 13). A block it ends is listed `stopped_thing`. Not a **hold** (a draw's amount): a **stop**. *(authority.md, A stop of one thing.)*

### Deals

- **Request.** An answer that does not yet bind. It waits on its open points, each closed one way: *when* (an `expires`: an end, passed as the cut says), *which* (on an open offer: only the offerer's yes; no keeper's receipt orders takers: Clocks, A side's clock counts only against that side), *whose* (the word stands). *(promises.md, Who may bind.)*
- **`crossed`.** Answers of one deal that bind and do not see each other. Each binds; the deal is read once under each accept (`per_accept`, row 117), and its stage is the furthest of them. No draw reason uses the word. *(folds.md, A promise's stage.)*
- **Bench.** The names that may lower a deal's stage: the confirmers its schema's grounds name and a referee both sides named. A signer who only says a thing happened (one named in a condition's `by` or an account word's `state.by`) is not on it. The bench changes what counts and what is owed, never who owns. *(promises.md, Disputes.)*
- **`terms.waits_on`.** A closed list of two: `goods` (the price waits on the receiver's `accepted` answer to the delivery) or `price` (what moves waits on the payee's signal that the price is paid). Left out, nothing is filled in: due is read from the deal's other terms alone. *(promises.md, Terms.)*
- **Due.** What a deal owes is due where the block that performs it has in its causal past the deal's binding accept, standing; the deal's condition, held; and what `waits_on` names, standing: with `price`, the payee's signal; with `goods`, the receiver's `accepted` answer to the delivery; with neither stated, nothing waits. An occurrence is due from its start. Due is read from the deal's own terms and signed words only: no node adds up payments to answer it (row 118). `pay_by` and `deliver_by` are latest times, never earliest. *(promises.md, Terms, Due.)*
- **The payee's signal.** "Paid in full", said the one way the protocol knows it: a counted `observe` of kind `paid` that speaks for the payee, `refs.subject` the deal, with `state.line` or `state.occurrence` where it is for one line or one occurrence. It is her word against herself: it releases what she was to be paid and makes due whatever waited on that price. As a condition it is `{ signal: "paid", by: [payee], subject: deal }`, read on every node. Her tool gives it only as rule 10 says. It is not `received` (this much came), and not the named reading `paid` (the sum). Built from existing fields; no new field. *(promises.md, Terms, The payee's signal.)*
- **Final.** A sale that states no taking back is final: a taking back is a new promise of the one who takes. *(promises.md, What a promise is.)*
- **Pattern.** A base schema and a worked example over the core: it adds no rule, no fold, no door op and no `protocol.json` entry (`spec/patterns/`). A recall and a warning are patterns. *(stage 5.)*

### Circles

All in circles.md, written by stage 6.

- **A circle's parts.** Its founding block's `pack`, counted in whole parts: `unit` `part`, `value` a whole number of at least 1, no `of` (`shape`). They are an amount of a measure like any other (Draws), with no rule of their own: a draw of a fraction of a part is of another kind and takes nothing (`unit`). The founder owns them first; the founding block is her start. A circle with no `pack` leaves its owners unsaid.
- **Unissued.** Parts of a circle's `pack` that the circle holds of itself, so that no person yet owns them; moved only by an `issue` move, and counted in no row.
- **Row.** One line of a circle's `state.rules`: `{ move, of, by, needed }` (that many holders of the role `by`) or `{ move, of, parts }` (that many of its parts). Every number is a whole number of at least 1, never `all` and never a share. `move` is one of a closed list, `sign`, `take`, `issue`, `admit`, `remove`, `change`, `rekey` (`protocol.json`, `circle.moves`; `rekey` is read only by the rekeys convention). No profile extends a list `valid` reads (row 115). With no row for a move, no one makes it. `change` rows (with `pack` and the founder's `roles`) are read only from the founding block, and nothing gets round the rows: there is no "everyone signed" way.
- **Hand.** A block made on its signer's role or her parts in a circle: a move `as` the circle, or a counted block that names it in `refs.confirms`. Its ends, under the cut: for a role, her leaving, her removal, her close, her member grant's `until`, and a change that takes the role away; for parts, every draw of hers on the circle's parts from where she signs it, landed or not, and her close. A move counts a hand only where the block that completes it has passed every end of that hand. For the cut, a move's signer is all its hands together: no signer of one of its hands, no actor she operates and no one under a grant she gave is the move's relier, and no later block of hers passes her hand's ends. So, with no clock, a gathered move waits until an outside relier uses it, and the circle bears that gap (rule 2). An id counts once (rule 7).
- **`not_voice`.** The fold code for a hand that counts for nothing in its row: no role by a named member grant in her hand's causal past; no parts on her line read whole; an open joiner; unissued parts; the one a `remove` move removes. In prose a circle's hand **counts**; "voice" stays a key of her list.
- **`ways_in`.** A reading (stage 7's test; row 112): every way a person could come to hold each role or part of a circle as its rows stand, and the fewest ids now in it who could take that way, for a stranger to weigh. It binds no one; no word reads it.
- **What a circle's word binds:** the circle's things, never an owner's or a member's other property, wage or body.
- **What a hand holds for the circle.** What a hand starts or receives for the circle (a block that speaks for the circle by a row that holds) is held on that key's line as the circle's, never hers: the same rule as What a tool holds for her (Draws). It reaches another hand's line only by a **take-up**, which continues her line (Lines, A line goes on). **Between hands:** while her seat stands, a `sign` move of the circle, signed by the key whose line holds the amount (any other key's draw on it is `not_on_line`), may hand it to another hand's line, by a draw `to_owner` the circle; the take-up is the accept `as` the circle of the key that takes it up, which continues her line from that draw (the block it answers) and lands it; this gives nothing away, so it is no `take` and No one is both sides (rule 6) does not reach it. **At her seat's end** (her leaving, her removal, the end of her member grant, her close), what is still held on her line as the circle's, and is in no draw that has landed or still holds for another, is freed to the circle, and the take-up is a later `sign` move that has that end in its causal past, read as the cut requires: it names in `state.counted_to` the block of her line it continues from (her own block that ended her seat, where she signed one; else her last block that speaks for the circle), as a key grant names the old key's last block, and the amount is then drawn along the line of the key that signed it. Until the end is seen as the cut requires (rule 1), such a take-up is waiting (`on: end`). Two accepts of one hand-over, or two take-ups, apart: Lines, One start. Her own key, once her seat has ended, draws nothing of the circle's (`not_owner`): she never owned it. A circle that names `refs.meter` is unaffected: its things were always counted on the meter's line. *(circles.md, A circle's line; folds.md, What is left.)*
- **Owners that end in no person.** Circles that own each other's parts are neither refused nor marked by any reason word: a draw onto such a ring takes as any draw does, and each circle's parts count by its owning circle's own rows. `circle_state`'s owners and `ways_in` show, for such a circle, that its owners end in no person (`no_person`); a stranger weighs that himself. Facts, never verdicts.
- **1.7 founding blocks are not valid in 1.8.** Each founder re-signs hers through one fixed map (06-circles.md, section 8), read back to her before she signs; only her key re-founds her circle.

### The test and rule sets

All written by stage 7: core.md sections 10 to 12, privacy.md, nodes.md, the door.

- **The test.** A rule is **protocol** only when, without it, two readers holding the same blocks could answer differently one of four questions about a signed block (*who signed these bytes; whom does it speak for; what of whose does it bind to whom, and on what condition; who owns what it moved*), and the people who rely on that answer are not all signers of one block that could name the rule instead. Everything else is a **reading** (computed from blocks, changing none of the four answers), **library** (a tool, or data a block carries or names by hash) or **a node's own business** (what a machine does with a block: store, send, seal, admit, forget, show). Four classes, no others.
- **Word.** An output that answers one of the four questions. Every other output is a reading. No word reads a view, a profile, a pattern or a reader's clock. **Conformance is per op:** a builder conforms for each op whose cases it passes, and one who passes the protocol ops (`door.protocol`) speaks FoodBlock. Pull, push, the sealed envelope and the compact form are conventions, as email is (row 108).
- **A named reading.** A reading a block may name by hash: the rule set that holds it (`state.rule_set`; a deal is read under the one its first promise states). A reader that runs it computes it; any other holds the block not read (`reading_not_run`), never guesses and never counts it. The condition shapes `settled`, `paid`, `ruled` and `reading` are named readings. The payee's signal (Deals) needs none and is read on every node; so title until paid, on a node that does not run the sum, is read only when written on the payee's signal (row 107). The sum of accepted payments (`paid`, `uncovered`) is a reading a node may run to show a person where a deal stands; a node that does not run it says so. It decides nothing that binds: Due never reads it (row 118).
- **Rule set.** Named by the hash of its manifest (`spec/rule-set.json`: the normative files, by hash). "1.8" is a label. `state.rule_set` (any block, optional): its signer's word on which rule set her block means; `valid` never reads it. A reader that does not run the set it names holds the block not read (`unknown_rules`).
- **Sound part, read part.** The sound part is `valid` (checks 1 to 6 and canonical.md), named by the hash of `spec/sound.json`, the file of its cases, so a wording fix changes no network. The read part is everything else, each reader's choice. ("Sound", in plain words, still means valid.)
- **Continues.** A rule set continues another when every block the earlier one reads gets the same answers under it. **A clean fork** keeps the sound part, or one that continues it: one network. **A split** changes it: another network.
- **A node's word.** What a node signs about what it does with blocks; it binds only that node (nodes.md, A node's word, renamed from A node's promise).
- **Handing on.** Whoever holds an opened sealed block may pass it, same header and signature, in an envelope of its own making, to a reader it chooses; it is the same block.
- **Keeping apart.** Three grains, each made of blocks that exist, none chosen for her: one sealing key for all, a key for one counterparty, an identity for one counterparty (privacy.md).
- **A mount.** `mount(blocks, into?)` and `unmount(mount)`: an opaque handle on a door's kept, judged store. An op given a mount answers exactly as given the same blocks, so a mount changes no answer and is never signed, sent or stored.

## 3. The cross-cutting rules

Each is one rule, stated once, in its home file. Every other file points to it.

1. **The cut** *(blocks.md, The cut).* A standing word (a grant, an offer, a request, a transfer to a named receiver, a key, a member grant, an account word, a claim, a named signer's word) is ended by an **end**: its signer's counted stop, a key grant that leaves the key out, a removal, a newer word of hers that takes its place, a time it states (`until`, `expires`), and for a named signer's word a pair on her key or her own `defeats`. A block made on such a word (a block under it, an answer to it, a block of the key, a payment to it) that has seen an end counts for nothing. Any other passes the word's ends when: an end has it in its causal past (it is inside that end's cut); or the word's signer names it in a later counted block of her own; or, where the word names a clock, `needed` lines received it before any line received an end; or, where the word names none, a relier's counted block uses it. To name or use a block is to name it in any ref but `prev` and `after`, or in `state.counted_to`. Until it passes, it is waiting, whether or not any end exists yet: with no clock, a stop can always still come. Where several ends reach one word, a block that is inside one end's cut must still pass each other end.
2. **Who bears the gap** *(blocks.md, The cut, last paragraph; decision 1).* Her word says which. If it names a clock (her own node will do), her stop counts from the moment that clock has it, and the clock's order decides. If it names none, she bears what was done under it until the relier has seen the stop, and her claim is on whoever went on using it. The protocol fills in neither.
3. **A named signer's word and her liability** *(promises.md, What stands behind a named signer; decision 2).* A condition names a signature, never a fact: what a named signer's word moves, it moves by the word of the one who named her, and he bears her. Her block is a reading: it binds her name to its truth and nothing of hers. Her word counts once it stands; once a relier used it, her own take-back moves nothing (The cut). If she promised nothing, a mistake binds her to nothing; she answers only for a promise she signed (an ordinary offer with `terms.backing`, conditioned on a ruling by the judge it names, taken by the other's accept), or if she lied on purpose to help take someone's goods, which is taking part in a theft and goes to the victim's judge.
4. **A fork** *(folds.md, A fork is a burden).* A pair proves its key lied; it never frees or favours its signer. A thing drawn twice: neither draw lands, its signer owns none of it and may not choose between them, each receiver holds his word, what she paid is `uncovered`, and it ends by a receiver's own letting go or by the judge the receivers choose. A word given twice (a limit, an offer's quantity, her promise under a grant): each block of the pair, and each later block of that key, binds the one it speaks for where a relier used it without the pair in his block's causal past. A named signer's pair is an end of her word for every block that had not used it. A fork on a sealed line is seen only by one who holds both branches.
5. **One measure, one line** *(folds.md, What is left).* Each owner's amount of a measure is drawn on along one line, which it comes onto at one start (Lines, One start). Another key of hers, a till, or a holder under her grant draws only from a part a draw on that line passed to its own line. What a tool holds for her comes back to her own key's line only by its hand-back or the grant's end (Draws, What a tool holds for her); by the same rule, what a hand holds for a circle comes back to the circle at her seat's end (Circles, What a hand holds for the circle). Two devices selling from one stock each hold a stated share.
6. **No one is both sides of a word** *(authority.md, Who a block speaks for).* A block that gives to its own signer, to an actor she operates, or to a circle in which she holds a role or a part, speaks for no one else by a circle's row or under a grant, unless the promise the grant names in `scope.on` names her as that side, or the circle's row for that (`take`) holds without her hand. For a circle, a move gives to her when it is a draw or transfer to her, a promise of the circle with her as the other side, or a grant naming her; or any of these to an actor she operates or a circle in which she holds a role or a part. A member grant is no take: who seats whom is the `admit` row's word (circles.md, Rules).
7. **A count counts ids** *(folds.md, Counts).* An id counts once, by any key that stands for it where the block stands: in a clock's `needed`, a circle's row, a list's `names`. A count is a whole number of named ids, never `all` and never a share of a set that can move: a set its signer can leave out of her causal past is one she can shrink.
8. **What stays public** *(privacy.md, the first paragraph).* One key writes public blocks or sealed blocks, never both: a device, or her AI, that writes both holds two keys. A sealed block, header and all, goes only to its audience, and by handing on to whom they choose. Nothing need be public for two people who know each other to deal. What must be shown is a list of who must be shown: a word with an open set of reliers (an open offer, an open grant, a let-go, a kind, a schema, a rule set named by hash, an address) is public or reaches only those it is handed to; a counted line is shown whole, as bodies, to everyone who relies on it; the pieces of a signer go to whoever must know who signed. A carrier sees who wrote, for whom, how big and when; a keeper sees what she is sent. The spec stops saying anyone can check a chain, its gaps and its forks.
9. **A hidden limit binds nobody** *(authority.md).* Only a limit the other side can read counts. `scope.limit_commitment` and the sealed limit leave; so does `scope.condition` (a fence only her own node computes): a product keeps her node's own lines in a field its own schema declares, which no fold reads.
10. **A tool performs only what a binding deal owes, when it is due** *(authority.md, Blocks that bind).* Under a grant, a tool's `accepted` answer to a payment or a delivery made `for` a deal that already binds its principal, for no more than that deal still owes her, binds her. So does its payee's signal (Deals) for such a deal, where she is the payee and the payments she or her tool has accepted toward that price come to at least the price by the deal's own terms: the tool does that sum for its own principal before it signs, and a tool's signal short of the price speaks for no one else. A tool's transfer toward a price she owes, or of an instance she owes, binds her only where the grant covers that type, the deal already binds her, it moves no more than is owed, it is due by the deal's own terms where the transfer stands, and its draw fits on the grant's line. Due is read from the deal's own terms and signed words only (Deals, Due): with `waits_on: price`, what waits is due once the payee's signal stands; with `waits_on: goods`, the price once the receiver's `accepted` answer to the delivery stands; with neither, nothing waits; no node adds up payments to answer it. A time in them is read on the clock the deal names, and with no clock a time is not shown against the payee or against her, so nothing is due early by a date alone. A grant that does not cover it gives no power to pay. The protocol fills in neither way: whether her tool pays is hers to sign.
11. **Nothing fills silence, where a mend had quietly filled it** *(each in its home file).*
    - **The two-block cash sale** *(things.md, A spot exchange).* The buyer signs first: a promise that names the instance and its price. The seller's transfer `for` it, to her, lands when signed, because her yes is in its past. It states `state.covers` (`required`): what the seller says she has had of the price, `{ value: "0", ... }` if nothing. A missing `covers` is never read as credit, and a buyer's silence never turns a seller's receipt into a debt.
    - **Off-record money** *(things.md, Transfers; promises.md, the account word).* It counts only on the payee's accept, or the signal of a signer her account word names in `state.by`. A transfer that names no instance and that nobody has accepted is listed `unanswered`, its signer's own word, and never counted. A receiver who has nothing to answer (a basic phone's "received") signs her own `observe` of kind `received` (the one kind for "this much came"; a signer her account word names signs the same kind, `refs.subject` the transfer), `refs.for` the deal, `state.amount` what came, `state.line` where the deal has `lines`: her word against herself, counted toward that line, or toward the price when she is the payee, by the stage (folds.md, What is left, The other side).
    - **`forfeit`** *(promises.md, Settling).* It covers the payer's own stop only: kept when a stop that speaks for the paying side is among the first onward stops of the deal and none that speaks for the payee is. A no-show signs nothing; money against one is a transfer conditioned `without` her arrival on a clock both named.
    - **A refrain with no bench** is never settled and never breached; its stake moves only by a bench name's word after its clock closes.
12. **No tables in the protocol** *(core.md, section 3).* No Unicode table, no time zone database, no currency list, no list of units. Text is well-formed Unicode compared byte for byte; a unit is its author's text.
13. **A word that lets another act for her is signed twice** *(blocks.md, Finality; section 5).* A word that gives another key or person the power to act or spend for her (a key grant that brings a key in, a grant that is not a stop or an answer, an `account` word, a hand-over of a claim: a transfer that names in `refs.what` a deal or a claim of invasion) stands only when the same key has signed it again after that key's wait has run, or a voice above that key has signed it. Until then it is waiting. A word that only narrows (a key grant that only takes its signer's own key out or only leaves voices out, a stop, a stop of one thing) is not under this rule, nor is a key grant that adds only an encryption key: neither gives power to act or spend. A word above its voice's cap stands the same way: only when a voice above that key has signed it. So a thief who held a key briefly leaves none of these words standing, and a thief with an unlocked phone is held to its cap.
