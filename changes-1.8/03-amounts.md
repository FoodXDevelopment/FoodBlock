## 1. What this stage does

1. **Money is an amount.** One shape, `{ value, unit }` or `{ value, unit, of }`, for every good. The money shape, the currency grammar, the issuer unit, `decimals`, the ISO rule and the 2^53 cap on sums leave.
2. **One rule counts everything that is drawn on.** A sack's `pack`, an offer's quantity, a pot, what a deal owes and a grant's limit are all measures. Each owner's amount is drawn on along one line. A draw holds at once, lands on the receiver's yes, is freed only by her no or an end she has seen, and never takes past what is left.
3. **A payment is not a draw.** What comes in is a plain sum toward a line or a price, in its own kind. What came beyond is shown, never dropped.
4. **A transfer names an instance, or names none.** Cash, an M-Pesa balance and an unweighed pile move nothing on the record and count on the receiver's accept.
5. **A part is the same instance.** A make takes from what its owner owns, and its output is hers. A kind is a kind by its own block. A seller's word reaches only the part that passed through his hands.
6. Six of the oracle's seven separate counting codes become one function, linear along one line.
7. Stage 2's leftovers that wait on the starts: an export is whole up to each fold's own start (N1), and two of its three guesses are settled (2.10).

**Harry's sack.** Harry signs a sack, `pack` 1 kg. He signs 0.6 kg to Maria inside her order: it holds at once, and lands because her yes is already in its past. He signs 0.6 kg to Amara: only 0.4 is left, so it takes nothing (`past`), is never cut to 0.4, and Amara's money shows `uncovered`. He bakes with `inputs: [{ from: sack, amount: 0.4 kg }]`: it takes, and the loaf is his. A sale of "the sack" now takes nothing.

**Five sacks and five pounds.** Maria lends Harry five sacks of maize: her transfer draws on her lot and lands on his yes; his promise of six sacks back is a price with `of` the maize kind. Sam's mill lends Maria £100: the promise's `terms.quantity` is `{ "100", GBP }`, its price `{ "110", GBP }`; Sam's transfer names no instance and counts on Maria's accept; her £110 counts on Sam's. Both deals read `done` by the same rule. In 1.7 the pounds loan was never `done`.

**Mama Achieng's pile.** She never weighed it. Leila orders 2 kg; Mama's transfer names no instance (or names the pile, which states no `pack`: `no_measure`, read the same way). It is no draw, so it does not land: it counts toward Leila's line on Leila's `accepted` answer to it, or her own `received` word, because her order said what she wanted, not that it came. Mama's accepted answer to Leila's shillings states `covers: { "100", KES }`. Nobody is shown owning part of a pile nobody measured; the shillings move nothing on the record.

**E2. Lines 16 and 17.** Delete from "- an amount is `{ value, unit }`, `value` a decimal string" to "Money in different currencies is never added or compared by the protocol." Put in their place:

> - **An amount** is `{ value, unit }` or `{ value, unit, of }`: this much of a good. `value` is a decimal (core.md, section 3), zero or more where it is promised, moved or paid. `unit` is its author's own text (`kg`, `sack`, `head`, `part`, `GBP`, `KES`, `BTC`): the protocol keeps no list of units and none of monies, and a reader may read a unit by any table she trusts, on her own node. `of`, when given, is the id of a thing: the kind this is an amount of ("FAQ maize", "Maria's bread credit"). It is compared, never opened, and a reader need not hold it. **Two amounts are of one kind** when their `unit` is the same text, byte for byte, and their `of` is the same id, or neither has one. Amounts of one kind are added and compared exactly; amounts of two kinds are never added, compared or converted. A transfer's `amount` beside `refs.what`, and a thing's `pack`, carry no `of` (`shape`): their kind is the measure's own (for an instance, its `refs.of`). **An amount is added only within one measure:** to amounts of the same measure (the block its transfer names in `refs.what`), or, with no measure named, toward the one line or price its transfer is `for`. No fold adds across measures, so "what Harry is owed, £80" never adds to £80 in hand.
> - **Money is an amount.** The protocol has no money shape and names no good as money. Money is the most saleable good, the one people take in order to pass on (Menger, *On the Origins of Money*): which good that is, is each person's choice and each reader's reading. So wherever this spec states a price, a payment, a cap or a stake, it is an amount of any good. **A claim to money is not money:** what someone owes is a deal she signed, handed on by a hand-over of what is owed (promises.md, Owed). A unit word alone does not say whether an amount is the good itself or someone's promise of it; one who wants it said writes `of`, the id of the issuer's kind (a bread credit, a voucher). An amount with `of` and one without are two kinds, so a promise counts toward a plain price only by the payee's `covers` (Mises: a money substitute is a claim, taken at par only by each taker's own choice).
> - **A sum** a fold gives is an amount whose `value` is exact, printed in one form so two builds agree: the shortest decimal text, with no exponent, no leading zero but a single `0` before the point, no trailing zero after the point, no point when there is no fraction, `0` for nothing, and never `-0`. It has no cap: `integers` bounds a count and `seq`, never an amount. Nothing is rounded: where a weighed price does not come to a payable sum, the payee's own `covers` closes the gap (promises.md, Settling).

> ## What is left
>
> A **measure** is an amount its owner stated: a thing's or a circle's `pack`, an open offer's `terms.quantity` or that of a promise a grant names in `scope.on`, a promise's `terms.backing` with the pay-ins its holder accepted, a grant's `scope.limit`, what a side of a binding deal is owed. What each owner owns of a measure is drawn on along one line (A line). A draw holds its amount from where it is signed. It takes when it is above nothing and, with the draws before it on that line that take and are not freed, comes to no more than what its owner owns of the measure. A draw that does not fit takes nothing and is never cut down to what is left. A payment toward a price is not a draw: it is a plain sum of what the payee accepted, counted toward the line or price it is `for`.
>
> **One whole.** A thing that states no `pack` is one whole: a draw on it states no amount and takes all its owner owns of it. An amount drawn on it takes nothing of it (`no_measure`) and is read as a transfer that names no instance (things.md, Transfers). Nothing is converted: an amount of another kind than the measure takes nothing (`unit`).
>
> **Who owns it first** is said where the measure is stated: whom the thing's block speaks for (a thing made `as` a circle is the circle's; one made `as` a circle by a block no `sign` row holds for has no owner: circles.md, Rules); for an output, the owners of what the makes it names took, or of the thing a keep names (things.md, A make, Produce of a thing); the side a binding deal owes; whom an offer, a head or a grant speaks for. Nothing else makes an owner. A draw by one who owns none of the measure on that line takes nothing (`not_owner`); where she owned some and it does not fit, `past`.
>
> **Her start, and her one line.** An owner's **start** for a measure is the block that speaks for her (signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant) where she came to own her amount: the block that states it (her thing, her make, her first use, her offer, her head), her yes on which a draw to her lands, or her accept of a part passed to that key. An output is always the owner's of what was taken (things.md, A make); three cases say only when the record shows it:
>
> 1. **A make that speaks for her**, signed by a key of hers, or by a tool of hers under her grant so that it binds her (authority.md, Who a block speaks for, Blocks that bind): the outputs it names from her inputs are hers from that block, her start for each. No further yes is needed: the make is her own word.
> 2. **A make by someone else from her goods** (toll work: a miller, a baker, an abattoir; the produce of her hen written by a keeper she dealt with): the output is hers from her `accepted` answer to it, or her tool's, inside a deal that already binds her and for no more than it owes her (authority.md, Blocks that bind, way 4). That answer is her start.
> 3. **Any other claim that a thing is hers**, by one who holds no grant of hers and has no deal with her that names the output as made from her goods: nothing is hers on the record until she herself answers `accepted`. A stranger's word never starts her line.
>
> Until her start, no one is shown owning the output, and a maker's own block never gives the maker the output of another's inputs. Her line for that amount is the line of the key that signed her start. A draw on it signed by any other key, hers or anyone's, takes nothing (`not_on_line`). Another key of hers, a till or a tool draws only on a part passed to its own line: a draw `to_owner` its own owner, which the accept of that key lands and which starts that key's line for that part; or a grant that names the instance (A draw, below). So two devices selling from one stock each hold a stated part. Ground: "first" and "had not seen" are facts only on one line; across keys no reader can know which came first, and a guessed order binds no one (Mises: only persons act, and the order of her acts is the order she signed them in).
>
> **One start.** An accept that brings an amount onto a line (her accept of a draw to her, a key's accept of a part passed to it, a hand's accept of a hand-over, a take-up: circles.md, A circle's line) is that line's start for it, and an amount has one start. Where two such accepts of one draw, or two take-ups of one freed amount, by two keys that may each give it, do not see each other (neither in the other's causal past), neither starts a line: the yes is given, so the amount is its receiver's (hers; the circle's for a hand-over or take-up), but on no line, and every draw on it by either key takes nothing (`not_on_line`); each receiver of such a draw holds its signer's word as in a fork (A fork is a burden). A later block of a key that may give that accept, whose `after` names both, is the one start. This is two keys acting apart, perhaps two people: it proves no one lied, lists no `pair` and taints no later block (two blocks of one key on one `prev` or one `seq` stay a pair). With no clock, the two are seen only by a reader who holds both; a reader holding one reads it as the start. Who wants a start final names a clock whose one line orders the accepts (blocks.md, A named clock: the first it received is the start, and a draw on the other takes nothing, `not_on_line`), or keeps one key for accepting. Where another fact already fixes the line, it wins and no second start can arise: a grant's end frees to the line of the key that signed the grant (below); a circle that names `refs.meter` counts on the meter's line; a part a voice passes lands only on the key its key grant names (identity.md, A voice's cap). Ground: one measure keeps one line, and an order no reader can check is not read.
>
> **A draw** is a counted block that speaks for an owner, names a measure, and takes an amount of what she owns of it (all she owns of it when it states none): a transfer `to_owner`; a let-go to an open receiver; a make naming it in `state.inputs`; an allocation; a payout; a block under a limited grant, at its `state.draws`; a grant passed on, at its own `limit`; a grant that names an instance in `refs.what`, at its `scope.limit`: the part it passes to its holder; a grant that names a promise in `scope.on`, at all of its quantity she has not drawn, passed to the grant's line (authority.md, Her quantity is the measure, Toll work). **A grant's draw never lands:** it names the grant's holder, and the part stays hers, held on her line and drawn on the grant's line by blocks that bind her, until it is freed (below), and then only what the grant's line has not drawn comes back. **What a tool holds for her:** what a tool makes or receives for her under her grant is held on the grant's line as hers, part of what that grant holds, exactly as a part passed to the grant is. **A hand-back:** a tool may always hand back to its principal what it holds for her, by a draw `to_owner` her of an amount on the grant's line. It needs no promise in `scope.on`, no type in `scope.types` beyond the grant's own existence, and no room under the limit, because it gives nothing away and only returns her own (authority.md, Blocks that bind, A hand-back). It lands on her own key's accept, which starts that key's line for the part (One start). It is not a case of No one is both sides (authority.md): no one else is a side. **At the grant's end** (an end that stands: her stop, its `until`, the removal of the tool's key), what is still held on its line and is in no draw that has landed or still holds for another is freed to her, on the line of the key that signed the grant, its one start (One start): a later draw by that key that has the end in its causal past may draw on it, any other key's takes nothing (`not_on_line`), and until the end is seen as the cut requires (blocks.md, The cut), such a draw is waiting (`on: end`). While the grant stands, her own key never draws on the tool's line (`not_on_line`): one measure, one line. She gets it by the tool's hand-back or by ending the grant. What a hand holds for a circle is held, handed between hands and freed by this same rule, at her seat's end in place of the grant's end (circles.md, A circle's line). A transfer of zero is no draw. The first taker after a let-go is no draw and keeps its own rule (Ownership).
>
> - **Holds.** From where it is signed on the line, a draw's amount is out of what its owner may draw on. Her own signed word counts against her at once (core.md, section 10).
> - **Lands.** The amount becomes the receiver's where the draw stands (blocks.md, Finality) and every word it names is in the causal past: her yes and its condition, if it carries one. **Her yes** is read from blocks, never from a deal's stage (which reads this fold): her own counted `accepted` answer to the draw; or, in the draw's own causal past, her own counted `accepted` answer to the deal's first promise the draw is `for`, or her own first promise of that deal that the draw's signer accepted. **A draw whose receiver's yes is already in its own causal past lands where it is signed** (provisional, awaiting the owner): a word acts on what is its signer's own when signed. This meets Finality: any end of the draw names the draw, so it has her yes in its past and frees nothing (Freed); nothing its signer can sign later sets it aside, so the cut has nothing to wait for. `stands` still makes such a draw wait on everything else it reads: a `wait`, a `reach`, a second signing (`confirm`), a block it names for its meaning that does not stand (`named`), the hands of a circle (`hands`), and its condition. Where her yes is not yet in its past, it waits for that yes, which, as a relier's block that answers it, also passes the cut. A draw that names no receiver lands where it stands. Her accepted answer may state `state.amount`, no more than the draw's: that much lands, and the rest is freed; one that states more, or another kind, is read as stating none. Nothing fills her silence: until her yes, no one is shown owning what is held.
> - **Freed.** The amount is its owner's again, for her later draws that have the freeing in their causal past, by the receiver's own counted `declined` or `stopped` answer, or by an end of the draw that the receiver has seen before any yes (blocks.md, The cut). An end she has not seen frees nothing yet: a later draw counting on it is waiting (`on: end`). Once she answers, or a block of hers sees the end, a waiting draw is read as any draw: it takes only with the freeing in its own causal past, so one signed before the freeing reads `past`, and its signer signs again. A no or an end that has her yes in its past frees nothing. An end of a draw is its signer's counted stop of it, or a time it states (`expires`) read only against the receiver's own dated word: her block dated at or after it has seen it, and her accept dated at or after it is late and no yes.
> - **What landed stays landed** (blocks.md, Finality): a payment reversed after the draw landed and stood is a sum the receiver owes the giver, and never takes goods from a later buyer.
>
> **One rule, read along the line.** From her start, in `seq` order, each draw of hers on the measure is read once, against a running amount; no fold compares every pair of draws. What she owns of it at a draw is what she first owned, plus the pay-ins she accepted and the landed draws to her whose start is on this line and in the draw's causal past, less the earlier draws on her line that take and are not freed there. The draw takes when its amount is above nothing and no more than that. One that does not fit takes nothing (`past`). It is never cut down to what is left: that would write a deal nobody signed. It stays its signer's word, listed with its reason. A draw that would fit only once an end she has not seen frees an earlier draw is waiting. A draw that took nothing never takes later; its signer signs again. No fold orders draws by hash, by the size of a causal past, or by arrival. Ground: you cannot give what you no longer have, and you did not promise more than you said; both are one subtraction (Rothbard, *The Ethics of Liberty*, ch. 19: title passes only by its owner's word).
>
> A fold counts a draw only on its owner's line read whole from the draw back to her start (A line).
>
> **What is taken stays in the measure or leaves it.** A draw on a thing, on a circle or on what is owed stays: the receiver owns that amount of the same measure, the same id, marks and readings. A make, a let-go, a payout, an allocation and a block under a grant leave: what a make took is ended (eaten, spoiled, made into its outputs); what a let-go took is no one's until a first use takes it (things.md, Letting go); the rest is counted on the other side.
>
> **Each reader of a measure says only what states it, who owns it first, what draws on it, and what a draw that does not take means there.** Ownership: the receiver got nothing, and what she paid is `uncovered`. An open offer: the allocation still binds the offerer and is shown `over_allocated`. A pot: the payout is the holder's word, not the pot's. What is owed: the hand-over hands on nothing. A grant: authority.md. A circle's parts: circles.md. This is the one rule; each place points here.
>
> **The other side.** A draw is one side of a hand-over. The other side is what it counts toward: a line, a price, a pot's pay-ins. That side is a plain sum, in its own kind, of what its receiver accepted, or of what her accepted answer says it covers (`state.covers`) where that is more. **One payment counts once, at the most its receiving side has said of it:** of her counted words about one transfer (each `accepted` answer, which says the transfer's amount or the `state.amount` it states in that kind, no more than the transfer's; each answer's `covers`; or, with none, the transfer's amount as a signer she named counted it), the one that says most counts, and only once: two answers of 10 l and 20 l to one transfer count 20 l. No one else's word raises it. **A receiver with nothing to answer** (a basic phone's "received") signs her own `observe` of kind `received`, `refs.for` the deal, `state.amount` what came, and `state.line` where the deal has `lines`: her word against herself, added toward that line, or toward the price where she is the payee, as an accepted transfer is. It has no status, so it accepts nothing. It has no order and refuses nothing: what came beyond what was owed is shown (`uncovered`), never dropped. A transfer that names a measure counts toward a line only when it lands as a draw. A transfer that names none has only this side: it counts on the receiver's accept, or the signal of a signer her account word names (promises.md, Where to pay me). A transfer that names an instance counts toward a price only where the price states an `of` and the instance's `refs.of` is that id; toward a price with no `of` it counts only for what the payee's accepted answer says it covers. Each half of a deal is counted this one way: toward what moves, and toward the price.
>
> Each reader lists the draws it read as `draws: [{ block, by, to, amount, takes, why, state }]`, sorted by block: `by` the owner it speaks for, `to` the receiver (null for none, `open` for a let-go), `amount` what it states (null for all), `takes` true or false, `why` one of `past`, `not_on_line`, `pair`, `unit`, `no_measure`, `not_owner` when it takes nothing, else null, `state` one of `held`, `landed`, `freed` when it takes, else null. A draw that is waiting is listed there and in `waiting: [{ block, on }]`.
